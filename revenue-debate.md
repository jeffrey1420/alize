# Alizé Revenue Model Debate
**Debater:** Revenue Model Debater
**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Purpose:** Challenge assumptions in the pricing structure that the previous pulse didn't cover

---

## Assumption 1: The Pilot Setup Fee (€3,000-6,000) Is the Right Entry Price

### Current View

The pilot is €3,000-6,000 setup with the first month included. This is positioned as "real investment but manageable for a 50-100 person company."

### Problem

The range is too wide. €3,000 vs. €6,000 is a 2x difference. A sales team presenting "€3,000 to €6,000" to a French PME buyer creates ambiguity that kills deals. Buyers default to the lower number, then feel deceived when the scope conversation pushes them to the upper bound.

More critically: at €3,000-6,000 for a pilot that includes the first month of managed service, Alizé is essentially asking for a €3,000-6,000 commitment before proving ROI. The pilot IS the proof of concept — but the fee structure treats it as a product purchase.

### Alternative

Fix the pilot at a single price: **€4,500 all-in** (setup + first month). This is psychologically simpler: one number, one line item. Include explicit deliverables: one agent, one workflow, connected to 2-4 tools, 30-day monitoring. If the client wants more, that's a change order at €1,500/day equivalent.

The €4,500 price is:
- Below the psychological threshold of "this is a big project" (€5k+)
- Above "this is a trial" (sub-€1k)
- Consistent with French ESN day rates (€600-900/day, so 5-7 days of consulting work)

### Recommendation

Replace the range with a single fixed price: **€4,500 pilot setup (first month included)**. Publish it. No negotiation. That's a positioning signal: "we know what this is worth."

## Assumption 2: The Managed Service at €800-1,500/Month Is Priced to Signal Premium Without Proving Value

### Current View

The managed service is €800-1,500/month per agent. The range is explained as "depending on complexity." This positions Alizé as premium but creates the same ambiguity problem as the pilot fee.

### Problem

Two problems at this price point:

**1. No anchor for what's included.** "Per agent" with no definition of agent capabilities means the buyer has no reference point. Is €800/month for a customer service agent the same as €800/month for a financial analyst agent? Same price, vastly different value delivery.

**2. The price doesn't signal "premium" — it signals "expensive."** At €800-1,500/month, a French DG asks: "What does €1,500/month get me that I can't get from Agentova at €80/month?" The answer is governance, management, and integration depth — but these aren't visible in the price itself.

**3. At 50-200 person companies, the budget owner often needs to justify the expense to themselves or a partner.** A flat "€1,200/month" is easier to present than "€800-1,500 depending on what we discover." Simplicity wins in B2B SaaS at this segment.

### Alternative

**Fixed tiers, no range. Sell capabilities, not agents.**

- **Starter:** 1 workflow — €1,200/month
- **Business:** 3 workflows — €2,800/month
- **ETI:** Unlimited + SLA + account manager — €5,500/month

This is what the pricing agent recommended in Pulse 1. The key shift: the buyer understands what "3 workflows" means (they can name them). They can't name "one agent."

### Recommendation

Replace per-agent billing with workflow-tier billing. Fix the prices. No range. Publish them. The €800-1,500/month range serves the sales team, not the buyer.

## Assumption 3: The 3-Month Minimum Commitment Protects Revenue

### Current View

The managed service has a "3-month minimum commitment." The assumption: this protects Alizé from churn at month 2.

### Problem

A 3-month minimum creates disproportionate buyer anxiety at the 50-200 person company level. The DG signs a pilot, likes the results, then hears "3-month commitment at €1,200/month = €3,600 total." The procurement conversation starts: "Can we do month-to-month after the first 3?" The answer has to be yes or no — and if it's no, you've introduced a friction point in a relationship you're trying to build trust in.

More importantly: the commitment doesn't protect revenue if the client is unhappy. A client who feels trapped at month 2 will cancel at month 3 and bad-mouth you. A client who chooses to stay month-to-month at month 4 is a more valuable reference than one who was contractually obligated to stay.

### Alternative

**Remove the minimum commitment. Replace with a 30-day pilot period, then month-to-month.**

The logic: the pilot (€4,500) already filters for commitment. If the client did the pilot and wants to continue, they're demonstrating intent. A month-to-month model at this price point:
- Reduces buyer anxiety significantly (no lock-in fear)
- Creates implicit pressure to deliver value every month (no coasting)
- Makes cancellation conversations happen when there's a problem, not when the contract ends — giving Alizé a chance to save the account

If revenue protection is critical, use an **annual prepay option** (2 months free if paying annually) rather than a minimum commitment. This is common in French SaaS and creates voluntary loyalty.

### Recommendation

Drop the 3-month minimum. Go month-to-month after pilot. Add annual prepay discount as an incentive.

## Assumption 4: The Multi-Agent ETI Package (€2,500-4,000/month) Is a Real Product

### Current View

The ETI package offers up to 5 agents, centralized dashboard, monthly steering committee, priority support, custom reporting. Setup: €8,000-15,000. Monthly: €2,500-4,000/month.

### Problem

This tier is aspirational fiction. Alizé has zero ETI clients, no demonstrated ability to run 5 simultaneous agents for a 250-500 person company, no steering committee format, no custom reporting infrastructure. The tier exists in the pricing table but not in reality.

**The specific problems:**
- **Setup at €8,000-15,000**: A French ETI of 250-500 employees has a procurement process. €8,000+ setup requires legal review, possibly board approval, and a vendor onboarding process that takes 2-3 months. Alizé can't deliver this without a sales process.
- **Monthly steering committee**: This implies an Alizé employee sitting in a video call once a month with the client's leadership. At €2,500-4,000/month, one steering committee per client consumes significant billable time. This doesn't scale.
- **Custom reporting**: This requires a BI/reporting layer that doesn't exist yet.

### Alternative

**Remove the ETI package from the public pricing page.** Keep it as a "contact us" conversation. There's no shame in saying "for ETI deployments, contact us to discuss" — this is how French enterprise SaaS companies handle custom pricing anyway.

The risk of publishing €2,500-4,000/month is that it anchors Alizé as expensive before the buyer understands the value. If the first number they see is €2,500/month and their budget is €1,000/month, the conversation ends before it starts.

### Recommendation

Remove ETI tier from published pricing. Keep it for direct conversations. Publish only: Diagnostic (free or €490), Pilot (€4,500), Starter (€1,200/month), Business (€2,800/month).
