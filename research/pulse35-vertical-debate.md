# Vertical Challenge — Pulse 35

## The Assumption Challenged

**D126/D145 claim:** Expert-comptables (accounting firms) are the right first vertical because:
1. Governance gaps make Microsoft Copilot insufficient for regulated data handling
2. Expert-comptables have compliance-sensitive workflows where Copilot's data residency and EU AI Act gaps are *disqualifying*
3. This vertical is conditionally valid — *only if* Louis has 5+ warm expert-comptable contacts (D145)

**The assumption it rests on:** Louis's actual network map will reveal 5+ warm expert-comptable contacts. Therefore the expert-comptable vertical is network-validated rather than theoretically imposed.

**The hidden assumption (that nobody challenged):** That D145's condition — Louis having 5+ warm expert-comptable contacts — is a *likely* condition, not a *fictional* one. D145 was written before U52 (Louis's network ICP map) was resolved. The conditional framing made the decision feel validated when it was only *hypothetically* validated.

---

## The Flaw

**The expert-comptable vertical was chosen based on theoretical appeal, not Louis's actual network map.**

Here is the chain of contradictions:

**1. D135 says "go where Grinto relationships exist first."**
But Grinto is a **web development agency**. Grinto's relationships are web agencies, e-commerce companies, digital product businesses — companies that needed a website or web app. These are not expert-comptables. The instruction to follow Grinto relationships leads *away* from accounting firms.

**2. D127 directly undermines D126's core rationale.**
D127 states: "governance gap overstated for 70% of ICP." If Copilot's governance gap only disqualifies it for 30% of the market, then governance cannot be the *primary* reason to pick expert-comptables as the vertical. D126 picked expert-comptables *because* of governance (Copilot can't handle regulated client data). D127 says that rationale only applies to 30% of prospects. This is not a minor contradiction — it means the vertical was chosen for a reason that doesn't hold for most of the market.

**3. U52 is still unresolved after 35+ pulses.**
The TODO.md shows U52 (Louis's warm network ICP map) as a critical unresolved gate that "blocks all GTM and vertical decisions." It has been listed as unresolved since at least Pulse 20. D145 made the expert-comptable vertical *conditional* on Louis having 5+ warm contacts in that space — but nobody has checked whether that condition is actually met. You cannot validate a conditional decision until the condition is evaluated.

**4. Louis's actual network context is Grinto (web agency internship) + MyDigitalSchool + co-founded web agency with Maëli and Gabin.**
None of these connections are expert-comptables. The web agency co-founded with Maëli and Gabin is almost certainly targeting the same web agency / digital product space — not accounting firms. Louis has spent his time in digital/tech circles, not in accounting firm networks.

**5. The vertical was likely chosen because it *sounds* defensible in a GTM debate, not because Louis's network maps to it.**
Expert-comptables are a convenient theoretical vertical: regulated, compliance-sensitive, Microsoft already has a gap there, they refer clients to each other. But a vertical is only real if the seller can actually reach warm contacts in it. A theoretically perfect vertical that requires cold outreach to build is a Month 9+ channel, not a Month 1-3 beachhead.

## Evidence

### Evidence 1: The D126/D127 Contradiction Is Fatal to the Governance Rationale

D126's explicit justification for expert-comptables: *"Governance gap is disqualifying for Copilot."*

D127's explicit finding: *"Governance gap overstated for 70% of ICP."*

If D127 is correct, Copilot's governance limitations only matter for 30% of the ICP. The core rationale for choosing expert-comptables — that governance disqualifies Copilot for this vertical — is only a strong reason to choose expert-comptables if:
- Expert-comptables are part of that 30% where governance *is* a hard gate, AND
- Louis has warm relationships in that 30%

Point 1 is plausible (accounting firms do handle sensitive client financial data). Point 2 is **entirely unvalidated**. Louis's documented network is Grinto (web agency), MyDigitalSchool (digital marketing school), and a web agency co-founded with Maëli and Gabin. These are not accounting firm connections.

### Evidence 2: D135 and D126 Are Pointing in Opposite Directions

D135 says: *"Go where Grinto relationships exist first."*

D126 says: *"Expert-comptables are the first vertical."*

Grinto is a web development agency. Its client relationships are companies that build digital products, run e-commerce stores, or need web/software development. If Louis follows D135 (follow the Grinto relationships) he ends up at web agencies, digital product companies, and e-commerce businesses — not accounting firms.

The two decisions are in direct conflict. One of them needs to give.

### Evidence 3: Budget — Do Expert-Comptables Actually Have €1,500/Month?

The pricing debate (D167, D38) established that €2,000/month retainer is not economically viable (cost-plus minimum is ~€7,963/month at realistic delivery costs). Even at €1,200-1,500/month, expert-comptables are being asked to pay a premium price for a service that:
- Their clients (PME/ETI) are already skeptical about AI spending (50% of AI adopters use only free tools)
- Their own firms are typically small (5-20 employees) and cost-conscious
- They have existing software relationships (Pennylane, Cegid, Sage) that are actively embedding AI features

Expert-comptables are not high-spenders by default. They are SMEs. The French accounting firm market is highly competitive, margins are compressed, and many small firms are actively using Pennylane or other SaaS tools that already claim to handle compliance. The "governance gap" that supposedly makes Copilot insufficient may be filled by their existing software vendor already.

### Evidence 4: U52 — The Network Map That Was Never Done

From the TODO.md, U52 reads: *"Louis warm network ICP map — map Louis's warm contacts by ICP-fit, company size, decision-maker access before any outreach."*

This has been listed as a critical gate since at minimum Pulse 20, and possibly earlier. It appears in the unresolved section as **U127** in Pulse 27, **U52** in Pulse 14, and was flagged as critical in Pulse 9, Pulse 12, and numerous times since. After 35+ research pulses, Louis has not mapped his warm network against ICP.

This means: every decision about which vertical to pursue has been made *without* the prerequisite information. D126 (expert-comptables as first vertical) was decided before U52 was resolved. D145 (vertical conditional on 5+ warm contacts) was also decided before U52 was resolved. These are conditional decisions that have never had their conditions evaluated.

### Evidence 5: The "Conditional" Framing Is a False Safety Net

D145 says the expert-comptable vertical is valid *only if* Louis has 5+ warm expert-comptable contacts. This sounds rigorous. But it is only rigorous if:
1. Someone actually checks whether Louis has those contacts
2. The check happens before commercial resources are deployed

Neither has happened. U52/U127/U136 have been listed as "STILL CRITICAL" and "STILL UNRESOLVED" across dozens of pulses. The conditional framing has functioned as a way to make D126 feel validated in the abstract while kicking the actual validation to a task that was never completed.

## Recommended Alternative

**Louis's actual warm network points toward web agencies / digital product companies — follow D135 and pursue that vertical first.**

If D135 ("go where Grinto relationships exist first") is correct, then Louis's first vertical is:
- **Web agencies** (Maëli and Gabin's web agency, Grinto's existing client relationships, MyDigitalSchool alumni network)
- **E-commerce businesses** (Grinto's client base, likely includes e-commerce companies that needed web development)

Why this fits better:

1. **Louis has actual relationships here.** He co-founded a web agency with Maëli and Gabin. He interned at Grinto. His MyDigitalSchool network is full of people working in digital agencies and e-commerce. This is the *actual* warm network.

2. **Web agencies have real operational pain.** They manage multiple client websites, handle content updates, manage domain renewals, handle customer support tickets, do reporting for clients. These are repetitive admin workflows — the exact use case Alizé is built for.

3. **No governance justification required.** Web agencies are not regulated. This sidesteps the D126/D127 contradiction entirely. Alizé doesn't need Copilot's governance gaps to matter — it just needs to be clearly better than the manual alternative.

4. **Web agencies can afford €1,500/month.** More importantly, they can justify it to themselves: an AI agent that handles client website updates, ticket routing, or reporting is a direct labor cost replacement. The ROI story is concrete: "this agent does what your CDO does for 20% of their time, at 10% of the cost."

5. **Louis can credibly sell to web agencies.** He understands the domain. He speaks the language. He knows what a web agency's operations look like from the inside. This is a prerequisite for D84 (Louis has a sales credibility ceiling) — selling to people who don't respect your domain expertise is a fast path to zero closes.

**The revised sequence:**

| Step | Action | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | Map U52 — but filter specifically for web agencies and digital product companies | If Louis's network actually has 5+ warm web agency contacts, that validates this alternative |
| 2 | If web agency contacts exist: define one specific workflow (e.g., "client ticket triage + escalation routing") | Narrower than "admin workflows" — one task, measurable ROI |
| 3 | Pilot 1: One web agency (Maëli/Gabin co-founded agency, or Grinto client) | Internal warmth, Louis understands the domain, no credibility gap |
| 4 | Pilot 2-3: MyDigitalSchool alumni at other agencies | Warm-ish through school network |
| 5 | Month 4+: Expert-comptables via association referrals | Only if warm network in accounting emerges, not as pre-planned vertical |

**What to kill:**
- D126 as a pre-determined vertical — expert-comptables are not validated by Louis's network
- The governance-gap rationale for vertical selection — D127 already undermined it for 70% of ICP, and Louis's network doesn't map to regulated industries anyway
- Treating U52 as "still being resolved" — it needs to actually be resolved before any more vertical decisions are made

**What to keep:**
- D135 ("go where Grinto relationships exist first") — this is the most network-grounded guidance in the entire GTM research
- D145's conditional framing — vertical should follow network mapping, not precede it
- The overall GTM motion (warm network first, then cold outbound, then partners)
