Each of these actions moves Alizé from research phase to commitment phase — and commitment phase forces the entity question into the open.

If Louis's internal model already knows he has a legal blocker, avoidance of those three actions is **rational**, not pathological.

**The entity question is the meta-blocker that makes all other blockers downstream of it irrelevant.**

## Challenge 3: Louis's Alizé Work May Already Belong to Grinto

### Assumption Being Challenged
The research process treats Alizé's positioning, workflows, and deliverables as Louis's intellectual property that he can commercialize.

### Why the Assumption Is Wrong or Incomplete

**Standard French employment contracts include IP assignment clauses.** This is the default under French labor law (Code de la propriété intellectuelle, L.111-1 and L.113-9). When an employee creates something within the scope of their employment, the employer owns the IP.

Louis is currently an intern/employee at Grinto. The question the pulse process has never asked:

> *"Did Louis create Alizé's research, positioning, workflows, and strategy within the scope of his Grinto employment?"*

If Louis conducted the Alizé research during Grinto hours, on Grinto equipment, or using Grinto resources — or if his employment contract includes a broad "work for hire" or " inventions" clause — then **Grinto may legally own all of Alizé's foundational IP.**

This is not a theoretical risk. The debate log itself shows:
- Grinto ring-fencing was flagged as a concern (D119)
- Grinto conflict was flagged as a structural issue (D74, U87)
- The debate log asked whether Louis's side venture is permitted under his Grinto contract (U116)

But the pulse process treated these as **timeline risks** (will Grinto delay Alizé launch?) rather than **IP ownership risks** (does Grinto own Alizé entirely?).

### What This Means for the Decision Tree

If Grinto owns the Alizé IP:
- Louis cannot sell, license, or transfer Alizé's positioning and workflows to any third party
- Any commercial agreement that includes those deliverables is potentially actionable by Grinto
- A co-founder who joins Alizé is joining a company that may have no defensible IP
- The "delivery playbook" (D88, identified as the technical moat) may belong to Grinto

**This is not a risk to mitigate. It is a question that requires a yes/no answer before any commercial activity.**

### Recommendation

Louis must ask Grinto directly, or have a lawyer review his contract: *"Does my employment agreement assign IP from side projects to Grinto? Specifically, does it cover projects developed outside work hours using non-Grinto resources?"*

If the answer is anything other than "clearly no," Louis needs either:
1. An explicit written carve-out from Grinto for Alizé, OR
2. A clean restart where Alizé's IP is developed after Grinto separation

## Challenge 4: The Kuroba Auto-Entrepreneur Cannot Sign AI Agent Service Agreements for Regulated SMEs

### Assumption Being Challenged
The debate log treats Kuroba (Louis's web agency, which he co-founded) as an existing legal entity that could potentially be used for Alizé commercial activity.

### Why the Assumption Is Wrong or Incomplete

**Auto-entrepreneur registrations in France are activity-specific.** The INSEE classification system (Code APE/NAF) assigns a primary activity code at registration. Auto-entrepreneurs must operate within their registered activity scope.

Louis registered as auto-entrepreneur for **Kuroba**, which is a web agency. The NAF code for web development is likely 62.01Z (Computer programming activities) or 63.11Z (Data processing, hosting and related activities).

**AI agent services for regulated SMEs** involve:
- Deploying AI systems with business integration
- Potentially handling client data under GDPR
- Ongoing operational responsibility for automated workflows
- Potentially regulated activity under EU AI Act (if the agents make consequential decisions)

These activities may fall outside the Kuroba auto-entrepreneur registration scope. This has two implications:

1. **Wrong activity code:** Louis's auto-entrepreneur entity is registered for web development, not AI agent services. Operating Alizé under the Kuroba auto-entrepreneur may constitute activity outside the registered scope — a legal irregularity.

2. **Wrong entity type for the risk:** Auto-entrepreneur status provides no limited liability protection. If Louis signs an AI service agreement for a regulated SME as a auto-entrepreneur, he is personally liable for any failures, data breaches, or consequential damages. Alizé's positioning explicitly promises governance, security, and measurable outcomes — Louis would be personally guaranteeing these as an individual auto-entrepreneur.

### What the Evidence Suggests

The debate log treated Kuroba as an asset (D192-D195: Kuroba as social proof, internal deployment target) without ever asking whether the Kuroba entity structure is appropriate for AI agent services.

The fact that Kuroba is a web agency (confirmed: "web agency" in D192, D194) while Alizé is an AI agent service suggests the activity codes do not match.

### Recommendation

Before using the Kuroba auto-entrepreneur for any Alizé commercial activity:
1. Confirm the Kuroba NAF/APE code
2. Consult on whether AI agent service delivery is covered
3. Consider whether a new legal entity (SAS, SARL, or EI) is required for Alizé's specific risk profile

## Challenge 5: The Binary Choice Framing Is a Category Error

### Assumption Being Challenged
The binary choice — "Alizé is a startup vs. side-project" (U91) — is the fundamental unresolved question blocking execution.

### Why the Assumption Is Wrong or Incomplete

**The binary choice is a category error.** The real question is not "what kind of project is Alizé?" but **"can Louis legally BE Alizé?"**

The startup vs. side-project framing assumes Louis has the option to choose. The legal reality may remove that choice entirely:

- If Grinto's IP assignment clauses cover Alizé's work → Louis cannot commercialize Alizé without Grinto's consent
- If the Kuroba auto-entrepreneur cannot cover AI agent services → Louis cannot sign commercial agreements under that entity
- If Louis has no registered Alizé entity → Louis cannot sign anything as "Alizé"

The binary choice is only meaningful if Louis has the legal capacity to make it. The pulse process has been asking Louis to choose between two options when three legal constraints may have already made that choice for him.

### Why This Explains 45+ Days of Non-Execution Better Than "Commitment Failure"

The commitment failure narrative requires assuming Louis is:
- Aware of his options
- Capable of executing
- Choosing not to

The legal structure narrative explains the same behavior with:
- Louis may be unconsciously recognizing that he lacks the legal infrastructure to proceed
- His avoidance of U163, U91, and U52 is avoidance of actions that would force the entity question
- The research process has been providing him with a socially acceptable reason to delay ( "I need more information before committing") when the real blocker (legal structure) has no research solution

### What This Means for the Decision Tree

If Louis cannot legally operate Alizé, the binary choice is moot. The decision tree for Alizé becomes:

```
Can Louis legally operate Alizé?
├── YES → Proceed with commercial planning; buy domain; declare binary choice
├── NO (Grinto IP) → Negotiate carve-out or delay until Grinto separation
├── NO (wrong entity) → Register appropriate legal structure first
└── NO (both) → Resolve both before any commercial activity
```

### Recommendation

**Louis must answer these questions in order before any commercial planning:**

1. **Grinto IP question:** Does my employment agreement assign side-project IP to Grinto?
2. **Entity question:** What legal entity is Alizé? (SAS, SARL, auto-entrepreneur, or none?)
3. **Activity scope question:** Does the chosen entity's registration cover AI agent services for regulated SMEs?
4. **Personal liability question:** What is Louis's personal liability exposure under each structure?
5. **Timing question:** How long does each resolution take, and what can be done in parallel?

The correct hierarchy is:

```
LEGAL STRUCTURE (gate)
    ↓
CAN LOUIS SIGN AGREEMENTS AS ALIZÉ? ──NO──→ STOP. Resolve entity first.
    ↓ YES

WHAT ENTITY STRUCTURE? ──────────────────→ Determines tax, liability, commercial capacity
    ↓
THEN: Binary choice, domain, network map, GTM motion, pricing
```

The pulse process has been making decisions at step 5 while step 1 remains unanswered.
