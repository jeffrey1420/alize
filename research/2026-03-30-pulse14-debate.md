## What Was Debated

Three independent specialist agents challenged assumptions in three areas:

1. **Louis GTM Capacity** — Whether Louis's warm network and sales capability can actually generate 6-8 pilots in Year 1
2. **Delivery Partner Model** — Whether the Louis-delivers-Month-1-then-contractor model is realistic
3. **Pilot Pricing Economics** — Whether the €4,500 pilot fee covers delivery costs and creates the right incentive structure

## Agent 1: Louis GTM Limitation (pulse-debate-louis-gtm-limitation.md)

### Challenged Assumption: "Louis IS the GTM motion" (D26)

**Core finding:** Louis-as-GTM is conditionally valid for first 2-3 pilots (Month 1-3), NOT the Year 1 primary channel. The warm network has a natural ceiling that hasn't been acknowledged.

### Key Arguments

**Network-to-ICP mismatch:** Louis's network likely skews toward digital/e-commerce (per Pulse 6), but D44 selected professional services as the first vertical. These don't overlap cleanly. The math requires 75-160 discovery calls to sign 6-8 pilots — at 3-5 calls/week, that's 15-32 weeks of full-time prospecting.

**Sales skill gap:** Louis is a developer. €4,500-€10,000 consultative sales requires discovery, scoping, objection handling, and closing — distinct skills not identified in any brief. The "qui a déjà fait ça?" (who else has done this?) problem is unsolvable for a first-time founder with zero reference clients.

**Warm network exhaustion:** After Month 5-6, the warm network is depleted. Cold outbound — which has 2-3% reply rates for unknown brands — becomes the fallback with no case studies to support it. No transition plan exists.

**12-month commitment unsellable:** D58 requires a DG to sign a 12-month contract with a Year-1 startup with no references. The DG conversation collapses at the first objection.

### Decisions Reached

| ID | Topic | Decision |
|----|-------|----------|
| D59 | Louis GTM timeline | Warm network is Month 1-3 only, not Year 1 primary. Explicit ceiling identified. |
| D60 | Network mapping gate | U28 must resolve before Month 1. If <30 ICP-fit warm contacts exist, restate Year 1 target downward. |
| D61 | Kill D58 for Year 1 | 12-month commitment tier deferred to Year 2. Year 1 default: 3-month initial + month-to-month after. |
| D62 | Bridge channel plan | Define 2-3 alternative channels activated in Month 4-6 when warm network depletes. |

### Unresolved
- How many of Louis's warm contacts actually fit the ICP? (U28)
- Does Louis have any sales training or experience?
- What is the realistic close rate for an unknown brand, first-time founder?
- What happens in Month 6 when warm network is exhausted?

## Agent 2: Delivery Partner Model (pulse-debate-delivery.md)

### Challenged Assumption: "Louis delivers Month 1 manually + documents, contractor executes Month 2+" (D23)

**Core finding:** D23 is incomplete — Louis delivering Month 1 alone is a recipe for a technically successful pilot that commercially fails. The first pilot's primary job isn't technical, it's credibility.

### Key Arguments

**Wrong person for the first pilot:** The pilot needs a business consultant for process mapping, change management, and stakeholder management — not a developer. Louis can build; he probably can't consult. These are different professions.

**Change management is 80% of why AI projects fail in SMEs.** The delivery challenge isn't building the agent — it's getting desk workers to actually use it. That requires a skill set Louis almost certainly doesn't have.

**"Document everything" doesn't transfer tacit knowledge.** Louis will make dozens of implicit decisions during Month 1. The playbook will capture what he did, not why he did it or what he'd do differently next time.

**Minimum viable delivery team for Month 1:**
- Delivery lead: French-speaking business consultant, client-facing, process mapping — needed from Day 1
- Technical executor: Louis, agent build and integration

### Recommendation
Partner Louis with a delivery specialist for Month 1. Cost: €500-800/day, 3-5 days total = €2,000-4,000 investment protecting a €4,500 pilot fee and case study.

### New Decisions
| ID | Topic | Decision |
|----|-------|----------|
| D63 | Delivery partner in Month 1 | A delivery lead (consultant) is required from Day 1, not Month 2. Louis cannot solo-deliver the first pilot. |
| D64 | Delivery milestones | Define explicit non-technical milestones: Day 7 process map signed off, Day 21 adoption check, Day 28 ROI document. |

### Unresolved
- Is Gabin qualified as delivery lead? (U41 — still unresolved)
- Who is the delivery partner if not Gabin?

## Agent 3: Pilot Pricing Economics (pulse-debate-pricing-economics.md)

### Challenged Assumption: "€4,500 pilot fee is at-cost + case study rights" (D24)

**Core finding:** The €4,500 fee does not cover realistic delivery costs. At €300/day × 15 days = €4,500, which is below market for a qualified implementation specialist (€400-600/day). The model only works if Louis is the delivery mechanism — which contradicts D31.

### Key Arguments

**Unit economics don't close at realistic day rates:**
- €450/day (realistic specialist rate) × 15 days = €6,750 delivery cost
- Pilot fee = €4,500
- Subsidy per pilot = -€2,250
- Louis's time is the subsidy — but D31 says Louis can't solo-deliver

**Selection bias:** The €4,500 price attracts cost-conscious buyers running a beauty contest, not transformation-committed DGs. It filters for "can afford €5K," not "ready to sign 12-month."

**12-month commitment (D54/D58) may reduce conversion:** A DG asked to sign a 12-month contract with a Year-1 startup with no references will push back. The commitment requirement is Alizé protecting itself — but it signals distrust to the buyer.

**Day 15 payment risk:** By Day 15, Alizé has delivered 70-80% of the work. The second €2,250 is due at the worst moment — when the client is deciding on the managed service and has every incentive to dispute or delay.

### Recommendations
1. Raise pilot fee to €6,500-7,500 for standard scope
2. Restructure to 70/30 payment (kickoff/acceptance), not 50/50 Day 15
3. Replace 12-month commitment with 6-month initial + 30-day exit clause after Month 3
4. Channel-differentiated pricing: €4,500 for warm network (first 5 pilots), €7,500+ for cold outbound

### New Decisions
| ID | Topic | Decision |
|----|-------|----------|
| D65 | Pilot fee restructure | €4,500 for warm network first 5 pilots as case study investment. Cold outbound minimum €7,500. |
| D66 | Payment structure | 70% at kickoff, 30% at client acceptance (Day 20-25), not Day 15. |
| D67 | Commitment structure | 6-month initial + 30-day exit clause after Month 3, not 12-month. |

### Unresolved
- Actual delivery day count for a single-workflow pilot
- Actual conversion rate by channel (warm vs cold)
- Payment enforcement mechanism for Day 20-25 invoice

## Summary for Louis

Three critical assumptions were challenged:

1. **GTM:** Louis's warm network has a natural ceiling (~30-100 qualified prospects). Year 1 target of 6-8 pilots requires 75-160 discovery calls — a full-time job. Warm outreach is Month 1-3 only. Bridge channels needed by Month 4-6.

2. **Delivery:** Louis cannot solo-deliver the first pilot. The first pilot needs a business consultant (process mapping, change management) alongside Louis (technical). This costs €2,000-4,000 but protects the case study.

3. **Pricing:** €4,500 pilot fee doesn't cover realistic delivery costs. The model works only if Louis is the delivery mechanism (€0 incremental cost). Since Louis can't solo-deliver (D31), the fee structure needs revision. Warm network pilots stay at €4,500; cold outbound minimum €7,500.
