## Debate A: Expert-Comptable Vertical Challenge (Vertical Strategist)

### Assumption Challenged
**D126:** Expert-comptables (administrative workflows around client management and regulatory reporting) as first beachhead — NOT professional services, NOT digital agencies. Governance gap is the differentiator.

**Verdict: D126 should be MODIFIED, not abandoned.** The admin workflows thesis is right; the expert-comptable vertical is premature.

### Arguments

**1. The Governance Wedge Cuts Against D126, Not For It**

D127 explicitly states the governance gap is "overstated for 70% of ICP." If governance is the moat but D127 partially disputes the moat, the vertical choice is fragile. The governance gap argument actually says: "we can't win on features, so we win on compliance." That's a defensive posture for a 1-person studio. That's not a beachhead — that's a bunker.

**2. The Network Dependency Is Unresolved and Dangerous**

U123/U129 (Louis's expert-comptable connections) are UNRESOLVED. We don't know if Louis has any warm relationships with accounting firms. Meanwhile, Louis's Grinto connections are confirmed and documented — but the strategy is anchored to an unknown while ignoring a known.

**3. The Domain Knowledge Gap Is Fatal for a Regulated Vertical**

U124 is UNRESOLVED. Louis cannot credibly sell into regulated admin workflows without understanding them deeply. A pilot scoped by assumptions will miss real pain points and fail to show measurable value.

### Alternative Recommendation

Lead with web agencies (Grinto connections), use that as the wedge, and expand into expert-comptables through existing client relationships — warm intros, not cold calls. The network dependency is inverted: go where you have relationships, not where you hope to build them.

### Proposed Decision Changes
- Kill "NOT digital agencies" from D126
- Add: U123 (validate expert-comptable network), U124 (domain discovery), U52 (document warm network), U91 (clarify available hours)
- Resolve: U95/U107 (web agency conflict policy)

## Debate B: Malt Channel Feasibility Challenge (Channel Strategist)

### Assumption Challenged
Malt.fr is a viable primary channel for Alizé's first-client acquisition, supplementing the demoted warm network track (D130, U130).

### Arguments

**1. Cold-Start Problem — Zero Reviews = Zero Visibility**

Malt's algorithm buries zero-review profiles. A new profile with 0 reviews is invisible in any competitive search. Chicken-and-egg: you need reviews to get clients, but you need clients to get reviews. No bootstrap path exists without first engaging clients elsewhere.

**2. The Workflow Problem — "AI Automation" Doesn't Convert**

Buyers search for specific, priced, solved problems. Alizé has no defined, priced, specific workflow offering yet. A listing that says "AI workflow automation for regulated industries" is meaningless. Malt listings that convert are specific: *"Automatisation de la saisie factures — 1500€ fixe, 3 semaines."*

**3. The Competition Problem**

Malt buyers use the platform to reduce search and trust costs. A new entrant with zero reviews and no portfolio is auto-filtered. Expert-comptables are sophisticated buyers who won't trust an unproven startup with no case studies.

### Viable Malt Strategy

Reverse the sequence: Use warm network for first 2-3 engagements, route them through Malt to accumulate reviews, then activate Malt as inbound. Define ONE specific priced workflow first (e.g., "automatisation réconciliation bancaire, 2500€ HT, 4 semaines") before any listing exists.

### Proposed Decision Changes
- Week 1's feasibility check should answer "what is Louis's first specific, priced workflow?" — not "can he list on Malt"
- Malt becomes a review-accumulation engine for warm network clients, not a primary inbound channel
- Primary channel for Month 1-3: direct warm outreach + association referrals

## Debate C: Commercial Layer Prerequisite Challenge (Business Strategist)

### Assumption Challenged
**U86/D84/D90:** "Senior commercial co-founder" as the critical prerequisite that gates all outreach. Louis cannot credibly close €7,500+ deals without one.

### Arguments

**1. The Price Point Mismatch**

This framing was built on a €7,500+ deal assumption. **D125 explicitly sets pilot pricing at €1,500/2-week diagnostic.** At €1,500, Louis doesn't need a senior commercial co-founder — he needs a clear offer and basic sales hygiene. €1,500 is a consulting budget item; a prepared founder can close it.

At €7,500+: Multi-stakeholder, negotiation, peer references, 30+ day cycles — Louis cannot credibly close these solo.

These are categorically different sales situations requiring different commercial profiles.

**2. The Timeline Mismatch**

Finding a real commercial co-founder takes 3-6 months minimum for an unfunded startup. The "resolve by Month 1 Week 2" expectation in U93 is internally contradictory with D90's "outreach blocked until person exists."

**3. The Definition Problem**

"Closing-capable person" conflates four very different profiles:
- Sales coach (€500-1,500 flat): Sufficient for €1,500 closes
- Junior SDR (€1,500-2,000/month): Handles outreach pipeline, Louis closes calls
- Senior closer (€8-15k/month + commission): Required for €7,500+
- Commercial co-founder (equity): 3-6 month search minimum

### Alternative Framing

**The Minimum Viable Commercial Layer for Month 1-2:**
- **Month 1:** Louis + sales coach (2-4 sessions, €500-1,500) — sufficient for €1,500 closes
- **Month 2:** Evaluate whether junior SDR needed based on pipeline volume
- **Month 3-4:** Begin commercial co-founder conversations for Phase 2 (€5,000+ deals)
- **Month 4+:** Commercial co-founder required as monthly retainers scale to €3,000+

### Proposed Decision Changes
- **D90 REFRAMED:** Warm outreach starts Month 1 with Louis + sales coach. Senior commercial co-founder gates €5,000+ deal pursuit, not €1,500 diagnostic closes.
- **D84:** Keep but relabel — accurate for €7,500+, but that's Phase 2 pricing, not D125
- **U86 REPLACED:** Replace "senior commercial co-founder prerequisite" with tiered commercial capability roadmap
- Month 1 Week 1: Find a sales coach or peer with PME closing experience to review Louis's outreach materials
- Month 1 Week 2: Louis does 2-3 roleplay discovery calls before any live prospect calls
