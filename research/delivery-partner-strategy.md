## TL;DR — Answer First

**Is finding a delivery partner the right next step?** Partially. The better immediate move: **get the first pilot client first, then staff the delivery partner against a concrete engagement.** Don't hire a partner into an empty pipeline. **The delivery partner question is secondary to the client acquisition question.** At pre-revenue stage, Alizé's bottleneck is clients, not delivery capacity. Every day spent sourcing a delivery partner before a client is committed is wasted.

## 1. What Profile Does the Delivery Partner Need?

The pulse debate correctly identified that business consulting skills are non-negotiable. Here are the exact specifications:

### Hard Requirements
- **French native speaker** — can run client discovery in French without accent barriers
- **PME/ETI experience** — has worked with 50-200 person companies, understands their pace, hierarchy, skepticism
- **Process mapping capability** — can identify operational workflows worth automating (not just any workflow, the RIGHT workflow)
- **Change management instincts** — knows how to get desk workers to adopt new tools without resistance
- **Availability:** 2-3 days/week for Month 1, scalable to full-time if pipeline grows

### Soft Requirements
- **Comfortable with AI as a tool** — not an AI skeptic, not an AI evangelist; a pragmatist
- **Business credibility** — not necessarily a former Big 4 consultant, but someone a French DG takes seriously
- **Structured and organized** — delivery documentation, milestone tracking, client communication
- **Entrepreneurial** — comfortable with ambiguity, early-stage startup context

### Nice-to-Have
- Existing relationships with target companies (professional services, consulting, accounting, logistics)
- Experience with EU AI Act or GDPR compliance conversations
- Knowledge of French business tool ecosystem (Sage, Cegid, Pennylane, etc.)

### What They DON'T Need
- **Technical AI/agent skills** — Louis owns that
- **Sales skills** — Louis and Gabin own client acquisition
- **Industry vertical specialization** — generalist PME operational experience is more valuable than deep vertical knowledge at this stage

### Profile Summary
> French business consultant, 30-50 years old, 8-15 years of PME operational consulting experience, comfortable with AI as an efficiency tool, available 2-3 days/week, success-fee motivated.

### The Role Definition (Critical)
The delivery partner's job is **not** to build agents. It's to:
1. Run the client discovery call (not the sales call — the delivery scoping call)
2. Map the client's actual operational workflow
3. Identify which workflow to automate first (and justify why)
4. Manage client expectations and stakeholder resistance
5. Measure and report ROI in business terms
6. Write the delivery playbook with Louis as the technical input

Louis's job is:
1. Execute the technical build per the agreed workflow spec
2. Test and validate the agent
3. Handle integration issues
4. Monitor the agent post-deployment

These are **different jobs**. Do not let them blur.

## 3. Contracting Model

### Recommended: Freelance / Independent Consultant (Portage Salarial or SASU)

**Why freelance over employee:**
- At pre-revenue stage, committing to a full-time employee is too risky
- The delivery partner role may not be needed at full capacity after Month 3
- Freelance allows scaling up or down with pipeline

**Portage salarial** (best option for the consultant, easiest for Alizé):
- The consultant works through a portage company (ex: Ekkaber, Jurismatic Portage)
- Alizé pays the portage company an invoice; the consultant gets a salary
- Benefits: consultant has employment protections, Alizé has no social contributions to manage
- Cost: day rate + ~10% portage fee
- Alizé cost: €550-770/day all-in (€500-700/day + portage fee)

**SASU / auto-entrepreneur** (cheaper for Alizé, more administrative burden):
- Consultant invoices as their own company
- Alizé pays net of VAT
- Benefits: cheaper, no portage fee
- Risk: consultant is fully independent, less obligation to deliver
- Best for: short-term fixed-scope engagements

### Budget Structure: Success-Fee Model

**The problem:** Alizé can't afford €550-770/day × 10 days = €5,500-7,700 in delivery costs against a €4,500 pilot fee.

**The solution:** Reduced base + success fee

| Component | Amount | Trigger |
|-----------|--------|---------|
| Base day rate | €300-400/day (40-50% discount) | Per day worked |
| Success fee | €1,500 per client converted to recurring | Client signs 3-month managed service |
| Total potential | €3,000-4,000 base + €1,500 success fee = €4,500-5,500 | 2-day delivery engagement |

**Why this works:**
- Alizé's cash exposure is capped at €3,000-4,000 per delivery engagement
- The success fee aligns incentives: delivery partner wants conversion
- If the pilot doesn't convert, Alizé pays €3-4k; if it does, total cost is €4,500-5,500 — roughly aligned to pilot revenue

**Month 1 cost projection:**
- 5 delivery days across 30-day pilot × €350/day = €1,750 base
- + 1 success fee per converted client = €1,500
- Total: €3,250 if client converts, €1,750 if not
- Against €4,500 pilot revenue: margin positive in both cases

**Month 2+:** If Alizé has 3 pilots running simultaneously, delivery partner needs 8-10 days/month. Contract renegotiates to 10 days/month at full rate or part-time retainer.

## 5. Challenging the Assumption — Is There a Better Path?

### Alternative A: Louis + Gabin Formalized Partnership

**What it is:** Louis (technical) + Gabin (business/delivery) operate as a 2-person partnership, splitting pilot fees 50/50 and owning the full delivery stack.

**Pros:**
- No additional cost — Gabin presumably has existing income or is co-founding
- Complementary skills: Louis builds, Gabin consults
- Faster decision-making — no external dependency
- Stronger co-founding story for future fundraising

**Cons:**
- Unclear if Gabin has PME consulting experience — the pulse debate didn't confirm this
- If Gabin is also technical (given the shared codebase), the delivery gap remains
- Partnership agreements take time to structure; delay = no pilots
- 2-person dependency is a single point of failure

**When this works:** If Gabin has 5+ years of business consulting or operations experience, this is the best option — no cash, no onboarding, no dependency on a third party.

**When this fails:** If Gabin is also technical (developer), Louis + Gabin = 2 developers, and the delivery problem persists.

**Recommendation:** Before sourcing a delivery partner, Louis must answer: does Gabin have client-facing business consulting experience? If yes, formalize the partnership and skip the external delivery partner for Month 1.

### Alternative B: Partner with a Boutique Consulting Firm (White-Label)

**What it is:** An existing consulting firm delivers Alizé agents to their existing PME clients. Alizé provides the agent infrastructure, the firm provides the client relationship and delivery expertise.

**Pros:**
- Instant pipeline — the firm has existing clients
- No hiring — delivery is the firm's problem
- Credibility — the firm's brand reassures PME clients
- Revenue share aligns incentives: they only earn if the client succeeds

**Cons:**
- Margin compression: 20-30% to the partner firm
- Less control over delivery quality and agent specs
- Risk of the firm becoming a competitor (they learn Alizé's model and build their own)
- French consulting firms move slowly — partnership negotiations take months

**Example target firms:**
- Small consultancies (2-10 people) targeting the 50-200 employee segment
- Digital transformation advisors with existing PME relationships
- ex-ESN consultants who went independent

**When this works:** Alizé has a warm introduction to a boutique firm founder who believes in AI agents.

**When this fails:** Cold outreach to consulting firms — they have their own agendas and Alizé is not a priority.

**Recommendation:** This is a Month 3-6 play, not a Month 1 play. Partnership negotiations take time Alizé doesn't have right now.

### Alternative C: Discounted First Pilot in Exchange for Case Study + Referral

**What it is:** Instead of finding a delivery partner, Louis delivers the first pilot himself at a heavy discount (€1,500) in exchange for: (a) a published case study, (b) a warm referral to one other company.

**Pros:**
- Zero delivery partner cost
- Louis learns by doing — the best education
- Real feedback loop from a real client
- Creates the reference client that attracts the delivery partner later

**Cons:**
- The pulse debate correctly identified: Louis may lack business consulting skills to make the pilot commercially successful
- If the pilot fails to convert due to poor change management, Louis learned the wrong lesson
- Discounted price attracts price-sensitive clients, not ideal first clients

**When this works:** If Louis can quickly bootstrap basic consulting skills (2-3 days of process mapping training) and the first client is forgiving and collaborative.

**When this fails:** If the client expects professional consulting and gets an enthusiastic intern. The case study becomes a warning, not a proof.

**Recommendation:** This is a fallback if Alternative A (Gabin partnership) isn't available and the delivery partner search fails. Don't lead with this.

### Verdict on Alternatives

| Alternative | Speed | Cost | Quality | Scalability | Recommended |
|------------|-------|------|---------|-------------|-------------|
| Delivery partner (freelance, success-fee) | Medium | Low-Medium | High | Medium | **Primary Month 1 path** |
| Louis + Gabin formal partnership | Fast | None | High if Gabin has consulting skills | Low-Medium | **Try first, then fallback** |
| Boutique firm white-label | Slow | High margin loss | Medium | High | Month 3-6 play |
| Discounted pilot by Louis alone | Fastest | None | Low-Medium | None | Fallback only |

## 6. 5-Step Sourcing Plan with Timeline

**Goal:** Delivery partner identified, contracted, and ready to deliver Month 1 pilot alongside Louis.

### Step 1: Confirm Gabin's Role (Day 1-3)
**Action:** Louis asks Gabin directly: does Gabin have client-facing business consulting or operations experience with PME/ETI companies?

- If **yes**: Formalize Louis + Gabin as delivery team. Skip to 5-step plan below (delivery partner only needed from Month 2).
- If **no or unclear**: Proceed to Step 2.

**Who:** Louis
**Output:** Clear answer on whether Gabin can serve as the delivery lead for Month 1.

---

### Step 2: Warm Outreach on Malt (Day 3-10)
**Action:** Louis creates a Malt profile (if not existing) and sends personalized messages to 15-20 freelancers matching the profile criteria:

**Search filters on Malt:**
- "Transformation digitale PME"
- "Conduite du changement"
- "Amélioration des processus"
- Location: Île-de-France
- Availability: disponible maintenant

**Message template:**
> "Bonjour [Name], je suis Louis, co-fondateur d'Alizé — un service d'agents IA gérés pour les entreprises françaises de 50-250 employés. Nous cherchons un consultant freelance pour un rôle de delivery lead sur nos premiers pilots: découverte client, cartographie des processus, gestion du changement. Profil idéal: expérience en transformation digitale PME, à l'aise avec les outils IA. Disponibilité: 2-3 jours/semaine, engagement initial 30 jours avec possibilité d'extension. Rémunération: taux journalier réduit + prime de conversion. Intéressé? Je peux vous envoyer le brief complet. Louis"

- Target: 20 messages
- Expected response rate: 10-20% (2-4 replies)
- Expected viable candidates: 1-2

**Who:** Louis
**Output:** 2-4 conversations started, 1-2 candidates for interview

---

### Step 3: Interview + Reference Check (Day 10-17)
**Action:** 30-minute video call with each viable candidate.

**Interview questions (ask all):**
1. "Décrivez-moi un projet de transformation digitale que vous avez mené pour une PME de 50-150 employés. Comment avez-vous identifié le processus à automatiser?"
2. "Comment abordez-vous la résistance des équipes lors du déploiement d'un nouvel outil?"
3. "Qu'est-ce qui vous attire dans le modèle Alizé vs un projet de conseil classique?"
4. "Quel est votre disponibilité sur les 4 prochaines semaines?"
5. "Avez-vous de l'expérience avec les outils IA agents (agents conversationnels, automatisation)?"

**Red flags:**
- No PME experience (only worked with large enterprises)
- AI skeptic or AI-only specialist with no operational consulting background
- Not available for 4+ weeks
- Day rate above €900 (above range for success-fee model)

**Reference check:** Always call 1-2 references from past PME clients. Ask: "Would you work with this person again? Why or why not?"

**Who:** Louis (and Gabin if available for the call)
**Output:** 1 preferred candidate, 1 backup

---

### Step 4: Trial Engagement Negotiation + Contract (Day 17-21)
**Action:** Structure the trial engagement with preferred candidate.

**Contract terms to propose:**
- Duration: 30 days (pilot period)
- Scope: Up to 5 delivery days (defined as: 1 discovery call, 1 process mapping session, 2 client check-ins, 1 ROI presentation)
- Base rate: €350/day (40% below market)
- Success fee: €1,500 paid at client conversion (signs ≥3-month managed service)
- Payment terms: Invoice at day 15 and day 30 of engagement
- Exit clause: Either party can terminate with 5 days notice; if terminated, only days worked are paid

**If candidate pushes back on reduced base rate:**
- Offer: €450/day base + €1,000 success fee
- Ceiling: €500/day base + €1,000 success fee (any higher and the pilot economics break)

**Sign the contract digitally (HelloSign, DocuSign, or just a PDF with email confirmation). Keep it simple.**

**Who:** Louis (with legal review if needed)
**Output:** Signed engagement letter with delivery partner

---

### Step 5: Month 1 Pilot Delivery with Shadowing (Week 1-4 of pilot)
**Action:** Run the first pilot with clear role division.

**Week 1 (Days 1-7):**
- Delivery partner runs discovery call with client (with Louis on the call)
- Louis observes: how does the partner ask questions? What does the partner look for?
- Delivery partner writes process map document (not Louis)
- Louis reviews process map and confirms technical feasibility

**Week 2 (Days 8-14):**
- Louis builds agent against agreed process map
- Delivery partner drafts change management plan (how to get the team to use it)
- Daily 15-min sync between Louis and delivery partner

**Week 3 (Days 15-21):**
- Agent deployed to pilot team
- Delivery partner runs team onboarding session
- Delivery partner monitors adoption metrics (not just agent accuracy)
- Louis handles technical issues

**Week 4 (Days 22-30):**
- Delivery partner compiles ROI document
- Delivery partner leads conversion conversation with client
- Louis provides technical backup
- Louis and delivery partner debrief: what worked, what didn't → this becomes the delivery playbook

**Playbook format (per the delivery debate's D33):**
Must include: stakeholder map, process map, success metrics (in client language), change management checkpoints, escalation triggers.

**Who:** Louis (technical) + delivery partner (business)
**Output:** First paying managed service client, case study permission, delivery playbook v1

**Risk 4: Delivery partner becomes a bottleneck if Alizé scales to 3+ pilots simultaneously**
- Mitigation: By Month 3, either bring on a second delivery partner or structure a partner firm relationship
