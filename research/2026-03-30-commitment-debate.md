# Commitment Audit: Is Time the Real Blocker?

## Assumption Challenged

**D91 / D221 / U91 framing** — the assumption that "Louis's time commitment is the Alizé blocker" and that resolving the hours question (25+ hrs/week, behavioral binary, etc.) will unlock execution.

## Arguments Against

### 1. D91 has been restructured 3 times. This is a pattern, not a problem.

The debate log shows D91 restructured at minimum 3 times across pulses (D91 original → D121 "aspirational until U91 provides data" → D221 behavioral binary). Zero actual measurement has occurred. When a structural decision is repeatedly redesigned without ever being tested in reality, the problem is not the structure. The problem is the decision-maker avoiding the commitment the structure is supposed to force.

Restructuring D91 is a displacement activity. Every iteration buys another few days of planning without forcing a single real commitment.

### 2. U91 is 91 days old. Time was never the blocker.

U91 — "Louis binary choice: startup vs side-project" — has survived 91+ days unresolved. This is not a time allocation problem. This is an identity and priority problem. Louis has three concurrent commitments: M1 master's, Grinto internship, and Kuroba web agency co-founded. He has not resolved whether Alizé is a startup or a side project, and that ambiguity has persisted for 3 months.

If time were the blocker, U91 would have been resolved when D91 set a 25-hour threshold. Instead, U91 survived D91. This proves the causal chain is reversed: the unresolved binary choice is what makes time allocation feel impossible, not the reverse.

### 3. D221's "behavioral binary" hides the real question

D221 replaced the binary with "6+ hours + delivery vs explicit opt-out." This reframes the problem as behavioral (are you showing up and delivering?) rather than time-based (are you hitting 25 hours?). But D221 still doesn't force the U91 decision.

A person can hit 6 hours/day plus delivery for 2 weeks and still not have resolved whether Alizé is a startup or a side project. The behavioral binary addresses symptoms, not cause. It's a more sophisticated form of the same avoidance.

### 4. 91 days of redesign, zero tracking

The debate log contains 27 pulses across a single day (2026-03-30), generating D1 through D134+. In all that structured debate, not one entry records actual hours tracked for even one week. D91 was set as a gating constraint, but the condition precedent (U91 providing actual data) was never met. D121 explicitly acknowledged this: "D91's 25 hrs/week aspirational until U91 provides actual data."

An aspirational constraint that has never been measured is not a commitment. It's a hope dressed as a decision.

### 5. Louis has 3 commitments. The question is which one wins.

Louis's three active commitments are M1 (structured, externally enforced), Grinto (structured, externally enforced), and Kuroba (co-founded, time-bound). Alizé is the fourth. None of the D91 restructuring addresses the structural conflict between these. It just asks "can you carve out 25 hours for Alizé?" without asking "which of these three do you actually intend to protect, and which are you willing to let degrade?"

This is an identity question, not a scheduling question.

## Arguments For

### 1. Time genuinely constrains execution at 25 hrs/week

If Louis can only give Alizé 10-15 hours/week, the execution plan as written (delivery rehearsal Month 1, external pilot Month 2, delivery partner Month 2, co-founder recruitment Month 2) is physically impossible. The debate log's own analysis (Pulse 21) confirms this: Month 1 demand is 160-245 hours. Time is a necessary condition for execution, even if not the sufficient one.

### 2. Behavioral binary (D221) is more honest than hourly targets

D221's "6+ hours + delivery vs explicit opt-out" is a better forcing function than a pure hour count. It ties commitment to outcomes rather than presence. If Louis is showing up and delivering, the 6-hour question is secondary. This is a genuine improvement over D91's original framing.

### 3. The debate log did surface the real issues

Pulse 21's capacity analysis (D88, D89, D90) correctly identified that the moat is Louis himself, that co-authorship is required, and that a commercial layer is a prerequisite. The system surfaced the right blockers. The problem is those blockers (U86, U87, U89, U91) remain unresolved in the log.

## Finding

**Time is a symptom, not the disease. The real Alizé blocker is the unresolved U91 binary (startup vs side-project) combined with structural conflicts between Louis's three existing commitments that no restructuring of D91 can resolve.**

The 91-day survival of U91, combined with three separate D91 restructurings that produced zero measurement, proves this is a commitment problem masquerading as a time problem. Louis has not decided if Alizé is his priority. Redesigning the commitment structure will not make that decision for him.

The behavioral binary (D221) is a more honest frame, but it still doesn't force the U91 resolution. Until Louis explicitly decides Alizé is a startup (which means one of his other commitments gets demoted or exited), any time-based commitment structure will continue to be restructured rather than honored.

**D91 should be deprecated. It has never been measured. Replace it with a single forcing function: U91 must be resolved by a specific date or Alizé is formally restated as a side-project with corresponding scope (1-2 pilots Year 1, not the current execution plan).**
