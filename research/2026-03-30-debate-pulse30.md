## Challenge 1: Pricing Architecture (D148/D149/D150)

**Challenging D148, D149, D150**

### Challenge 1a: €490 Deliverable Signals Low Confidence, Not Low Friction

D148 sets €490 for a written diagnostic deliverable. But D84/D87/D107/D108 established Louis is a credibility ceiling — an M1 master student with no case studies, no track record. A €490 price from an unknown brand says "I don't believe this is worth €490 either." French ESN day rates are €600-900/day. €490 for a written deliverable (2-3 days of skilled work) = €163/day. This is intern-level pricing, not professional services pricing.

**Contradicts:** D150 (single-price premium framing), D84/D87 (credibility ceiling established), D107 (price point unsellable without credibility)

**Verdict:** €490 deliverable reinforces the credibility problem rather than solving it.

### Challenge 1b: D148/D149 Are Philosophically Inconsistent with D150

D150 says "single price framing preferred; price outcome not feature set." But D148 creates a three-step funnel: FREE call → €490 deliverable → €7,500 pilot. This is more tiered, not less. The free call also captures time-wasters when Louis's capacity (U91, unresolved across 30 pulses) is already oversubscribed.

**Contradicts:** D6 (workflow-tier to reduce complexity — D148 increases it), D150 rationale

**Verdict:** The funnel structure contradicts the single-price philosophy D150 is trying to establish.

### Challenge 1c: Unit Economics Don't Work at €7,500 Pilot / €1,000/Month

€7,500 pilot vs €1,000/month managed service = 7.5 months to recover acquisition cost. With a 3-month minimum commitment, Alizé collects €3,000 against €7,500 invested. Loss of €4,500 per early-exit client. D110/D111 established pilots 1-3 as CAC at €1,500-2,000. D149's €7,500 minimum warm network directly contradicts D110-112's CAC model.

**Contradicts:** D110/D111 (CAC model for pilots 1-3), D28 (35-50% conversion rate), D72/D73 (€7,500 giving ~€1,500 margin at 10 days — but D110 says pilots 1-3 are CAC)

**Verdict:** The unit economics require either higher monthly retainer or lower pilot price. Currently internally inconsistent.

### Challenge 1d: "Credited Toward Pilot" Creates Undefined Refund Dynamics

€490 credit against €7,500 pilot = 6.5% (negligible). Against €1,500 pilot = 33% (meaningful but awkward remainder). The credit doesn't integrate with D52's staged payment model (50%/50% kickoff/day 15). If prospect takes the deliverable but doesn't proceed, the refund conversation is undefined.

**Contradicts:** D52 (staged payment), D150 (price outcome not feature set)

**Verdict:** The credit mechanism is undefined in its mechanics and creates friction rather than removing it.

### Alternatives Proposed
- **Alt 1:** €1,500 diagnostic (no credit, no free call), discovery form for qualification
- **Alt 2:** Tiered pilot by reference count (€2k pilots 1-3 CAC → €5k with 1 ref → €7.5k with 3+ refs)
- **Alt 3:** Kill credit, replace with "€490 off first managed service month" (49% first month free incentive)

## Challenge 2: Category Positioning (D143 — "Regulated SMEs")

**Challenging D143, D97, D145**

### Challenge 2a: "Regulated" Signals Compliance Assets Alizé Doesn't Have

"Regulated" invites compliance questions Louis can't answer in Month 1: EU AI Act Article 10 documentation, DPAs, data residency certificates, audit logging architecture. D42 explicitly killed EU AI Act from near-term messaging. D143 reintroduces it through the category constraint.

**Contradicts:** D42 (EU AI Act removed from near-term), D108 (credibility assets required before claims)

**Verdict:** "Regulated" invites the exact conversation D42 says to avoid.

### Challenge 2b: "Regulated" Unnecessarily Narrows TAM

D127 established governance gaps are only disqualifying for ~30% of the ICP. By framing as "regulated SMEs," Alizé self-selects out of the 70% where "regulated" is either irrelevant or actively off-putting. Most 50-200 person French service companies don't think of themselves as "regulated" — they think of themselves as "busy" and "understaffed."

**Contradicts:** D127 (70% of ICP not governance-constrained), BRIEF.md ICP definition (regulated is not in the ICP)

**Verdict:** Self-inflicted TAM narrowing with no corresponding benefit for 70% of target prospects.

### Challenge 2c: D97's "Governance Disqualifying" Assumption Is Unvalidated

D97 (from Pulse 21) states Copilot governance gaps are "disqualifying" in regulated industries. But this was raised as an assumption to consider, not validated fact. No evidence exists that 50-200 person French companies in legal/healthcare/finance actually run procurement processes that disqualify Copilot on governance grounds. D69 deprioritized expert-comptables to Month 9+ partly because of the compliance burden.

**Contradicts:** D127 (70% ICP not governance-constrained), D129 (willingness to pay unresolved)

**Verdict:** D97 is speculation that should not anchor the category. The regulated wedge may be 5% of ICP, not 30%.

### Challenge 2d: Compliance Theater vs. Compliance Substance

Agentova and Magic AI don't claim "regulated." They position on outcomes. "Regulated SMEs" before having compliance documentation is like calling yourself "Michelin-star quality" before hiring a chef. D75 killed "delegation with accountability" as buzzword soup. "Regulated" has the same problem — it means everything and commits to nothing.

**Contradicts:** D75 (buzzword compliance language fails buyers), D108 (credibility requires assets)

**Verdict:** "Regulated" is compliance theater without compliance substance.

### Alternatives Proposed
- **Option A:** "AI workflow automation for companies that use multiple business tools" — integration-intensive, not compliance-constrained
- **Option B:** "AI agents that actually connect to your business tools" — D128's "results not licenses" framing without the compliance claim
- **Option C:** "Back-office AI automation for service firms" — narrow but honest, governance remains available as a secondary differentiator

## Challenge 3: Window Framing (D82/D151/D152)

**Challenging D82, D151, D152**

### Challenge 3a: The Window Closes Before It Opens

D151 says the 3-6 month window is for *starting* cold outbound. But D47 established 4 hard blockers for cold outbound: website + completed pilot + ICP confirmed + delivery confirmed. Per D99-D103, the first external pilot is Month 2 at earliest. These are sequential, not parallel:

```
Month 1: Prerequisites
Month 2: External pilot begins
Month 2-3: Pilot completes
Month 3: Cold outbound CAN start
```

Effective cold outbound window: ~3 months (Month 3-6), not 6 months. If any blocker slips, the window compresses to 1-2 months or disappears.

**Contradicts:** D151 (cold outbound starts Month 1-2 implied)

**Verdict:** Cold outbound is Month 3-4 minimum. The stated 3-6 month window overstates when cold outbound becomes possible.

### Challenge 3b: D82 and D76 Are Incompatible

D76 (Pulse 18) established a 6-18 month market window. D82 (Pulse 19, one pulse later) replaced it with 3-6 months without acknowledging the contradiction. 3-6 and 6-18 share no overlap. One is a market window (competitive), one is a personal endurance window (Louis). They cannot be substituted for each other.

D12 says Mistral threat is 18-36 months away. Three independent constraints:
- Market window: 6-18 months (D76)
- Mistral threat: 18-36 months (D12)
- Louis's capacity: 3-6 months (D82)

**Contradicts:** D76 vs D82 (same decision record, incompatible numbers)

**Verdict:** The binding constraint is Louis's capacity — but calling it a "market window" conflates personal exhaustion with competitive timing.

### Challenge 3c: Execution-Dependent, Not Timing-Dependent — "Window" Is the Wrong Metaphor

D78, D83, D12, D76 all point to the same conclusion: Alizé's moat is operational depth, not first-mover advantage. If Alizé executes well, it builds switching costs, benchmark metrics, and client relationships that protect it regardless of when competitors enter. If it executes poorly, the window is irrelevant.

The "window" metaphor externalizes the problem (market event) when the actual problem is internal (can Louis sustain this?). The framing can cause Alizé to rush into bad pilots, accept unfavorable terms, or skip prerequisites.

**Contradicts:** D82/D152 urgency framing, D78/D83 (depth creates moat)

**Verdict:** The window exists, but it's not the strategic variable. Execution quality is.

### Challenge 3d: "Capacity Window" Creates a Desperation Narrative

D152 renamed "effective window" to "capacity window" to clarify it's about personal exhaustion. But "capacity window" still signals desperation — we're running out of time before we break. French PME buyers are risk-averse and tuned to detect vendor desperation. A desperate vendor signals an unreliable vendor. Internally, desperation framing increases error rate and makes Alizé more likely to accept bad deals.

**Contradicts:** D87 (credibility window — failed closes have reputational consequences), D152 framing

**Verdict:** "Capacity window" creates urgency theater that experienced buyers detect and that poisons internal decision-making.

### Alternatives Proposed
- **Framework 1:** Execution milestones — replace "3-6 month window" with specific milestones (warm pilot signed Month 1-2, first case study Month 3, cold pipeline active Month 3-4)
- **Framework 2:** Capacity-based planning — answer: how many hours can Louis commit? What needs to stop? What capacity additions are needed and when?
- **Framework 3:** Moat-first positioning — replace urgency with depth logic: "build switching costs that make leaving painful, regardless of when competitors enter"
