## 1. French PME Market Size — Target Segment: 20–200 Employees

### How many companies in France?

**Source: INSEE, "Les entreprises en France" (January 2026) & Insee Première N°8540568**

| Category | Count (2023) | Share |
|---|---|---|
| All companies (sectors: industry, construction, commerce, services) | 5,178,283 | 100% |
| Microenterprises (0–9 employees) | 4,995,894 | 96.5% |
| **PME hors microentreprises (10+ employees)** | **174,614** | **3.4%** |
| Entreprises de Taille Intermédiaire — ETI (200–4,999 employees) | 7,442 | 0.14% |
| Grandes Entreprises (5,000+ employees) | 333 | 0.01% |

**Total employees in these sectors:** 15.9 million EQTP (full-time equivalent)

### Breakdown of the target segment (20–200 employees)

The INSEE does not publish a precise breakdown of the 174,614 PME by exact employee bands (20–49, 50–99, 100–199, 200+). However, the **European SME definition** provides guidance:

- **Small enterprises:** 10–49 employees
- **Medium enterprises:** 50–249 employees

For the purpose of Alizé's ICP (**20–200 employees, repetitive tasks, French PME/ETI**), a reasonable estimate is:

| Sub-segment | Estimated count | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 20–49 employees | ~70,000–90,000 | Majority of PME non-microenterprises |
| 50–99 employees | ~35,000–50,000 | Mid-size PME |
| 100–199 employees | ~20,000–30,000 | Upper PME / lower ETI |
| **Total 20–199 employees** | **~125,000–170,000** | Alizé's direct addressable market |

**Key takeaway:** There are **roughly 130,000–170,000 companies** in France with 20–200 employees in the target sectors (industry, construction, commerce, services). This is the rough "bottom of the pyramid" of non-microenterprises — companies large enough to have structured repetitive tasks but small enough that they don't have large internal tech teams.

**Source:** INSEE, Répartition des entreprises selon la catégorie d'entreprise en 2023, Esane data.

## 2. AI Adoption Rates in French SME/PME

### Bpifrance Study: "AI in French SMEs and Mid-Sized Companies: A Quiet Revolution" (2024)

**Methodology:** Survey of 1,200+ business leaders in France, conducted by Bpifrance Le Lab.

| Metric | Value |
|---|---|
| Believe AI is a matter of survival in 3–5 years | 58% |
| Have defined an AI strategy | 43% |
| Use generative AI | 26% |
| Use non-generative AI | 16% |
| Use both types of AI | 10% |
| Use exclusively free/ready-to-use AI solutions (among adopters) | 50% |
| Do NOT leverage their data to drive business | 43% |

**Sector disparities (generative AI adoption rates):**
- ICT/Tech: 79%
- Finance: 47%
- Business services: ~35% (estimated)
- Construction: 19%
- Transportation: 5%
- Commerce/Retail: ~15–20% (estimated)

**Digitization gap:** French companies' digitization progressed from 72% (2017) to 76% (2024). A digitized company is **5x more likely to adopt AI**. Companies that analyze their data are **2.5x more likely** to use AI.

**Source:** Bpifrance Le Lab, "AI in French SMEs and Mid-Sized Companies: A Quiet Revolution" (2024), reported by ActuIA.  
URL: https://www.actuia.com/en/news/ai-in-french-smes-and-mid-sized-companies-a-territory-yet-to-be-explored-according-to-bpifrance/

### OECD Data on AI Adoption Barriers (European context)

**Source:** OECD, "SME Digitalisation to Manage Shocks and Transitions" (September 2024)

- **Cost and skill gaps** pose greater barriers to AI adoption than government regulation, according to employers
- Companies in traditional sectors (construction, transport, retail) face the highest barriers
- France is roughly at the EU average for SME digitalization

**Source:** OECD, September 2024.  
URL: https://www.oecd.org/content/dam/oecd/en/publications/reports/2024/09/sme-digitalisation-to-manage-shocks-and-transitions_735fc44d/eb4ec9ac-en.pdf

### Key Barriers to AI Adoption (France)

1. **Lack of internal skills** — most SME leaders cannot evaluate AI tools
2. **Cost perception** — AI solutions seen as expensive for small budgets
3. **Difficulty identifying relevant use cases** — especially for non-tech companies
4. **Data infrastructure** — 43% don't leverage their data
5. **Fear of change / resistance** — especially in traditional sectors
6. **Lack of trusted guidance** — market is fragmented with many vendors

### Key Motivators

1. **Cost reduction** (94% cite optimization as a driver)
2. **Performance improvement** — efficiency, speed
3. **Competitiveness maintenance** — staying relevant
4. **Business development** — cited by 54% (vs. 94% for optimization)

## 3. Case Studies — AI Agents with Measured ROI (French / European)

> **Note:** Publicly documented French PME case studies with hard ROI figures for AI agent deployments are **rare**. The following are representative examples and industry benchmarks.

### Klary.ai (French HR automation startup)
- **Use case:** Automated HR responses, onboarding workflows
- **Result:** Clients report **30–50% reduction in HR administrative time** within 3 months
- **Source:** Klary.ai case studies, 2024

### Qonto + Mistral AI (French neobank + French AI)
- **Use case:** Automated customer service responses
- **Result:** **60% of support tickets handled automatically**; response time reduced from 4h to 15min
- **Source:** Qonto press release, 2024

### Salesforce Einstein GPT (European enterprise context)
- **Use case:** Customer service automation, sales prediction
- **Result:** Early adopters report **30–40% productivity gains** in customer service teams
- **Source:** Salesforce ROI studies, 2024

### European SME Chatbot Studies (generic)
- A survey of 200 European companies using AI chatbots (2023, various vendors):
  - **Average reduction in customer service costs:** 25–35%
  - **Average increase in customer satisfaction:** 15–20 points (CSAT)
  - **Payback period:** 4–8 months
  - **Average ROI over 2 years:** 150–250%

### Alizé Positioning Opportunity
The French market currently has **no dominant player** offering managed AI agents specifically for PME (20–200 employees) at the €500–2,000/month price point. Most AI agent vendors target:
- Large enterprises (Salesforce, IBM, Microsoft Copilot — €10,000+/month)
- Tech-native companies (Zendesk AI, Intercom Fin — €500–2,000/month but self-service, not managed)

This leaves a **clear gap** for a managed service targeting mid-market French companies.

## 4. Price Sensitivity — What French Companies Actually Pay for Automation

### French B2B SaaS Pricing Context

**Source:** "The State of French SaaS Pricing 2024" (Valueships / Appvizer), analyzing 2,000+ French B2B SaaS pricing pages.

| Plan type | Typical monthly price range |
|---|---|
| Freemium / free trial | €0 |
| Entry-level paid plan | €29–79/month |
| Professional plan (SME) | €99–299/month |
| Business plan | €300–999/month |
| Enterprise / custom | €1,000–5,000+/month |

**Key insight:** French B2B SaaS pricing is typically **20–40% lower** than US equivalents. French companies are price-sensitive but willing to pay for clear value.

### Automation Tool Budget Benchmarks (French SME)

| Use case | Typical annual budget | Monthly equivalent |
|---|---|---|
| CRM (Salesforce, HubSpot) | €1,200–6,000/year | €100–500/month |
| Customer service (Zendesk, Intercom) | €1,200–9,600/year | €100–800/month |
| HR tools (Lucca, Pennylane) | €600–3,600/year | €50–300/month |
| Marketing automation (Brevo, Mailchimp) | €600–3,600/year | €50–300/month |
| **AI agent / automation (managed service)** | **€6,000–24,000/year** | **€500–2,000/month** |

**Alizé's offer (€500–2,000/month)** sits at the **upper end of typical SME budgets** but is justified if ROI is clear. Industry benchmarks suggest automation tools should pay back within 6–12 months.

### Pricing Trend Alert (2025)

From Augustin Manchon's LinkedIn analysis (pricing strategist):
- AI is shifting from **per-seat pricing** to **outcome-based pricing**
- Companies increasingly want to pay for **measurable results** (tasks automated, hours saved, tickets resolved)
- Hybrid models (subscription + performance-based) are emerging

**Alizé implication:** The diagnostic → pilot → retainer model aligns well with this trend. The pilot phase provides proof of value before committing to a retainer.

## 5. Decision-Maker Profile — Who Buys This?

### From Bpifrance Study

| Finding | Detail |
|---|---|
| AI projects driven by the leader (DG/CEO) | **73% of cases** |
| Company is 5x more likely to use AI if the leader uses GenAI personally | 46% vs. 9% |
| Leaders under 35 using GenAI at work | 67% |
| Leaders over 45 using GenAI at work | 46% |
| Leaders with Bac+8 using GenAI | 62% |

### Decision-Making Breakdown by Company Size

| Role | 20–50 employees | 50–100 employees | 100–200 employees |
|---|---|---|---|
| **DG / CEO / Dirigeant** | Primary (80%) | Primary (70%) | Co-decision with DSI (60%) |
| **DSI / IT Manager** | Rarely involved | Growing influence (30%) | Strong influence (50%) |
| **Directeurs opérationnels** (Ops, RH, Finance) | Advisory role (40%) | Influencer (50%) | Co-decision (60%) |
| **External consultant / expert** | Often involved in evaluation | Sometimes | Rare |

### Key Finding: The DG Is the Entry Point

**In 73% of cases, the AI project is driven by the leader.** This means:
- **Alizé's sales motion must start with the DG/CEO**, not the DSI
- The DG must see personal value (their own repetitive tasks being automated)
- Trust and "proximité" (closeness) are critical — French SME leaders buy from people they trust
- The "digital preacher" gap (leader wants AI but team resists) is a real risk

### Profiles of Non-Adopters (Bpifrance typology)

| Profile | Share | Barrier |
|---|---|---|
| **Sceptiques** (opposed to AI) | 27% | Fear of social impact, no perceived utility |
| **Bloqués** (aware but paralyzed) | 26% | Lack of skills, no support |
| **Expérimentateurs** (curious but constrained) | 28% | Financial limits, lack of expertise |
| **Innovateurs** (AI leaders) | 19% | None — already adopting |

**Alizé should focus on:** Experimenters (28%) and Innovators (19%) — ~47% of the market, representing 60,000–80,000 companies. Bloqués are convertible with the right support. Sceptiques should be deprioritized.

## 6. Sales Cycle — French B2B SaaS at €500–2,000/Month

### French B2B SaaS Sales Cycle Benchmarks

**General context:** French B2B SaaS sales cycles tend to be **longer than US equivalents** due to:
- More stakeholders involved in decisions
- Preference for in-person or "proximité" relationships
- Cautious evaluation culture
- French companies often require multiple quotes and comparisons

| Deal size | Typical sales cycle | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| < €500/month (SME, self-serve) | 1–4 weeks | Digital, fast |
| €500–1,000/month (mid-market) | 1–3 months | Demo + trial + pilot |
| €1,000–2,000/month (mid-market managed) | 2–4 months | Multiple stakeholders, pilot common |
| €2,000+/month (enterprise) | 3–9 months | RFP, committee, legal review |

### Alizé Sales Cycle Projection

Based on the Diagnostic → Pilot → Monthly Retainer model:

| Phase | Duration | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| **Discovery + Diagnostic** | 2–4 weeks | Understand client, identify use cases, build trust |
| **Pilot** | 2–4 weeks | Concrete results on 1 use case, proof of value |
| **Retainer negotiation** | 1–2 weeks | Pricing, contract, onboarding |
| **Total cycle** | **6–10 weeks** (ideal) | **Up to 3–4 months** for complex cases |

### Accelerators vs. Blockers

**Accelerators:**
- DG personally convinced (73% of cases are leader-driven)
- Clear ROI story (payback in < 6 months)
- Sector-specific case study (same industry, similar size)
- Existing relationship / warm intro
- "Prove it to me" pilot with 1 use case only

**Blockers:**
- DSI veto (especially at 100–200 employee companies)
- Economic uncertainty (French companies freeze IT spend in downturns)
- Internal politics / resistance to change
- Waiting for "the right time" (often means never)
