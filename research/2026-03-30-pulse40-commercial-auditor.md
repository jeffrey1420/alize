## The Assumption Being Challenged

From existing research:
- **D182:** Demo-led motion — prototype-first, zero-pitch, build working agent, show it
- **D183:** First 1-2 pilots as "research partnerships" — no cost in exchange for feedback
- **D84:** Louis cannot credibly close €7,500+ deals without senior commercial co-founder

- **D201:** Service model viable at 3-6-12 clients. Louis needs clients, not delivery partners.

The combined assumption: demo-first + free pilots = viable Year 1 GTM because it sidesteps Louis's credibility gap.

## Three Specific Questions

### Question 1: If D183 makes first pilots FREE, what is the conversion trigger? When does Louis ask for money?

**Finding: No credible conversion trigger exists in the current structure.**

D183/D186 as described in existing research contains no commercial term. "In exchange for feedback" is not a pricing mechanism. After 30 days of free work:

- The client has a working AI agent
- The client has zero contractual obligation
- The client has zero financial commitment
- The psychological momentum is toward "let's continue this informally"

D190 (Pulse 37) explicitly kills this: "Free pilot has NO credible path to revenue — no conversion trigger, wrong prospect selection, 'gratuit = suspect' in French PME culture."

**What the research says instead:**
- D197/D198 (Pulse 38): Embed pricing callout at minute 5-8 of demo, before "any questions?" moment
- D199: "French pricing script: ROI calculation + price + immediate next-step ask"
- D192: Conversion script exists in debate log — but has Louis ever rehearsed it? Has it been tested with a real prospect?

**The real problem:** The debate log has generated 40+ decisions but Louis has sent **zero outreach messages** across all 39 pulses. The conversion trigger discussion is moot until someone answers an outreach. The trigger exists in the research. It hasn't been used.

**Verdict on Q1:** The trigger IS defined (D197-D199), but it's theater until Louis sends his first message. Free pilot structure without a Day-1 commercial conversation is broken. But the debate has already identified this. The gap is execution, not missing decisions.

---

### Question 2: If Louis's credibility ceiling (D84) applies at every price point, does "free" actually solve the credibility problem or mask it?

**Finding: Free masks the credibility problem. It doesn't solve it.**

D84 states Louis can't credibly close €7,500+ deals. D184 then challenged whether €2,000 is validated within his ceiling. D191 (Pulse 37) resolves this:

> "Louis's solo-close credibility ceiling applies at every price point — not just above €7,500. The demo motion sidesteps pitch credibility but does not solve operational credibility."

This is the critical insight the existing research has been circling but never fully stated:

**Louis's credibility gap is operational, not pitch-related.**

The demo solves "does this product work?" — technical credibility. But the DG's real unasked question is: "will this person still be here in 6 months when the agent fails at 11pm on a Friday?" That question is answered by Louis's profile, not his prototype.

**What "free" actually does:**
- Delays the credibility collision — prospect sees working agent before hearing price
- Selects for budget-constrained, politically safe "let's try it free" buyers
- Creates zero urgency to convert to paid — the client has everything they want already
- Signals desperation in French PME culture ("gratuit = suspect")

**What "free" doesn't do:**
- Give Louis operational credibility he doesn't have
- Create a contractual obligation that forces a commercial conversation
- Select for buyers who have self-qualified by paying

**The structural insight:** The existing research (D182/D183) treats credibility as a price problem — lower the price, lower the credibility bar. But D191 correctly identifies the real constraint: Louis's credibility gap is structural and exists at every price point for pitch-led close. The demo motion is the right instinct. But the demo must be paired with a commercial trigger on Day 1, not deferred.

**Verdict on Q2:** Free masks the credibility problem by delaying it. It doesn't solve it. The demo is correct. The commercial trigger must be embedded in the demo (D198), not deferred to a post-pilot conversation.

### Question 3: Is "we built it for ourselves first (Kuroba)" the missing ingredient that makes demo-led work for Louis specifically?

**Finding: Yes — but for a reason the existing research hasn't fully articulated.**

D192 (Pulse 37) identifies Kuroba as "completely absent from external materials — add to all qui c'est responses." D194-D195 (Pulse 38) escalate this:

> "Kuroba = Month 1 internal deployment target. The ideal first proof point. Zero credibility problem, zero sales close needed."
> "'We built it for ourselves first' (Kuroba, Maëli/Gabin named) is the strongest available social proof."

**Why Kuroba is the missing ingredient:**

The existing research correctly identifies that Louis's credibility gap is operational, not pitch-related (D191). The question is: what signals operational credibility?

A demo shows technical credibility. A Kuroba deployment shows operational credibility.

When Louis says "we built it for ourselves at Kuroba," he is answering a different DG question than "does this product work?" He is answering "has this person actually deployed this in production, managed a client relationship, and delivered an outcome?" Kuroba is proof of operational delivery — exactly what the DG's unasked question requires.

**The specific mechanism:**
- Kuroba is a named, real company with named co-founders (Maëli, Gabin)
- It's a working deployment, not a demo
- It's in a similar vertical (web agency = service firm)
- Louis can say: "We run this at our own company. Here's what it does. Here's the output. Here's how long it's been running."
- This answers "will you still be here?" with "I've been running this for X months at my own company"

**Why this matters for demo-led specifically:**

The demo-led motion (D182) works when the demo shows something the prospect recognizes as real and operational. A demo of a Slack→Notion ticket agent built for a hypothetical company is a prototype demo. A demo of the same agent running live at Kuroba is a deployment demo. The latter is what the DG needs to see — and it costs Louis nothing to show.

**But the existing research undersells this:** D192 calls Kuroba "Louis's strongest credential." That's right but incomplete. Kuroba is not just a credential — it's the specific mechanism that makes the demo-led motion credible. Without Kuroba, the demo is "look what I built." With Kuroba, the demo is "look what's been running for 3 months at a company like yours."

**Verdict on Q3:** Yes — Kuroba is the missing ingredient. But only if it's positioned as a live operational deployment, not a reference. "We built it for ourselves first" is the strongest possible answer to the operational credibility question that the demo alone cannot answer.

## Verdict on the Overall Assumption

**"Demo-led + free first pilots = viable Year 1 GTM"**

**VERDICT: Demo-led = viable. Free first pilots = broken. Kuroba = the qualifier that makes demo-led work.**

The assumption is partially right but internally contradictory:

| Component | Status | Reasoning |
|-----------|--------|-----------|
| Demo-led motion | ✅ Viable | Correct mechanism for Louis's credibility profile; shows product, not founder |
| Free first pilots | ❌ Broken | No conversion trigger, wrong prospect selection, "gratuit = suspect" |
| Kuroba as social proof | ✅ Required | Makes demo-led operationally credible; absent from all materials |

**The compound finding:** Demo-led motion is the right instinct (D182). It correctly sidesteps pitch credibility. But it must be paired with:
1. A live Kuroba deployment as the social proof anchor
2. A pricing trigger embedded in the demo at minute 5-8 (D198), not deferred
3. A Day-1 commercial conversation — not "after the free month"

The existing research has already identified all three components (D190, D197, D198, D192, D194). The gap is execution: zero messages sent, Kuroba absent from materials, pricing script unrehearsed.

## What Should Change Based on Findings

### Changes to the Commercial Motion

1. **Kill "free pilot" as a standalone concept.** D183/D186 must be replaced with "demo → live Kuroba proof → priced engagement on Day 1." Free is not a phase — it's a specific tactic with a specific trigger.

2. **Kuroba must appear in every first-touch material.** Not just in the demo — in the LinkedIn outreach template, the email signature, the landing page, the "qui c'est" section. "We built it for ourselves first at Kuroba" is the answer to the unasked DG question.

3. **The demo must include pricing at minute 5-8 (D198).** This is non-negotiable. Leaving the pricing conversation to a follow-up call is how free pilots become zero-revenue engagements.

4. **Day 1 of any engagement includes an explicit commercial conversation.** Not: "let's do a free month and then see." But: "this month is free/included, at the end of the month we have a 15-minute conversation about whether and how to continue. Here's what that looks like."

5. **Louis's credibility ceiling applies at every price point for pitch-led close (D191).** This means the demo must do more work, and Kuroba must be doing the credibility work that Louis's profile cannot. The commercial motion must never rely on Louis's personal credibility — it must rely on the product's demonstrated credibility.

### Changes to External Materials

1. **Add "co-founded Kuroba web agency" to ALL external materials immediately.** This is D192, unexecuted after 40+ pulses. This single change likely has more credibility impact than any positioning decision made in the research.

2. **Remove "free" from all external language.** Replace with "first month included" — same economic structure, different psychological signal. "Included" implies value being extended. "Free" implies desperation.

3. **Landing page must feature Kuroba prominently.** Not "case study coming soon." A live URL to Kuroba.studio or equivalent, with the specific agent workflow described. "We run this ourselves" is the strongest headline.

## The Core Finding

The existing research correctly identified the three components (demo, free pilots, Kuroba) but treated them as independent decisions. They are not. They form a single GTM mechanism:

**Demo-led shows the product works. Kuroba shows it's been running in production. The commercial trigger on Day 1 converts the demonstration into a business relationship.**

Remove any one component and the mechanism fails:
- Demo without Kuroba = unproven prototype
- Kuroba without demo = referral, not acquisition
- Demo + Kuroba without a commercial trigger = free work with no revenue

The assumption "demo-led + free = viable" is wrong because it treats free as a price accommodation. Free is not the solution to Louis's credibility ceiling. Kuroba + a live deployment + an explicit commercial trigger on Day 1 is the solution. Free is a negotiating tactic with a specific trigger, not a business model.
