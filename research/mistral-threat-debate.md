## 2. Challenged Assumptions

### Assumption #1: "French infrastructure is a moat"

**Wrong. This is aspirational, not defensible.**

Mistral is French. OVHcloud is French. Mistral's models run on French and European infrastructure by default. If Mistral launches managed agents, French hosting and EU AI Act compliance are not advantages — they're table stakes. Louis cannot assume that "we're French and they're American/cloud-first" is a differentiator in 2027.

**What IS defensible:** The specific *configuration* of that infrastructure for specific vertical workflows. Mistral won't build compliance wrappers for accounting firms or legal practices — that's too niche. But Alizé can. That's where the real moat lives.

### Assumption #2: "Vertical depth creates a moat against Mistral"

**Partially wrong. Vertical depth creates friction, not a moat.**

Going deep in professional services (accounting, legal, consulting) is the right instinct. But Louis must understand what "deep" actually means:
- **Shallow depth:** Knows the industry jargon, has 3 case studies, made some integrations. Mistral can replicate this in 6 months with a couple hires and a partner ecosystem.
- **Real depth:** Has built 200+ workflow automations specific to French expertise-comptable firms. Has regulatory change detection built into agents. Has relationships with industry bodies. Has trained models on industry-specific document patterns. **That's a moat.**

Real vertical depth takes 18-24 months and significant client traction. If Alizé goes deep in 1-2 verticals and Mistral enters in 2027, those 18-24 months of accumulated workflow expertise are genuinely hard to copy. But only if the depth is real — not just "we've done a couple pilots in accounting."

### Assumption #3: "Mistral will stay infrastructure/API focused"

**Don't count on this.**

Mistral's CEO has publicly discussed agent frameworks. They have the capital ($2B+ raised, $400M ARR growing fast). They have the brand (French tech champion). They have partnerships (OVHcloud, possibly others). The question isn't whether Mistral CAN enter managed agents — it's whether they WILL prioritize it over API revenue.

**Reality check:** Mistral's current TAM is enterprise API consumers. Managed agents for PME/ETI is a different go-to-market motion entirely — it requires sales teams, implementation resources, customer success. This is not Mistral's core competency today. But they could acquire it (buying an ESN or a vertical SaaS) or build it (hire a 20-person services team).

**Assumption to replace this:** Mistral won't enter managed agents in 2026. They might in 2027. They will in 2028 if Alizé demonstrates real traction. Build for 2027 defense, not 2026 panic.

## 3. What's Actually Defensible vs. What's Aspirational

### DEFENSIBLE (real moats):

1. **Accumulated vertical workflow knowledge (18-24 months to build)**
   - Specific agent workflows for specific industries
   - Regulatory change detection (e.g., French tax law updates automatically reflected in agent behavior)
   - Integration patterns for industry-standard French software (Sage, Cegid, Pennylane for accounting)
   - This cannot be replicated by downloading Mistral's API

2. **Client relationships and trust (12+ months to build)**
   - A 100-person company that has been with Alizé for 12 months, has 3 agents running, and sees measurable ROI — that relationship has switching costs
   - Not just contractual switching costs — operational switching costs (re-training, re-integrating, re-configuring)
   - Mistral cannot buy this. They have to earn it.

3. **Reference clients with public case studies (6-12 months)**
   - "Here is a French accounting firm that saved 15 hours/week on invoice processing using Alizé"
   - This is content that Mistral cannot manufacture in 6 months
   - It takes time, which means it creates a window

4. **MCP-first technical architecture (already in place)**
   - Skills portability, self-hosted MCP servers, French infrastructure — this is real and Mistral doesn't have it today
   - But this becomes table stakes by 2027, not a durable moat

### ASPIRATIONAL (not defensible against Mistral):

1. **"Managed service + deployment + governance"** — Mistral can offer this. They have the model, the infra, the brand. If they decide to compete, they can build this in 12 months.
2. **"French provider"** — Mistral is more French than Alizé. This is not an advantage.
3. **"EU AI Act compliance"** — Native for Mistral given French/European HQ. Not a moat.
4. **Pricing alone** — If Mistral enters at €200/agent/month, Alizé's economics break. But Mistral's API business is higher-margin than managed services, so they may not want to compete on price in this segment.

## 4. If Mistral Enters — 3-Step Survival Strategy

### Step 1: LOCK IN (Months 1-6) — Get signed contracts before Mistral launches

**Why:** Mistral entering a market takes 6-12 months from decision to product. Use that window.

**Actions:**
- Launch pilot program aggressively: 5 pilots at discounted rates (€1,500 setup + €500/month) in exchange for 12-month commitments and case study rights
- Prioritize: Professional services firms (consulting, accounting, legal) — highest switching costs, most fragmented, most ROI-observable
- Target: Companies with 50-150 employees, 3+ business tools, demonstrable repetitive task burden
- Get LOIs signed now, even for pilots starting in 60 days
- **Key metric:** 5 signed pilots with LOIs by end of Month 2

**Why this works:** A company with 3 Alizé agents running for 9 months is not going to rip and replace for Mistral. The integration work, the team training, the trust — it's sticky. Mistral's sales team will have to displace an incumbent, not capture a greenfield opportunity.

### Step 2: GO DEEP (Months 3-12) — Build one vertical so deep Mistral can't replicate it in 12 months

**Why:** Generalist positioning dies against Mistral. Niche positioning survives.

**Actions:**
- Pick ONE vertical: professional services (accounting/bookkeeping firms or consulting) is the best bet — high volume of repetitive admin tasks, uses standard tools (Sage, Cegid, Pennylane), ROI is measurable in hours saved, regulatory environment creates stickiness
- Build 5-7 pre-built agent workflows specific to that vertical (not generic)
- Example for accounting firms: invoice processing → coded entry → reconciliation → tax deadline alerts → client Q&A from knowledge base
- Build a vertical-specific "agent pack" that includes all workflows + compliance templates + industry-specific RAG corpus
- Get 2-3 reference clients in that vertical with documented ROI (e.g., "18 hours/month saved per accounting clerk, €8,400/year in labor cost reduction")
- Publish case study. Get testimonial. Get industry body endorsement if possible.

**Why this works:** Mistral can build a "French accounting agent" in 6 months. They cannot build 15 accounting-specific workflows, tested in production, with French regulatory change detection, validated by 3 reference clients, in 6 months. The gap is real.

### Step 3: PARTNER (Months 6-18) — Build ecosystem relationships Mistral can't easily replicate

**Why:** Channel partnerships create distribution leverage and credibility signals that Mistral can't buy.

**Actions:**
- Partner with 2-3 French accounting software vendors (Sage France, Pennylane, Cegid) for integration certifications or referral arrangements
- Partner with 1-2 industry associations (e.g., Conseil Supérieur de l'Ordre des Experts-Comptables, SYNA, etc.) for credibility and lead flow
- Explore referral arrangements with business consulting firms (acceleration partners)
- Build a "certified partner" program: small French ESNs that implement Alizé for their clients
- Goal: 30% of new leads coming from partners by Month 18

**Why this works:** Mistral will go direct to enterprises with a product. They won't build a partner ecosystem for the PME/ETI segment for at least 2-3 years. Alizé can own the channel relationships in this segment.
