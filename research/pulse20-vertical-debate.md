# PULSE 20 — Vertical Commitment vs. Evidence: Should Alizé Pick a Vertical in Month 1?

**Agent:** Vertical Strategy Challenger
**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Verdict:** Alizé should NOT commit to professional services as the vertical in Month 1. The existing research (D44, D10) assumes this choice is made. It isn't. Workflow-first with horizontal outreach is the correct Year 1 strategy. Vertical focus emerges from evidence, not theory.

---

## The Case AGAINST Committing to a Vertical in Month 1

**D44 and D10 are leap-of-faith decisions dressed up as strategy.**

D44 says: "Professional services (consulting firms, 10-50 employees) first." D10 says: "ONE vertical, professional services first." These decisions are cited as settled but they rest on zero empirical evidence. No pilot has been delivered. No client feedback exists. No conversion data. No workflow rejection data. The entire vertical selection is a hypothesis being treated as a conclusion.

The research has never asked: what evidence would disprove "professional services first"? If the first three warm contacts — all from Louis's network — are in logistics, e-commerce, or manufacturing, does D44 still hold? If professional services DGs consistently say "I don't have the budget" or "we're too small for this," does the vertical survive? The research commits to a vertical without defining the conditions under which it would be abandoned. That's not strategy. That's narrative construction.

**Professional services is the hardest vertical to sell into for an unknown brand.**

Consulting firms and small professional services practices are, by definition, the most discerning buyers in the PME ecosystem. They sell expertise. They've seen every technology trend cycled through client pitches. They're sophisticated enough to evaluate AI agents critically and experienced enough to be skeptical of vendor promises. A 23-year-old M1 student pitching AI workflow automation to a 20-person consulting firm is the worst possible buyer-seller dynamic: the buyer has more domain expertise than the seller.

Meanwhile, the "professional services" vertical has the thinnest ROI math. Consulting firms bill by the hour. Their cost structure is people. An AI agent that "removes repetitive tasks" threatens to reduce billable hours — which is the opposite of what a consulting firm's DG wants. You're pitching efficiency to a business whose model is billed time. That's not a fit. That's a category mismatch.

**The "pick one vertical" advice comes from SaaS verticalization theory that doesn't apply here.**

The "go deep in one vertical" playbook works for SaaS companies because: (1) they build once, sell many times, (2) product-led growth benefits from concentrated word-of-mouth, (3) PLG requires network effects within a buyer community. Alizé is a services company. It deploys once per client, with significant customization. The "vertical concentration creates flywheel" logic applies to product companies with low marginal costs per additional customer. Alizé's marginal cost per additional client is high (delivery hours). Vertical concentration doesn't reduce costs; it just concentrates the risk of picking wrong.

## The Case FOR Staying Horizontal in Year 1

**Workflow-first is honest about what Alizé actually is at this stage.**

D77 proposes "back-office admin AI for service firms" as a framing. This is closer to right but still constrained by "service firms." Alizé doesn't know if it serves consulting firms, logistics companies, or e-commerce businesses. What it DOES know is that all of them have: email overload, meeting admin, CRM updates, document processing, internal Q&A. These workflows are horizontal. They exist in every sector.

The "back-office admin" frame — without the "service firms" constraint — is actually the right Year 1 positioning. Every PME, regardless of sector, has the same administrative friction. The differentiation isn't sector — it's that Alizé is the only provider offering managed AI agents that actually connect to these workflows. The sector is discovered from clients, not imposed on them.

**Network-first outreach (D26) is orthogonal to vertical-first strategy.**

D26 says: "warm network outreach primary Year 1." Louis's warm network isn't organized by vertical. It's organized by personal relationship. If Louis's network clusters around digital agencies (from Grinto), e-commerce founders (from Kuroba), and school校友 (from MyDigitalSchool), the vertical distribution of his network will be diverse, not concentrated in professional services. D44 assumes the network maps to "consulting firms." It might not.

If Alizé pre-commits to professional services and Louis's warm network is actually e-commerce and logistics, the entire GTM plan is misdirected from day one. The vertical should follow the network ICP mapping (U52), not precede it.

## The Challenge to Existing Research

**I'm challenging D44 and D10 directly — these are treated as settled decisions but they are hypotheses.**

D44 says: "Professional services (consulting firms, 10-50 employees) first." The evidence cited for this is: (1) they have repetitive administrative tasks, (2) they use multiple business tools, (3) they can afford the pricing. None of these criteria are unique to consulting firms. Logistics companies, e-commerce businesses, and manufacturing firms all meet the same criteria. The selection of consulting firms as the vertical is arbitrary — it's the "obvious" choice for people who think about AI agents, not the empirically validated choice.

**I'm challenging the assumption that vertical focus creates any advantage at 6-8 clients.**

D10 says: "ONE vertical, professional services first." At 6-8 clients in Year 1, Alizé has no meaningful vertical depth in any sector. 6-8 clients across a horizontal positioning means roughly 2-3 clients in each of 3 sectors. That's enough to identify patterns but not enough to claim vertical expertise. The "vertical depth creates moat" argument (D78) requires 5+ clients per vertical with deep operational integration. Year 1 Alizé won't have that regardless of which vertical it picks. The vertical focus strategy is premature by at least 12 months.

**I'm challenging D77's "service firms" constraint as unnecessary.**

D77 says: "back-office admin AI for service firms." Why the constraint? If the workflow is meeting report generation (D81), that's useful for any company with meetings — which is every company. The "service firms" qualifier narrows the market without adding differentiation. Any Alizé competitor can say "back-office admin AI." The constraint is a self-imposed limitation based on no evidence.

## Weaknesses in My Position

**Horizontal positioning is harder to market.**

"AI agents for back-office admin across all sectors" is a broader, less memorable message than "AI agents for consulting firms." Vertical focus gives you a story, a community, a reference group. Horizontal gives you a feature. This is a real marketing challenge and I may be underweighting it.

**Consulting firms may actually be a good fit despite my objections.**

The buy-vs-build problem I raised for digital agencies and e-commerce (D44, Pulse 11) might not apply to consulting firms. Small consulting firms don't build software — they deliver expertise. An AI agent that handles their admin doesn't threaten their billing model; it protects their margin. And consulting firms are often early adopters of productivity tools because they're sensitive to time cost. My objections to professional services may be wrong.

**Louis's warm network may actually be professional services.**

I don't know Louis's network. He may genuinely have 30+ warm contacts in consulting firms who would be ideal first clients. If the network maps to professional services, my argument is weakened substantially. I am arguing against a vertical choice I cannot evaluate because U52 (network ICP mapping) hasn't been resolved.

---

## Recommendation

**What Alizé should actually do:**

1. **Kill D44 and D10 as Month 1 commitments.** They are hypotheses, not validated strategy. Do not build a GTM plan around professional services until U52 (network ICP mapping) is resolved.

2. **Define the vertical AFTER the first 3-5 warm contacts are qualified.** Map Louis's network against ICP criteria (50-250 employees, 3+ business tools, operational admin pain). Whatever clusters emerge from that mapping IS the vertical. Do not impose vertical from theory.

3. **Use "back-office admin AI" WITHOUT the "service firms" constraint.** The frame is: "Alizé automates the back-office admin that slows down every growing business." Horizontal enough to match whatever network presents, specific enough to mean something.

4. **D77's constraint should be removed.** "Back-office admin AI for [ALL] PME" — let the vertical emerge from client evidence, not from a assumption made in Month 1.

5. **The first pilot is not a vertical commitment.** It's a workflow commitment (meeting report generation, per D81). The VERTICAL emerges from the third or fourth pilot, once there's enough network evidence to know which sectors are actually responding.

**The real risk:** Pre-committing to professional services in Month 1, pursuing the wrong network segment, and wasting 3 months on non-ICP contacts before realizing the vertical assumption was wrong. The vertical should be discovered, not selected.

*Analysis complete. Recommendation: reject D44/D10 as premature commitments. Define vertical from network ICP mapping (U52), not from theory. Remove "service firms" constraint from D77. Let vertical emerge from evidence.*
