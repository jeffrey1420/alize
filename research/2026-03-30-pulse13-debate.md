# Alizé Research — Pulse 13 Debate
**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Topic:** Conversion mechanism, lock-in positioning, and the website-as-blocker assumption
**Agents:** 3 specialist agents (conversion-guarantee, lock-in, website)

---

## What Was Debated

Three independent specialist agents challenged assumptions in three areas:

1. **Conversion Mechanism** — Whether the current guarantee structure (50% refund) and framing ("ownership transfer", month-to-month) actually drive conversion with French PME buyers
2. **Lock-in Positioning** — Whether "no lock-in" and "client controls" are genuine advantages or liabilities for Alizé
3. **Website as Blocker** — Whether the live website is a genuine prerequisite for commercial activity or a cold-outbound artifact that shouldn't block warm outreach

---

## Agent 1: Conversion Mechanism (pulse13-conversion-guarantee-debate.md)

### Challenged Assumption 1: "50% Refund If Unsatisfied" Is a Sufficient Guarantee

**The problem:** The 50% refund covers post-pilot disappointment, not pre-pilot commitment. For an unknown brand selling to a French PME buyer, the real fear isn't "what if I'm unhappy?" — it's "what if Alizé disappears mid-pilot?" A refund doesn't protect against vendor abandonment or execution failure. And French buyers perceive collectability of a refund as low.

**New approach:** Replace guarantee as a trust mechanism with staged payment + signed exit criteria:
- 50% at kickoff, 50% at day 15
- If Alizé doesn't deliver working results by day 15, client exits without the second payment (automatic, not adversarial)
- Refund language appears only in contract, never in sales materials
- Performance bond framing: if agent doesn't achieve agreed metrics in 30 days, Alizé delivers next workflow at no additional cost

### Challenged Assumption 2: "Operational Ownership Transfer" Framing Compels Sign-Off

**The problem (D29 challenged):** "Transfer" implies the DG gives something away. In a 20-50 person company, operational control is the DG's primary source of authority. "Transfer" triggers control anxiety. It also creates accountability ambiguity — if Alizé "owns" the workflow, who's accountable when it breaks? And in a French context, "transfer" triggers workforce concerns (what happens to the two people currently doing this work?).

**New approach:** Replace with **"delegation with accountability"** — Alizé operates the workflow, client retains oversight and can intervene anytime. "If it breaks, we fix it. If it doesn't perform, we adjust. You always have visibility." Frame the existing employee as the supervisor of the agent, not the replaced worker.

### Challenged Assumption 3: Month-to-Month Signals Confidence

**The problem:** Month-to-month signals Alizé doesn't trust itself to deliver — committed vendors ask for 12-month contracts. It creates perverse incentives (every month is a renewal conversation) and doesn't match how French PME buyers think about vendor relationships (they prefer established relational vendor models). Irony: lock-in, framed correctly, can be a selling point.

**New approach:** Kill month-to-month as the default. Replace with **12-month retainer with performance-based exit clause after month 3**. Month-to-month becomes a premium flexible option (20% surcharge). Never call it "no lock-in" — call it "flexibility after trust is established."

## Agent 2: Lock-in Positioning (pulse13-lockin-debate.md)

### Challenged Assumption 1: "No Lock-in" Is a Competitive Advantage

**The problem:** French business culture rewards vendors who embed themselves. "No lock-in" signals Alizé could leave when things get difficult — fragility, not freedom. Counterintuitively, deep integration (custom MCP connectors, process ownership, training, institutional knowledge) creates natural switching costs that are the mark of a valuable vendor, not a predatory one.

**New approach:** Remove "no lock-in" from all public-facing positioning. Replace with commitment language: "We build agents that become essential to how you operate." Test headline: "Des agents IA qui font partie de votre équipe — pas un outil que vous remplacez."

### Challenged Assumption 2: "Client Controls the Agent" Is a Positive Frame

**The problem:** PME buyers don't want control — they want relief. Control without expertise is a liability. Most Alizé clients never want to touch agent configuration — they want to point at a problem and have it solved.

**New approach:** Shift from "control" to "accountability." Sell: "We take responsibility for the agent's performance — you just measure the results." Remove "client controls" from pitch decks. Keep it only as a secondary technical point ("You can always review agent actions in the audit log").

### Challenged Assumption 3: Agentova Is the Primary Competitive Reference Point

**The problem:** The price gap is too large to share a frame. Agentova (€80-150/month) serves micro-enterprises; Alizé (€1,200-2,500/month) serves PME/ETI. Referencing Agentova implies Alizé is the "premium Agentova" — both inaccurate and underselling. D25 already acknowledges naming competitors is wrong. Category-defining companies don't compete by comparison.

**New approach:** Stop using Agentova as a primary comparison. Invest in category creation: "For companies that need AI agents that actually work in your operations — not just demos — Alizé is the managed service that builds, runs, and optimizes them for you."

## Agent 3: Website as First Blocker (pulse13-website-debate.md)

### Challenged Assumption 1: Website Is the First Blocker Because It Enables Cold Outbound

**The problem:** D47 treats "live website" as a universal first blocker, but it conflates two separate GTM channels. D26 already resolved that warm outreach is the primary Year 1 channel, with cold outbound secondary. But D47 applies the cold outbound requirement to warm outreach, which doesn't need it. Warm network recipients decide based on who Louis is and whether the offer is relevant — not whether alize.studio exists.

**New approach (D51):** Separate the timelines. Warm outreach to Louis's network can begin immediately using LinkedIn + direct messages. Cold outbound follows Month 2+ after website, case study, and ICP are locked. Don't let the slower track gatekeep the faster track.

### Challenged Assumption 2: Website Is the Minimum Viable Commercial Presence

**The problem:** For a productized consulting service selling to warm-network prospects, the MVI commercial presence is Louis himself — his LinkedIn profile, his reputation, his pitch materials. French SME leaders buy from people they know and trust, not from websites. A Loom video, a shared Google Doc proposal, or a direct calendar link requires zero website infrastructure.

**New approach:** Redefine "commercial presence" for warm channels. The MVP for warm outreach is: (1) Louis's LinkedIn profile positioned around AI agent deployment, (2) clear verbal and written pitch, (3) a one-page scope document for the €4,500 pilot, (4) optionally a shared Notion/Google Doc page. None of these require a domain purchase or hosting setup.

### Real First Blocker Identified

**The website is not the real first blocker. The real first blocker is the absence of warm outreach materials and the absence of outreach itself.** Louis could send a Loom video tomorrow and start scheduling discovery calls — no website required. The actual question: does Louis have a sufficient body of internal materials ready to send in a warm outreach context?
