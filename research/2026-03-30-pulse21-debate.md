## DEBATE 1: The Louis Capacity Crisis — Can Alizé Actually Execute?
**Agent:** Capacity Crisis Strategist

### The Assumption Being Challenged
That Alizé's execution plan can proceed without explicitly resolving how many hours Louis has available for Alizé work per week (U89). Every plan assumes Louis can deliver — but nobody has done the math.

### The Case FOR
The research has implicitly treated U89 as a sequencing problem: resolve strategy first, then figure out capacity. Louis's current overload is framed as temporary — it resolves once Alizé reaches revenue viability. Founders routinely operate at 100%+ for months before revenue materializes.

### The Case AGAINST

**Louis's actual time budget:**
- M1 master's: 20-25 hrs/week
- Grinto internship: 20 hrs/week
- Web agency co-founder: 10-15 hrs/week
- Personal/sleep/commute: 56 hrs/week (non-negotiable)
- **Total committed: 106-112 hrs/week**
- **Remaining for Alizé: 56-62 hrs/week maximum**

**Month 1 Alizé demand:**
- First pilot delivery (manual): 10-15 days × 8h = 80-120 hrs
- Warm outreach (50-75 contacts, 8-12 discovery calls): 30-40 hrs
- Playbook documentation: 20-30 hrs
- Technical build: 20-40 hrs
- Delivery partner coordination: 10-15 hrs
- **Total: 160-245 hrs**

The gap between available hours and demand is not a margin of safety — it is a structural impossibility if any single task runs long.

**D88's "playbook as moat" is a single-point-of-failure moat.** If Louis is the sole author of the delivery playbook, Alizé's moat is a person, not a system. One illness destroys it.

**U86 (senior commercial co-founder) cannot be "resolved" — it is a recruitment problem with no guaranteed timeline.** Every plan involving closing deals assumes this person exists. It does not.

### Verdict

**U89 is not an unresolved item — it is a structural contradiction that invalidates the current plan.** Louis does not have 56-62 hours of slack. The research has confused "available on paper" with "available in reality."

## DEBATE 2: Microsoft 365 Copilot — Alizé's Primary Competitive Threat
**Agent:** Competitive Threat Strategist

### The Assumption Being Challenged
That Agentova is Alizé's primary competitive threat. Agentova serves micro-SMBs at €49-99/month. Alizé serves PME/ETI at €800-1,500/month minimum. These are non-overlapping segments. The actual competitive threat is embedded in tools French companies already use: Microsoft 365 Copilot, Google Workspace AI, Salesforce Einstein.

### The Case FOR Copilot as the Real Threat

**Zero deployment advantage:** Copilot ships with Microsoft 365. No procurement cycle, no vendor evaluation, no onboarding. Alizé requires a new vendor, new contract, new data processing agreement.

**Price compounding:** Copilot at €30/user/month = €3,000/month for 100 users. Alizé's all-in cost (€800-1,500/month per agent + €3,000-6,000 setup) is 3-5x for comparable coverage.

**Integration depth already exists:** Copilot already handles meeting summaries, inbox triage, document Q&A, calendar management — exactly the workflows Alizé targets.

**Microsoft's incremental velocity:** Microsoft ships Copilot updates monthly. Every quarter it closes the gap on workflows Alizé claims as territory.

**Trust and procurement:** French PME already trust Microsoft. Buying from Alizé means new vendor approval, new RGPD review, new contract type.

### The Case AGAINST

**Copilot is a productivity layer, not a workflow automation engine:** It helps individuals work faster. It does not automate cross-system business processes. It does not connect CRM to ERP to HRIS to execute vendor onboarding.

**Copilot has no governance architecture:** No role-based agent permissions, no audit logging of agent actions, no human-in-the-loop gates, no per-client data isolation. These are Alizé's explicit differentiators — and they are disqualifying for regulated buyers.

**Microsoft moves slowly in enterprise features:** Copilot Studio agents require configuration, governance planning, and integration work — exactly Alizé's managed service territory. Microsoft has never been good at professional services delivery in France.

**French data sovereignty:** Genuine concern about US cloud providers under CLOUD Act for a subset of security-conscious buyers.

### Verdict

**Agentova framing is wrong — confirmed.** But Microsoft Copilot is not the existential threat it appears. It is a category validator and long-term risk, not an immediate displacement threat.

The real danger: Alizé's messaging sounds like "we also do AI for your Microsoft tools" — it loses to Microsoft on price and convenience. If Alizé's positioning is "we automate your business processes end-to-end, governed, on French infrastructure" — it occupies territory Microsoft cannot easily replicate.

## DEBATE 3: The First-Pilot Client Profile — Internal vs External
**Agent:** First-Pilot Strategist

### The Assumption Being Challenged
D14/D82: "20-30 person companies are the better first pilot target." The deeper assumption: the first pilot MUST be a paying external client relationship.

Every pilot plan assumes Alizé needs to find, close, and deliver to a paying stranger. This is wrong or backwards.

### The Case FOR Internal First Pilot

1. **No sales barrier.** Louis cannot credibly close €7,500+ alone (D84). The credibility window is 3-6 months (D87). Every failed close burns it. An internal pilot eliminates the sales problem entirely.

2. **Real operational data.** Louis knows Grinto's workflows. He can instrument and measure in days, not weeks. External clients require weeks of discovery.

3. **Working proof without credibility problem.** The proof of concept doesn't need to convince a DG — it needs to work.

4. **Clean case study ownership.** Internal deployment produces documentation Louis owns entirely. No legal review, no redaction, no "we don't want this published."

5. **Honest reality:** cold outreach to strangers is currently impossible. U86, U87, U88, U89 all unresolved. "Find a paying external pilot" is a plan with no substrate.

### The Case AGAINST

1. **No third-party credibility.** An internal case study reads as self-serving. The strongest objection: "of course it worked — you built it for yourself."

2. **Grinto conflict is a landmine.** IP ownership questions, conflict of interest — deploying Alizé at Grinto while interning there creates legal and political problems.

3. **Web agency is too small.** 2-3 person agency has no organizational complexity. The workflow problems Alizé solves don't exist at that scale.

4. **Sales problem deferred.** Internal pilot produces zero commercial learning. The sales problem is still unsolved when Month 2 arrives.

### Verdict

**The first deployment should be internal — but it is a "delivery rehearsal," NOT a client pilot.**

Separate the objectives:
- Finding a paying external client requires: U86, U87, U88, U89 all resolved. These conditions are not met.
- Proving delivery capability requires: real workflow, real data, real outcomes. Grinto or web agency provides this without those conditions.

The sequence:
1. Month 1: Internal delivery rehearsal (web agency — no conflict, Louis owns output)
2. Month 2: First external pilot (20-30 person companies, with actual delivery evidence)
3. Month 3+: Senior commercial co-founder conversations, case study in hand

**Hard deadline:** 4 weeks maximum for delivery rehearsal. If outcomes not measurable by Week 4, reassess before pursuing external clients.
