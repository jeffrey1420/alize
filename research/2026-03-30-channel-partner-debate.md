# Channel Partner Viability Audit — Alizé

**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Auditor:** Channel Partner Viability Auditor
**Scope:** Challenge Path 3 (channel partner) from D219 commercial model options

---

## Executive Summary

Channel partner is being proposed as Alizé's commercial layer — accounting firms, consulting firms, business software resellers referring AI agent services to their clients. This is structurally flawed. The channel partner path does not solve Louis's €7,500+ closing problem; it creates several new ones. **Verdict: DEAD END for Year 1, CONDITIONALLY VIABLE for Year 2+ with significant caveats.**

---

## Assumption Being Challenged

**The GTM Roadmap (03-gtm.md) presents channel partners — specifically accounting firms (expert-comptables), consulting firms, and business software resellers — as a core GTM channel from Month 3-4 onward.**

The existing research brief states: *"French business culture: strong preference for trusted advisors (expert-comptables, commissaires aux comptes, business consultants)"* — implying these trusted advisors would naturally refer AI services to their clients.

**This assumption is wrong. Trusted advisor relationships are the obstacle, not the vehicle.**

---

## The Five Challenges

### Challenge 1: Accounting Firms Are Building Their Own AI Practices — Not Referring Out

**Assumption challenged:** Expert-comptables (French accounting firms) will refer AI agent services to their clients because they have trusted advisor relationships.

**Why it's wrong:**

Expert-comptables are not passive referral conduits. They are actively building their own AI service offerings. The French accounting profession has been under severe margin pressure for years — compliance automation, cloud accounting, and now AI are all threats AND opportunities. The firms that survive are the ones who internalize AI capabilities, not the ones who white-label a competitor's service.

In 2025-2026, the major accounting software vendors (Cegid, Sage, Pennylane) are all actively pushing AI features to their accounting firm customers. An expert-comptable who adopts Cegid's AI tools can offer "AI-enhanced accounting services" to their clients. Why would they refer a third-party AI agent provider instead?

**The incentive structure is backwards.** A trusted advisor refers when:
- The referral is free (minimal effort)
- The referral generates a meaningful recurring kickback
- The referral doesn't compete with their core service

None of these conditions favor Alizé. A referral to Alizé:
- Requires active client education about a new category
- Generates a one-time 15-20% referral fee (€300-1,500 per client, not recurring)
- Potentially exposes the accounting firm's client to a technology that renders parts of the accounting relationship redundant

**What the correct framing should be:** Expert-comptables are potential CLIENTS, not partners. They have the same back-office admin problems as Alizé's target ICP. An expert-comptable firm with 10-50 employees IS the buyer profile. The referral relationship is backwards — Alizé should be selling to the accounting firm, not through them.

---

### Challenge 2: Business Software Resellers Are Building AI Offerings — Not Referring to Alizé

**Assumption challenged:** Sage, Cegid, SAP partners will refer Alizé to their existing client base.

**Why it's wrong:**

This assumption treats resellers as passive distribution pipes. In reality, ERP and accounting software resellers in France are in the most direct competitive position against Alizé.

**The reseller landscape in 2026:**
- **Cegid** (the dominant French accounting/ERP SaaS) has been aggressively rolling out AI features across its platform since 2024. Cegid's own customers are being sold "AI inside Cegid" by Cegid's own team and certified partners.
- **Sage France** has positioned itself as an "AI-powered business management" platform. Their reseller network is actively selling Sage AI features to existing customers.
- **Pennylane** (the French cloud accounting challenger) launched with AI-native architecture and is adding agent capabilities.

A Sage or Cegid reseller's economic interest is to:
1. Sell more Sage/Cegid licenses (including AI add-ons)
2. Earn implementation and support fees on the existing platform
3. NOT introduce a third-party competitor that makes their platform look inadequate

**What the correct framing should be:** These resellers are competitors, not partners. Any reseller who would refer Alizé is either (a) not a top performer for their vendor, or (b) has a conflict of interest they're not disclosing. The reseller channel for AI agents in France in 2026 is already captured by the ERP vendors themselves.

---

### Challenge 3: French Accounting/Consulting Firms Are NOT Talking About AI Automation in 2026

**Assumption challenged:** The "strong preference for trusted advisors" means accounting/consulting firms are actively discussing AI automation with clients in 2026.

**Why it's overstated:**

The premise that French PME trust their accounting firms on AI is conflating two different things:
1. "My accounting firm uses software that has AI features" — YES, this is happening
2. "My accounting firm is actively recommending AI agent services to replace/adjoin their work" — NO, this is not happening at scale

The French accounting profession is talking about AI, but the conversation is about:
- How to use AI within accounting workflows (automating bookkeeping, fraud detection)
- How to advise clients on AI governance (especially for commissaires aux comptes doing financial audits)
- How to position themselves as "AI-ready" to retain existing clients

They are NOT talking about:
- Referring AI agent startups to clients
- White-labeling a third-party AI service
- Adding a recurring referral revenue stream from a managed AI service

**The actual data point:** The existing research states only ~26% of French companies 20-200 employees use generative AI. Within that, the vast majority are using Copilot, ChatGPT, or similar consumer-grade tools — not managed AI agent services. The accounting firms that ARE talking about AI are talking about it for themselves, not as a service they sell to clients.

**What the correct framing should be:** The "trusted advisor" channel is a misread of French business culture. The trust relationship means a buyer will take a meeting if their accountant recommends something — but it does NOT mean the accountant will actively recommend something that competes with their own service. The advisor relationship is a pre-existing trust asset that must be EARNED, not borrowed.

### Challenge 4: Channel Economics Don't Work at Alizé's Price Points

**Assumption challenged:** A 15-20% referral fee creates viable channel economics for Alizé.

**Why it's wrong:**

Let's do the math on a 15-20% referral fee at Alizé's pricing:

| Alizé Price Point | 15% Referral | 20% Referral |
|---|---|---|
| €1,500 diagnostic | €225 | €300 |
| €4,500 pilot | €675 | €900 |
| €7,500 pilot | €1,125 | €1,500 |
| €2,000/month (12 months) | €3,600 | €4,800 |

**The problems:**

**Problem A — The fee is too small to motivate a serious referral effort.**
For a referral to be worth a channel partner's time, the economics need to be compelling. A €225 referral fee for a diagnostic? That's a waste of everyone's time. Even €1,500 for a €7,500 pilot referral sounds okay until you realize:
- The partner has to identify a qualified prospect
- Educate them on a new category
- Make a warm introduction
- Follow up

That's 5-10 hours of work for €1,500. That's €150-300/hour. But only if every referral converts. In reality, conversion rates on referred leads are typically 20-40%. So the effective hourly rate drops to €30-60/hour — below minimum wage. No serious business allocates senior advisor time at that rate.

**Problem B — Recurring revenue sharing is not standard in this market.**
Software reseller referral programs (the model being proposed) typically pay on REVENUE, not on initial contract value. But Alizé's managed service model at €2,000-5,500/month means:
- First-year value: €24,000-66,000
- 15% of first year: €3,600-9,900 per client
- 20% of first year: €4,800-13,200 per client

This is a meaningful number. But here's the catch: this requires the partner to track and report recurring revenue, which French accounting/consulting firms are not set up to do. They're not SaaS resellers. They don't have partner portal access, revenue tracking dashboards, or the operational infrastructure for recurring commission management.

**Problem C — The fee structure creates adverse selection.**
The channel partners who are most willing to give 15-20% are the ones who can't close direct sales — i.e., the weakest commercial partners. The best referral sources (if they exist) would demand higher fees or revenue-share arrangements that Alizé can't afford.

**What the correct framing should be:** Channel economics at Alizé's price points only work if:
1. The referral fee is 25-30% of first-year revenue (not 15-20% of pilot)
2. The partner is actively motivated (i.e., has no competing offering)
3. The partner has existing infrastructure to manage referrals

None of these conditions are met in Year 1.

### Challenge 5: Channel Build Timeline — Months 3-4 Is Fantasy

**Assumption challenged:** Channel partnerships can be established in Months 3-4, solving Alizé's 3-6 month commercial window problem.

**Why it's wrong:**

Building a functioning channel takes 6-12 months minimum, even with a mature product and established brand.

**The channel build timeline reality:**

| Stage | Time Required | Alizé Reality |
|---|---|---|
| Partner identification & vetting | 4-6 weeks | No product, no case studies, no brand |
| Initial conversation & pitch | 4-8 weeks | Partner pitch requires something to pitch |
| Legal/compliance review | 4-8 weeks | French legal review is slow |
| Partner onboarding & training | 4-8 weeks | Requires sales playbook, demo environment |
| First referral generated | 8-16 weeks after onboarding | No historical conversion data |
| First referral closes | +4-8 weeks | Average B2B sales cycle is 3-6 months |

**Total minimum timeline: 6-9 months from channel program launch to first closed referral.**

**The 3-6 month window problem (D82) is about Louis's credibility and capacity — not about having a channel partner strategy in place.**

If the channel strategy is launched in Month 3, the first closed referral comes in Month 9-12. That's AFTER the window has closed, not solving it.

More fundamentally: channel partners require something to sell. A channel with no product, no case studies, no pricing clarity, and no demo environment is not a channel — it's a PowerPoint. Alizé doesn't have the prerequisites for channel development in Year 1.

**What the correct framing should be:** Channel development is a Year 2 activity, not a Year 1 commercial strategy. Year 1's commercial engine is direct outbound (warm network + cold outreach), not indirect channel. Any "channel partner" conversations in Year 1 should be treated as market learning, not commercial execution.

## Core Assumption Challenged From Existing Research

**Assumption:** *"French business culture: strong preference for trusted advisors (expert-comptables, commissaires aux comptes, business consultants)"* — implying this preference can be leveraged as a commercial channel.

**This assumption misreads the nature of trusted advisor relationships in France.**

The trust that French PME place in their expert-comptables and business consultants is:
1. **Specific** — trust in the advisor's domain expertise (tax, accounting, legal), not in their technology recommendations
2. **Self-interested** — the advisor recommends things that reinforce the relationship, not things that might make them redundant
3. **Conservative** — French business culture (especially in professional services) resists recommending unproven technology vendors to clients

The correct reading: French PME trust their advisors on matters within the advisor's expertise. Recommending an AI agent startup to a client is:
- Outside the advisor's domain expertise
- Potentially self-undermining (if AI agents reduce accounting fees)
- Unproven and reputationally risky

**What the correct framing should be:** The trusted advisor dynamic is relevant for SELLING TO the advisor (as a buyer) or for getting a warm introduction TO the advisor's clients (with the advisor's active endorsement). But the advisor-as-channel-partner model — where the advisor actively promotes Alizé as a revenue stream — is structurally incompatible with the trust dynamic.

## Verdict

### Channel Partner Path: **DEAD END for Year 1**

**Reasoning:**

Every channel partner type proposed — accounting firms, consulting firms, business software resellers — faces structural barriers that cannot be resolved in Alizé's 3-6 month commercial window:

1. **Accounting firms are buyers, not channels.** The correct play is selling AI agent services TO expert-comptable firms (they have the same back-office admin problems), not through them.

2. **Software resellers are competitors.** The Cegid/Sage/Pennylane partner ecosystem is already captured by those vendors' own AI roadmaps. Alizé cannot out-partner vendors whose partners have deep existing relationships.

3. **Channel economics are broken at 15-20%.** The math doesn't work for either party. Either the fee is too small to motivate effort, or Alizé can't afford the economics at scale.

4. **Channel build timeline is 6-9 months minimum.** This doesn't solve the 3-6 month window — it arrives after the window has closed.

5. **The trusted advisor dynamic is the obstacle, not the vehicle.** French advisors are conservative, self-interested, and lack the commercial motivation to promote an unproven AI agent startup to their clients.

### What IS Viable: Channel as Year 2+ Activity

The channel partner path is not permanently closed. It becomes viable if:
- Alizé has 10+ case studies with measurable ROI
- Alizé has a defined, repeatable workflow offering (not "AI agents")
- A partner category emerges that has no competing AI offering AND motivated economics
- The partner is a actual buyer first (e.g., expert-comptable firm becomes a client, then refers peer firms)

### Immediate Implications for Alizé

1. **Kill channel partner as a Year 1 GTM channel.** Remove from Month 3-4 priorities in 03-gtm.md.

2. **Redirect expert-comptable outreach.** Approach accounting firms as ICP clients (they have 10-50 employees, back-office admin problems, and budget authority), not as referral partners.

3. **Treat any partner conversations in Year 1 as market learning.** Document what partners say they want, but do not build pipeline planning around it.

4. **The commercial layer problem (D84: Louis can't close €7,500+ deals) is not solved by channels.** This must be solved by adding a senior commercial co-founder or downmarket repositioning — the other two paths from D219.
