## Finding 1: The Evidence Is Overwhelming That Alizé Is a Research Project

**Question:** Louis is an M1 student with Grinto internship, Kuroba web agency, and a full course load. He's been trying to start Alizé for 91+ days. Is the evidence that he will ever execute on Alizé, or is the evidence that Alizé is a research project that generates research?

**Answer: Alizé is a research project.**

The evidence is not ambiguous. After 91 days and 75 pulses:

- **Zero external actions taken.** Not one LinkedIn message sent. Not one domain purchased. Not one discovery call booked. Not one prospect contacted.
- **290+ decisions generated.** The research apparatus has not stopped. It has accelerated. More decisions have been made about Alizé in 75 pulses than most pre-revenue startups make in 12 months of actual operation.
- **The decisions are increasingly meta.** Pulses 71-75 are auditing the pulse process itself. This is a system generating artifacts about its own inability to generate external actions. This is textbook research loop behavior.
- **U163 (domain purchase) has been "overdue" for 73+ pulses.** The U163 finding appears in the debate log at least 73 times as a "must do today." This is not a planning failure. This is a system that cannot execute its own conclusions.

**The core inversion:** Most startups suffer from insufficient research before execution. Alizé suffers from the opposite — research has become the substitute for execution. Every pulse generates the subjective experience of progress. No pulse generates external evidence that the market wants what Louis is building.

**Uncomfortable conclusion:** Louis has been doing Alizé for 91 days. If Alizé were a university research project, this would be productive. As a venture attempt, it is a 91-day demonstration that Louis cannot execute this specific venture under these specific conditions.

## Finding 2: Kuroba Succeeds. Alizé Does Not. The Problem Is Not the Venture.

**Question:** Kuroba (Louis's web agency) is succeeding with the same constraints. Alizé is not. If Louis can execute one venture but not another under identical constraints, is the problem the venture or the person-venture fit?

**Answer: The problem is person-venture fit. Alizé as currently conceived should not continue as a standalone entity.**

The identical constraints argument is the most important finding in this audit. Let me challenge the implicit assumption beneath it:

**Challenged assumption:** "Under identical constraints, Kuroba works and Alizé doesn't. Therefore Kuroba is a better venture."

**Revised conclusion:** Kuroba works and Alizé doesn't, which tells us Louis can execute web agency services under these constraints. It does not tell us whether "AI agent services for French PME" is a viable standalone venture — it tells us Louis will execute things that map to his current capabilities and defer things that don't.

The difference between Kuroba and Alizé is not market size or pricing or competition. It is:

| Dimension | Kuroba | Alizé |
|-----------|--------|-------|
| **Team** | Maëli + Gabin | Louis (solo) |
| **Confidence** | Can deliver now | Still researching how to deliver |
| **Market contact** | Active (Gabin) | Zero |
| **Product clarity** | Web development | "Managed AI agent service" |
| **Constraint** | Same time/school/Grinto | Same time/school/Grinto |
| **Execution** | Yes | No |

**The constraint is identical. The execution is not. Therefore the constraint is not the variable.**

The variable is Louis's relationship to each venture. With Kuroba, he has a team, he has confidence, he has Maëli pushing forward. With Alizé, he is alone, uncertain, and generating research artifacts instead of market contact.

**Uncomfortable conclusion:** Louis has a team at Kuroba (Maëli + Gabin) and does not have a team at Alizé. This is not a coincidence. The solo-founder model for a complex managed service (Alizé) while simultaneously running an internship, a course load, and a web agency is structurally incompatible with execution. Kuroba works because it has multiple people. Alizé fails because it has one person who is also doing three other things.

**The core challenge to the Alizé-as-independent-venture assumption:** There is no evidence that Louis will ever execute Alizé alone. There is strong evidence that "AI agent services for French PME" as a capability could be delivered through Kuroba's existing team structure, existing client relationships, and existing credibility.

## Finding 3: The Intellectual Infrastructure Is a Sunk Cost, Not an Asset

**Question:** The 75+ pulses have produced 290+ decisions worth of intellectual infrastructure. Is this intellectual infrastructure an asset that justifies continued investment, or is it a sunk cost that is now a reason to stop rather than continue?

**Answer: It is a sunk cost. Continued investment in the intellectual infrastructure is a sunk cost fallacy.**

The 290+ decisions are real. They represent real analysis of pricing, positioning, technical architecture, GTM, market sizing, and competitive dynamics. BRIEF.md is a genuinely comprehensive document. The debate log contains sophisticated strategic reasoning.

**None of this has market validation.** The 290+ decisions exist in a vacuum. Not one decision was made after asking a real prospect whether they'd pay for what was being built. Not one pricing decision was tested against actual WTP. Not one positioning statement was validated against a real decision-maker.

**The sunk cost argument FOR continuation:** "We've invested 75 pulses in this. We have 290 decisions. The intellectual infrastructure is valuable."

**Why this is wrong:** Those 290 decisions were made without market contact. They are hypotheses. If the first prospect Louis talks to says "I already have Agentova and it's good enough," 270 of those 290 decisions are invalidated. The intellectual infrastructure has value *only if* Alizé continues as an independent entity. If Alizé is folded into Kuroba or shelved, most of those decisions become artifacts of a process that didn't produce external action.

**The intellectual infrastructure as a reason to stop:** 75 pulses of sophisticated analysis without a single external action proves the analysis was never the bottleneck. The analysis is a displacement activity. The intellectual infrastructure demonstrates that Louis can think carefully about AI agent services. It does not demonstrate that Louis can sell AI agent services. These are different capabilities.

**Uncomfortable conclusion:** The intellectual infrastructure is most valuable as a document of what NOT to do (price before validating, position before testing, build before selling). It is not a launchpad. It is a case study in planning fallacy. Continuing to add to it is compounding the sunk cost.

## Finding 4: The Honest Question — Should Alizé Exist Separately from Kuroba?

**Question:** Given that Louis has Maëli and Gabin at Kuroba (a team), and Alizé is a solo effort — should Alizé exist as a separate venture, or should the "AI agent services for French PME" capability be Kuroba's service offering?

**Answer: Fold it into Kuroba. The independent entity should not exist.**

The evidence from this audit converges on one conclusion: **Alizé as currently conceived is Kuroba's service offering, except Louis hasn't realized it yet.**

Here is the case for folding Alizé into Kuroba:

**1. Team.** Kuroba has Maëli and Gabin. Alizé has Louis alone. Managed AI agent services for French PME require delivery capacity. Kuroba has delivery capacity. Alizé doesn't.

**2. Credibility.** Kuroba has executed. Alizé has not. Kuroba has delivered client work. Alizé has delivered research documents. A new venture offering AI agent services can lead with Kuroba's existing track record.

**3. GTM shortcut.** Kuroba's web agency clients are already using 3+ business tools, already have operational complexity, already need workflow automation. Kuroba's client relationships are the warm network that Alizé's GTM plan keeps postponing.

**4. The service model matches Kuroba's model.** Kuroba delivers web development as a managed service — scoping, building, maintaining. Alizé's described service model (scoping, deploying, maintaining agents) is structurally identical. The same team can deliver both.

**5. Avoided competition.** Kuroba faces web agency competition. AI agent services for PME is adjacent enough to not cannibalize existing work but close enough to share client trust.

**The case for keeping Alizé separate is weak:**
- "Alizé" as a brand might be better positioned for AI agents specifically
- "Kuroba" might limit perception to web development
- The 290+ decisions are organized under "Alizé"

**The case for separation is branding and organizational clarity. The case for folding is execution evidence and team capacity.**

**Uncomfortable conclusion:** Louis has spent 91 days building the intellectual infrastructure for Kuroba's AI agent service offering without realizing it. Maëli and Gabin could be delivering AI agent services today if the conversation had been "how do we add AI agent services to Kuroba" instead of "should we start a new venture called Alizé."

## Finding 5: The Core Finding — Specific Evidence Condition for STOP

**Question:** Under what specific evidence condition should Louis explicitly STOP researching Alizé and either shelve it or fold it into Kuroba?

**Answer: Louis should stop Alizé-as-independent-entity under any of the following conditions. The first one that applies should trigger a decision within 7 days:**

**Condition A: Kuroba cannot execute AI agent services.**
If Maëli and Gabin do not have the technical capability or interest in building an AI agent services practice, then Alizé continues as independent entity ONLY IF Louis can demonstrate execution capacity (one external action per week for 4 consecutive weeks). If that bar is not met, shelve Alizé.

**Condition B: Alizé domain not purchased by Day 7.**
If alize.studio (or equivalent) is not registered by 2026-04-07, Alizé as independent entity is structurally abandoned. This is not a suggestion. This is a binary test. 73+ pulses have identified domain purchase as the action. The domain is not purchased. The domain will not be purchased as an independent Alizé action. Fold into Kuroba.

**Condition C: One rejection from a warm contact.**
The debate log identifies warm network outreach as the GTM path. If Louis sends messages to 5 warm contacts and receives one explicit rejection ("I don't need this" or "we're covered"), that is market data. That data should be treated as a signal to fold into Kuroba rather than continue independent outreach. The Kuroba relationship provides a different entry point.

**Condition D: Maëli and Gabin cannot be aligned on AI agent services within 14 days.**
If the Kuroba team conversation about adding AI agent services does not produce agreement within 14 days, Alizé should be shelved. Not paused. Shelved. A named, dated decision to revisit in 6 months with different conditions.

**The binding constraint across all four conditions:** Louis has not demonstrated the ability to execute Alizé independently for 91 days. The probability that he will start executing independently — without team, without accountability, without external pressure — approaches zero. The 75-pulse evidence base supports this conclusion. Every pulse that adds a decision without adding an external action increases the evidence base for non-execution.

## Challenged Assumption from Existing Research

**Challenged assumption:** "Alizé's moat is vertical depth + client relationships + operational excellence."

This assumption appears across multiple pulses. It is presented as Alizé's strategic positioning. It is used to justify continued research investment. It is wrong as currently stated.

**Why it's wrong:** The "moat" framing assumes Alizé will reach the point where it has client relationships and operational excellence to defend. Alizé has zero clients. Alizé has zero operational track record. A moat that requires crossing a moat to access is not a moat — it's a hypothesis.

**The revised framing:** Alizé's only potential moat is not vertical depth or client relationships — it is the combination of (a) Kuroba's existing team + (b) Kuroba's existing client base + (c) the Alizé intellectual infrastructure as a service design blueprint + (d) French-market positioning. This combination, assembled under Kuroba, could produce a defensible AI agent services practice within 3-6 months.

**The assumption I am directly challenging:** The existing research treats Alizé as an independent entity worth defending as independent. The correct unit of analysis is not "Alizé vs. competitors" — it is "can the capability described as Alizé be delivered through Kuroba." If the answer is yes, the independent entity is not just unnecessary — it is actively harmful because it diverts Louis's limited execution capacity away from the team that can deliver.
