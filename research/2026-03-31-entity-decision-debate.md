# Entity Decision Debate: Kuroba-Alizé Relationship
**Date:** 2026-03-31
**Session:** alize-pulse-entity
**Assumption Challenged:** "Kuroba and Alizé are separate ventures, operated in parallel by the same founders."

---

## The Core Assumption Under Examination

The research corpus treats Kuroba and Alizé as two distinct projects sharing founders — a parallel operation structure. This framing has governed how credibility, sequencing, and resource allocation have been discussed across 64+ pulses.

**The assumption is wrong. Or more precisely: it may be actively harmful to treat them as separate when the evidence suggests they should be treated as one sequential story.**

The Kuroba Sequencing Auditor (2026-03-31) and the Pulse 64 Kuroba Evidence Transfer Challenge have already established that Kuroba-first as a *delivery rehearsal strategy* fails on multiple grounds (scale mismatch, adversarial dynamic absence, evidence type mismatch). This debate takes a different cut: whether the *entity relationship itself* — how Louis presents himself externally — is misconfigured, and what the correct relationship between the two ventures actually is.

---

## The Four Debated Questions

### Question 1: Is the Kuroba brand MORE credible than an unnamed Alizé for Louis's first outreach?

**Short answer: Yes. But "more credible" is the wrong frame.**

Kuroba has done something Alizé has not: it exists. It has clients, deliverables, a domain, and a co-founders-with-skin-in-the-game structure. An unnamed Alizé has none of these things. Of course Kuroba is more credible as a credential — it is an actual reference point.

But the relevant comparison is not "Kuroba vs. unnamed Alizé." It is "Kuroba credential in service of Alizé pitch vs. no credential at all." The first question assumes Kuroba and Alizé compete for the same credibility signal. They don't. They serve different purposes.

**The actual credibility question for first outreach is:**

- Does saying "I co-founded Kuroba" make a prospect more likely to trust me with their Alizé pilot? **Yes.**
- Does saying "I founded Alizé" (when Alizé has no external evidence) make a prospect more likely to engage? **No — it's a claim with no backing.**
- Does leading with Kuroba contaminate the Alizé pitch — make it seem like "the web agency person trying to sell me something AI"? **Possibly, depending on how it's framed.**

**The framing problem:** "Co-founder of Kuroba" positions Louis as a web services provider who is now branching into AI agents. This is not a credibility boost for Alizé's positioning — it is a category assignment. The prospect hears "web agency" and files the conversation accordingly. Alizé's premium positioning (€1,500-7,500 pilots, managed service, EU AI Act compliance) is inconsistent with a "web agency co-founder" frame.

**The Kuroba credential is worth something — but it comes at a framing cost that must be managed explicitly.**

### Question 2: Should Louis lead with "co-founder of Kuroba" rather than "founder of Alizé" in first-contact situations?

**Recommendation: Yes — but only with a specific transition sentence, and only if the Kuroba-Alizé relationship is coherently explained in the same message.**

The current framing problem: Louis has two ventures, one with evidence (Kuroba) and one without (Alizé). Leading with "founder of Alizé" invites the question "what have you built?" — to which there is no satisfying answer. Leading with "co-founder of Kuroba" provides immediate proof of execution capability.

**The critical condition:** The Kuroba credential only converts to Alizé credibility if Louis explains the relationship in the same breath. If he says "I co-founded Kuroba" and stops, he's a web agency person. If he says "I co-founded Kuroba, and I'm now building Alizé to deploy AI agents for French PME" — that's a different sentence. The second sentence changes what the first sentence means.

**The actual first-contact formula should be:**

> "I co-founded Kuroba, a web agency that's running AI-powered workflows for our clients. I'm now building Alizé to do the same for larger operations — managed AI agents that handle back-office work."

This framing:
- Uses Kuroba as proof of execution (not as the product)
- Positions Alizé as the escalation (not the competitor)
- Avoids the "web agency trying to sell me AI" problem by naming the relationship explicitly
- Gives the prospect a clear mental model: "this person already does this somewhere smaller"

**What this means for the "separate ventures" assumption:** If Kuroba and Alizé are truly separate, this framing doesn't work — because there's no logical connection between "I run a web agency" and "I also want to sell you AI agents." The separate-ventures framing makes Kuroba a credibility liability, not an asset, for Alizé outreach.

**The framing only works if Kuroba and Alizé are explicitly connected — which means they are not truly separate ventures in the way the research has assumed.**

### Question 3: Does operating Kuroba first (as is currently happening) consume the trust/capital needed to launch Alizé?

**Answer: Yes — but not in the way the question assumes. The consumption is not of Alizé's resources. It is of Louis's limited hours, attention, and credibility window.**

The Pulse 21 capacity analysis established that Louis has ~56-62 hours/week across Grinto, Kuroba, and M1. The Alizé window is 3-6 months (D82). Operating Kuroba "in parallel" with Alizé is a scheduling statement that ignores the capacity crisis.

But the deeper consumption is **credibility window consumption**. The Kuroba Sequencing Auditor established that Kuroba has been "imminent" for 18+ pulses without executing. Every week spent preparing Kuroba's AI deployment is a week not spent on external Alizé outreach that would produce market-validated signals.

**The consumption math:**

| Activity | Hours/Week | External Validation Produced |
|----------|-----------|---------------------------|
| Kuroba-first (prep + deployment) | 15-20 | Zero — internal operation |
| Alizé warm outreach (D26, D118) | 5-10 | Immediate — prospect responses, objections, interest signals |
| Alizé pilot delivery | 20-30 | 30-60 days later — actual case study or rejection |

**The "separate ventures operated in parallel" assumption is what makes Kuroba-first seem like a viable strategy.** If the ventures are truly separate, operating them in parallel is just efficient resource allocation. But they are not separate — they compete for Louis's limited hours, and they compete for the same credibility window (3-6 months). Every hour on Kuroba is an hour not on Alizé. The parallelism is a fiction created by the "separate ventures" framing.

**The actual relationship:** Kuroba and Alizé are sequential ventures sharing one founder's bandwidth. The "parallel" framing has allowed Kuroba to consume the resources that should go to Alizé without anyone flagging it as a trade-off.

### Question 4: Is there a world where Kuroba IS the Alizé pilot — i.e., deploy an AI agent for Kuroba's own workflow as the first commercial proof?

**Answer: Yes — but with a fundamentally different framing than "Kuroba as internal rehearsal." The framing must be "Kuroba as first Alizé client," not "Kuroba as delivery practice."**

This is the most commercially viable version of the Kuroba-first strategy, and it has been misdiagnosed in the existing research.

**The correct frame:**

- **Wrong frame:** "Let's use Kuroba to practice deploying AI agents before we do it for real clients" (Kuroba as rehearsal)
- **Correct frame:** "Kuroba is the first company to use Alizé's managed AI agent service; here's what it does for their operation" (Kuroba as first client)

The difference is not semantic. In the wrong frame, Kuroba is an internal experiment whose results are soft and non-commercial. In the correct frame, Kuroba is a paying (or at-cost) client whose engagement produces a real case study with real metrics.

**What this means in practice:**

1. **Louis signs a pilot agreement with Kuroba** (even if at no cost or heavily subsidized) — this makes it a commercial relationship, not an internal test
2. **One specific workflow is automated** — not "we're going to run all of Kuroba through Alizé," but one defined workflow with measurable outcomes
3. **The case study is published** with Kuroba's name: "Kuroba, a 3-person web agency, used Alizé to automate [specific task]. Result: [specific metric]."
4. **The case study is used in Alizé outreach** — "We already run this for Kuroba; here's what it looks like"

**The "separate ventures" assumption destroys this option.** If Kuroba and Alizé are truly separate, deploying Alizé for Kuroba looks like using your own company as a fake reference — which is exactly the credibility problem Alizé is trying to avoid. But if the ventures are explicitly connected — "Alizé is the managed AI agent service built by the Kuroba founders, and Kuroba is our first client" — then the case study is genuine, the relationship is transparent, and the reference is legitimate.

**The operational constraint:** Kuroba is a 2-3 person web agency. The workflow automated for Kuroba must produce a case study that is relevant to Alizé's ICP (50-200 employee service firms). This means the workflow choice matters enormously. Automating Kuroba's invoicing produces a case study about 3-person firms, not 100-person firms. Automating Kuroba's client project status updates produces a case study about project management workflow — more relevant but still a scale gap. The workflow must be chosen for its case study value to Alizé's target market, not for its operational value to Kuroba.

**The verdict on Question 4:** There is a commercially viable world where Kuroba is the Alizé pilot — but only if:
- The framing is "first client" not "rehearsal"
- The workflow is chosen for ICP-relevant case study value
- The deployment is treated as a commercial engagement (with a simple agreement)
- The "separate ventures" fiction is abandoned and the connection is named explicitly

## What the "Separate Ventures" Assumption Gets Wrong

The assumption that "Kuroba and Alizé are separate ventures, operated in parallel" has produced three downstream errors in the research corpus:

**Error 1: Kuroba-first was treated as a resource-efficient parallel operation when it is actually a sequential resource consumer.**

The "parallel" framing made Kuroba-first seem costless — "we can prepare Kuroba's AI deployment while also doing Alizé outreach." The capacity analysis (Pulse 21) and the Kuroba Sequencing Auditor both show this is false. Every hour on Kuroba is an hour not on Alizé outreach. The parallelism is a scheduling fiction.

**Error 2: The Kuroba credential was treated as a standalone credibility asset when its value depends entirely on how the Alizé connection is framed.**

If Kuroba and Alizé are separate, "co-founder of Kuroba" is just a prior job — irrelevant to Alizé. If they are connected, "co-founder of Kuroba" is proof that the founders have already done this somewhere smaller. The separate-ventures framing makes Kuroba a liability for Alizé outreach (category contamination: "web agency person"). The connected-ventures framing makes it an asset (proof of execution).

**Error 3: Kuroba as internal deployment was framed as "delivery rehearsal" when it should have been framed as "first commercial client."**

The Pulse 63 Kuroba Evidence Transfer Challenge correctly identified that Kuroba as rehearsal produces no commercial evidence. But it stopped short of the reframing that makes Kuroba commercially useful: Kuroba as Alizé's first paying client. This reframe only works if the "separate ventures" assumption is replaced with a connected narrative.

## Concrete Recommendation on Entity Sequencing

### The Correct Sequencing: Kuroba IS the Alizé Pilot

The recommendation is not "Kuroba first → Alizé" or "Alizé first" or "simultaneous." The recommendation is: **Kuroba is the first deployment of the Alizé service. Alizé is the company that runs Kuroba's AI workflows. These are not two separate ventures operated in parallel. They are one venture at two scales.**

**Concrete actions:**

1. **Reframe Kuroba as Alizé's first reference client.** Not "we're using Kuroba to practice." Not "Kuroba is a separate venture." Kuroba is the company where Alizé's managed AI agent service is already running. Publish the case study with Kuroba's name attached.

2. **Choose one workflow for Kuroba that has ICP-relevant case study value.** The Pulse 63 Kuroba Evidence Transfer Challenge is correct that scale mismatch limits what Kuroba can teach Louis about delivery. But it incorrectly concluded Kuroba deployment was useless. It is not useless as a *marketing proof point* even if it is limited as a *delivery rehearsal*. Choose the workflow that produces the most compelling case study for Alizé's target ICP, not the workflow that is most operationally valuable to Kuroba.

3. **Louis leads with the connected story, not the credential alone.** First outreach message: "We built Kuroba's AI operations with Alizé. We're now offering the same service to companies with 50-200 employees." The Kuroba credential is the proof. The Alizé pitch is the escalation. The connection must be explicit in the same sentence.

4. **Kill "Kuroba-first as preparation" framing.** D99-D103 established Kuroba as an internal rehearsal target. The Kuroba Sequencing Auditor and the Kuroba Evidence Transfer Challenge both correctly identified this framing as flawed. Replace it with: "Kuroba is already running on Alizé. Here's the case study. Want the same for your operation?"

5. **Alizé outreach starts immediately alongside Kuroba case study preparation.** These are not sequential — the outreach can begin with "we're building this for Kuroba, our first client" framing. The Kuroba case study is the evidence that arrives during the outreach cycle, not the prerequisite that gates it.

### What the "Kuroba credential" is worth in a first outreach message about Alizé specifically

**Worth: High — but only if the connection is explicit and the case study exists.**

The Kuroba credential answers the question every prospect asks at first contact: "have you done this before?" Without Kuroba, Louis has no answer. With Kuroba, he has: "Yes — we built it for Kuroba, our first client. Here's what it does for them."

The Kuroba credential in first outreach:
- Answers "have you done this?" with a concrete yes
- Provides a named reference (Kuroba) that the prospect can conceptually evaluate
- Demonstrates that the founders have skin in the game (they've deployed it for their own company)
- Positions Alizé as the escalation of something already working, not a startup with a deck

**The value is conditional on three things:**
1. A real case study exists (not "we're working on it" — actual metrics, actual workflow)
2. The connection to Alizé is explicit in the same message
3. The case study workflow is relevant enough to Alizé's ICP that the prospect can see the parallel

If all three are true: the Kuroba credential is the most credible thing Louis can say in first outreach about Alizé. If any one is false: the credential either doesn't transfer ("that's a 3-person agency, not my 100-person firm") or actively hurts ("they're still testing it").

---

## Final Verdict

The assumption that "Kuroba and Alizé are separate ventures, operated in parallel" is not wrong in an organizational sense — they are separate legal entities. It is wrong in an operational and strategic sense: the ventures compete for the same founder's hours, the same credibility window, and the same market positioning. Treating them as parallel has allowed Kuroba to consume Alizé's launch momentum without anyone naming the trade-off.

**The correct relationship is not "parallel ventures." It is "Alizé is the managed AI agent service that runs Kuroba. Kuroba is Alizé's first client and proof of concept. The founders are building the service by running it on their own operation first."**

This is the same model that every successful managed service company uses: build your service using your own operation as the first customer, document the results, and sell the documented service to others. It is not mysterious. It is not a "rehearsal." It is a first commercial deployment with maximum founder involvement and zero external dependency.

The separate-ventures framing has been Alizé's most expensive unchallenged assumption. Kill it.
