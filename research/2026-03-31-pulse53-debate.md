## Debate 1: Commercial Model — Monthly Recurring Revenue for Year 1

**Assumption challenged:** Monthly recurring revenue (€2,000-2,500/month) is Alizé's primary commercial model.

**Verdict: MODIFY** — Recurring is the right destination, wrong Year 1 vehicle.

### Key Arguments

**Structural Reason 1: 2-person operation cannot honor SLA commitments.**
A monthly retainer with next-business-day SLA requires someone reachable every working day. Louis is simultaneously technical builder, salesman, and potentially employed elsewhere. Adding 4+ clients on recurring SLAs while solo-delivering creates an untenable liability concentration. The recurring model borrows SaaS legitimacy without SaaS delivery infrastructure.

**Structural Reason 2: First-time buyers don't buy on promise.**
French PME decision-makers at 50-200 employees have never purchased AI agent services. Asking for €2,000-2,500/month before any demonstrated delivery is asking for trust that doesn't exist. The "gratuit = suspect" problem (D211) applies in reverse: a €2,500/month demand before trust is equally cultural mismatch. D226 and D182 already point toward demo-first and prototype-first. The commercial model should match the sales motion.

**Structural Reason 3: Retainer creates liability that projects don't.**
Monthly billing creates a psychological anchor: "what did we get this month?" every 30 days. AI adoption in PME involves a 2-3 week ramp where agents are underutilized. If clients evaluate month 1 value during the hardest adoption period, they will not renew. The adoption curve problem is absorbed into fixed-scope projects, not recurring retainers.

### The Alternative

| Stage | Product | Price | Purpose |
|-------|---------|-------|---------|
| Entry | AI Workflow Setup | €3,000-5,000 | Deliver one working agent, 2-4 weeks |
| Evaluation | Proof Period | €1,200-1,500/month | Client evaluates in real operations, months 1-3 |
| Earned Retainer | Managed Service | €2,000-2,500/month | Ongoing optimization, offered at month 3 review |

**Conversion mechanism:** The project IS the trust-building mechanism. The proof-period is not a retainer SLA — it's a structured evaluation with explicit success criteria. The retainer is offered at the month 3 review, only with evidence in hand.

**What stays:** €2,000-2,500/month is still the target for Year 2+ clients. The tiered pricing structure (D43) applies to the managed service tier.

### Decisions Affected

- **Kill as stated:** Recurring revenue as Year 1 primary commercial model
- **Keep:** €2,000-2,500/month managed service as earned destination
- **Add:** Project-first entry commercial motion for Year 1

## Debate 2: GTM Strategy — Warm Network-First Assumption

**Assumption challenged:** Alizé's Year 1 GTM is primarily warm outreach via Louis's personal network, supplemented by cold outbound.

**Verdict: KILL warm network-first as primary channel. Cold outbound IS primary Year 1 channel.**

### Key Arguments

**Challenge 1: Network ceiling is already acknowledged, contradicting the "primary" framing.**
D59 already states: warm network is Month 1-3 only. This directly contradicts D26 which calls it the "primary" channel. If warm covers 2-3 pilots and cold must cover 4-5 to hit Year 1 targets, cold outbound IS the primary channel. The assumption contradicts itself.

**Challenge 2: Warm outreach by Louis is not a scalable channel.**
Louis's capacity is constrained by Grinto (unresolved across 50+ pulses). Louis's credibility ceiling prevents solo closes above certain price points (D84). Louis cannot solo-close AND solo-deliver simultaneously. Network depletion is real: 50 warm contacts at ICP fit = 2-3 months of outreach, not 12. A channel with all four constraints cannot be primary.

**Challenge 3: Personal relationships are contaminated by commercial pressure.**
Failed pilots with personal contacts create 2x damage: lost customer AND damaged relationship. Warm contacts expect preferential pricing and soft terms. Social pressure to accept non-ICP fits prevents proper qualification. "Honest framing" (D120) wastes both parties' time without qualifying the opportunity.

**Challenge 4: Resource allocation is misdirected.**
D90 says warm outreach is blocked until a closing-capable person exists. D84 says Louis can't solo-close. Yet Month 1 planning allocates time to warm outreach that Louis can't convert. Resources are being spent on a channel that cannot produce revenue in its current configuration.

### The Alternative

**Primary Year 1 Channel: Cold outbound, backed by Month 1 case study assets.**

| Month | Activity | Output |
|-------|----------|--------|
| Month 1 | Louis + commercial closer build outbound engine | ICP refinement, targeting list, objection handlers |
| Month 1 | Warm network produces 1-2 pilot case studies | Proof assets that make cold outbound credible |
| Month 2 | Cold outbound launch with case study assets | Qualified pipeline |
| Month 3+ | Cold outbound primary, warm referrals bonus | Repeatable scalable pipeline |

**The inversion:** Warm network is how you build the credibility that makes cold outbound work. It is not the channel itself.

### Decisions Affected

- **Kill:** D26 warm network-first as primary channel framing
- **Kill:** U26 as stated (warm vs. outbound supplementary question)
- **Add:** Cold outbound = primary Year 1 pipeline; warm network = Month 1 credibility generator
- **Add:** U86 (commercial co-founder) is the real GTM prerequisite, not network mapping

## Debate 3: Competitive Naming — "Never Name Competitors"

**Assumption challenged:** Alizé should never name competitors in any external materials.

**Verdict: MODIFY** — Contextual rule, not absolute principle.

### Key Arguments

**Finding 1: Silence leaves Alizé undefined.**
Without competitor naming, prospects default to evaluating Alizé against whatever AI tool they already know. They use the incumbent's criteria, incumbents don't have Alizé's brand recognition. The prospect's mental frame becomes: "why not just use Copilot?" — and if Alizé has never addressed Copilot, they lose by default.

**Finding 2: Silence on Copilot is an implicit endorsement.**
French SMBs have been sold Microsoft 365 + Copilot bundles aggressively since 2024. When Alizé never mentions Copilot, prospects read this as "Alizé probably can't compare." D140 already treats "we have Copilot" as a buying signal — the implication is that naming Copilot pre-qualifies prospects who have found Copilot insufficient.

**Finding 3: Naming a competitor does prospect qualification work for free.**
"Copilot answers questions. Alizé runs your workflows." — this phrase (D139) pre-selects prospects who have hit Copilot's ceiling. It qualifies before the email is opened. The alternative approach ("we automate repetitive admin tasks") lets Copilot users self-select as non-prospects without Alizé learning why.

**Finding 4: D96 ("implicitly exclude Copilot") requires prospect inference.**
Prospects don't read positioning materials alongside competitive analysis. They're skimming on their phones. "Implicit exclusion" means the prospect must infer what Alizé is from what it doesn't say. Most won't.

### The Contextual Rule

**Never name in:**
- Cold outreach (D25 correct — keep)
- Blog content / paid advertising

**Name in:**
- First demo call: use D140's discovery question proactively — "what does Copilot NOT do for you?"
- Sales proposals: when prospect raises competitor concern
- Sales playbook objection handlers: "why not Copilot?" (already D95)
- Landing page (test): "when Copilot isn't enough" as A/B variant

**The wrong way:** "We're better than Copilot because..." — feature war, elevates Microsoft.
**The right way (D139):** "Copilot answers questions. Alizé runs your workflows." — category contrast.

### Decisions Affected

- **Kill:** D96 as absolute rule
- **Modify:** "Never name competitors" becomes "never in cold outreach, always in demos"
- **Keep:** D25 (cold outreach), D94 (Agentova out of marketing), D95 (Microsoft in sales playbook), D139 ("when Copilot isn't enough")
