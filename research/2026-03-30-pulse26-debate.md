## Debate 1: Copilot Assumptions Under Fire (by Microsoft/Copilot Specialist)

### What Was Decided (Pulse 21)
- D95: Name Microsoft in sales playbook objection handling
- D96: Do NOT name Microsoft externally
- D97: Consider regulated industries where Copilot governance gaps are disqualifying
- D98: Evaluate Copilot Studio as platform/reseller
- U97-U102: Various unresolved questions about M365 penetration, Copilot adoption rates, willingness to pay

### Challenge 1: "Governance Gap Is Disqualifying" Is Wrong for 90% of ICP

Microsoft 365 Copilot in 2026 has real governance features: Purview data isolation, immutable audit logs, sensitive actions protection, French/European cloud data residency. These are not perfect but they're not absent.

More critically: EU AI Act's high-risk provisions apply to credit scoring, hiring, biometric surveillance, medical devices — NOT to internal knowledge Q&A or meeting note summarization. For the non-regulated 60-70% of target ICP, governance gaps are not disqualifying.

Narrowing to regulated industries per D97 reduces TAM by 70-80%.

### Challenge 2: "Don't Name Microsoft Externally" (D96) May Cede the Most Important Ground

Naming Microsoft is free advertising. "The Copilot for companies that need results, not licenses" directly addresses the value-vs-tool distinction. Category creation requires CMO-level budget Alizé doesn't have. Strategic anchoring ("we're in the same category as Microsoft, here's why we're better for your situation") is the better move.

### Challenge 3: Copilot Studio + French ESN Is a More Present Threat Than D98 Frames

French ESNs (Capgemini, Sopra Steria, Atos, Accenture France) all have Microsoft Practice groups building Copilot Studio agents. They have existing client relationships. An ESN co-selling Copilot Studio to an existing client doesn't need to win a new relationship — they just expand the contract.

D98's "platform/reseller" reframe misunderstands the competitive dynamic. Alizé's MCP-first architecture is explicitly an alternative to Microsoft's proprietary agent framework.

### Challenge 4: U102 (Willingness to Pay) Is the Most Dangerous Unknown

For a 100-person company:
- Copilot: €3,000/month (100 users × €30) — already in the Microsoft ecosystem
- Alizé managed service: €800-1,500/month per agent + €3,000-6,000 setup

The comparison isn't Alizé vs Copilot — it's Alizé + Copilot. Most buyers will have Copilot anyway. Alizé needs to position as additive, not competitive.

### Recommendations from This Debate

| ID | Recommendation |
|----|----------------|
| R1 | Ditch "regulated industries wedge" as primary strategy — lead with workflow automation |
| R2 | Reverse D96 — name Microsoft in external materials carefully ("results not licenses") |
| R3 | Build "Copilot comparison" one-pager for sales playbook (not marketing) |
| R4 | Position Alizé as additive to Copilot, not competitive — price comparison needs reframing |

## Debate 2: The First-Client Acquisition Paradox (by GTM Strategist)

### The Paradox
Alizé plans to acquire first clients through Louis's personal network. But:
1. Louis's network ICP-fit is unknown (U52 unresolved)
2. Louis can't solo-close (D90 gates outreach)
3. Louis has Grinto + M1 + web agency — hours mathematically insufficient
4. "Honest framing" ("I'm working on something new") signals unproven startup asking for a favor

### Challenge 1: Network Math Doesn't Support 3-5 Pilots

Working backward from realistic rates:
- 15 warm contacts × 40% reply × 55% qualification × 40% close = ~1.3 pilots

Even at 10-15 warm accounts, the numbers barely support 2 pilots, not 3-5. The network math is aspirational, not a plan.

### Challenge 2: "Honest Framing" Fails at This Price Point

"I'm working on something new" signals:
- "I'm pre-revenue and unproven"
- "I'm asking you to take a risk on something undefined"
- "This is a personal project, not a company"

At €1,500-7,500 engagements, buyers need credibility signals. Problem-led framing works better:

> "I've been mapping how [specific company type] handles [specific repetitive admin task]. Most spend 8-12 hours/week on it. I think there's a structured way to cut that in half — want to see if it applies?"

### Challenge 3: Warm Network Conversions — The Honest Math

| Stage | Rate | Notes |
|-------|------|-------|
| Reply rate (warm) | 35-50% | Warm is ambiguous — 10-15 are the absolute warmest |
| Qualification rate | 40-55% | Must match ICP |
| Discovery → Proposal | 50-60% | Depends on who runs discovery |
| Proposal → Signed | 40-60% | Depends on closer credibility |

Overall: ~0.9-2 pilots from 10-15 warm contacts. To get 3-5 pilots, Louis needs 30-50 ICP-fit warm contacts.

### Challenge 4: D90 and D118 Are in Direct Conflict

D90 gates outreach until a closing-capable person exists. D118 starts warm outreach in Month 1. If Louis can't close (D74/D84) and no commercial co-founder exists yet (U86), outreach is gated — yet D118 says outreach starts. These decisions contradict each other.

### Most Dangerous Assumption: "Louis's Warm Network Is the Primary GTM Channel"

Why this kills Alizé:
1. Depends on unknowns that may not resolve (U52, U123 unresolved)
2. Creates false sense of progress (15 contacts = a plan, not a hope)
3. Consumes Louis's limited hours on a channel that may not produce
4. Contradicts D90's own gating logic
5. Single point of failure with no fallback

### Concrete Alternative: Two-Track Month 1 GTM

**Track A: Warm Network (Parallel, Gated)**
- Resolve U52 (map all 50+ contacts by ICP-fit, size, decision-maker access)
- Resolve U123 (expert-comptable adjacent contacts)
- Build outreach materials (pitch deck, scope template, pilot one-pager)
- Gate: outreach starts only when D90 satisfied AND U52 resolved AND materials complete

**Track B: Category Credibility (New Primary)**
- Publish one concrete workflow article per week from Day 1
- List on Malt.fr with specific workflow offering (Week 2)
- Draft association outreach letter for expert-comptable body (Week 3)

## Debate 3: "AI Operations Company" — The Second Identity Trap (by Identity Specialist)

### What Pulse 18 Decided
- D79: Replace "productized consulting" with "AI operations company, temporarily in consulting mode"
- D80: Alizé = company that happens to do consulting in Years 1-2; platform builder in Years 2-3

### Challenge 1: "AI Operations" Implies Infrastructure That Doesn't Exist

| Audience | Hears "AI Operations Company" | Reality |
|----------|-------------------------------|---------|
| Investors | Infrastructure, SLAs, scalable tech | Louis deploying Mastra on a VPS |
| Clients | 24/7 system, professional support | Next-business-day email SLA |
| Partners | Enterprise-grade systems | First deployment is still in pilot |

The gap between label and reality shapes Louis's decisions toward infrastructure investment when he should be focused on winning clients and documenting workflows.

### Challenge 2: "Platform Builder in Years 2-3" Is Planning Fiction

The platform decision has been killed twice before (D21, D27). D80 re-animates it without acknowledging the reversal. What would actual platform decision at Month 12 require?
- Evidence of reusable workflow patterns across 5-10 clients
- At least 1 engineer dedicated to platform (requires funding or margin)
- A product vision clients would pay for as software

None of these exist. D80 has no triggering criteria — it's framed as inevitable when it should be conditional.

### Challenge 3: Expert-Comptables Point Away From "AI Operations"

Expert-comptables (D126's chosen vertical) want a trusted partner, not an operations infrastructure company. They want to know:
- Who is this person? Do they understand our world?
- Can they show us examples with other cabinets like ours?
- What happens if something goes wrong?

"AI operations company" answers none of these. "French AI service firm for regulated industries" answers all of them.

### Challenge 4: Internal Behavioral Drift

If Louis thinks of Alizé as an "AI operations company," he will:
- Build monitoring dashboards before having clients who need monitoring
- Make hiring decisions based on "operations capability" rather than delivery capability
- Frame client conversations in terms of SLAs and infrastructure

The internal identity shapes operational decisions. "AI operations" shapes them toward infrastructure investment. "Service firm for regulated industries" shapes them toward client acquisition and delivery excellence.

### The Concrete Alternative

> **"French AI service firm for regulated industries — we deploy, manage, and optimize AI agents for businesses that need compliance-native automation."**

| Audience | What They Hear |
|----------|----------------|
| Expert-comptable buyer | Service firm, French, understands regulation |
| Potential employee | Service delivery, not pure engineering |
| Partner (accounting firm) | White-labelable, attractive as partner |
| Investor | Services firm with recurring revenue, can translate to ARR story |
| Louis himself | Client services business, not infrastructure company |

**What Louis should delete:**
- "Temporarily in consulting mode" — implies consulting is inferior
- "Platform builder in Years 2-3" — speculative, no triggering criteria
- "AI operations company" — implies infrastructure that doesn't exist

**What Louis should add:**
- "Regulated industries" — explicit, matches vertical decision
- "Compliance-native" — actual differentiator vs. generic AI tools
- "Managed service" — honest about delivery model
