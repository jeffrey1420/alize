# Alizé Research — Pulse 16: Governance Differentiator Debate
**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Topic:** Is governance/accountability actually Alizé's differentiator?
**Agent:** Governance Strategist

---

## What Was Debated

Challenged the assumption that "delegation with accountability" and governance/security features constitute Alizé's key competitive differentiator. The existing research (D53, D56, D41) positions these as primary differentiation points.

---

## Agent Analysis

### The "Accountability" Claim Is Industry Boilerplate

Every consulting firm, every ESN, every freelance AI consultant in France claims to take accountability. "We're accountable for results" is the default promise of anyone charging €350/day. When a DG hears this from Alizé, they hear exactly what they hear from the five other vendors they talked to last month. There is no differentiated signal.

The phrase "delegation with accountability" is worse than silence — it's jargon that sounds substantive but communicates nothing specific.

### French PME Buyers at 20-50 Employees Don't Buy Governance

The decision maker at a 30-person company is typically the founder/CEO/DG wearing multiple hats. They don't have a procurement department, a CISO, or an IT team asking about EU AI Act compliance. They care about three things: does this solve my problem, can I trust the person, and is the price reasonable.

Governance frameworks are invisible to this buyer — until something goes wrong. Leading with compliance posture is like leading with "we have liability insurance." Technically reassuring, but not a reason to buy.

### The EU AI Act Is a 2027-2028 Story Being Used for 2026 Positioning

Pulse 4 already established: EU AI Act compliance is not a near-term driver (D42). But if it's not relevant until 2027-2028, it shouldn't be in external positioning at all — not even as a secondary point. Leading with something that doesn't matter to the buyer today is worse than saying nothing about it. It signals that Alizé is out of touch with what buyers actually care about.

### Where Governance Actually Matters: Regulated Verticals

Audit trails, access controls, and compliance documentation are genuinely valuable for:
- Expert-comptables (accountants) — regulatory obligations under anti-money laundering rules
- Legal practices — client confidentiality, bar association rules
- Healthcare admin — patient data, RGPD compliance
- Financial services — BaFin equivalent French regulations

In these contexts, governance is not a differentiator — it's a gate. You can't sell to expert-comptables without it. But for a general "PME 20-200 employees" without specific regulatory obligations, governance is a feature, not a reason to buy.

---

## Key Findings / Challenges

1. **"Accountability" is not a differentiator** — every vendor claims it
2. **French PME 20-50 employee buyers don't prioritize governance** — they buy on trust and price
3. **EU AI Act is 2027-2028 relevant, not 2026** — remove from all near-term positioning
4. **Governance is a gate for regulated verticals, not a differentiator for general PME**
5. **Replace governance claims with specific, concrete outcome statements**

---

## What This Means for Alizé

**Remove "governance" and "accountability" language from hero and headline positioning.**

External materials should lead with:
- What the agent actually does (concrete workflow description)
- How fast it delivers (timeline)
- What the client doesn't have to do anymore
- One specific proof point if available

Governance features (audit logs, access controls, compliance documentation) move to:
- "How we're built" technical section
- Sales conversations triggered by buyer questions about security
- Contracts and SLA documents

If Alizé wants governance to be a differentiator, the path is: pick one regulated vertical (expert-comptables or legal) where governance is a hard requirement, and build from there. But that requires a vertical pivot — not just positioning language.
