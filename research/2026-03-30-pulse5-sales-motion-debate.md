# Pulse 5: First 90 Days Sales Motion — Debate

**Role:** Lead Sales Strategist  
**Date:** 2026-03-30  
**Context:** Alizé is a new, unknown brand targeting French PME/ETI (50-200 employees). Offer: €4,500 pilot setup + €1,200-3,000/month managed service. Team: 2 devs + 1 designer + 1 founder (Louis). Personal network is the only near-term GTM asset available.

---

## Executive Summary

| Assumption | Verdict | Risk |
|-----------|---------|------|
| Personal network → 2-3 pilots in 90 days | **Plausible but fragile** | Network exhaustion, long sales cycles, no slack for slippage |
| Expert-comptable partner channel | **Not viable Year 1** | Missing prerequisites, wrong segment alignment, premature timing |
| Content/SEO inbound funnel | **Realistic only after Month 9** | 12-18 month ramp, no tactical alternatives for 90-day window |

**Bottom line:** The 90-day plan is built on optimistic assumptions that each work in isolation but collapse under combined pressure. Louis needs a parallel track that doesn't depend on personal network exhaustion or SEO miracles.

---

## Challenge 1: Personal Network → 2-3 Pilots in 90 Days

### The Conversion Math (Unpacked)

The assumption states: Louis's personal network will yield 2-3 pilots in 90 days.

Let's run the actual funnel:

```
Network size (realistic):          ~40-60 warm contacts in ICP
  ↓ Respond to outreach (30%):     ~15-18 meetings booked
  ↓ Advance to proposal (25%):     ~4-5 qualified opportunities
  ↓ Close rate (40-50%):           ~2 pilots
```

This math is **tight**. Here's the problem:

**French B2B sales cycle for new vendor + unknown brand:**
- Best case (warm intro, small company): 5-7 weeks
- Typical case (warm/cold mix, new vendor): 9-13 weeks
- 90 days = ~13 weeks

You have **marginally enough time** for one full cycle. If cycle is 9+ weeks, you're looking at Month 3-close for first deals, with no slack for delays.

### The Realistic Scenario

| Week | Activity | Outcome |
|------|----------|---------|
| 1-2 | Warm outreach to 40-50 contacts | 12-15 responses, 8-10 meetings |
| 3-4 | Discovery calls (8-10) | 3-4 advance to scoping |
| 5-7 | Scoping + proposal (3-4) | 2 proposals, 1 LOI |
| 8-10 | Negotiation + contract | Pilot #1 signed |
| 9-11 | Pilot #2 follows | Pilot #2 signed |
| 12-13 | Deployment begins on #1 | Month 1-2 deployment |

**Realistic 90-day output: 1-2 pilots signed, 1-2 in late-stage pipeline**

### Failure Modes

**1. Network exhaustion (HIGH PROBABILITY)**
- Louis has one network. You burn it on failed attempts, you don't get a second chance.
- Every cold-to-warm email that goes unanswered or rejected is a wasted touch.
- With €4,500+ price point, network contacts will defer ("let's revisit after you have case studies") or will want discounts.
- **Failure mode:** First 10 outreach attempts all defer to "show me results first" → no pilots in 90 days.

**2. Sales cycle compression is a fantasy (MEDIUM-HIGH PROBABILITY)**
- The brief assumes French B2B cycles can be shortened via warm relationship.
- Reality: Warm relationships reduce prospecting friction but don't compress evaluation time.
- A 100-person company still needs to run internal evaluations, get finance sign-off, and get IT/security review—even for €4,500.
- **Failure mode:** Meetings happen, enthusiasm occurs, then prospect goes dark for 3-4 weeks for internal approvals → cycle extends to 14+ weeks.

**3. Wrong network segment (MEDIUM PROBABILITY)**
- Louis's network may skew toward:
  - Micro-SMBs (< 20 employees) — too small for Alizé economics
  - Large enterprises — too complex, wrong decision-maker
  - Peers/agency types — not the COOs/Operations leads who buy
- **Failure mode:** 8 of 10 meetings are with 15-person consultancies → not ICP.

**4. Free work for network deals (HIGH PROBABILITY)**
- "Friends and family" pricing destroys pricing credibility and learning.
- If Louis discounts from €4,500 to €1,500 for network pilots, he learns nothing about real price sensitivity.
- **Failure mode:** Two network pilots at €1,500 → Louis prices the real product at €3,500 based on bad data.

**5. Time trap (HIGH PROBABILITY)**
- Louis is 25% of the company. If he's spending 15-20 hours/week on sales outreach + discovery + proposals + follow-up, he's not coding.
- Every "just one more coffee meeting" is a developer-day lost.
- **Failure mode:** Pipeline is full of warm conversations, no closed pilots, but Louis hasn't shipped in 6 weeks.

### What Would Actually Work

1. **Hard ceiling on network outreach:** 30 warm contacts max. After 30, shift to systematic outbound.
2. **Segment the list first:** Filter for 50-200 employee companies in professional services, healthcare admin, logistics. Don't meet with micro-SMBs or solo founders.
3. **Price discipline:** Offer a 10% network discount (€4,050) if they proceed within 2 weeks. Not €1,500 — that's not a pilot, that's charity.
4. **Parallel track from Day 1:** Don't wait for network to exhaust before starting outbound. Both tracks simultaneously.

## Challenge 2: Expert-Comptable Partner Channel

### The Recommendation

The existing research suggests expert-comptables (~20,000 firms) as a partner channel, citing their access to ~130,000 client companies.

### Why This Is Premature for Year 1

**The partner channel math requires pre-conditions that don't exist:**

| Prerequisite | Status | Gap |
|-------------|--------|-----|
| Track record with 3+ clients in their segment | ❌ None | Can't pitch without case studies |
| Understanding of their tech stack (Cegid, Sage, Pennylane) | ❌ None | No integrations built |
| Referrals to them (reciprocity) | ❌ None | Why would they refer to you? |
| Known brand in accounting community | ❌ None | Zero awareness |
| Clear value proposition per firm size | ❌ None | "AI agents" means nothing to them |
| Established commission/referral structure | ❌ None | French market doesn't use % referral |

**The expert-comptable reality check:**

Expert-comptables in France are:
- Conservative by nature — they advise on risk, not adopt it
- Already stretched thin — 60-70% work >50 hours/week in tax season (Jan-Apr, Sep-Oct)
- Invested in their own tool ecosystem — Cegid, Pennylane, Sage, isow
- Skeptical of new vendors — 3-5 cold calls/week from software vendors
- Regulated — they have professional liability; recommending an unknown AI vendor is a risk

**The segment alignment problem:**

Expert-comptables serve micro-enterprises and small PME (mostly < 20 employees). Alizé's ICP is 50-200 employees.

- A typical expert-comptable has 30-80 clients
- Maybe 5-10 of those are in Alizé's ICP
- That's a 7-15% addressable overlap
- For a channel to be worth the investment, you'd need firms with 50+ potential Alizé clients in their book

**What "partner channel" actually requires in Year 1:**

1. **Months 1-3:** Build 2-3 case studies with accounting firms or their clients. Learn their workflows.
2. **Months 4-6:** Develop a pitch for expert-comptables: what problem does Alizé solve for their clients? What's the referral economics?
3. **Months 7-12:** Active outreach to 50-100 expert-comptables. Convert 5-10 as referral partners.
4. **Year 2:** Meaningful referral volume.

**Timeline: 12-18 months before this channel produces a single paid client.**

### What Can Go Wrong

**1. Wrong expectations set internally (HIGH)**
- If Louis reads "expert-comptable channel" in the GTM plan and allocates time to it in Months 1-3, he's building a channel that won't produce revenue for a year.
- **Failure mode:** Month 6 review shows 0 closed clients from this channel → panic, plan revision, lost momentum.

**2. The "we'll partner with them" conversation goes nowhere (HIGH)**
- Expert-comptables get 3-5 vendor outreach attempts per week.
- Without a compelling reason to switch from their existing tool partnerships, they won't engage.
- **Failure mode:** 20 cold emails to expert-comptables → 2 responses, both "not interested."

**3. They refer you to the wrong prospects (MEDIUM)**
- Expert-comptables may refer micro-SMBs (their main book) rather than the 50-200 employee companies that fit Alizé.
- **Failure mode:** Louis spends Month 4-6 chasing 10 referrals, 8 are too small for Alizé economics.

**4. Compliance liability fear (MEDIUM)**
- If an expert-comptable refers Alizé to a client and the client has an AI-related compliance issue, the expert-comptable fears exposure.
- **Failure mode:** Even interested firms back away when you ask them to formally refer.

**5. Misalignment on what "partnership" means (MEDIUM)**
- Expert-comptables expect: co-branded materials, joint marketing, potentially a revenue share
- Alizé in Year 1 can offer: referral fee (5-10% first year), case study co-authorship
- **Failure mode:** Expectation mismatch causes negotiation breakdown.

### Verdict on Expert-Comptables

**Don't invest meaningful time in this channel until Month 9+.** The prerequisites (case studies, integrations, brand recognition) don't exist. Any effort before then is wasted.

**Exception:** If Louis has a specific personal relationship with an expert-comptable (friend, family, close business associate), that's a warm intro worth pursuing as a single relationship, not a channel strategy.

**What to do instead for Year 1:**
- Target accounting/bookkeeping firms that are themselves 50-200 person companies (outsourced accounting, DAF-as-a-service firms)
- These are decision-makers for their own AI adoption, not referral intermediaries
- They face the same operational repetitive tasks as Alizé's ICP

## Challenge 3: Inbound Funnel via Content/SEO

### The Assumption

The brief suggests that after 3-6 months of content production and LinkedIn activity, Alizé will generate inbound leads that reduce cold outreach dependency.

### Why This Fails in Year 1

**SEO timeline reality:**

|seo Stage | Timeline | Realistic Outcome |
|----------|---------|-------------------|
| Domain age + initial content | Month 1-3 | Google still learning, zero ranking |
| First keywords indexed | Month 3-4 | Page 3-5 for long-tail terms |
| Modest rankings (page 2-3) | Month 6-9 | 10-30 visits/month, 1-2 leads |
| Meaningful traffic | Month 9-12 | 50-100 visits/month, 3-5 leads |
| Consistent inbound | Month 12-18 | 100+ visits/month, 5-8 leads |

**The French B2B SEO problem:**

- "Agent IA PME" and "automatisation IA entreprise France" are already competitive terms
- French digital agencies with 50+ writers are producing AI content daily
- A new site with 2-3 articles/month will be outpaced by established players
- True organic SEO differentiation takes 18-24 months for competitive French B2B terms

**LinkedIn organic is a myth for unknown brands:**

- LinkedIn's algorithm prioritizes engagement, not fresh content from new accounts
- Louis's personal network of 500 connections sees 10-20% of posts
- Without 1,000+ engaged followers, organic LinkedIn reach is 50-150 impressions per post
- To generate 5 inbound leads from LinkedIn organic, Louis would need 50+ posts (1 per business day) AND virality

**The math doesn't work:**

- Required inbound leads (Month 4-6 target): 2-3/month
- Conservative conversion rate: 3% contact → qualified lead
- Required inbound contacts: 66-100/month
- At 2% click-through from Google: need 3,300-5,000 monthly visitors
- At 0.5% click-through from LinkedIn: need 13,000-20,000 impressions

**Louis's content team: 0 people and 0 hours available.**

### What Actually Happens

**Month 1-3:** Louis publishes 8-12 LinkedIn posts. Gets 20-50 views each. 2-3 comments. No inbound inquiries. Motivation drops.

**Month 4-6:** Louis writes 4-5 long-form articles. Publishes to website. Google doesn't rank them. Gets 3-4 direct visitors from direct links. No leads. Content effort redirected to "we'll do this later."

**Month 7-9:** Website has 8 articles. No meaningful SEO footprint. Louis stops publishing. Entire inbound strategy abandoned.

### What Replaces Content/SEO as the Year 1 Pipeline Engine

**1. Systematic outbound (primary, Month 1+)**
- 50 targeted cold emails/week via Lemly/Apollo
- ICP filter: 50-200 employee companies in Île-de-France, professional services, healthcare admin, logistics
- Track in CRM (Notion is fine early stage)
- 1-2% response rate → 0.5-1 reply per day
- 10-15% of replies → qualified meetings
- **Realistic Month 2-3 output: 3-5 qualified conversations from outbound**

**2. Warm outbound to companies actively searching (Month 3+)**
- When someone searches "agent IA PME" or "automatisation IA France" → they see Google Ads for Alizé
- Cost: €200-500/month for targeted French B2B keywords
- Volume: 50-100 clicks/month at €3-8/click
- Conversion: 3-5% to inquiry at €490 diagnostic
- **Realistic Month 4-6 output: 2-3 inbound leads from paid search**

**3. Events and communities (Month 2+)**
- French B2B events: VivaTech (June), OBLOCC (Lyon), smaller vertical meetups
- Not as sponsor (too expensive), but as attendee + active participant
- French Slack communities: FrenchWeb, Maddonly, sector-specific groups
- **Realistic: 1-2 warm connections per event, 10-15 qualified conversations per quarter**

**4. Podcast/editorial coverage (Month 4+)**
- French AI/business podcasts looking for guests
- Maduca, Tech&Frenc, FrenchWeb
- Louis shares real AI agent deployment stories (anonymized)
- Generates warm inbound from listeners
- **Realistic: 5-10 warm inbound after 3-4 podcast appearances**

**The 90-day pipeline formula:**

```
Month 1:  Network (8-10 meetings) + Outbound (start 50 emails/week)
Month 2:  Network (3-4 closed) + Outbound (20-30 conversations) + Events (1-2)
Month 3:  Outbound dominant (pipeline) + Paid search (Month 3.5+) + Podcasts (if scheduled)
```

**Content/SEO is a Month 9+ initiative.** Don't pretend it's a Month 3-4 pipeline driver.

## Verdict: Recommended Actions for Louis

### Immediate (Week 1-2)

**1. Stop calling this a "personal network GTM." Rename it: "Warm outreach to qualified ICP contacts."**
   - The framing shift matters. Network implies relationship; ICP outreach implies sales discipline.
   - Apply strict ICP filters before reaching out: 50-200 employees, professional services/healthcare admin/logistics, Île-de-France.

**2. Build the prospect list before writing any emails.**
   - Target: 50 companies that fit ICP, with identified decision-maker (LinkedIn profile, email)
   - Sources: LinkedIn Sales Navigator (30-day trial), Inorio, French business databases
   - Don't start outreach until you have 50 qualified names + emails.

**3. Set hard timeline: 30 warm contacts, then pivot.**
   - If 30 warm contacts don't produce 3+ qualified opportunities by Week 6, shift to cold outbound.
   - Don't spend Month 3 still warming the same network.

### Week 3-6

**4. Launch systematic cold outbound at 25 emails/day (50/week).**
   - Use Lemly or Apollo for email finding + sequencing
   - Track every touch in a spreadsheet (Phase, Status, Next Action, Last Contact)
   - Goal: 2-3 replies/week, 1 qualified meeting/week from cold outbound

**5. Define the "free diagnostic" conversion path.**
   - Given zero brand recognition, €490 is a barrier. Replace with: free 30-minute discovery call.
   - Use call to qualify, then propose the €490 diagnostic as the paid next step.
   - This gives Louis a lower-friction first conversion.

**6. Do NOT approach expert-comptables as a channel.**
   - Any meeting with an expert-comptable is a learning meeting, not a sales meeting.
   - Goal: understand their clients, workflows, pain points. Not referrals.

### Month 2-3

**7. Set a "closed pilot or closed reason" rule.**
   - By Week 12 (end of Month 3), Louis should have either: (a) 2+ signed pilots, or (b) a clear list of 5 reasons prospects are saying no.
   - If reasons are price → revisit pricing. If reasons are trust → invest in social proof. If reasons are timing → improve urgency framing.

**8. Allocate €300-500/month to Google Ads (start Month 3).**
   - Target: "automatisation tâches administratives," "agent IA entreprise," "AI agent France"
   - Budget: €300-500/month for 50-80 clicks. Use to test landing page + diagnostic offer.
   - Don't expect meaningful lead volume — this is testing the machine, not generating pipeline.

**9. Book 1 podcast appearance per month starting Month 2.**
   - Find 3 French AI podcasts. Pitch Louis as a "practitioner who deploys AI agents for French businesses."
   - Share real stories (anonymized). Build authority without publishing 50 LinkedIn posts.

### The 90-Day Test

By Day 90, Louis should be able to answer:

| Metric | Target | Acceptable | concerning |
|--------|--------|------------|------------|
| Qualified meetings held | 15+ | 10-14 | <10 |
| Proposals sent | 5+ | 3-4 | <3 |
| Pilots signed | 2+ | 1 | 0 |
| Pipeline value (Month 4-6) | €15k+ | €8-14k | <€8k |
| Outbound reply rate | 3%+ | 2-3% | <2% |

**If numbers are "Acceptable" or better:** Continue current motion, add content/SEO in Month 4.
**If numbers are "Concerning":** Re-evaluate ICP fit, pricing, or offer structure. Don't add more channels — fix the core motion first.

---

## Summary

The 90-day sales motion has structural weaknesses:

1. **Personal network math is tight** — 2-3 pilots is possible but has zero slack. Network is a launchpad, not a pipeline.
2. **Expert-comptable channel is Year 2**, not Year 1. Don't allocate time to it until prerequisites exist.
3. **Content/SEO is Month 9+**, not Month 4-6. It's a compounding asset for Year 2, not a Year 1 pipeline driver.

**The actual 90-day engine:**
- Weeks 1-2: Build prospect list, apply ICP filters, write outreach sequences
- Weeks 3-6: Warm outreach (30 contacts) + cold outbound (50/week)
- Month 2-3: Systematic outbound + first paid search experiments + podcast outreach
- Month 3 end: Evaluate closed pilots and pipeline health. Decide on scaling or pivoting.

Louis is the entire sales function. He needs to be systematic, ICP-disciplined, and ruthless about what NOT to do (expert-comptable meetings, SEO content, free network pilots).
