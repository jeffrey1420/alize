## Arguments For

### 3. Cognitive comfort

## Arguments Against

### 1. Positioning words are meaningless without product-market evidence

Positioning is a hypothesis about how a market will receive a product. You test positioning by showing it to real prospects and observing their reaction. You do not test positioning by debating it internally.

The Alizé team has run 54+ internal positioning debates. Zero of these debates produced evidence. They produced words. Words are not evidence.

The underlying product is undefined:
- **Who** is the first customer? Louis's warm network has never been mapped (U52 unresolved after 54 pulses)
- **What** specifically does Alizé do? The hero names "invoice processing, CRM updates, FAQ responses" — but there's no evidence these are the actual pain points of any real prospect
- **Workflow** — what does the product actually look like? What's the user experience?
- **Price** — what is anyone paying, or willing to pay?

You cannot position a product that doesn't exist in a tested form. You're positioning an idea. Ideas don't have markets. Products do.

### 2. D226's specific claims are invented, not validated

"We take over your invoice processing, CRM updates, and client FAQ responses — the 15 hours a week of repetitive data work that your senior staff are doing manually"

Let's count the assumptions embedded in this sentence:

- That the target customer does invoice processing (which? AP? AR? Both?)
- That they do it manually (vs. using existing software)
- That it takes 15 hours/week (where does this number come from?)
- That senior staff are doing it (vs. junior staff, or an external accountant)
- That the target customer uses a CRM (what CRM? how actively?)
- That they have client FAQ responses to manage (vs. clients who don't ask questions)
- That "15 hours a week of repetitive data work" is the primary pain

Every element of this hero sentence is invented. Not validated. Invented. P51-D3 froze positioning at D226 "until first paying client" — but the pulses kept coming anyway, as if D226 itself was being treated as unsettled when it should have been treated as a testable hypothesis.

### 3. 54 pulses of positioning debates: the actual track record

After 54+ research pulses, Alizé has:
- 15+ different positioning statements
- 7+ verticals proposed and killed
- 1 frozen hero (D226) that was supposed to hold until paying client evidence
- Zero paying clients
- Zero reference cases
- Zero measurable use cases
- Louis's warm network unmapped after 54 pulses

The probability that one more internal positioning debate produces a *useful output* — defined as something that attracts a real customer, not just sounds better in a Slack thread — is effectively zero. This is not a criticism. It is a statistical observation. When an activity is repeated under the same conditions with the same inputs and produces no output, continuing the activity under the same conditions will not produce different results.

The debate format itself is the problem. Internal debate cannot test external market response. It can only produce more words.

### 4. Positioning before product is backwards

The standard early-stage sequence is:
1. Build something specific enough that a specific person will pay for it
2. Find that person
3. Learn what they actually needed
4. Adjust the product, not the words
5. The words then describe what actually happened

Alizé has been doing step 5 first, in a vacuum, for 54 pulses. This is not a sequence problem. It is a sequencing inversion that has consumed real time and produced no learning.

## Recommendation

**Stop all positioning work until a paying client exists.**

What should replace positioning work:

### 1. Map Louis's warm network (U52)
This is the only immediate source of real signal. Who does Louis know? What do they actually do all day? What would they pay to stop doing? This is not positioning work. This is evidence gathering. Do this first.

### 2. Pick one specific workflow and build it for one specific person
Not "invoice processing" in general. One specific invoice workflow for one specific type of company. Build it. Show it to someone. Watch their face. Repeat.

### 3. Charge money
Not a pilot. Not a beta. Charge money. Even €200/month for a scoped thing. Price is the only positioning signal that matters. Everything else is speculation.

### 4. Only after a paying client exists: write down what you did

Positioning at early stage is not a noun — it's a verb. It's something you do *after* the market tells you what you are, by paying you. The words come from evidence, not the other way around.

**In summary: D226 is not a positioning problem. It's an evidence problem. And evidence comes from customers, not debates.**
