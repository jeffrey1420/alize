## Agent 1: Activation Bottleneck Auditor

**Challenged:** P68-D4 — "One Mastra agent, one task, 30 minutes, one screen recording = minimum viable proof asset"

### Key Findings

**P68-D4 will not break the paralysis.** It will produce a screen recording that joins the archive of built-but-unused artifacts. The 30-minute proof action is structurally identical to every prior commitment Louis has made. The content changes; the pattern doesn't.

### The 30-Minute Claim Is Not Supported by Evidence

Louis has a documented history of commitments that expire at the moment execution is required:

- **P67-D9**: Outreach message written and approved. Never sent. (68 pulses ago)
- **U163**: Domain purchase — "5 minutes, €10." Never executed.
- **U246**: Cron job disable — "1 minute, one command." Not done across 4 separate kill decisions.
- **Kuroba**: "Imminent" for 18+ pulses. Landing page still not finished.
- **Emails 01-04**: Written, reviewed, approved. Never sent.

The claim that a 30-minute task is meaningfully different from these commitments has no supporting evidence. The prior commitments were not longer or more complex. They failed at the same point: the boundary between "document produced" and "external action taken."

### The Causal Chain Is Broken

P68-D4 assumes:
```
Build proof asset → Proof demonstrates capability → Message gets sent
```

Louis's actual causal chain:
```
Build proof asset → File saved → Task marked complete → Another prerequisite identified → Archived
```

The screen recording is never the blocker. The blocker is the external action that follows. Louis has built things and not sent things for 91+ days. P68-D4 adds another step to a chain that already terminates before the message is sent.

### The Undeployed Artifact Graveyard

| Artifact | Produced | Used |
|----------|----------|------|
| Email sequence (01-04) | Multiple pulses ago | Never sent |
| P67-D9 outreach message | 68 pulses ago | Never sent |
| Kuroba landing page | "In progress" 18+ pulses | Not deployed |
| Domain alize.studio | Never purchased | N/A |
| Legal entity questions (U238) | Identified | Not answered |

### The Real Bottleneck

**Accountability avoidance in external-facing actions.**

Louis consistently performs tasks that have no external witness: writing documents, reviewing decisions, debating tradeoffs. Louis consistently fails at tasks with external witnesses: sending emails, buying domains, deploying to real users.

The pulse process is structured as an accountability-avoidance machine. Every pulse produces a document. Documents can be reviewed, debated, and improved without anyone outside the session knowing.

### One Specific Structural Intervention

Replace the proof asset with a sent message. The task should be: "Send P67-D9 to one warm contact. Report the response." This is structurally different because:
1. Requires an external action with a real recipient
2. The response provides real market data
3. Bypasses the 18+ pulse Kuroba deferral
4. Completes the causal chain P68-D4 leaves broken

If Louis cannot send one email after 68 pulses, the question is not "how do we build a better proof asset?" It is: what does Louis believe will happen when he sends the message, and why is he avoiding finding out?

## Agent 2: Day-of-Week Outreach Timing Auditor

**Challenged:** "Send P67-D9 today" — P68-D5

### Key Findings

**March 31 (Tuesday) is a favorable day — but the timing of this instruction is catastrophically wrong.**

### Day-of-Week Analysis

March 31, 2026 is a Tuesday. Tuesday is one of the best days for French B2B LinkedIn outreach:
- Monday: Bad — weekly planning dominates
- Tuesday–Thursday mornings: Best window
- Friday afternoon: Poor — people mentally checked out
- Weekend: Irrelevant

Tuesday is favorable. Not optimal (Wednesday/Thursday marginally better), but meaningfully better than Monday or Friday.

### Time-of-Day Analysis

Current time: 5:39 AM Paris. French decision-makers are not on LinkedIn at 5:39 AM. Optimal windows:
- 9:00–10:30 AM Paris
- 2:00–3:30 PM Paris

This cron fires at 4:39 AM UTC. Louis is asleep. The outreach would land in contacts' inboxes at 5:39 AM — buried under overnight notifications, deleted or ignored before 9 AM. By the time Louis acts, the optimal morning window is missed or cutting close.

### "Send Today" Has Never Worked

P67-D9 was approved 68 pulses ago. "Send today" has been issued, debated, re-issued, and re-debated. No message was sent. This is not a timing problem. It is an execution paralysis problem.

### The Cron Architecture Creates Structural Mismatch

The cron fires every 5 minutes starting at 4:39 AM. Louis is asleep. The instruction sits unread until morning. "Today" now means whatever day Louis gets around to checking — which has consistently meant "not today."

### Recommendation

**Tomorrow, Wednesday April 1, between 9:00–10:00 AM Paris time.**

Wednesday is the single best day for B2B outreach. An instruction generated tonight would reach Louis when he's awake and in the optimal window.

The cron should fire at ~7:00 AM Paris (5:00 AM UTC), not 4:39 AM. That gives Louis 2 hours to see the instruction before the window opens.

**Specific instruction this pulse should produce:** "Send P67-D9 tomorrow (Wednesday April 1) between 9:00 and 10:00 AM Paris time. Do it first thing."

## Agent 3: Venture Mortality Auditor

**Challenged:** Whether Alizé is viable given Louis's documented behavior patterns and constraints

### Key Findings

**Alizé as designed is structurally incompatible with Louis's demonstrated behavioral profile.**

### Louis's Actual Capacity

| Role | Entity | Status | Estimated Hours |
|------|--------|--------|----------------|
| M1 Student | MyDigitalSchool Caen | Active, full-time | 15-20 hrs/week |
| Intern | Grinto (web agency) | Active | 20-30 hrs/week |
| Co-founder | Kuroba (2 weeks old) | Active, consuming Month 1 | 8-15 hrs/week |
| Founder | Alizé | Planned launch | 0 hrs/week (zero external actions) |

**Total committed:** 43-65 hrs/week
**Realistic surplus for Alizé:** 5-12 hrs/week
**This number will decrease, not increase, over the next 60 days.**

### The M1 Academic Calendar Problem

March 31, 2026 — Louis is in the final term of M1. French master's programs: Semester 2 runs January to May/June, with exams in May-June. This means:
- Now through April: Coursework winding down, but project deadlines and dissertations heating up
- Late May-June: Exam period. Non-negotiable. Louis cannot simply "work more."
- July: M1 ends. "I'll launch after M1" is the same genre of deferral as "I'll start after the domain is bought."

### The Kuroba Consumption Problem

Kuroba is 2 weeks old. It will enter its first real client inflection point within 30-60 days. When that happens, Louis gets pulled into founding decisions, client negotiations, and co-founder alignment — all time-intensive and unpredictable.

**Honest assessment: Louis has approximately 5-8 hours/week of reliably available capacity for Alizé right now. That number will compress, not expand.**

### Is There Room for a Third Venture?

No. Not in the traditional startup sense. Alizé as a full venture requires 25+ hours/week minimum in Month 1. Louis has 5-8.

The binary framing that 68 pulses have avoided: Alizé can be a **side project** (5-8 hrs/week, slow build) or a **startup** (requires one or more existing commitments to be formally reduced). There is no version where Alizé gets 25 hours/week without something else being cut.

### Why Kuroba But Not Alizé

Louis executes on Kuroba. He researches Alizé. Same person, different relationship to the process.

**Most supported explanation:** Alizé requires outbound sales to external unknowns. Kuroba involves technical builds with co-founders who hold Louis accountable. Louis has demonstrated he can build things (Kuroba's domain, IA workflow, Grinto technical work). He has not demonstrated he can sell things (zero outreach in 68 pulses).

The pattern is not random — it is a behavioral profile.

### Does Alizé's Design Match Louis's Behavioral Profile?

**No. Alizé as designed is structurally incompatible.**

| Requirement | Louis's Demonstrated Capacity |
|-------------|------------------------------|
| Outbound sales to external prospects | Zero evidence of sales capability |
| Discovery calls with DG-level decision-makers | Zero evidence of this skill |
| Objection handling on price and credibility | Zero evidence |
| External client delivery accountability | Zero evidence |

### The Venture Viability Verdict

**Viable as side project. Not viable as designed startup.**

Three paths forward:

**Path A — Side project with external accountability:** Louis finds a commercial co-founder who closes deals while Louis focuses on technical delivery. Louis's hours stay at 5-8/week. The venture moves slowly. This is Alizé as a consulting practice, not a startup.

**Path B — Formal commitment restructure:** Louis reduces at least one existing commitment (Grinto, Kuroba, M1) by end of April. Creates 20-25 hrs/week for Alizé. Kuroba either gets a third co-founder who can carry it, or Louis reduces his role. This is the startup path. It requires explicit, dated decisions.

**Path C — Explicit shelve:** Louis acknowledges that March 31, 2026 is not the right time to launch Alizé. He revisits in July after M1 ends, with fresh capacity and a clearer picture of Kuroba's trajectory. This is not failure — it is realistic capacity management.

**What does NOT work:** The current structure — research without execution, planning without action, venture design without capacity.

### The Critical Question the Research Process Has Avoided

If Louis cannot send one email to one warm contact after 68 pulses of research and 91+ days of existence, what would need to change for him to do so?

The answer is not more research. The answer is not a better proof asset. The answer is not a different day of the week or time of day.

The answer is either:
1. Louis deciding, in one sentence, that Alizé is worth the social risk of external exposure — and acting accordingly
2. Someone else making that decision for him

The research process cannot make either happen. The research process can only document that it hasn't happened yet.
