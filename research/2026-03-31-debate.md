## Debate 2: French/Hosting Positioning Validation

**Agent:** Buyer-Validation Auditor (subagent 25c03375)
**Challenge:** Whether "French AI company" and "French hosting / EU AI Act" positioning actually matters to French PME buyers.

### Key Findings

**Assumption challenged:** That data sovereignty and French hosting are purchase triggers for the target buyer (French PME/ETI, 20-250 employees).

**Arguments:**

**What the brief claims vs. what was assumed:**
- BRIEF.md states "French companies prefer French providers for data sovereignty reasons" as fact — no primary research backs this
- The competitor research document explicitly flags this gap: "no specific 2025-2026 survey found on French PME readiness/willingness to pay for AI agents specifically"
- The EU AI Act compliance claim is listed as market context without buyer research confirmation

**French hosting is not a purchase filter:**
- Actual buyer barriers identified in research: skills gap, data readiness, cultural conservatism, trust in AI decisions
- Data sovereignty is absent from this list — it is an IT/compliance concern, not an operational buyer concern
- A DG at a 50-person accounting firm has outsourced compliance (external firm). They are not fielding calls about GDPR or EU AI Act.
- Exception: regulated industries where a compliance officer whispers concerns (niche, not mass market)

**"We're French" is table stakes with a credibility bonus:**
- Functions as a friction reducer (language, support accessibility, political risk reduction) not a competitive differentiator
- Agentova is Swiss-based and has thousands of French users — the market has not validated "French hosting" as a filter criterion
- Worth signaling, not worth leading with externally

**EU AI Act compliance does not resonate at PME scale:**
- The EU AI Act's compliance burden applies to high-risk AI systems — a 50-person accounting firm's invoice processing agent is not high-risk
- "EU AI Act compliance" as a claim requires the buyer to understand what it means and why it applies to them
- The compliance framing lands with IT/procurement at grandes entreprises, not DG buyers at PME

**What actually drives DG buyer decisions at 50-person firms:**
- Specific recognized pain point with measurable impact
- Clear ROI before commitment
- Someone to call if it breaks
- Proof it works for similar businesses
- Minimal process disruption

**Verdict:** French/hosting positioning is comfort noise, not purchase trigger. Keep it in the "how we're built" section. Remove it from hero, positioning claims, and cold outreach. Lead with the specific work the agent does, the specific pain it removes, and the specific outcome. The sovereignty framing is defensive reassurance for IT buyers — DG buyers want to know if it solves their problem.
