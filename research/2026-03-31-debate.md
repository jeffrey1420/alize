### Agent 1: Commercial Model Challenger

**Assumption challenged:** "Louis must deliver Kuroba/internal pilots before any external commercial motion begins." (D113, D170, D194, D241)

**Challenge 1 — Category mismatch:** Kuroba is a 2-3 person web agency. Alizé targets 20-50 person service firms. These are different operational complexities, different decision-maker levels, different workflow stakes. A 2-3 person agency case study does not convert a 30-person service firm. D242 correctly identified scale mismatch as a problem — but Kuroba was still approved as Month 1 internal deployment. These are contradictory.

**Challenge 2 — Sequencing inverts value creation:** The correct order for a capital-constrained, execution-limited founder is: sell first (client commits money), then deliver. Building capability in a vacuum with no client deadline produces infinite deferral. D183's "research partnerships" logic was right: get a real client in the room, then figure out delivery. Kuroba-first keeps Louis in solo-build mode for Month 1 with no external accountability and no commercial validation.

**Challenge 3 — Delivery education has zero commercial value until Alizé exists:** The Kuroba deployment builds AI agent infrastructure for Louis's own web agency. This produces Kuroba operational efficiency and Louis's delivery learning — but zero external credibility for Alizé's target market. Commercial value of Kuroba-first is entirely contingent on Alizé existing as a separate business.

**What the research hasn't considered:** The asymmetry between delivery capability (incremental, buildable over time, contractable) and commercial credibility (binary — you have a named reference or you don't). Commercial credibility requires external validation that cannot be generated internally. The research treats delivery capability as the scarce resource; commercial credibility is actually scarcer.

**New finding (P56-D1):** Kill "Kuroba internal deployment as Month 1 prerequisite." Replace with: Louis finds one warm contact who will commit to a paid diagnostic this week. Kuroba deployment runs parallel to client delivery — it is not a gate. First external pilot IS the delivery education, not a prelude to it.

**New finding (P56-D2):** Flip the delivery model. Sell first → deliver with contractor support → build playbook from that experience. Not: build delivery capability → then find clients.

**New finding (P56-D3):** Commercial credibility takes priority over delivery capability. Delivery contractors (D205) fill delivery gaps. The gate should be commercial credibility (a client in the room), not delivery readiness (Kuroba internal work).

### Agent 2: GTM Motion Challenger

**Assumption challenged:** "Human-mediated sales (warm network outreach or cold outbound) is the correct Year 1 GTM motion for Alizé."

**Challenge 1 — The research already identified the failure:** P54-D2 (same session): "Self-serve products as Year 1 pipeline bridge until sales capacity proven." D182: demo-led, prototype-first, zero-pitch. D183: first pilots as research partnerships, removing the sales close entirely. P55-D3: stop positioning work until paying client. 55 pulses, zero external actions. The warm/cold debate produced 250+ decisions. The execution failure rate is 100%.

**Challenge 2 — Warm network framing is wrong category:** Warm network should be a social proof factory, not a sales channel. D172 frames warm as revenue source. D173 frames warm referrals as the growth flywheel. But the warm network's real function is generating external evidence (case studies, testimonials, named references) that makes ALL OTHER channels work. Current framing: Louis reaches out → attempts to close → delivers → hopes for referral. Corrected: Louis builds working agent → publishes demo/template → warm network consumes and opts in → delivery → referrals follow.

**Challenge 3 — Human-mediated sales is structurally wrong:** For a one-person operation with three simultaneous jobs (Grinto internship, Kuroba co-founder, M1 master) and no closing skills, human-mediated sales is the wrong mechanism. The research has been debating the color of the bike shed for 55 pulses while the obvious answer — productized, self-serve, inbound-first — sits unaddressed in P54-D2 as a "bridge" rather than the primary motion.

**What the research hasn't seriously modeled:**
1. What Alizé looks like as an inbound-first, productized service (templates, playbooks, working demos accessible without a sales call)
2. Whether D172's "€1,500 fixed-fee project" can work as a self-serve purchase (landing page → buy → deliver) rather than a sold deal
3. Whether P54-D2's "self-serve bridge" should be the PRIMARY motion, not the bridge

**New finding (P56-D4):** Warm network = social proof factory. Louis sends warm contacts a link to the working demo, not a pitch message. "Look what we built" not "would you be interested."

**New finding (P56-D5):** Productized, self-serve, inbound-first is the primary Year 1 GTM motion, not the bridge. Landing page → "Buy this workflow automation" (credit card, no sales call) → discovery call scheduled. Louis's role shifts from "salesperson" to "product builder + content publisher."

**New finding (P56-D6):** Stop asking "how does Louis reach out to humans?" Start asking "what does Louis need to publish so humans reach us?"
