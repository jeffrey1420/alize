### Agent 1: Commercial Viability Auditor

**Assumption challenged:** P53-D1 — "project-first → proof period → earned retainer" as Year 1 commercial model

P53-D1 requires four discrete execution events: close €3-5K → deliver → convert to €1,200-1,500/mo proof period → expand to €2,000-2,500/mo. The debate log documents zero commercial executions across 56+ pulses. Product of execution probabilities = 0 × 0 × 0 × 0 = 0 regardless of how sound each individual step is.

**Challenge 2 — Pricing unvalidated against credibility ceiling:**
D84 said Louis can't close €7,500+ deals. P53-D1 proposes €3-5K closes. Whether Louis can close at the lower price point was never tested. D84 was reclassified as "untested hypothesis" in D257. €3-5K requires more credibility, more trust, and more delivery evidence than a free call — which was also never executed.

**Challenge 3 — Proof period creates second execution gauntlet:**
The 90-day proof period at €1,200-1,500/mo requires Louis to run a managed service engagement with no documented delivery capability. The transition from "project done" to "retainer signed" requires a second commercial conversation Louis has never demonstrated he can execute.

**What would disprove this:** Any single €3-5K close, any 90-day retainer conversion, or any 5 discovery calls booked in one week.

**P57-D1:** Commercial model debates are premature. First demonstrate execution on a single irreversible action (domain purchase, one message, one call). Any commercial model works if Louis executes it. None work if he doesn't.

### Agent 2: GTM Auditor

**Assumption challenged:** P56-D5 — "productized/self-serve/inbound-first is primary GTM motion"

**Challenge 1 — Self-serve product building is harder than sales:**
P56 replaced human-mediated sales (impossibility for Louis) with self-serve product build (also impossibility). Self-serve requires shipping real product with real UX, payment flows, and onboarding. This is categorically harder than sending a LinkedIn DM.

- SEO takes 12-18 months (D32 confirmed) — "Month 1 self-serve inbound test" contradicts this

**Challenge 3 — Self-serve has its own full execution stack:**
Payments, customer support, content marketing, conversion rate optimization. Louis has demonstrated zero capacity for any of them.

**What would disprove this:** Live product + inbound pipeline within 30 days of adopting P56-D5.

**P57-D2:** Self-serve/inbound-first is a Year 2 target state, not a Month 1 GTM motion. Warm outreach (direct, personal, zero product required) is the actual Month 1 GTM motion. The sequencing from P56 is backwards — self-serve is what Alizé evolves into after 3+ paying clients and a working demo worth sharing.

### Agent 3: Competitive Threat Auditor

**Assumption challenged:** "Regulated SMEs" is a defensible positioning wedge vs Pennylane/Cegid building AI-native accounting workflows

**Challenge 1 — Incumbent data advantage is structural, not temporal:**
Pennylane raised €40M+, Cegid is worth €2B+. Both have multi-year head starts on training data, integration partnerships, and regulatory relationships. Alizé cannot out-invest them in accounting-specific AI. The "governance gap" that was supposed to differentiate (D126) was already killed by D127 (governance gap overstated for 70% of ICP).

**Challenge 2 — "Regulated SMEs" describes a segment, not a moat:**
47+ pulses debated "governance as differentiator" without ever specifying the exact regulatory constraint that creates a wedge. "Regulated SMEs" invites comparison to better-funded incumbents in the same market. D83's four-part moat (client depth + switching costs + benchmark metrics + temporal regulatory window) is correct but "regulated SMEs" alone delivers none of those four elements.

**Challenge 3 — Incumbent AI timelines are compressed:**
Pennylane and Cegid are actively building AI features. If they launch native AI agents in 2026-2027 (likely given funding and technical capacity), the governance wedge disappears for accounting workflows entirely. Alizé would be entering a market where the incumbent just caught up.

**What would disprove this:** Primary research with expert-comptables showing WTP for compliance-specific workflow automation that Pennylane/Cegid cannot deliver at that price point.

**P57-D3:** "Regulated SMEs" is a market segment descriptor, not a moat. The defensible wedge is not "we serve regulated companies" — it's "we serve one specific regulatory workflow where the compliance implementation cost breaks the incumbent's economics." Use regulatory framing only when: (1) a specific regulatory requirement can be named, (2) a specific implementation differentiator can be demonstrated, (3) a buyer willing to pay for that compliance gap has been identified.
