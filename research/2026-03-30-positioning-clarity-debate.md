# Positioning Clarity Debate — Pulse 42
## Alizé: Differentiated Positioning or Pile of Demotions?

**Auditor:** Positioning Clarity Auditor  
**Date:** 2026-03-30  
**Context:** 42 research pulses, 200+ decisions, positioning refined continuously since Day 1

---

## Assumption Challenged

**The assumption being challenged:**  
That "back-office admin AI for service firms" (D77) is a sufficient positive positioning claim for a DG buyer.

This assumption treats "specificity about WHO" as equivalent to "specificity about WHAT." Describing a target audience ("service firms") without describing a specific outcome, a specific pain, or a specific job-to-be-done is not positioning — it's target description. Any well-run marketing agency could write "back-office admin AI for service firms" in 10 minutes. It tells the buyer who the product is for. It does not tell the buyer why they should care.

---

## What the Positioning Looks Like After Removing All Demoted Elements

Strip every element that was demoted or removed across 42 pulses:

**REMOVED:**
- "AI employee" → removed (French labor law risk)
- "Managed service" → moved from hero to "how we work" section
- "MCP" → removed from all external materials
- "French sovereignty / EU AI Act" → moved to "how we're built"
- "Delegation with accountability" → replaced with specific task/outcome language

**SURVIVING ELEMENTS (what's left in the hero):**
- "AI agents that do real work — not demos, not chatbots, not promises"
- "Time saved, fewer repetitive tasks, more speed, more control"

**THE HONEST TRANSCRIPT OF THE HERO AFTER DEMOTIONS:**

> "We use AI agents to automate repetitive back-office tasks for service companies. You'll save time. You'll have more control. It's not a chatbot."

That is the positive claim. Read it again. Does that make a DG buyer stop scrolling? Does it answer "why you, not the consultant I'm already working with"? Does it answer "why you, not Copilot"? Does it answer "why you, not my intern using ChatGPT"?

No. It reads like a category description with a promise attached. Every automation tool in this market says the same thing.

## Is There a Coherent Positive Claim Left?

**No.** Not a differentiated one. There is a coherent category description — "AI agents that automate back-office admin for service firms" — but that is not a positioning claim. That is a product definition.

The difference:
- A **positioning claim** answers: "Why is this worth my attention AND my money AND changing how I work?"
- A **product definition** answers: "What does this product do and who is it for?"

Alizé's post-demotion hero is a product definition wearing positioning clothes.

The promise of "time saved, more control" is the same promise as:
- Zapier (automate workflows, save time)
- Make.com (visual automation, more efficiency)
- Power Automate (automate processes, reduce manual work)
- Any RPA vendor for the past 15 years
- A motivated intern with API access

The differentiation from 42 pulses of refinement is almost entirely negative — "here's what we're NOT" — rather than positive — "here's specifically what we ARE."

## Is This a Positioning Problem or a Product/Market Fit Problem?

**This is primarily a product/market fit problem that positioning cannot solve.**

Here's the evidence:

1. **The vertical has changed 5+ times** (professional services → expert-comptables → digital agencies → e-commerce → professional services → ???). No amount of hero-copy iteration survives a vertical flip.

2. **The first workflow is still undefined at Pulse 42.** D81 was revised, D126 reversed it, and the current pulse (41) notes U125 ("which specific workflow within expert-comptables") is still unresolved. You cannot write differentiated positioning copy for an undefined product delivered to an undefined buyer for an undefined job-to-be-done.

3. **The pricing has swung from €490 diagnostic → free → €1,500 → €4,500 → €7,500 → €1,500 → tiered.** Price is a positioning signal. A price that moves this much across 42 pulses signals a product that hasn't found its market tolerance point, not a positioning problem.

4. **The delivery model has changed 6+ times** (Louis solo → contractor → delivery partner first → Louis solo again → internal rehearsal → external pilot Month 2 → Louis delivers Month 1). The buyer doesn't see this, but the sales team does — and inconsistent delivery models produce inconsistent case studies, which produce inconsistent positioning evidence.

**What positioning can do:**  
Positioning can take a clear product/market fit signal and amplify it. It can find the sharpest way to express what's already working. It cannot manufacture differentiation where the product doesn't yet have a defensible, specific, reproducible answer to "what exactly do you do that I can't get cheaper or faster elsewhere."

**Verdict:** Positioning is a symptom. The disease is an undefined core product with insufficient evidence that any specific version of it works. Until the first 2-3 pilots produce consistent case studies with specific metrics, the positioning will continue to drift because there is no empirical anchor to hold it steady.

## The ONE Sentence

**What a DG buyer needs to hear to say "yes, that's what I need":**

The sentence must answer three questions simultaneously:
1. What is the specific work being done? (not "admin" — what ADMIN work)
2. What is the specific pain being removed? (not "time" — what TIME specifically)
3. What makes this different from alternatives they already have?

**The ONE sentence:**

> "We take over your invoice processing, CRM updates, and client FAQ responses — the 15 hours a week of repetitive data work that your senior staff are doing manually — and run it so you never have to think about it again."

**Why this works for a DG buyer:**

- "15 hours a week" is a number a CEO can personally validate — they know who on their team is doing this work
- "Invoice processing, CRM updates, client FAQ responses" — specific enough that the buyer recognizes their own problem, general enough to be repeatable
- "Senior staff" — signals this isn't replacing an intern, it's freeing up expensive talent
- "Never have to think about it again" — replaces the vague "ownership transfer" with an emotional outcome

**The test:** Would a CEO of a 40-person consulting firm read this and immediately think of the specific employee who is buried in this work? If yes, it converts. If they read it and think "that sounds like a tool for someone else's problem," it doesn't.

**Does Alizé have this sentence?**  
No. Current language ("back-office admin AI for service firms") is a category description, not a specific pain claim. It passes the category-recognition test (buyer knows what it means) but fails the "that's my problem" test (buyer doesn't immediately see themselves).

## Verdict

### Summary

After 42 pulses of positioning refinement, Alizé has accumulated a pile of demotions — things that were removed from the headline because they were either legally risky ("AI employee"), technically meaningless to buyers ("managed service"), too nerdy to convert ("MCP"), or insufficiently differentiated alone ("French sovereignty"). The positive claim left standing is "AI agents that automate repetitive back-office tasks for service firms."

This is a product definition, not a positioning claim. It is accurate. It is honest. It is not differentiated.

### The Core Problem

The positioning keeps evolving because the positioning is fine — the product clarity is insufficient. You cannot write a sharp positioning sentence when:
- The vertical has changed 5+ times
- The first workflow is still undefined after 42 pulses
- The delivery model has changed 6+ times
- The pricing has moved by an order of magnitude

Positioning is the last 10% of product/market clarity. Alizé is trying to do positioning work when 60-70% of the product/market evidence hasn't been generated yet.

### What Would Actually Fix This

One completed pilot with a named client, specific workflow, measurable ROI, and a client willing to be referenced. Not a case study for the website. Evidence that the thing being sold actually works and produces the promised outcome.

That evidence, once generated, will make positioning obvious. Until then, the positioning will continue to drift because there is nothing to anchor it.

### The Assumption Challenged (Explicitly)

**Assumption challenged:** "Back-office admin AI for service firms" is specific enough to convert a DG buyer.

**Finding:** It is specific enough to pass the category-recognition test. It is not specific enough to pass the "that's my problem" test. A DG buyer reading "back-office admin AI for service firms" recognizes the category. A DG buyer reading "we handle your invoice processing and client FAQ responses so your senior staff never have to touch them again" recognizes their own organizational pain.

The former describes a product. The latter creates urgency.

### Final Answer to the Four Questions

1. **What's left after removing demoted elements:** A category description ("AI agents for back-office admin work") with a generic efficiency promise ("time saved"). Not differentiated. Not wrong. Not compelling.

2. **Is "back-office admin AI" specific enough to convert a DG buyer:** No. It's specific about the buyer type but vague about the specific work. DG buyers don't have "back-office admin" problems. They have specific employees buried in specific repetitive tasks. The sentence needs to name the task, not the category.

3. **Positioning problem or product/market fit problem:** Product/market fit problem, primarily. Positioning will find its voice once there is empirical evidence of what actually works. Until then, any positioning is speculation.

4. **The ONE sentence:**  
"We take over your invoice processing, CRM updates, and client FAQ responses — the 15 hours a week of repetitive data work that your senior staff are doing manually — and run it so you never have to think about it again."
