# Pulse 27 — Expert-Comptable Vertical Challenge

## The Assumption Being Challenged

**D126:** Expert-comptables (administrative workflows around client management and regulatory reporting) as first beachhead — NOT professional services, NOT digital agencies. Governance gap is the differentiator.

**D126 should be MODIFIED, not abandoned.** The kernel is right (admin workflows as beachhead), but the vertical is chosen on false premises.

---

## Argument 1: The Governance Wedge Cuts Against D126, Not For It

D127 explicitly states the governance gap is "overstated for 70% of ICP." If this is true, then:

- The **defining rationale** for expert-comptables (governance gap as moat) applies to at most 30% of the narrowed ICP
- Regulatory/reporting workflows are operationally critical across many verticals, not uniquely in accounting
- Building a vertical strategy on a moat that D127 himself partially disputes is fragile

**The governance gap argument actually says: "we can't win on features, so we win on compliance."** That's a defensive posture for a 1-person studio with Grinto running. That's not a beachhead — that's a bunker.

If governance is overstated as a differentiator for 70% of ICP, the vertical choice should follow where governance is *actually* relevant, not where we hope it might be a moat.

---

## Argument 2: The Network Dependency Is Unresolved and Dangerous

Three unknowns sit directly under D126's feet:

- **U123/U129 (UNRESOLVED):** Has Louis actually met any expert-comptables? Does he have warm connections, loose ties, or zero relationship?
- **U52 (UNRESOLVED):** Louis's warm network is unknown. We don't know who he can actually reach.
- **U91 (UNRESOLVED):** Louis's actual hours are unknown. Even if he has connections, does he have bandwidth to cold/warm outreach into a new vertical?

D126's strategy implicitly assumes: (a) Louis has expert-comptable relationships, (b) those relationships are trust-bearing enough to pilot with, (c) Louis can sustain focus on a regulated vertical while Grinto runs.

**None of these are confirmed.** D126 is building on quicksand.

**The counterpoint:** We DO know Louis has Grinto connections. That's confirmed, documented, real. The strategy is anchored to an unknown while ignoring a known.

---

## Argument 3: The Domain Knowledge Gap Makes a Credible Pilot Impossible

**U124 (UNRESOLVED):** Does Louis understand expert-comptable administrative workflows deeply enough to:
- Scope a pilot that addresses real pain?
- Design automation that fits existing processes?
- Speak the language of client onboarding, regulatory reporting, document collection?
- Identify which workflows are actually painful vs. assumed painful?

Without U124 resolved, Louis cannot design a credible pilot. A pilot scoped by assumptions will either:
1. Miss the real pain points → pilot fails to show value
2. Require heavy discovery work mid-pilot → burns time Louis doesn't have
3. Reveal ignorance → damages credibility with a warm lead (if one exists)

**Louis cannot sell into a regulated vertical he doesn't understand.** Even if U123 is resolved (he has connections), U124 being unresolved means he walks into meetings underarmed.

---

## Alternative Recommendation

**Lead with web agencies, not expert-comptables — using Grinto as the wedge.**

**Why web agencies make sense:**

1. **Network is known.** Louis has Grinto connections. U52 is implicitly resolved for this vertical.
2. **Domain is familiar.** Web agency admin workflows (project intake, client onboarding, file collection, reporting, scope management) are adjacent to Louis's existing work. U124 is closer to resolved by default.
3. **Hours are tractable.** Web agencies have recognizable, bounded workflows. Scope is easier to define than regulatory reporting cycles.
4. **The conflict (U95/U107) is actually a feature.** If Louis has visibility into Grinto's operations, he has insider access to real workflows. The conflict flag means "be transparent" — not "avoid entirely."
5. **Paves the path to expert-comptables.** Web agencies serve accounting firms. Louis can meet expert-comptables *through* his web agency network. That's a warmer, more legitimate path than direct cold outreach.

**The revised beachhead logic:**

- **Year 1:** Web agency admin workflows as beachhead (Louis's network + domain adjacency + manageable scope)
- **Year 2:** Expand into expert-comptables through web agency relationships (warm introduction, not cold call)
- **Use governance selectively** — not as the wedge, but as a proof point for the subset that genuinely needs it

## Summary

D126's instinct (admin workflows as beachhead) is correct. The vertical (expert-comptables) is not yet validated. The three unresolved unknowns — network, domain knowledge, and bandwidth — make expert-comptables a premature commitment.

**The safe path:** Start where Louis's network actually is (web agencies), build credibility on familiar ground, and let expert-comptable relationships develop organically through existing client relationships.

**The aggressive path (if Louis wants to pursue expert-comptables directly):** Spend 2 weeks on U123/U124/U52 validation before committing to any vertical. The vertical decision should follow the network validation, not precede it.
