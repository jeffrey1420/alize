## Challenge 3: The "3-6 Month Window" Is a Capacity Constraint, Not a Market Event

**The assumption:** D82 establishes a "3-6 month effective window" based on Louis's capacity constraints. D87 says this window is a "credibility window" where failed closes create permanent network narrative.

**The problem:** The debate log conflates two different kinds of windows:

1. **Market windows** — Something in the external environment changes. A competitor enters. A technology becomes obsolete. A regulatory deadline passes. The window has a specific close event.

2. **Capacity windows** — The founder runs out of time, energy, or capital. The window has a specific exhaustion point.

D82 is built on capacity, not market. But the framing ("effective window of 3-6 months") implies market urgency — that something changes at Month 6.

**What actually changes at Month 6?**

The debate log does not answer this question. Let me test the plausible options:

**(a) Competitors enter:** D76 set the first-mover window at 6-18 months, not 3-6. Microsoft Copilot threat is assessed as 18-36 months out (D12). So Month 6 is not a competitor-threatened deadline.

**(b) Louis's capital runs out:** U118 (Louis's financial capacity) is unresolved. No one has asked whether Louis can fund 3-6 months of no revenue. If he can, there's no capital close event at Month 6. If he can't, the window is financial, not market — and the plan should say that explicitly.

**(c) The market learns about AI agents and becomes skeptical:** No evidence for this as a Month 6 event. French PME AI adoption is described as "behind US peers" — suggesting undersupply of trust, not oversupply of skepticism.

**(d) Something else:** The debate log doesn't specify.

**The false urgency problem:** "Effective window of 3-6 months" is being used to justify urgency in the GTM plan — that is, to compress decision-making and justify moving fast. But if the window is a capacity constraint (Louis can only sustain this intensity for 3-6 months), that is a founder-exhaustion risk, not a market risk. It should be framed as such: "Louis can sustain full-time Alizé effort for approximately 3-6 months before Grinto + web agency + Alizé becomes unsustainable." That's honest. "The effective window is 3-6 months" with no further explanation implies market closing, which is false.

**D87's credibility framing is more honest:** "failed closes create permanent network narrative" — this is the real risk. If Louis burns warm network contacts with failed pitches, the network closes. That's a reason to be selective and well-prepared, not a reason to move fast.

**Verdict on Challenge 3:** The 3-6 month window needs a precise definition. If it's a capacity window, say so: "Louis can sustain X hours/week on Alizé for approximately Y months." If it's a market window, identify the specific close event. "Window of 3-6 months" with no specification is not a planning assumption — it's a rhetorical device that creates urgency without accountability.

## Challenge 4: Association Outreach Is a Month 9+ Channel Disguised as Month 1-3 Supplement

**The assumption:** D130 says "supplement with Malt + expert-comptable association outreach." U131 asks "which expert-comptable body to approach first?" Both are listed as active channels in the near-term plan.

**The problem:** Neither U131 nor the debate log at large has answered which body to approach. There are multiple expert-comptable bodies in France with different structures, decision timelines, and appetites for vendor endorsements:

- **CSOEC** (Conseil Supérieur de l'Ordre des Experts-Comptables) — national professional body
- **CRCC** (Compagnie Régionale des Commissaires aux Comptes) — for statutory auditors, different population
- **ANECS** — association of younger experts-comptables, less institutional weight
- **UJEP** — union of small accounting practices, represents very small firms (<5 employees)
- **Regional chambers** — each has some autonomy on local initiatives

The relevant body for Alizé's ICP (20-50 person accounting firms doing client management and regulatory reporting) would likely be CSOEC or a regional chamber — but this has never been established.

**The institutional endorsement timeline problem:** These bodies do not endorse vendors. They are professional associations with governance structures, elected leadership, and member constituencies. Getting an association to formally refer a vendor involves:

1. Internal discovery meeting (Month 1-2)
2. Staff or committee review (Month 3-6)
3. Possibly a member survey or consultation (Month 6-9)
4. Formal approval process (Month 9-12)
5. Actual referral program launch (Month 12+)

This is optimistic. Some professional bodies explicitly prohibit endorsement of commercial vendors to protect members from conflicts of interest. The CSOEC publishes ethical guidelines that restrict commercial endorsements.

**Six months is not realistic to get a single association referral** unless:
- The association has an existing "preferred vendor" program (rare)
- A board member has a personal relationship with someone at the association (possible, but not modeled)
- The association is actively looking for AI automation content for their own member communications (possible as a co-marketing angle, not referral)

**The distinction between co-marketing and referral:** Alizé could potentially get on a conference agenda or write an article for an association newsletter in 3-6 months. That is not the same as the association referring its members to Alizé as a trusted vendor. The debate log conflates these.

**D135 says "expert-comptables validated through warm intros, not cold outreach"** — this is the right instinct. The association path is about building relationships with individual practitioners who happen to be association members, not about getting the institution to endorse Alizé.

**Verdict on Challenge 4:** Association outreach as institutional referral (UJEP, CSOEC) is a Month 9+ channel. Treating it as a Month 1-3 supplement is a planning error. It should be reframed as: individual warm introductions to accounting firm partners who are association members — which is the same as general warm network outreach, not a distinct channel.
