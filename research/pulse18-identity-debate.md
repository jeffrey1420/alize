# Pulse 18: Attacking the "Productized Consulting" Identity

## Premise

D37 frames Alizé as "a productized consulting practice, not a SaaS business, for Years 1-2." This identity has shaped hiring (consultants over engineers), pricing (day rates disguised as tiered offers), and sales motion (relationship-driven). This analysis attacks that identity from three angles and concludes with a recommended reframe.

---

## Angle 1: THE HIRING TRAP

**The problem:** "Productized consulting" is the worst of both worlds for talent recruitment.

**Great engineers don't join consulting firms.** They join product companies where they can build scalable systems, own technical decisions, and compound their learning. The moment Louis pitches Alizé as "consulting," the best engineering candidates filter themselves out. They're not wrong to do so — consulting firms treat engineers as delivery resources, not product builders.

**Great consultants don't join small practices either.** They go independent (€800-1,200/day day rates) or join established firms (McKinsey, BCG, premium boutiques) where the brand does the selling. A freelancer billing €900/day isn't going to take a salaried position at Alizé when they can keep 100% of their rates. An ex-BCG consultant isn't joining a 2-person shop calling itself "productized consulting."

**Who actually builds the technical moat?** Louis. That's the answer. At 6-8 clients, "productized consulting" means Louis is the engineer, the consultant, the account manager, and the sales rep. The identity doesn't attract the talent needed to build the moat — it repels it.

**The trap deepens:** Even if Louis hires "consultants" to handle delivery, they're not building the tooling, automation, or knowledge management systems that would create Alizé's competitive advantage. A €60K consultant delivered Alizé's service is still a body in, body out arrangement. There's no leverage, no compounding technical value.

**Verdict on hiring:** "Productized consulting" as an internal identity actively prevents Alizé from building the team that could create a durable technical moat. The identity signals "service delivery" not "technical product building." Great people self-select out.

---

## Angle 2: THE FUNDRAISING TRAP

**The problem:** "Productized consulting" gets 0x valuation premium. It might get negative consideration.

**VCs fund scalable software leverage.** The valuation model is simple: recurring revenue × multiple, where the multiple is determined by growth rate, retention, and scalability. A consulting firm with €200K in retainer revenue gets a services multiple: 1-2x revenue, if that. A SaaS business with €200K ARR gets 5-15x revenue, depending on growth. The difference is not fair — it's just how the market works.

**The term "productized consulting" is a red flag in pitch meetings.** Sophisticated angels and seed VCs have heard this framing before. It's often used by founders who want SaaS-like revenue without doing the hard work of building a product. They know the difference between a business with product leverage and a business that's billing hours with better UX.

**What actually happens if Louis raises on this identity:**
- Angel round (Year 1): Angels might invest, but at a services multiple. They're buying equity in Louis's time, not in a scalable asset. Expect 1-1.5x revenue at best, with heavy preference for revenue already contracted.
- Seed round (Year 2): Seed VCs will ask "why can't you just grow organically?" The answer is usually uncomfortable — because consulting revenue doesn't scale linearly, and Louis can't bill 25 hours a day. Without a product story, there's no venture math.

**The foreclosed options:** If Alizé ever wants to raise, the "productized consulting" identity needs to be replaced before the first investor call. Better to build the correct identity from Month 1 than to have to do a painful rebrand narrative when fundraising.

**Verdict on fundraising:** The identity forecloses premium valuation. If Louis wants the funding option to be valuable, Alizé needs an identity that implies software leverage, not service delivery.

---

## Angle 3: THE CLIENT PERCEPTION TRAP

**The problem:** "Productized consulting" is internal jargon that clients don't care about — but the identity underneath it matters a lot.

**French PME buyers at €1,500-2,500/month don't think "SaaS" or "consulting."** They think: "am I getting results? Is this person/company reliable? Am I getting my money's worth?" The label matters less than the perception. A client who pays €2,000/month for an "AI agent service" expects software that works. A client who pays €2,000/month for "consulting" expects a senior person's time and judgment.

**Here's the critical insight:** Alizé's clients ARE buying a service — but not the kind of service "consulting" implies. They're buying operational AI, not strategic advice. The confusion comes from the word "consulting" carrying the wrong connotations: junior bodies, PowerPoint decks, vague recommendations.

**Does the identity affect how the business operates?** Yes, fundamentally. If Louis thinks of Alizé as "consulting," he will:
- Scope projects as deliverables (reports, recommendations, reviews)
- Bill by time or milestone
- Hire consultants who deliver those deliverables
- Build a business where revenue = hours × rate

If Louis thinks of Alizé as "AI operations" or "AI delivery platform," he will:
- Scope engagements as ongoing operational outcomes (tasks automated, processes running, errors resolved)
- Bill as a managed service retainer
- Hire engineers and operators who build and run systems
- Build a business where revenue = clients × platform value

**The "expensive agency" risk is real.** Without a clear product/technical identity, Alizé gets compared to web agencies, consulting firms, and freelancers. The differentiation collapses to price, and at €1,500-2,500/month, Alizé is expensive. Clients need a clear reason why a team of humans with AI tools is worth more than a freelancer with AI tools.

**Verdict on client perception:** The label matters less than the underlying operational model, but the identity Louis uses internally shapes what he builds. "Productized consulting" as internal identity drives consulting behaviors, which leads to an agency-style business, which leads to "expensive agency" client perception. The self-fulfilling prophecy is real.

---

## Conclusions

### Is "Productized Consulting" a Useful Internal Identity?

**No. It's a limiting label masquerading as pragmatism.**

"Productized consulting" tells Louis: hire consultants, bill by the day, build relationships, grow organically, don't invest in platform. That's a fine recipe for a lifestyle business. It's a terrible recipe for a venture-backable, talent-attracting, technically differentiated company.

The honest framing — "we're a consulting firm, not SaaS" — might be comforting because it manages expectations. But it also manages ambition downward.

### What Should Alizé's Identity Actually Be?

**For hiring:** "AI delivery" or "AI operations." Engineers want to build and run systems, not deliver consulting engagements. The hiring brand should signal technical work: we're building, we're automating, we're operating AI at scale. Not: we're a premium consulting firm with better tooling.

**For fundraising:** "AI services platform" or "AI operations platform." VCs fund platforms. They fund leverage. "Platform" signals recurring revenue AND scalability. If Louis ever fundraises, the pitch is: "We're the platform that lets French SMEs run production AI agents without building in-house teams. We have X clients, Y% net retention, and a roadmap to Z." That's a fundable story. "We do consulting with AI" is not.

**For client acquisition:** "AI agent management" or "AI operations partner." Clients aren't buying consulting. They're buying AI that works. The positioning should match what clients actually experience: ongoing operational delivery, not project-based advice.

### The Core Question: Company That Happens to Do Consulting, or Consulting Practice That Builds Software?

**Louis should think of Alizé as a company that happens to do consulting in Years 1-2, with the explicit plan to become a company that builds software in Years 2-3.**

The consulting phase is strategic, not an identity. It's how Alizé earns revenue while validating demand, building workflows, and accumulating the domain knowledge needed to productize. But the identity from Day 1 should be: **"We build AI operation systems. Our current delivery mechanism is a premium managed service. Our future asset is the platform we build while delivering that service."**

Every client engagement should be designed to extract reusable patterns. Every workflow should be documented as potential product IP. The consultant hat is temporary. The platform builder identity is permanent.

### The Recommended Rebrand

**Replace "productized consulting" with:**

- **Internal identity:** AI operations company, temporarily in consulting mode
- **Hiring identity:** AI delivery (we build and run AI systems)
- **Fundraising identity:** AI operations platform
- **Client-facing identity:** AI agent management partner / AI operations

The underlying economics might not change in Year 1. Louis will still be the primary deliverer. Revenue will still be retainer-based. But the identity shift changes what he builds, who he hires, and how he pitches when fundraising becomes relevant.

**Productized consulting is a trap: it feels honest, but it forecloses the future.** Better to be a software company in disguise than a consulting firm in denial.

---

*Analysis complete. Recommendation: abandon "productized consulting" as internal identity immediately. Replace with "AI operations" framing. Build toward platform identity from Month 1.*
