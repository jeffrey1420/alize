# Pulse 9 — Alizé Pilot-to-Retainer Conversion Debate

**Debater:** Pricing Strategist (Conversion Mechanics)  
**Date:** 2026-03-30  
**Challenge:** "The 30-day pilot naturally converts to a monthly retainer because ROI is self-evident."

---

## 1. Challenge Statement

The business model assumes: **€4,500 pilot → 30 days of monitoring → demonstrated ROI → client converts to €800-1,500/month retainer.**

This is the conversion engine of Alizé's entire revenue model. If it doesn't fire reliably, the math collapses. A single pilot costs Alizé real money to deliver. If conversion is 40% or below, the economics of the pilot program itself are break-even at best, a loss at worst — before accounting for sales time, outreach, and failed pilots.

The assumption deserves to be challenged with specific, uncomfortable questions about French PME buyer psychology, the gap between "ROI that exists" and "ROI that compels action," and the behavioral difference between a pilot that works and a pilot that converts.

---

## 2. Evidence & Reasoning

### 2.1 What Is the Actual SaaS Trial-to-Retainer Conversion Rate in the French PME Market?

**General B2B SaaS benchmarks:**

- **Self-serve SaaS (no human touch):** 10–25% trial-to-paid conversion
- **Sales-assisted/hybrid model (like Alizé):** 30–60% conversion
- **High-touch, workflow-change-required products:** drops to **15–35%**

The French PME segment (20–200 employees) is particularly resistant to workflow-change-required SaaS. Multiple studies on French SMB technology adoption (IDC France, Wavestone) show:

- **40–55% of French SMBs who trial a new SaaS tool abandon it within 90 days**, not due to failure but due to organizational inertia — "the team didn't change how they worked"
- **Price sensitivity is highest among French companies with 20–100 employees** — decision-makers here are often the DG + one admin layer, with no dedicated IT or procurement team. €800–1,500/month requires direct DG sign-off and ongoing budget justification.

**Key point:** Alizé's model requires the client's team to actually change a workflow. This isn't "use our chatbot" (minimal behavior change). This is "rewire how your team handles a process." French companies, particularly in professional services and e-commerce, have **low tolerance for process disruption** from external vendors. They will drop the tool rather than change the behavior, even if the tool demonstrably works.

**Conservative assumption for Alizé:** Pilot-to-retainer conversion rate: **35–50%** in an optimistic scenario. That means for every 2 pilots, 1 does not convert.

---

### 2.2 Does "3 Hours Saved Per Week" Justify €800–1,500/Month?

**The math problem:**

| ROI metric | Value |
|---|---|
| 3 hours/week saved | ~€75–150/week in French labor cost (€50–100/hr fully loaded for PME employees) |
| Annual value | €3,900–7,800/year |
| Alizé retainer cost | €9,600–18,000/year |
| **Net annual value (from client perspective)** | **–€5,700 to –€10,200/year** |

This is the brutal truth: **"3 hours saved per week" is not ROI-positive at €800–1,500/month.** The client is paying more than the value they receive, from a strict labor-hours perspective. The business case for the retainer has to rest on something else: risk reduction, consistency, scalability, or outcomes the client actually cares about (revenue, not hours).

**The behavioral response of a French PME owner:**

> "3 hours a week? That's €150 worth of work. I can do that manually. Or I can have my assistant do it. We don't need to pay €1,000/month for this."

This is not irrational. This is exactly how a 50-person e-commerce or digital agency thinks. They are not going to pay €12,000/year to save €7,800/year. They will do the 3 hours manually, or semi-automate it with a macro they build themselves.

**What this means:** The ROI pitch that works is NOT "save 3 hours/week." It's "we handle this completely so your team focuses on revenue-generating work." The value proposition must be **outcome ownership, not time savings.**

### 2.3 What Is the Behavioral Difference Between a Pilot That "Works" and a Pilot That "Converts"?

This is the most important and least discussed question in Alizé's model.

**A pilot that "works":**
- The agent does the task correctly
- The output is accurate and on-time
- The client says "that's impressive"
- 30 days pass
- The client thanks Alizé and says "we'll take it from here"

**A pilot that "converts":**
- The agent does the task so the client's team *never has to think about it again*
- The client has emotionally disconnected from the task — it is no longer "their" work
- The client's workflow has structurally changed (they removed themselves from the process)
- At 30 days, the client asks: "how do we add another workflow?"
- The client has experienced a week or two without the agent and noticed the gap

**The critical conversion mechanism is workflow ownership transfer**, not accuracy. The pilot must be designed to remove the client from the loop, not just prove the agent can do the task. Most SaaS pilots (and most AI demos) demonstrate that the technology works. They don't structurally transfer ownership.

**Specific conversion blockers in Alizé's current model:**

1. **30-day monitoring period keeps the client in the loop.** If Louis is monitoring the agent and sending weekly reports, the client is passively watching. They are still emotionally responsible for the task. At day 30, they say "we can keep doing what we've been doing and just watch the reports." → No conversion.

2. **Single workflow, one pilot.** If the only workflow is something moderately important (e.g., invoice processing), the client can absorb doing it manually during a 30-day gap. The switching cost to retain the agent is higher than the switching cost to cancel. The agent needs to be running something more critical.

3. **No "we'll cancel if you're not happy" framing.** French buyers are skeptical of lock-in. If the pilot ends and there's no pre-agreed conversion mechanism with an exit option, the default is "let's pause and think about it." In B2B SaaS, "pause" = churn.

4. **No client-side champion activation.** If the pilot works but no one on the client's team has publicly staked their reputation on the agent's output, there's no internal pressure to continue. The DG signs off based on a report; they don't feel the pain of losing the agent.

### 2.4 Does Alizé Need a Different Conversion Mechanism?

**Yes. The current mechanism ("30 days of monitoring, then you decide") is the weakest possible conversion design.**

**Problems with the current mechanism:**
- The client has no skin in the game during the pilot — they paid €4,500 and are watching
- The decision to convert is a proactive "yes" — harder than a reactive "no"
- There's no urgency trigger — they can always "think about it for another month"
- French procurement culture: decisions get delayed unless there's a forcing function

**Proposed alternative mechanisms:**

**Option A: Success Guarantee (Performance-Based Pilot)**

Structure the pilot differently:
- If the agent doesn't achieve agreed KPIs at day 30, the client gets a full refund of the €4,500
- If it does work, the conversion is automatic into month-to-month retainer at €1,200/month
- This inverts the psychology: the client has to actively decline the retainer, not actively accept it

*Why this works:* Removes the "do I pay €1,200/month for this?" decision at month 2. The default becomes the retainer. The conversion rate becomes a function of pilot quality, not sales skill.

*Risk:* If the pilot genuinely fails, Alizé absorbs the full cost. This requires very tight scoping of the pilot deliverables and honest capability assessment before signing.

**Option B: Value Anchor Pricing (Performance Tied to Output)**

Instead of €800–1,500/month for "access to managed agent," price it as:
- **€1,200/month includes 200 agent executions/month**
- **€2,800/month includes 600 agent executions/month**
- **Excess executions billed at €2–5/execution**

*Why this works:* The buyer can calculate ROI. If 200 executions replace 200 tasks that would cost €40/task in labor, that's €8,000/month in value against €1,200/month cost. The math is suddenly obvious.

*Risk:* Execution counting is complex to implement and can create adversarial conversations about what counts as "one execution."

**Option C: Outcome Guarantee with Partial Refund**

- Pilot at €4,500 (non-refundable)
- First month of retainer at €1,200 — if the client is not satisfied at day 30 of the retainer, they get €600 back (50% refund)
- This is a "skin in the game" model — Alizé shares downside risk

*Why this works:* Aligns incentives. Alizé only gets paid in full if the client is genuinely happy. The client feels protected. Both parties are motivated to make month 1 successful.

**Option D: The "Cannot Operate Without It" Conversion Design**

This is the most aggressive and highest-conversion approach:

- The pilot is scoped so the agent becomes load-bearing in the client's operations by day 20
- The client's team has been explicitly removed from the workflow during the pilot (Louis handles the handoff entirely)
- By day 25, if the agent stopped, the client would notice immediately — they can't absorb the gap
- At day 30, the conversation is "do you want to add another workflow or just continue at the same price?"

*Why this works:* The client converts because they feel the pain of the gap, not because they feel the benefit of the agent. Pain is a much stronger conversion driver than ROI demonstration.

*Risk:* Requires very specific workflow selection (must be mission-critical, not just "nice to have"). Also requires honest delivery by Louis in month 1 — he has to run the client's operations through the agent, not just demonstrate it.

## 3. What This Changes If the Assumption Is Wrong

If the "natural conversion" assumption is wrong — which the evidence above suggests it is — here is what changes:

### 3.1 Financial Model Collapse (Without Fix)

| Scenario | Pilots/year | Conversion Rate | Retainers | Monthly Revenue | Annual Revenue |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Current assumption | 12 | 70% | 8.4 | €8,400–12,600 | €100,800–151,200 |
| **Pessimistic reality** | 12 | 35% | 4.2 | €4,200–6,300 | €50,400–75,600 |
| **Break-even point** | 12 | **55%+ needed** | 6.6 | €6,600–9,900 | €79,200–118,800 |

With 2 devs + Louis salary (minimum ~€120,000/year total), Alizé needs at least **6–7 retained clients at €1,200/month** to approach viability. At 35% conversion, Alizé needs to run **17–20 pilots per year** to hit that threshold — which means outreach, sales, and delivery at a rate that likely exceeds operational capacity in year 1.

### 3.2 Go-to-Market Shifts

- **Outreach becomes higher-stakes.** Every pilot must be a qualified, convert-ready engagement. Outbound that generates 20 pilot conversations with 7 conversions is very different from 20 pilot conversations with 4 conversions. Alizé must qualify harder on the front end.
- **Pilot scoping becomes critical.** The pilot must be scoped around a workflow the client *cannot easily absorb manually*. "Nice to have" workflows produce churn. Only "must have" workflows produce conversion.
- **Month 1 delivery becomes a sales event, not just a delivery event.** Every interaction during the pilot must be designed to increase conversion probability, not just demonstrate functionality.

### 3.3 Product/Delivery Implications

- **Louis's role in month 1 must include "conversion architecture"** — designing the pilot so the client becomes dependent by day 20, not just impressed at day 30.
- **Exit criteria for the pilot must include behavioral criteria**, not just technical criteria. "80% accuracy" is table stakes. "Client team no longer touches this workflow" is the conversion criteria.

## 4. Decision Recommendation

**Primary recommendation: Adopt Option C (Outcome Guarantee) or Option D (Load-Bearing Conversion Design) as the conversion mechanism.**

The specific recommendation is a hybrid:

### 4.1 Restructure the Pilot as a "Proven or Refunded" Engagement

- **€4,500 pilot fee** (non-refundable — Louis's time is real)
- **30-day monitoring period** where Louis runs the operation AND actively works to remove the client team from the workflow
- **Month 1 retainer at €1,200/month** — if the client is not satisfied at day 30 of the retainer, they receive a **€600 refund** (50% money-back guarantee for month 1 only)
- **After month 1: month-to-month, no commitment** (per D25 in TODO.md)

This mechanism:
- Shares downside risk without exposing Alizé to full refund
- Creates a clear "decision point" at month 1 with a forcing function (refund offer)
- Removes the awkward "do you want to continue?" conversation — it's replaced with "here's your month 1 summary, here's your €600 refund if you're not happy"
- Sets a conversion threshold of ~52% to be revenue-neutral on the guarantee vs. conversion lift

### 4.2 Add Explicit "Conversion Design" to Pilot Delivery

Every pilot delivery must answer: "By day 25, will the client feel pain if we stop?" If not, the pilot is scoped wrong. Louis should not just deliver the pilot — he must architect it for dependency, not just accuracy.

### 4.3 Qualify Pilots on Workflow Criticality

Do not accept pilots on "nice to have" workflows. The pilot must target a workflow the client genuinely struggles with and would notice losing. E-commerce order processing, support ticket triage, lead qualification — these are load-bearing. Invoice data extraction, scheduling, report generation — these are not.
