# Pulse 25 — Delivery Readiness Debate: Does Louis Need to Become Client-Facing?

**Date:** 2026-03-30  
**Agent:** Delivery Readiness Agent  
**Question:** Does Louis need to become client-facing before Alizé can launch — or should Alizé find a partner who fills that gap from day 1?

---

## CONTEXT SUMMARY

Alizé is a French managed AI agent service targeting PME/ETI (50-250 employees). Louis is:
- M1 master student
- Web developer intern at Grinto
- Co-founder of Kuroba web agency
- Co-founder of Alizé

**Key prior decisions creating today's tension:**
- **D90:** Warm outreach is BLOCKED until a "closing-capable person" exists
- **D113 (now challenged by D122):** Louis delivers pilot 1 solo
- **U117 (unresolved):** Can Louis run discovery calls, manage expectations, course-correct mid-engagement?
- **U91 (still unresolved):** What are Louis's actual weekly hours across all commitments?

The core debate: Is client-facing capability a prerequisite for Alizé to launch, or a skill Louis can develop, a partner can fill, or can be structurally avoided?

---

## ASSUMPTION 1: "Louis CAN Become Client-Facing with Self-Training"

### Arguments FOR This Assumption

**1. Louis's authenticity is a feature, not a bug.**

French PME buyers, especially in professional services, respond to founder-led sales. A 22-year-old founder who built the product is more credible than a polished sales rep who didn't. The "I'm building this for you" framing — honest about being early-stage — can work in Louis's favor with smaller PME buyers who value direct access over corporate formality.

**2. The first pilots are low-stakes learning opportunities.**

If pilots 1-2 are priced at €1,500-2,000 as CAC investments (D110), the bar for "success" is lower. The client expects imperfection. Louis can learn on the job without catastrophic consequences, especially if:
- The pilot is tightly scoped (single workflow, single integration, per D115)
- There is a senior commercial presence on file for objection handling
- The client has been pre-qualified as tolerant of a learning-stage vendor

**3. 4 weeks of focused training is enough for basic discovery.**

The gap is not "Louis needs to become a sales professional." The gap is "Louis needs to run a competent discovery call and not fumble the DG conversation." Basic discovery skills — open questions, qualifying criteria, pain mapping, scope definition — can be learned in 10-20 hours of structured practice. This is not a 6-month transformation. It's a 2-week sprint.

**4. Client-facing skills compound.**

Every discovery call Louis runs makes the next one better. Every objection he hears, he can prepare for next time. If Alizé's GTM is fundamentally network-first and Louis is the network-first outreach engine, his client-facing skills improve naturally through volume. Self-training + practice = capability.

---

### Arguments AGAINST This Assumption — The Real Challenge

**1. The time cost is non-trivial and unaccounted for.**

Louis is currently:
- M1 master student (coursework + exams)
- Web developer intern at Grinto (40+ hours/week likely)
- Co-founder of Kuroba web agency (active commitment)
- Co-founder of Alizé (this initiative)

U91 is still unresolved: we do not know how many hours Louis actually has per week. Until that number is known, any assumption about "Louis training himself" is speculation. If Louis has 8-10 hours/week for Alizé, client-facing skills development competes directly with delivery execution and technical building.

**2. Learning on real clients carries asymmetric risk.**

The first impression Alizé makes on its first pilots determines whether the next 3-5 prospects convert. If Louis fumbles the discovery call — can't answer "qui d'autre a déjà fait ça?", can't handle "c'est trop cher," can't navigate the "je vais devoir vendre ça à mon équipe" objection — the pilot doesn't get signed. The case study doesn't exist. The 3-6 month window (D82) starts closing with nothing to show.

**The math is unforgiving:**
- 10-15 warm conversations to generate 2-3 signed pilots at 15-20% close rate
- Louis needs maybe 30-40 discovery calls to get good
- At 15% close rate on early calls, that means 20-25 failed or fumbled conversations
- Each fumbled conversation is a burned warm lead — the highest-value asset Alizé has

**3. "Self-training" for DG-level conversations is a fiction.**

Louis has never run a discovery call with a DG of a 50-200 person French company. This is not something YouTube videos teach. The specific failure modes are:
- Collapsing at "qui a déjà fait ça?" (no answer possible as a new brand)
- Being outmaneuvered by "on va y réfléchir" (the French deflection that has no good response)
- Missing buying signals because he doesn't know what they look like
- Over-explaining the technical product when the DG cares about cost and risk
- Under-explaining when the DG asks a technical question and Louis defaults to his strength

These are not skills that transfer from web development. They require live practice with real decision-makers. The "learning on real clients" path means burning 5-10 warm leads to train Louis before he can close reliably.

**4. The "closing-capable" bar in D90 is not about basic skills.**

D90 doesn't say "Louis needs to be able to schedule a call." It says warm outreach is blocked until a "closing-capable person" exists. Closing capability means:
- Handling objections that kill deals
- Reading when a prospect is genuinely interested vs. being polite
- Moving from proposal to signature
- Knowing when to push and when to retreat
- Navigating the French DG's specific concerns about vendor lock-in, team adoption, and IT resistance

Louis cannot develop closing capability in 4 weeks of self-training. Closing is learned through doing, with real stakes, over months.

## ASSUMPTION 2: "Finding a Partner Is Better Than Louis Developing These Skills"

### Arguments FOR This Assumption

**1. Partner = immediate capability without the training investment.**

If a partner exists who can run discovery calls, handle objections, and close — Alizé doesn't need to spend 4-6 weeks training Louis. Louis stays technical (where his actual value is), and the partner handles commercial. This is a classic founder/operator split: builder and seller.

**2. The partner brings credibility that Louis doesn't have.**

A senior commercial person with a track record of selling services to French PME changes the dynamic entirely. Prospects respond differently to a 35-year-old consultant with 10 years of PME experience than to a 22-year-old M1 student. The partner's credibility compensates for Alizé's new-brand disadvantage.

**3. D90 is already pointing at this answer.**

D90 says warm outreach is blocked until a closing-capable person exists. If Louis cannot become that person in 4 weeks, and U91 reveals insufficient hours for self-development, the logical implication is: find the closing-capable person externally. D90 was written with this assumption in mind.

**4. Time is the scarcest resource in a 3-6 month window.**

If the effective window is 3-6 months (D82), every week spent on skills development is a week not spent on outreach, delivery, or product. The ROI on finding a partner is higher than the ROI on training Louis, because the partner generates revenue immediately while Louis's training generates capability only after weeks of practice.

---

### Arguments AGAINST This Assumption — The Real Challenge

**1. "Partner" is undefined and possibly unavailable.**

Who is this partner? The TODO list shows U86 (senior commercial co-founder) is unresolved. U78 (sales partner) is unresolved. There is no identified candidate. "Find a partner" is a plan that starts from zero and assumes a solution exists.

The French market for senior commercial people with AI workflow services experience is small. At Alizé's current stage (no revenue, early product, equity-only or below-market salary), attracting a senior closer is extremely difficult. This is not a plug-and-play solution — it's a major recruiting challenge that could take months.

**2. A partner has no Alizé-specific knowledge.**

A senior commercial person joining in Month 1 doesn't know:
- The product (it's still being built)
- The first workflow (D81 meeting reports, but this is still being validated)
- The delivery playbook (doesn't exist yet)
- The pricing structure (evolving)
- The competitive landscape (Microsoft/Copilot objection handling)

They can close, but they can't pitch without a product to pitch. The partner model assumes Alizé has enough defined to brief a closers. It doesn't yet.

**3. A partner creates a new dependency, not a solution.**

Louis is currently the bottleneck. A partner becomes a second bottleneck — and one Alizé has less visibility into. If the partner has other clients, their availability is not in Alizé's control. If the partner leaves after Month 3, Alizé is back to zero commercial capability with no internal skills developed.

**4. The 4-week question: what does a "closing-capable person" actually bring that Louis can't learn?**

Let's be specific about what "closing-capable" means for a €1,500-7,500 pilot sale to a French PME DG:

| Skill | What It Means | Can Louis Learn It in 4 Weeks? |
|-------|--------------|-------------------------------|
| Discovery call structure | Ask right questions, qualify properly | Yes — with coaching |
| Objection handling | "Too expensive," "IT won't like it," "who else?" | Partially — some, but not all |
| DG conversation management | Navigate up to DG level, handle their specific concerns | Partially — basic level |
| Closing language | Move from proposal to signature, handle delays | No — requires experience |
| Political navigation | Internal champions, blockers, decision-making process | No — requires PME context knowledge |
| Trust calibration | Know when a prospect is serious vs. exploratory | No — requires pattern recognition from many calls |

Louis can learn discovery call structure and basic objection handling in 4 weeks with a sales coach. He cannot learn closing language, political navigation, or trust calibration without doing it dozens of times.

**But here's the counter:** For the first 2-3 pilots (which are CAC investments at €1,500-2,000), the close probability matters less than the discovery quality. Louis can run discovery, get to a proposal stage, and a mentor/senior advisor can handle the close. This is a division of labor, not a full partner hire.

**5. The "find a partner" option may take longer than 4 weeks.**

If U86 (senior commercial co-founder) is unresolved, and no candidate exists today, the partner route could take 2-3 months to close. Meanwhile, Louis has 4 weeks of "client-facing training" to do anyway while waiting for the partner. The timelines may not be sequential — they may be parallel.

## ASSUMPTION 3: "Client-Facing Capability Is a Prerequisite"

### Arguments FOR This Assumption (It Is a Prerequisite)

**1. D90 explicitly makes it one.**

Warm outreach is blocked until a closing-capable person exists. This is a decided dependency — not a suggestion. Alizé has already decided that outreach cannot proceed without a commercial layer. Therefore client-facing capability IS a prerequisite by decision, not by assumption.

**2. You cannot run discovery calls without someone who can run them.**

If Louis cannot run a discovery call (U117), and no one else can, then discovery calls don't happen. If discovery calls don't happen, pilots don't get signed. If pilots don't get signed, there is no case study, no revenue, no Alizé. Therefore client-facing capability is, functionally, a prerequisite.

**3. U117 is unresolved for a reason.**

U117 asks directly: Can Louis run discovery calls, manage expectations, course-correct mid-engagement? If this were a clear yes, D90 wouldn't exist as a blocker. The unresolved status implies the answer is "not clearly yes." This suggests Louis's client-facing capability is a genuine gap, not a minor skills gap.

---

### Arguments AGAINST This Assumption — The Third Option

**1. "Prerequisite" assumes the wrong conversation structure.**

The current model assumes Alizé needs to run a standard commercial sales process: outreach → discovery call → proposal → close. This is the structure that requires client-facing capability as a prerequisite.

**But what if the first pilots don't use that structure?**

**Option A: "Research Partnership" Framing**

Frame the first 2-3 pilots as "research partnerships" with a different conversation structure:
- Louis reaches out: "I'm building this and I need real-world feedback. Would you let me automate one of your workflows for free in exchange for telling me what worked and what didn't?"
- No discovery call — the "discovery" happens by watching the client work
- No commercial close — the client opts in based on seeing the output
- No case study pressure — the first 2-3 are explicitly learning exercises

This sidesteps client-facing capability entirely. Louis doesn't need to run a discovery call if the client is buying based on seeing a working agent, not based on being sold to.

**This framing works if:**
- The first workflow (meeting report generation, D81) has a demo-able output
- Louis can show a working prototype before the "partnership" conversation
- The warm contact is motivated by curiosity/access, not just cost

**The challenge:** This only works for warm contacts who are intrinsically motivated by the product. It doesn't scale to cold outreach. And it requires Louis to have a working prototype before outreach — which may require technical development first.

**Option B: Hybrid Discovery (Louis + Mentor on Call)**

Louis runs the discovery call but has a senior mentor/coach on the line or immediately available:
- Louis schedules the call (his network, his relationship)
- Louis runs the first 15-20 minutes with coaching notes
- A senior advisor (not necessarily a formal partner) joins for the close
- Louis observes how the close is handled

This requires 1-2 hours from a senior person per call — not a full-time hire. If a mentor/advisor exists (former professor, Grinto founder, Kuroba co-founder, industry contact), this is a low-cost way to build Louis's skills while maintaining close quality.

**Option C: Lead with Demo, Not Pitch**

Louis doesn't pitch. He demos. He builds a working agent for the prospect's specific workflow and shows up with a demo. The conversation becomes "does this solve your problem?" not "should you buy this?" The discovery call transforms into a product evaluation.

This requires Louis to build the agent before the call, which:
- Only works for warm contacts with a known, specific workflow
- Requires Louis to have identified the workflow in advance (not from a discovery call)
- Only works for technically simple, demo-able workflows

Meeting report generation (D81) is actually ideal for this: it's demo-able in 5 minutes, the output is tangible, and the ROI is measurable. Louis could say: "I built a meeting report agent for my own use. Would it be useful for you?" The demo closes the conversation without a discovery call.

## THE UNRESOLVED VARIABLE: U91

U91 — Louis's actual weekly hours — is the forcing function that determines which path is viable.

**If Louis has 25+ hours/week for Alizé:**
- Path A (Louis trains) is viable — he can do skills development AND delivery

**If Louis has 15-20 hours/week for Alizé:**

- Louis cannot be the primary commercial engine
- Alizé is a side project, not a primary execution plan (D91 already flagged this)
- Either find a partner (Path B) or treat the window as Months 3-6 of Year 1

## POSITION: PATH C (REFAME) + PATH A (TRAIN PARALLEL)

### Recommendation

**Louis should not spend 4 weeks becoming client-facing before launch. He should reframe the first pilots as research partnerships and invest that 4 weeks in building a demo-able prototype instead.**

**Rationale:**

1. **D90's blocker is about "closing-capable," not about "discovery-capable."** If Louis can demo a working agent to a warm contact and the close happens because the prospect sees value — not because Louis pitched — the discovery call requirement is structurally sidestepped.

2. **The first workflow (meeting report generation, D81) is demo-able in 5 minutes.** This is the feature, not a bug. Louis builds a meeting report agent for himself first, shows it to a warm contact who has the same problem, and the conversation becomes "want one for your team?" not "should you buy this?"

3. **The 4 weeks of "training" is better spent building.** If Louis's actual skills are technical, and Alizé's first mover advantage is operational depth (D76/D78), then Louis's time is better allocated to building the best meeting report agent on the market than to learning how to run a discovery call.

4. **Louis can learn discovery in parallel without blocking outreach.** The two activities are not mutually exclusive:
   - Week 1-2: Louis builds meeting report agent, starts showing it to warm contacts (demo-led, not pitch-led)
   - Week 1-2: Louis also gets 2-3 hours of sales coaching on basic discovery
   - Week 3-4: Louis runs actual discovery calls with coaching notes, but only after he has something to show
   - By Month 2: Louis has both a working prototype AND basic discovery skills

5. **D122's "simulated delivery rehearsal" before live delivery is correct.** Louis should not cold-approach real clients without having practiced the delivery. But the rehearsal should be technical delivery (can he build a working agent?) not commercial delivery (can he pitch?). The commercial side should be prototype-led.

### What Louis Still Needs Regardless of Path

1. **U91 resolved this week.** No planning is valid without knowing his actual hours.
2. **U117 self-assessment.** Can he run a basic discovery call? If the answer is "no, and I can't prepare to in 2 weeks," a partner or mentor is needed.
3. **A working prototype before Month 1 outreach.** Demo-led conversation requires a demo to show.
4. **A senior mentor/advisor for closes.** Not necessarily a full partner. A 2-hour call commitment per week from someone with PME sales experience closes the gap without a full hire.

### What Louis Does NOT Need (Yet)

1. **Full sales training program.** 4 weeks of structured training is overkill for what the first pilots require.
2. **A co-founding commercial partner.** Recruiting a senior closer takes months and may not close the window gap.
3. **Client-facing capability as a prerequisite.** It can be a parallel track, not a blocking one.

## BOTTOM LINE

The question "Does Louis need to become client-facing?" is the wrong question to ask first. The right question is: **"What conversation structure allows Alizé to get first pilots without requiring Louis to be a fully-formed sales professional?"**

The answer is: **a demo-led, research-partnership framing for the first 2-3 pilots** — combined with parallel skills development and mentor support for closes.

Louis becoming client-facing is a valid long-term investment. It is not a prerequisite for Month 1 launch. The 4 weeks is better spent building the prototype that makes the demo possible.
