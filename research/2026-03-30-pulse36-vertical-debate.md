# Alizé Pulse 36 — Vertical Selection Debate

## Agent: Category and Positioning Auditor

---

### Assumption Challenged

**The assumption under review:** "Targeting web agencies via Grinto relationships is the right first vertical for Alizé."

This assumption rests on two supporting claims:
1. D135: "Go where Grinto relationships exist first"
2. D181: Expert-comptables vertical KILLED; web agencies/digital product companies confirmed as first vertical

Both claims need scrutiny. Neither has been stress-tested against the conflict embedded in Louis's own business interests.

### Key Findings

#### Finding 1: The Circular Conflict Is Fatal and Was Never Resolved

**The problem is explicit in the context:**

- Louis is **co-founder of a web agency** with Maëli and Gabin
- Louis is **intern at Grinto**, a Caen web agency
- D135 says: "Go where Grinto relationships exist first"
- D181 says: Web agencies are the confirmed first vertical

This creates a three-way collision:

| Entity | Relationship to Louis | What they do |
|--------|----------------------|--------------|
| Grinto (internship) | Employer | Web development |
| Maëli/Gabin web agency | Co-founder | Web development + AI services? |
| Alizé | Co-founder | AI agents for web agencies |

If Alizé sells AI agents to web agencies, it is simultaneously:
- **Competing with Louis's own co-founded agency** (if that agency also does AI integration work)
- **Serving Grinto's client relationships** (the same market Louis's agency competes in)
- **Training the market Louis's agency lives in** to buy from Alizé instead of building internally

The debate log explicitly flagged this. **U95** (web agency conflict) was marked unresolved when D100 was decided, and again when D181 was decided. U107 labeled the conflict "must resolve before delivery rehearsal proceeds." U173 says: "Resolve web agency vs. Alizé conflict — can Louis sell AI agents to web agencies when he's co-founded one?"

**This question has been listed as critical and unresolved for 14+ pulses.**

The assumption that "web agencies are the first vertical" cannot stand until someone answers: does Louis's web agency compete with Alizé's target clients? If yes, Alizé has a structural conflict of interest baked into its GTM from day one.

**The answer is almost certainly yes.** The web agency co-founded by Maëli and Gabin — which Louis co-founded — is almost certainly targeting the same web agency / digital product market that D181 says Alizé should pursue. Louis cannot credibly sell AI automation to companies he also sells web development to. The moment a web agency client of Louis's agency asks "can you add AI capabilities to our offering?", Louis faces a direct choice between Alizé's revenue and his agency's revenue.

#### Finding 2: Web Agencies Are Not an ICP for Managed AI Agents — They ARE the People Who Build These Tools

**The category problem:**

Alizé's managed AI agent service is designed for companies that:
- Lack internal technical capability to build AI integrations
- Need someone to design, deploy, and manage agents for them
- Want governance, security, and ongoing optimization

Web agencies are definitionally technical companies. They:
- Build digital products for a living
- Have developers or access to developers
- Work with APIs, webhooks, and automation tools
- Are exactly the profile that would build AI agent workflows using n8n, Zapier, Make.com, or direct API integrations

**The contradiction is structural:** Alizé's target customer is a company that *cannot or will not build AI tools themselves*. A web agency is a company that *does exactly this for their clients*. The moment a web agency needs an AI agent, their instinct is to build it — not to pay a managed service provider €2,000/month to do it for them.

Compare this to the original ICP from the BRIEF: "French PME/ETI 50-250 employees, already use 3+ business tools, have teams losing 2-5 hours/week on repetitive tasks, want to advance in AI but lack internal technical capability."

A 5-person web agency does not fit this description. They are technically capable. They don't lose 2-5 hours/week to repetitive tasks they can't automate — they automate tasks as a core competency.

**The web agency vertical was selected because of Louis's network, not because web agencies are an ICP match.**

#### Finding 3: D135 ("Go Where Grinto Relationships Exist") Creates a Navigation Paradox

D135 says: "Go where Grinto relationships exist first."

Grinto is a Caen web agency. Its relationships are:
- Companies that needed web development
- Likely e-commerce businesses, local businesses, other digital product companies

Following Grinto relationships means:
1. Approaching Grinto's former/current clients
2. Louis doing outreach to companies he knows through his internship at Grinto

The problem: Grinto is a competitor to Louis's co-founded web agency. If Louis approaches Grinto's client relationships to sell them AI agents, he is:
- Approaching companies that Grinto has an existing relationship with
- Potentially positioning Alizé as an alternative to services Louis's own agency offers
- Using his position at Grinto to generate leads for a company that competes with his own

This isn't "warm outreach." It's using an employer's business relationships to benefit a competing venture. The ethical and legal dimensions aside, practically speaking: the moment Grinto finds out, the internship relationship ends. And the moment those clients realize Louis was using Grinto connections to build a competing AI business, his reputation in the Caen digital community suffers.

**The D135 instruction is not network-first GTM. It is network-exploitation of a current employment relationship for competitive gain.**

#### Finding 4: The Vertical Has Changed Four Times — This Is Not Discovery, It's Flailing

The research log shows the following vertical history:
- **D10 (Pulse 2):** "ONE vertical, professional services first"
- **D44 (Pulse 11):** Professional services → expert-comptables (accounting firms)
- **D126 (Pulse 25):** Expert-comptables confirmed as first vertical (governance wedge)
- **D181 (Pulse 35):** Expert-comptables KILLED → web agencies confirmed

In 35 pulses, Alizé has been told to pursue:
1. Professional services (consulting firms)
2. Expert-comptables (accounting firms)
3. Web agencies / digital product companies

**What this pattern actually shows:**
- Each vertical selection was driven by whoever made the most compelling argument in a given pulse, not by validated network data
- U52 (Louis's network ICP map) has been listed as "critical gate" and "still unresolved" across 35+ consecutive pulses
- The vertical keeps changing because the foundational input (who Louis actually knows) was never collected

The BRIEF says Alizé targets "French PME/ETI 50-250 employees." The vertical selection process has been entirely decoupled from this ICP definition. Web agencies at 2-5 employees don't fit the ICP. Yet D181 confirmed them as the vertical.

**The real problem is not which vertical is correct. The problem is that vertical selection keeps happening before the prerequisite network mapping is completed.**

#### Finding 5: The Fundamental Assumption That Needs Revision

**The wrong assumption:** "Alizé should select its first vertical based on Louis's existing network relationships."

This assumption has driven every vertical decision — professional services, expert-comptables, and now web agencies — and all three were wrong or unvalidated for different reasons.

The correct framing is: **"Alizé should define its ICP precisely, then determine whether Louis's network can reach that ICP within a reasonable warm outreach window."**

These are different questions. The first question (what vertical should we pick?) assumes the network is the constraint. The second question (can Louis reach ICP-fit companies with his current network?) treats the ICP as fixed and the network as an input to measure.

The problem with picking verticals based on Louis's network:
1. Louis's network is narrow (web agency context, MyDigitalSchool, Grinto) and all points to the same segment
2. Selling to his own network means selling to people who know him as a student/intern, not as a vendor
3. The network-based approach produced three failed vertical selections in 35 pulses
4. It creates structural conflicts (web agency competes with Alizé's supposed target)

**The alternative:** Define Alizé's ICP without reference to Louis's network. Find the segment where Alizé's differentiation is strongest and the problem is most acute. Then ask: can Louis reach this segment? If yes, warm outreach. If no, cold outbound or partner channel.

This reframing doesn't guarantee success — but it prevents the circular logic of "we target web agencies because Louis knows web agencies, even though Louis co-founded a web agency and that creates a conflict."

---

### Verdict

**The assumption "targeting web agencies via Grinto relationships is the right first vertical" is wrong on three independent grounds:**

1. **Conflict of interest:** Louis co-founded a web agency. Selling AI agents to web agencies means competing with his own clients and his own company. This conflict is unresolved after 14+ pulses of listing it as critical.

2. **Category mismatch:** Web agencies are technically capable builders, not the ICP for managed AI agents. The BRIEF's ICP (50-250 employee companies lacking technical AI capability) does not describe a 5-person web agency.

3. **Navigation paradox:** D135 instructs Louis to leverage Grinto relationships. Grinto is his current employer. Using employer relationships to build a competing business is not a GTM strategy — it's a liability.

**Any one of these would be sufficient to kill the assumption. All three together make it untenable.**

---

### What Changed

The shift from expert-comptables (D126) to web agencies (D181) was presented as a correction based on network reality. It is actually a lateral move from one unvalidated vertical to another:

- Expert-comptables were chosen because governance gap makes Copilot insufficient (D126) — but D127 said governance gap is overstated for 70% of ICP
- Web agencies were chosen because Louis's network is in digital/tech (D181) — but Louis's network in digital/tech includes companies he co-founded and competes with

Neither vertical was validated against Louis's actual contact list (U52 remains unresolved). Neither vertical was validated against the BRIEF's ICP definition (50-250 employees, technical AI gap). The D181 decision replaced one assumption-based vertical with another.

### Recommendations for Louis

**1. Resolve the conflict of interest before choosing any vertical.**
Before D181's web agency decision is treated as final: can Louis's web agency (co-founded with Maëli and Gabin) be ring-fenced from Alizé? Are there contractual non-compete provisions? Is there a formal separation? Until this is resolved, any vertical Louis's network touches has a structural conflict. The U95/U107/U173 questions need answers, not more pulses listing them as unresolved.

**2. Actually complete U52 — the network map — before the next vertical decision.**
35 pulses of vertical debate without a network map is not rigorous process. It is avoidance. Louis needs to write down: 20 names, their companies, their size, their relationship to Louis, and whether they fit the ICP (50-250 employees, repetitive admin tasks, no internal AI capability). This takes one afternoon. It has been deferred for 35 pulses. Do it now.

**3. Redefine the ICP filter as a hard constraint, not a preference.**
The BRIEF says 50-250 employees. A 2-5 person web agency fails this filter. A 20-person consulting firm passes it. A 15-person accounting firm passes it. Louis's network may genuinely contain ICP-fit companies in different sectors — but the filter hasn't been applied consistently because U52 was never completed.

**4. If the network map reveals no ICP-fit companies in Louis's warm network, change the GTM, not the ICP.**
Warm outreach only works if the warm network fits the ICP. If Louis's 20 warmest contacts are all 2-10 person companies, the warm outreach strategy needs to be rebuilt around a different mechanism — not the vertical forced to fit the network anyway.

**5. Get a clear legal/contractual answer on Grinto before leveraging those relationships.**
The D135 instruction to "go where Grinto relationships exist" has ethical, legal, and reputational dimensions that the research pulses never addressed. If Louis uses Grinto's client relationships to build Alizé, what happens when Grinto finds out? The Caen digital community is small. The damage to Louis's professional reputation there could be permanent.
