# Pulse Debate: Alizé's Identity Crisis

## The Framed Assumption
"Alizé is a managed AI agent service — SaaS-like recurring revenue, platform leverage, scalable margins."

## The Challenge

**This is a consulting practice wearing a SaaS costume.**

Here's the math that exposes the truth:

### Year 1 Unit Economics

| Clients | Monthly Revenue | Annual Revenue | 1 Employee Cost | 2 Employee Cost | Annual Margin |
|---------|----------------|---------------|----------------|-----------------|---------------|
| **6** | €7,200 | €86,400 | ~€80K | ~€160K | **Negative** |
| **8** | €9,600 | €115,200 | ~€80K | ~€160K | **Thin/Negative** |
| **12** | €14,400 | €172,800 | ~€80K | ~€160K | **Viable** |

At 8 clients and €1,200/month, Louis is billing €115K/year. One French employee costs €60-80K all-in. Two employees cost €120-160K. **You cannot hire one employee at 8 clients. You cannot survive one bad month.**

### The Hybrid Reality

Alizé at 6-8 clients is:
- **NOT** a SaaS business (no product leverage, no scalable CAC, Louis is the product)
- **NOT** a traditional consulting firm (AI agents extend his capacity, differentiated tooling)
- **IS** a premium professional services firm with an AI augmentation layer

The €800-1,500/month is not SaaS ARR. It's **retainer billing** for high-touch service delivery. The "managed" prefix is accurate — but "managed service" implies SaaS infrastructure leverage. At 8 clients, Alizé has no infrastructure leverage. It has Louis and a contractor.

### The Network Exhaustion Problem

Louis's GTM is warm network-first. Here's the failure mode:
- Month 1: 3 clients from warm network → revenue €3,600-4,500
- Month 2: 0 new clients → revenue drops to €0 if any churn
- Month 3: Cold outbound kicks in → 333-500 touches for 1-2 discovery calls

**The business model is fragile.** Warm network is the fastest path to first clients, but it is not a durable pipeline. At month 3, Alizé faces a gap. The "SaaS" framing gives false comfort — this is a services business with a discovery problem.

### What to Call It

**"Premium AI operations partner."**

Not SaaS. Not consulting. "AI operations" signals: we run your AI, we take operational responsibility, this is ongoing. For fundraising: call it "AI services platform" (VCs hear platform multiples, not body-shop multiples). For recruiting: call it "AI delivery" (engineers want to build, not just consult).

### 3 Recommendations

1. **Don't raise VC on SaaS metrics at 8 clients.** Pitch as a pre-Series A services business: validated demand, premium pricing, network GTM. Target €200-400K pre-seed if raising at all — the numbers support it, the story doesn't support €2M at this stage.

2. **Plan for 6-client viability, not 12-client viability.** At 6 clients × €1,200/month, Alizé needs one contractor (€40-50K/year part-time) + Louis living on the setup fees (€3-6K per pilot). The 6-client model works if: (a) pilots convert reliably, (b) Louis is billing 60%+ of his time, (c) setup fees fund operations between retainer gaps.

3. **Build the platform roadmap into client contracts.** Every pilot should include: "Client agrees to Alizé using workflow patterns to build standardized agent templates." This converts consulting deliverables into product IP. At month 12, Alizé has 10 standardized workflows and a decision: build platform on proven patterns or keep delivering as premium service.

## Verdict

**Alizé is a premium professional services firm with AI augmentation, not a SaaS business.** The "managed AI agent service" positioning is correct for marketing — it differentiates from consultants and from generic SaaS. But internally, Louis must run it as a consulting practice: tight project scoping, high utilization discipline, retainer pipeline management. The SaaS framing becomes true only at 15+ clients with 2+ full-time delivery people. That's Month 18-24, not Month 6.

Stop dressing it up. The honest identity is faster to build, easier to fund at this stage, and clearer for hiring.
