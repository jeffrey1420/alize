# Debate Finding: First Outreach Message Architecture

**Date:** 2026-03-31
**Agent:** Outreach Message Architect
**Topic:** First warm outreach message for Louis (Alizé)

---

## The Three Assumptions — Challenged

### Assumption 1: "Problem-first framing is always correct"

**Challenged.**

Problem-first is the default advice for cold outreach, where the contact owes you nothing. A warm contact already has context: they know Louis, they know his background (Kuroba co-founder, Grinto network), and they likely have some awareness of what he's building.

For warm contacts, the relationship IS the opener. The correct sequence is:

1. **Relationship hook** — acknowledge the existing connection
2. **Observation** — name something specific to their business situation
3. **Offer** — what Alizé does and why it's relevant to them
4. **Social proof** — Kuroba as evidence, not promises
5. **CTA** — specific, low-friction

Problem-first to a warm contact signals that Louis is using a template, not thinking about them. The risk of problem-first with warm contacts: it can feel extractive ("I see your problem, let me sell you something") rather than connective.

**Decision: Reject problem-first for warm outreach. Replace with relationship-first framing.**

---

### Assumption 2: "Never mention price in first outreach"

**Partially challenged — with an important distinction.**

The "never mention price" rule was designed to prevent two things: (1) price being the only filter that eliminates interest, and (2) price being quoted without value context. Both are valid concerns for cold outreach.

For warm outreach, however, the calculus is different:

- **False expectations are MORE damaging with warm contacts** — they have Louis's personal phone number and will call him directly when the real number arrives.
- **Price transparency is a filter, not a deterrent** — it eliminates time-wasters and qualifies serious buyers before Louis spends an hour on discovery.
- **D198 already embedded pricing in the demo** (minute 5-8), which means the pricing conversation happens in the first call anyway. Hiding it before the first message just delays qualification.

The correct approach: **anchor the value, name the price range, qualify on the call.** Don't bury the number, but don't present it without context either.

**Decision: Partially reject — mention pricing directionally in first outreach ("à partir de X€"), not as a full menu.**

---

### Assumption 3: "Case studies are required before cold outreach"

**Rejected.**

This assumption has been tested repeatedly in the debate log and found to be a perfectionist's trap. Key evidence:

- D183: First pilots framed as "research partnerships" — zero case study requirement
- D186: "First 1-2 pilots are free learning investments" — proof is built through delivery, not before outreach
- D195: Kuroba named as Louis's strongest available social proof — a live internal deployment beats an anonymized case study
- D197: Louis sends 5 LinkedIn messages "today" — no deck, no landing page, no case study required
- D202: "Expert-comptables vertical — needs reassessment" — vertical emerges from evidence, not before outreach

Louis's legitimate proof points for first outreach (zero commercial pilots):

1. **Kuroba internal deployment** — "I built it for myself first" is the strongest available credibility signal. It's named, real, and demonstrable.
2. **Louis's practitioner credibility** — 40+ pulses of AI agent research and building. He is not a rep. He is the builder.
3. **Grinto connection** — the network context gives warm contacts a reference frame without requiring case study evidence.

The real question isn't "do we have enough proof?" It's "do we have enough honesty about what we have?"

**Decision: Reject — case studies are not required. Louis has Kuroba. Use it.**

## The Message

**To:** Warm contact (French business, 20-200 employees, service operations context)
**Channel:** LinkedIn DM or email
**Language:** French
**Word count:** ~120 words

---

> **[Prénom],**
>
> J'espère que tu vas bien. Je reviens vers toi parce que tu connais le contexte — et j'aimerais ton avis brut sur quelque chose que je construis avec Alizé.
>
> On construit des agents IA qui gèrent les tâches récurrentes de A à Z — pas des démos, pas des chatbots, des agents qui travaillent vraiment dans tes outils (email, CRM, support, docs). Pas de configuration, pas de prompt à écrire. L'agent fait le travail, tu supervises.
>
> Je l'ai d'abord déployé chez Kuroba — mon agence web — sur le traitement des demandes client. Ça fonctionne.
>
> Prix : à partir de 1 500€/mois pour un agent en production. Pilot gratuit pour les 2 premiers si le fit est là.
>
> Tu connais quelqu'un en service (cabinet conseil, finance, ops) qui perd du temps sur des tâches récurrentes ?
>
> **Louis**

## What This Message Achieves

| Element | Purpose |
|---------|---------|
| "Tu connais le contexte" | Warm relationship acknowledgment — not cold |
| "J'aimerais ton avis brut" | Invites input, not a sales pitch |
| "Pas des démos, pas des chatbots" | Pre-empts the default objection |
| Kuroba named | Real proof point — live deployment, not promises |
| "Ça fonctionne" | Three words, not a case study |
| Price range stated | Qualifies serious buyers, eliminates time-wasters |
| "Pilot gratuit pour les 2 premiers" | Removes price risk for early adopters |
| Referral CTA | Low-friction ask that doesn't require commitment |

---

## Proof Points Louis Can Legitimately Claim

Given **zero commercial pilots**:

1. **Kuroba internal deployment** — Named, real, working. Louis uses Alizé's own product. "We ate our own cooking" (D194/D195). This is stronger than an anonymized case study because it's verifiable.
2. **Louis as practitioner-builder** — Not a sales rep. The person who built the system is the one pitching it. This is credible at the warm network level.
3. **Specific workflow claim** — "traitement des demandes client" is specific enough to be credible, general enough to apply across verticals.

What Louis **cannot** claim (yet):
- Number of clients served
- Specific ROI metrics from commercial deployments
- Named enterprise references

---

## CTA Rationale

**"Tu connais quelqu'un qui…"** is a referral ask, not a close ask. It's the correct CTA for first warm outreach because:

- It doesn't require the contact to commit personally
- It converts warm contact into a channel (D173: warm referrals only AFTER delivery — but asking for referrals before delivery is still valid outreach, not a close)
- It respects the relationship — it's an ask for help, not a demand to buy
- It qualifies the contact's network without requiring the contact to self-qualify

**When to escalate to a direct ask:** After the contact responds positively to the referral question. Then Louis can say: "Et toi — est-ce qu'il y a un process dans ton entreprise qui te prend trop de temps ?"
