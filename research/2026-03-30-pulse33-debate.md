## Agent 1: Delivery Architect

**Assumption challenged:** "Alizé needs a delivery partner or contractor before the first pilot can be scoped/delivered." (D16)

**Verdict: Premature structural commitment. Louis delivers. Contractor enters with leverage.**

### Key Findings

**1. The delivery partner is framed as a precondition, but it's actually a deferral.**

D16 says "Implementation contractor needed by Month 1-2." But D23 says "Louis delivers Month 1 pilot manually + documents." These contradict. If Louis delivers Month 1 manually, the delivery partner is not needed for the first pilot — only for the second. The assumption conflated "first pilot delivery" with "scaling delivery to client 2."

**2. The delivery partner was designed for the wrong pilot.**

The delivery partner concern assumed complex, bespoke workflows (professional services firms). If the first pilot targets a digital agency with standardized marketing workflows, Louis can configure and run it solo. Standardized APIs (Hootsuite, SEMrush, Google Analytics) don't require a delivery specialist.

**3. The delivery partner search should NOT block outreach.**

The delivery contractor should join AFTER the first pilot is scoped — with a documented playbook, real client data, and a real scope. Contracting a delivery partner with an empty brief (no pilot yet, no documented workflow) is hiring someone to figure out the job. That's expensive and slow.

**Concrete revision to D16:**
- **Louis delivers Pilot 1 solo**
- **Louis + delivery contractor execute Pilot 2 from documented playbook**
- Delivery partner search starts in parallel with Pilot 1 scoping, not as a blocker

## Agent 2: GTM Auditor

**Assumption challenged:** "Warm network outreach is the right primary GTM channel for Alizé's first pilots." (D26)

**Verdict: Warm ≠ easy. The "warm network" plan is a planning artifact, not a sales motion.**

### Key Findings

**1. D26 conflates reply rate with close rate.**

Warm outreach gets 40-60% reply rates (real). But reply rate ≠ conversion rate. D26 assumed 30-50% close rates on warm calls — but those figures assume a competent sales person. Louis is not a sales person. He is an intern. Pulse 17 already flagged: "Louis should NOT solo-close warm network deals at €7.5k — too many conflicts." Warm contacts know Louis works at Grinto. The moment a prospect asks "is this Grinto or Alizé?" Louis is in a credibility hole.

**2. Louis's network probably doesn't match the ICP.**

D44 selected professional services (consulting firms) as first vertical. Pulse 23 flagged that Louis's network skews digital/e-commerce. These are different buyer types. Warm outreach efficiency drops when the contact doesn't self-identify as having "operational process problems." U28 (network ICP mapping) was never resolved.

**3. Zero execution is behavioral evidence.**

Louis has sent zero outreach messages after 32 pulses. This is not a scheduling problem. Either the GTM motion is overwhelming, or Louis has looked at his warm network and concluded it's not viable. Either way: 32 pulses without a single message sent is planning paralysis, not strategy.

**4. "Month 2+ cold outbound" has no case studies to support it.**

D26 says cold outbound activates Month 2+. But Alizé has no case studies, no brand, no proof points. Cold outbound for an unknown brand = 2-3% reply rate. That's not a strategy — it's a hope.

**The actual viable GTM motion given Louis's constraints:**
- **Phase 1:** Productized fixed-fee projects (€1,500 flat, 2-week delivery, specific workflow). Removes the trust barrier. Generates revenue AND case study simultaneously.
- **Phase 2:** Referral-led after delivery. Ask each client for ONE intro to someone with the same problem. Client-vouched intros = 60-80% reply, 30-40% close. This is the real "warm network" play.
- **Phase 3:** Cold outbound activates only after 3+ case studies exist.

**2-Week Sprint Louis could run:**
- Week 1: Define ONE productized offer (specific workflow, €1,500 flat, 2-week delivery). Build a simple portfolio page. Find 20 ICP-fit companies. Send 20 emails.
- Week 2: Follow up. If ONE person says yes — deliver. Document the outcome. Ask for ONE referral.

## Agent 3: Pricing Architect

**Assumption challenged:** "Alizé should charge a pilot fee + monthly retainer." (structural assumption across D1-D80+)

**Verdict: Wrong model. The pricing ARCHITECTURE is broken, not just the numbers.**

### Key Findings

**1. The pilot+retainer structure demands trust Louis hasn't earned.**

Louis asks buyers to pay €3,000-6,000 upfront for an unproven deliverable from an unknown vendor, pitched by an intern, with no case studies. Buyers will calculate: "What happens if the agent doesn't actually work? I still owe him €800/month?" The pilot fee is not a trust-builder — it's a trust-tax.

**2. Louis is pricing below cost and doesn't know it.**

From Pulse 31 unit economics: pilot delivery costs €4,600-7,350. Louis's proposed pilot fee: €3,000-6,000. Every pilot is structurally a loss before the retainer even starts. The BRIEF.md pricing (€3,000-6,000 pilot, €800-1,500/month) is internally convenient, not market-validated.

**3. The variable is not price — it's proof.**

If Louis can show a working agent doing a real task: €490 diagnostic = easy yes, €1,500/month = easy yes. If Louis cannot show a working agent: no price will close the deal. The current pricing model treats price as the variable. It should be: what does the buyer need to SEE before they pay?

**4. Alternative models evaluated:**

| Model | Pros | Cons | Verdict |
|-------|------|------|---------|
| Time-and-materials (day rate) | Aligns effort with payment | Unpredictable revenue; Louis bottleneck | Not Alizé |
| Success fee (% of savings) | Deeply aligned incentives | Defining "savings" is a minefield; French buyers want predictable costs | Theoretically elegant, operationally broken |
| Flat consulting day-rate retainer | Simple mental model | Louis capacity is the real constraint | Partial improvement |
| Free pilot + success-based retainer | Removes trust barrier | Louis works for free; conversion rate unknown | Better alignment, still risky |
| Kill pilot fee + raise retainer | Higher conversion | Math still doesn't work unless capacity scales | Structural rethink needed |

**Recommended pricing architecture (revised):**
- **Diagnostic:** €490 (entry point, low-risk)
- **Pilot:** FREE (Louis proves he can deliver; structural pilot loss accepted as CAC)
- **Month 1-3:** €1,200-1,500/month (proof period)
- **Month 4+:** €1,800-2,500/month (proven value, price increases)

**The real task:** Build one agent that demonstrably works, for one client, on one real workflow, and document the results. Everything else is noise.
