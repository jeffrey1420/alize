## Intention-to-Decide vs Actual Decision

The debate log has been treating "I want to do Alizé" as evidence of a decision. It is not. It is evidence of an intention to decide.

**Three states that get conflated:**

**Predecision**: "I am considering X. I have not committed to X. My future options remain open."
- Evidence: Expresses interest, does research, generates documents, attends meetings about X
- What it does NOT do: Constrains future options, allocates resources, closes pathways

**Decision (commitment)**: "I have chosen X. I have taken an action that makes Y harder or impossible. My future options are now constrained."
- Evidence: Resource allocation, legal commitments, domain registration, first paying customer, team contracts signed
- What it DOES do: Raises the cost of reversing course, creates external observers whose expectations must be managed

**Decision without action**: "I have decided X but have not yet executed." This is a genuine state — someone who has booked a flight has decided to travel even if they haven't boarded. But the flight must be booked. The domain must be registered. The entity must be formed.

**Louis's behavior with Alizé (predecision indicators):**
- Says "I want to do Alizé" — predecision
- Generates research documents — predecision behavior
- Attends 64 pulses of research discussion — predecision behavior
- Has not bought the domain — predecision indicator
- Has not confirmed legal entity — predecision indicator
- Has not sent a single external outreach message — predecision indicator
- Has not built a single AI agent for Alizé — predecision indicator

**Louis's behavior with Kuroba (actual decision indicators):**
- Co-founded Kuroba — creates legal and financial commitment, raises cost of exit
- Domain purchased — kuroba.studio exists
- Built the business with Maëli and Gabin — real resource allocation
- Working at Grinto while running Kuroba — actual tri-manage, not aspirational
- Has external clients — market-validated decision

**The critical difference**: Louis made the Kuroba decision. He is still in predecision about Alizé. The debate log's 260+ decisions are the evidence of predecision — the work of intending to decide, not the work of having decided.

**The social commitment hypothesis**: Louis may have made informal commitments — to Maëli, to Gabin, to himself — that feel like decisions but aren't. "I want to do Alizé" said in the presence of co-founders generates an informal obligation that raises the psychological cost of saying "I decided not to." This creates a third state: commitment without decision. Louis has told people he wants to do Alizé, which makes it harder to walk away, but has not taken the actions that would make walking away costly. He is simultaneously committed (can't easily exit) and uncommitted (hasn't invested). This is the most uncomfortable possible position — obligation without agency.
