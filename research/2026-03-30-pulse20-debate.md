## Debate 1: Louis's Credibility Ceiling — Is His Profile a Structural Blocker?

**Agent:** Louis Credibility Strategist

### Core Question
Is Louis's profile (M1 student, Grinto intern, web agency co-founder) a fundamental ceiling for selling €7,500-10,000 AI agent pilots to French DGs and COOs?

### Verdict: Louis's Profile is a Ceiling Unless a Senior Commercial Layer Exists

**Case FOR ceiling (structural):**
- The DG credibility problem is structural, not tactical. A 23-year-old M1 student cannot credibly claim peer-level operational context with 45-year-old DGs who have run companies through economic cycles. The Grinto internship and web agency co-founder role are real but not senior. Adding a sales partner creates a two-person credibility problem — Louis has no seniority, the sales person has no product authority.
- The €7,500-10,000 price point triggers formal procurement territory. Social proof becomes mandatory. Social proof comes from credentials the DG recognizes — and Louis's credentials don't yet register.
- Grinto conflict compounds: any DG who Googles Louis sees "Grinto intern" next to "Alizé AI agents" and immediately asks questions about overlap and conflict.

**Case AGAINST ceiling (conditional):**
- Louis's profile fails when he *performs seniority he doesn't have*. It works when he's positioned as: "I'm the technical architect who built this system. Here's how it works and why it solves your problem."
- Young technical founders are credible in French tech. The startup nation narrative is alive — VCs fund 25-year-olds routinely. The comparison class matters: Louis doesn't need to be credible as a business operator, just as someone who understands AI agent deployment deeply.
- The web agency co-founder credential is evidence of client delivery, scope management, and accountability — skills that translate to the DG's operational reality.

**The stronger argument:** Case 2 wins conditionally — but the condition is unmet. Louis needs a named senior commercial co-founder or advisory board member with industry credibility who can be the primary relationship owner. This isn't a "sales hire." It's a co-founder with network and reputation. Without this person, Louis's profile caps Alizé's deal size at €2,000-3,000, not €7,500+.

### Challenge to Existing Research
- **D74** bundles three problems as equivalent (skills + format + conflict). The Grinto conflict is structural and underaddressed. Even with perfect sales skills, Louis's credential gap is structural.
- **U75** asks "technical or also sales?" but doesn't ask: can Louis's profile support DG-level trust regardless of support? Answer: no, not without a senior commercial layer.
- **D82's "3-6 month window"** is framed as capacity constraint. It should be framed as a credibility window. After 3-6 months of solo-close failures, the network categorizes Alizé as "young founders who can't close" — and that narrative is sticky.

### Recommendation
1. Louis is technical architect, never closer, never solo in a closing meeting
2. A named senior commercial co-founder/advisor with industry credibility is a prerequisite for €7,500+ deals — not a "nice to have"
3. Resolve Grinto conflict publicly before any warm outreach — exit the internship or explicitly address the ring-fencing
4. If senior commercial layer isn't secured by month 3: pivot to €2,000-3,000 pilots where credibility matters less
5. The 3-6 month window is a credibility window, not a capacity window

## Debate 2: Technical Moat — Build NOW or Delivery First?

**Agent:** Technical Moat Strategist

### Core Question
Should Alizé invest in building proprietary technical assets NOW (custom MCP connectors, workflow templates, benchmarking data) or focus entirely on client delivery, treating technical differentiation as secondary?

### Verdict: Build Technical Moat NOW — But Embedded in Delivery, Not Separate

**Case FOR technical moat NOW:**
- D83 identifies switching-cost architecture and benchmark metrics as the moat. D27 says defer infrastructure to Month 12+. These are in direct contradiction. You cannot build switching costs into clients retroactively — they must be embedded from day one.
- D81's meeting report generation is a good pilot beachhead but has zero IP ceiling. Zapier, Make.com, n8n all have this. The moat must be the SECOND workflow — one that produces proprietary data that compounds.
- "Client depth alone is a moat" (D78) is wrong without technical infrastructure. Client depth without a system is a dependency on one person (Louis). When he burns out in Month 6, what exists? Client relationships in his head.
- The "do delivery now, build IP later" position has no natural endpoint. Revenue pressure is always immediate. Technical investment is always deferred.

**Case AGAINST technical moat NOW:**
- Louis cannot do everything. Every hour writing code is an hour not closing. Speed to revenue is existential in a 3-6 month window.
- "IP from delivery" has historical precedent: Basecamp, Shopify, Mailchimp all started manually before building products. The discovery-through-delivery model is valid.
- Building MCP connectors before knowing what clients need is premature optimization. The service-first approach (D27) surfaces what actually needs building.

**The stronger argument:** Both cases are partially right. The resolution: build technical assets that simultaneously accelerate delivery AND create compounding defensibility. The delivery playbook IS the technical asset. Benchmark infrastructure must exist from the first deployment. Switching-cost architecture is a deployment scope decision, not a Month 12 decision.

### Challenge to Existing Research
- **D83 and D27 are contradictory.** D83 says switching-cost architecture + benchmark metrics are the moat. D27 says defer all infrastructure to Month 12+. You cannot build retroactive switching costs.
- **D81's beachhead assumption.** Meeting reports have zero proprietary data compounding. A better beachhead: invoice processing → cost基准 data; recruitment → compensation benchmarking. Neither is as easy to explain but both produce proprietary assets.
- **"Technical depth vs client depth" is a false dichotomy.** They are the same work if structured correctly.

### Recommendation
1. The delivery playbook IS the technical asset — structured documentation built during delivery, not after
2. Build benchmark infrastructure from Day 1 (not Month 12) — retroactive benchmarking is useless
3. The second workflow (after meeting reports) should have data compounding potential
4. Switching-cost architecture is a first-pilot scope decision — integration depth, data ownership, workflow ownership defined at deployment
5. Do fewer pilots at higher quality rather than more pilots with accumulated technical debt
6. Do NOT build a parallel MCP connector library or "platform" before clients exist — D27 is correct on this

## Debate 3: Vertical Commitment — Pick NOW or Let Evidence Decide?

**Agent:** Vertical Strategy Challenger

### Core Question
Should Alizé commit to a specific vertical (professional services, D44) in Month 1, or stay horizontal and let vertical emerge from delivery evidence?

### Verdict: Kill D44/D10 as Month 1 Commitments — Vertical Should Emerge from Evidence

**Case AGAINST vertical commitment NOW:**
- D44 ("professional services first") and D10 ("ONE vertical, professional services first") are leap-of-faith decisions treated as conclusions. No pilot has been delivered. No client feedback exists. No conversion data.
- Professional services is the hardest vertical to sell into for an unknown brand. Consulting firm DGs are sophisticated, skeptical, and evaluate every technology trend through the lens of "does this threaten my billing model?" AI that removes admin tasks may reduce billable hours — the opposite of what a consulting firm's economics want.
- The "pick one vertical" SaaS playbook doesn't apply to a services company. SaaS vertical flywheels require low marginal cost per additional customer. Alizé's marginal cost is high (delivery hours). Vertical concentration doesn't reduce costs — it concentrates the risk of picking wrong.

**Case FOR vertical commitment:**
- Some structure is needed for coherent messaging and efficient GTM. "AI agents for back-office admin" without any vertical constraint is too generic.
- Professional services DGs are often early adopters of productivity tools because they're sensitive to time cost.
- Louis's warm network may genuinely cluster around consulting and professional services.

**The stronger argument:** Case against is stronger for Month 1. The vertical should follow the network ICP mapping (U52), not precede it. If Louis's warm network is e-commerce and logistics, D44 misdirects the entire GTM plan.

### Challenge to Existing Research
- **D44 and D10** assume "professional services" without defining what evidence would disprove it. That's narrative, not strategy.
- **At 6-8 clients in Year 1**, Alizé has no meaningful vertical depth regardless of which vertical it picks. 2-3 clients per vertical is enough for pattern recognition, not vertical expertise. The "vertical depth creates moat" argument (D78) requires 5+ clients per vertical — unachievable in Year 1.
- **D77's "service firms" constraint** is unnecessary narrowing. "Back-office admin AI" applies to any sector with meetings, email, and documents.

### Recommendation
1. Kill D44 and D10 as Month 1 commitments — they are hypotheses
2. Define vertical AFTER mapping Louis's warm network against ICP criteria (U52)
3. Use "back-office admin AI" WITHOUT the "service firms" constraint — let sector emerge from evidence
4. First pilot = workflow commitment (meeting reports, D81), not a vertical commitment
5. Vertical emerges from the third or fourth pilot once network evidence exists

## Cross-Cutting Theme

All three debates converge on the same structural problem: **the research has been making permanent decisions from zero empirical evidence.**

- Louis's credibility ceiling exists but the research hasn't treated it as a prerequisite
- Technical moat must be built into delivery from day one but the research has deferred it to Month 12+
- Vertical commitment is treated as settled but it should be treated as an open question until U52 is resolved

**The meta-finding:** Alizé's strategy is still in hypothesis mode. The decisions that have been marked "resolved" (D44, D10, D74, D83, D27) are actually still open until three things happen: (1) Louis's network is mapped, (2) a senior commercial co-founder is secured, (3) the first pilot delivers. Until then, the "resolved" decisions should be treated as working assumptions, not commitments.
