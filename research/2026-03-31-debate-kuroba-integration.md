## The Case for Independence

**What independence preserves:**

1. **Alizé's positioning is distinct from Kuroba's.** Kuroba is a web agency (websites, design). Alizé is an AI agent service for business workflows. Conflating them risks diluting both brands — prospects who want a website don't need AI agents, and AI agent buyers who want operational depth don't want a web agency's overhead.

2. **The pricing and business model are fundamentally different.** Kuroba sells deliverable projects (a website). Alizé sells recurring managed services (AI agents running continuously). These require different sales motions, different metrics, different delivery teams. Mixing them creates organizational schizophrenia.

3. **Alizé's intellectual infrastructure is substantial.** 290+ decisions, a full BRIEF.md, competitive positioning, pricing architecture. This represents real work. Folding it into Kuroba means either abandoning that infrastructure or forcing it into a context where it doesn't naturally fit.

4. **The 12-month upside of independence.** If Alizé works — real pilots, real MRR, real case studies — it becomes a standalone business worth significantly more than a service offering within a web agency. The upside of independence is a real company. The upside of folding in is a service line.

## The Case Against Independence

**The structural argument (from Strategic Exit Auditor, Pulse 76):**

1. **Louis doesn't have the capacity to run two ventures.** Alizé requires 25+ hours/week (per D91's aspirational threshold). Louis is an M1 student with a Grinto internship and co-founder responsibilities at Kuroba. The evidence across 75+ pulses shows Louis cannot execute Alizé as designed. Kuroba, with the same constraints, succeeded because it has Maëli + Gabin. Alizé has Louis alone. The variable is team capacity, not market opportunity.

2. **Alizé's addressable market requires a team Louis doesn't have.** The brief targets 50-200 employee French PME. These companies buy from established firms with references, case studies, and a commercial presence Louis cannot manufacture alone in Month 1. Kuroba has an existing client base and a team that can execute. Alizé has a research apparatus and no one to deliver.

3. **290 decisions is a sunk cost.** The intellectual infrastructure has no market value until Alizé produces revenue. As a service offering within Kuroba, that infrastructure becomes immediately useful — it informs how to position AI services, what to charge, what workflows to automate. As a standalone venture, it's an accumulation of unresolved research with no external validation.

4. **The domain problem is structural, not logistical.** alize.studio has not been bought after 75+ pulses. This is not a matter of priority — it's a symptom of Louis not having committed to Alizé as a real venture. Buying a domain requires deciding the venture is real. Louis hasn't decided. Kuroba's domain was bought immediately.

## Recommended Path Forward

**Kill Alizé as an independent entity. Fold AI agent services into Kuroba as a named service offering.**

This does not mean abandon the Alizé positioning. It means:

1. **Kuroba launches "AI Workflow Automation" as a service line** — using Alizé's intellectual infrastructure (positioning, pricing, workflows) but under Kuroba's brand, with Kuroba's team
2. **Maëli + Gabin become the delivery team** — Louis brings the AI agent knowledge; Maëli/Gabin bring client relationships and execution capacity
3. **Alizé's research becomes Kuroba's AI practice blueprint** — 290 decisions inform how Kuroba sells and delivers AI services, without requiring Louis to run a standalone venture
4. **Alizé domain is not purchased** — if the venture isn't independent, the domain isn't needed
5. **Louis's role becomes AI specialist/technical lead within Kuroba** — not CEO of a solo venture
