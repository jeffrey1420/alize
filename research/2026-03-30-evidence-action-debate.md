# Evidence vs Action Debate — Alizé Pulse 49

## Core Question
Is "proof before action" the right sequencing for Alizé, or is this framing itself the obstacle?

---

## Assumption Challenged
**D175 / D227 "Proof before pricing"** — The accumulated research positions "build evidence first, then sell" as the correct sequence. This assumption is wrong for a solo founder with no capital, no revenue, and 48 pulses of research already conducted.

---

## Argument For Evidence-First

- Alizé has zero paying clients. Pricing, positioning, and vertical decisions were all made in a vacuum.
- D248: "zero attempts made" to validate anything commercially.
- Entering a sales conversation without proof of delivery is credibly risky (D84).
- A working Kuroba deployment (D194) would make demo-led motion credible (D182).
- French PME buyers are conservative. Named case studies and specific metrics are required to close at €2,000+/month.

**Verdict:** Evidence-first is theoretically correct for a well-capitalized startup with time.

---

## Argument Against Evidence-First

**Research as procrastination is the real failure mode.**

1. **48 pulses, zero action.** The evidence-first doctrine has produced 80+ research documents and zero commercial activity. At what point does "we need more evidence" become "we're afraid to act"?

2. **The evidence doesn't exist yet because no one has tried.** You cannot generate proof-of-delivery without doing a delivery. The correct sequence is not "proof then action" — it's "action generates proof."

3. **Louis has no capital runway.** A well-funded startup can spend 3 months building proof. Louis cannot. The evidence-first framing assumes resources that don't exist.

4. **"Proof before pricing" paralyzes rather than enables.** D175 says build one working agent first, then price. But Louis has been building research documents instead of building agents. The doctrine creates infinite deferral.

5. **The first client IS the evidence.** The first pilot doesn't just generate revenue — it generates the case study, the testimonial, the demo material, the pricing validation. You cannot separate "getting evidence" from "getting the first client."

6. **A bad pilot is better than a perfect research document.** Even a failed first engagement generates real data: what the buyer rejected, what they valued, what they'd pay for. No research document generates that.

---

## Recommendation

**Kill "evidence-first" as a sequencing doctrine. Replace with "minimum viable evidence":**

- One LinkedIn message sent = minimum viable evidence that outreach is possible
- One discovery call = minimum viable evidence that the problem framing resonates
- One working demo = minimum viable evidence that the technology works
- One paying client = minimum viable evidence that the model is viable

**The right question is not "do we have enough evidence to act?" but "what is the smallest action that generates the most useful evidence?"**

For Alizé right now, that means:
1. Send one message today (not five, not a network map — one)
2. Have one real conversation this week
3. Build the simplest possible working demo (not a platform — one agent doing one task)

48 pulses of research have produced diminishing returns. The 49th pulse should produce one action, not one more document.
