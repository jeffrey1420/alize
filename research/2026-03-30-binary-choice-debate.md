# Binary Choice Debate: Challenging the "Startup vs. Side-Project" Framing

**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Agent:** Binary Choice Agent
**Purpose:** Challenge the assumption that Louis must make a binary choice between "startup" and "side-project" before Alizé can move forward

---

## Introduction

After 33 research pulses generating 175+ decisions, the process arrived at D169: *"Louis's binary choice (startup vs side-project) unblocks all other actions."* U161 was elevated to the status of the single most critical blocker: *"Louis must write it down and date it."*

This analysis challenges that framing. The binary choice assumption is itself a form of decision avoidance — dressed up as decisive action. Three specific assumptions within this framing deserve scrutiny before Louis commits to a label that may solve nothing and foreclose options unnecessarily.

---

## Assumption 1: The Binary Choice Is Real, Not a False Dilemma

### The Assumption
Louis must choose between "startup" (full commitment, incorporated company, funding ambition, growth orientation) and "side-project" (hobby economics, part-time hours, no formal structure). These are treated as mutually exclusive categories with fundamentally different action implications.

### Evidence from the Debate Log
The debate log itself provides the evidence against this framing. Consider what Louis's actual situation looks like:

- **M1 student** (master's degree, ongoing coursework obligations)
- **Internship at Grinto** (with documented conflicts of interest — Grinto competes with Alizé target clients per D74, D87)
- **Web agency co-founder** (with Maëli and Gabin — a separate business with its own commitments)
- **56-62 hours/week** of available time after these three obligations (Pulse 21 capacity analysis)
- **Month 1 demand of 160-245 hours** for Alizé execution

The capacity analysis in Pulse 21 concluded: *"Louis has ~56-62 hrs/week after M1 + Grinto + web agency; Month 1 demand is 160-245 hrs; plan is physically impossible."*

The binary framing — "declare yourself a startup or a side-project" — pretends the problem is a labeling decision. It is not. The problem is a capacity math problem that no label resolves.

### Counter-Argument
What Louis actually needs is not a category label but a realistic capacity allocation. The debate log shows that D91's "25+ hrs/week by Month 1 Week 3" threshold was aspirational without data. U91 (Louis's actual hours) went unresolved through 33 pulses. Until Louis knows how many hours he can actually commit — measured, not estimated — the binary choice is premature.

**The counter:** "Startup" and "side-project" describe commitment levels, not capacity realities. A declared "startup" with 15 available hours per week is still a side-project in practice. The label changes nothing about what can actually get done.

### Recommended Resolution
**Replace the binary choice with a capacity commitment.** Before choosing a label, Louis must:

1. Track actual hours across all commitments for two weeks (U162, unresolved since Pulse 31)
2. Calculate his realistic Alizé-available hours per week
3. Let that number determine what is actually doable in Month 1, not a declared intention

The choice should then be: "Based on X available hours, Alizé is structured as a [part-time project / growing venture] with a [specific, limited] Month 1 scope." This is honest where the binary framing is theater.

## Assumption 2: Making the Choice Would Actually Unblock Execution

### The Assumption
Choosing "startup" or "side-project" is a forcing function that will unblock the paralysis. Once Louis writes down his choice and dates it, the blocked decisions (U52 ICP mapping, U91 hours, U123 expert-comptable network validation) will become actionable.

### Evidence from the Debate Log
The debate log demonstrates the opposite. Consider what actually blocked Alizé's progress across 33 pulses:

- **Pricing:** D148, D149, D150, D155, D156, D157, D158 — six challenges to the pricing structure without resolution. Louis's pricing was structurally below cost for months without him knowing (Pulse 33 unit economics analysis).
- **Hours tracking:** U91 unresolved through 30+ pulses. This is a 15-minute timer app, not a research problem.
- **ICP mapping:** U52 unresolved through 33 pulses. This is a LinkedIn crawl and a spreadsheet, not a research problem.
- **Domain purchase:** D168 states "buy alize.studio — €10, 5 minutes, no research required." This was identified as the only true Day 1 blocker. Louis did not buy it.

The evidence shows that the blocking issues are execution problems, not decision problems. Louis has been unable to execute on items that require zero research: buying a domain (5 minutes), tracking his hours (ongoing timer), mapping his LinkedIn network (a few hours of structured work). Declaring "startup" or "side-project" does not make these tasks easier or more likely to happen.

### Counter-Argument
The binary choice is a **procrastination accelerant** disguised as a forcing function. It allows Louis to say "I can't do anything until I choose" — which is more comfortable than doing the unglamorous operational work (tracking hours, mapping contacts, buying the domain). The binary choice is the avoidance of last resort because it feels like a decision when it is actually a delay.

**The counter:** D168 was correct. The domain is the only true Day 1 technical blocker. Everything else is downstream of Louis doing his own personal operational work. No research agent, no pulse process, and no binary choice declaration will make Louis's own tasks more tractable.

### Recommended Resolution
**Separate the binary choice from the execution blockers.** The binary choice (if it matters at all) affects long-term orientation, not Week 1 actions. The domain purchase, hour tracking, and contact mapping should happen regardless of how Louis eventually labels Alizé.

Concretely:
1. Buy the domain today — 5 minutes, €10, zero research
2. Track hours for two weeks starting today — zero research
3. Map top 20 warm contacts in a spreadsheet — a few hours, zero research
4. Make the binary choice only after Steps 1-3 are complete — when Louis has real data about his capacity and network

## Assumption 3: "Startup" Means Something Specific and Uniform

### The Assumption
The binary framing treats "startup" as a coherent, understood category. Choosing "startup" implies: incorporation, outside investment ambition, growth-oriented organizational structure, the intention to build something that scales beyond Louis's personal service delivery.

### Evidence from the Debate Log
Across 33 pulses, "startup" was never defined operationally. The debate log shows a constant drift in what Alizé is or isn't:

- **D79:** "Productized consulting" — then rejected (hiring trap, fundraising trap, consulting behaviors)
- **D80:** "Platform builder in Years 2-3" — then removed by D134
- **D143:** "Regulated SMEs" — killed by D159 (signals compliance assets Louis doesn't have)
- **D133:** "French AI service firm for regulated industries" — killed by the same logic
- **Final identity from Pulse 26:** Outside-in framing: "AI workflow automation for regulated SMEs" — then killed in Pulse 30

The brief itself (BRIEF.md) describes Alizé as having a clear market positioning and pricing structure, but the debate log shows all of this was challenged and many elements were killed or revised. The notion that "choosing startup" creates clarity is unsupported — Alizé's identity has been contested for 33 pulses without resolution.

More critically: what does "startup" commitment require from Louis specifically?

- **Incorporation?** At what cost? With what legal structure (SAS, SARL, auto-entrepreneur)?
- **Funding ambition?** Raising external capital requires a completely different legal structure, shareholder agreement, IP assignment, and time commitment that a "side-project" does not.
- **Full-time transition?** When? After the internship ends? After M1 completes?
- **Co-founder search?** D86 identifies a commercial co-founder as a prerequisite for closing €7,500+ deals — is this part of the "startup" commitment?

None of these questions were answered in 33 pulses. Treating "startup" as a single choice that unblocks everything ignores that "startup" is itself a bundle of unresolved sub-decisions.

### Counter-Argument
**"Side-project" has operational clarity; "startup" does not.** An auto-entrepreneur side-project with part-time hours, no outside investment, and a service-delivery business model is entirely viable for Alizé's Year 1 execution (D172: "productized fixed-fee project as primary Year 1 revenue source"). The debate log itself concluded in Pulse 33 that Alizé should act as a service business in Year 1, not a venture-backed startup.

The binary framing pushes Louis toward "startup" by default (because it sounds more serious, more committed) without acknowledging that the venture path requires decisions and resources Louis hasn't made and may not want.

**The counter:** Alizé's most realistic near-term path (per D172, D173, D174, D175) is a service business with productized offerings. This is structurally identical to a "side-project" by the debate log's own logic — and it might be the correct path regardless of what Louis calls it.

### Recommended Resolution
**Define what "startup" actually means before asking Louis to choose.** If "startup" means:
- Incorporating with outside investment ambition
- Searching for a commercial co-founder
- Building toward a platform (D80, since killed)
- Pursuing venture funding in 12-18 months

Then "startup" is a multi-month, multi-decision project that Louis cannot execute for 3-6 months minimum due to his existing commitments.

If "startup" just means "I am serious about this and I will prioritize it," then the binary framing is irrelevant — Louis can be serious about Alizé while keeping it as a formal business structure that starts lean.

**The actual choice is not "startup vs. side-project." It is:**
1. What legal structure do I want for Alizé, and when?
2. Do I want external investment, and on what timeline?
3. What is my minimum viable commitment in hours per week for the next 6 months?

These three questions are answerable without a "startup vs. side-project" label, and answering them actually unblocks execution.

## Synthesis: Why the Binary Framing Persists

The binary choice framing is attractive because it converts a complex, multi-dimensional situation into a single declarative act. It feels like progress. It gives agents something to write in a decision log. It transforms a person's life situation — with all its messy constraints — into a clean categorical assignment.

But Louis's situation is not a clean categorical assignment. He is:
- A student with a master's degree in progress
- An intern with contractual constraints (U94, U116 unresolved — Grinto ring-fencing feasibility)
- A co-founder of a web agency with its own obligations
- A person who has not tracked his actual available hours across any of these

Telling Louis to "choose startup or side-project" is asking him to resolve a complex multi-constraint optimization problem with a two-word answer. It is not a solution. It is the avoidance of the solution.

## Recommended Resolution (Not "Louis Must Choose Now")

**Replace the forcing function with a data collection sprint.**

1. **Days 1-3:** Buy alize.studio. Set up hour tracking. No research, no decisions — just execution.
2. **Days 4-14:** Track actual hours across M1, Grinto internship, and web agency. Calculate real Alizé availability. Write the number down.
3. **Day 15:** Based on real data, define a Month 1 Alizé scope that fits within available hours. Do not over-scope.
4. **Week 3:** With actual hours known and actual scope defined, revisit "startup vs. side-project" — but as a legal structure question, not an identity question.

The binary choice, if it must exist, is not a label for Louis's ambition. It is a legal structure decision that follows from honest capacity data, not the other way around.

**If Louis cannot commit 15+ hours per week to Alizé for the next 6 months, Alizé is a side-project — regardless of what Louis writes down. And that is fine.** The debate log's most important finding is that Alizé's most viable Year 1 path is a lean service business (D172), not a venture-backed startup. A lean service business started part-time with honest capacity is more likely to succeed than a "startup" declared in a vacuum.

The binary choice framing should be abandoned in favor of a capacity-first, data-driven reframe.
