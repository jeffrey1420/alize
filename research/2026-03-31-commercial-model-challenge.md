# Commercial Model Challenge — 2026-03-31

## Assumption Challenged

**"Project-first commercial motion" is the right primary model for Alizé Year 1.**

This encompasses the P53-D1 flow: €3,000-5,000 AI workflow setup → €1,200-1,500/month proof period → €2,000-2,500/month earned managed service.

The assumption being challenged is not just the pricing structure. It is the entire premise that Louis can execute a project-based sales motion when 54+ research pulses have produced zero external commercial actions, and when the founder has no documented sales velocity at any price point.

---

## Arguments For

The project-first model has surface-level logic:

1. **Lower risk for the client.** A finite setup fee (€3,000-5,000) feels less scary than an open-ended commitment. It lets prospects "try" Alizé without signing a long-term contract.

2. **Proof period validates before full commitment.** The €1,200-1,500/month proof period creates a natural progression: prove value in 3 months, then convert to a retainer. This mirrors how sophisticated buyers want to buy.

3. **Higher ticket = fewer deals needed.** At €2,000-2,500/month managed service, Louis needs fewer clients to hit revenue targets. This is mathematically appealing.

4. **Setup fee filters for serious buyers.** The upfront investment theoretically weeds out price-shoppers and creates psychological commitment before the proof period begins.

5. **Templates/playbooks (P54-D2) could lower Louis's execution burden.** If self-serve products can generate pipeline without direct sales involvement, this addresses the founder's capacity constraint.

---

## Arguments Against

**The project-first model fails on every dimension that matters for Alizé's current situation.**

### 1. €3,000-5,000 Setup Fee Is Fantasy Given Zero Sales Velocity

Louis has executed **zero external commercial actions** across 54+ research pulses. No discovery calls. No demos. No pilots. No outreach. Not even a domain registration for alize.ai.

The €3,000-5,000 setup fee requires Louis to:
- Identify a qualified prospect
- Get on a call with them
- Diagnose their problem
- Present a custom project scope
- Negotiate price
- Close

Each of these steps is a separate failure mode. Louis has no documented evidence of executing any of them. The setup fee is not a "lower barrier" — it is a **higher barrier** that requires more trust, more credibility, and more sales skill than a simple monthly subscription.

A €3,000-5,000 setup fee also requires Louis to deliver a completed project. That means he must:
- Actually build something
- Manage scope
- Handle revisions
- Get sign-off

Louis is an intern web developer with no documented project delivery track record for AI workflows. The setup fee assumes delivery capability that does not yet exist.

**Verdict on the setup fee: It requires sales skills Louis doesn't have AND delivery skills Louis hasn't demonstrated. This is not a "low-risk entry point." It is two hard problems stacked on top of each other.**

### 2. The Project → Proof Period → Retainer Flow Adds Failure Points, Not Conversion

The three-step flow (setup → proof → retainer) is presented as a conversion funnel. In reality, it is a **failure multiplier**:

- **Setup phase dies:** Louis can't get prospects to agree to a €3,000-5,000 project
- **Proof period dies:** Client won't commit to 3 months at €1,200-1,500/month
- **Retainer conversion dies:** Client满意 but won't commit to €2,000-2,500/month ongoing

Each transition is a separate moment where a deal can die. A three-step funnel has more failure points than a one-step close. The "proof period reduces risk" argument is correct from the client's perspective — but it does nothing for Louis's conversion rate. It just stretches the sales cycle and multiplies the opportunities for rejection.

**The flow was designed to reduce client risk. It was not designed to address Louis's lack of sales execution. These are different problems.**

D219 called the commercial model "structurally broken" for exactly this reason: the model requires a sales motion Louis cannot execute, layered on top of a delivery requirement Louis hasn't proven.

### 3. P54-D2 Self-Serve Bridge Has the Same Execution Problem

P54-D2 proposed templates and playbooks sold at low price points as a "Year 1 pipeline bridge." This is a better conceptual fit for Louis's current capacity — but it has its own fatal flaw.

Louis hasn't built the templates. Louis hasn't built the playbooks. Louis hasn't executed a single Kuroba internal deployment (identified as the first target 18 pulses ago). The self-serve model requires Louis to:

- Build a product (templates/playbooks)
- Set up payment infrastructure
- Create landing pages
- Drive traffic
- Handle support

This is more executable than a €3,000-5,000 sales cycle — but it is still more than Louis has demonstrated he can do. The self-serve bridge is the right **direction**, but it is not a near-term solution. It is a 6-12 month build, not a Year 1 immediate action.

**The self-serve model is the correct long-term answer to "what should Alizé's commercial motion look like." It is not a viable Year 1 bridge given Louis's current execution track record.**

### 4. The Real Problem: No Commercial Model Works When Zero External Actions Have Been Taken

This is the challenge that supersedes all others.

**Louis has not bought a domain.** The company does not have a web presence. There is no landing page, no case studies, no social proof, no product to sell. There is only research — 54 pulses of research.

The 54 pulses have generated **zero external actions**. Not zero sales. Zero actions that move the business forward in any commercial direction. No outreach. No domain. No product. No delivery. No experiments.

This is not a commercial model problem. This is an **execution pathology**. No commercial model — project-first, self-serve, downmarket, channel partner — works when the founder hasn't executed a single step of any of them.

The Kuroba internal deployment was identified 18 pulses ago as the obvious first step: it is Louis's own company, he controls the relationship, he can execute without external validation, and it produces the internal proof point needed for future sales. It is still not executed.

**The commercial model debate is a distraction. The real question is: why has Louis executed zero external actions across 54 research pulses?**

## Recommendation

**The project-first commercial model should be abandoned as the primary Year 1 motion. Not because the model is wrong in theory — but because Louis has demonstrated he cannot execute any commercial model, project-first or otherwise.**

The immediate action is not to redesign the commercial model. It is to answer one question:

**Why has Louis executed zero external actions in 54+ pulses?**

Until this question is answered, no commercial model redesign will matter. The symptom (wrong commercial model) is being treated while the disease (complete execution paralysis) goes unaddressed.

### Specific Recommendations

**1. Execute Kuroba before designing any commercial model.**
Kuroba is Louis's own company. No external sales required. No prospect persuasion needed. It is the only action that produces evidence without requiring Louis to overcome his credibility gap. 18 pulses of inaction on this is the clearest signal in the entire dataset.

**2. If a commercial model must be chosen, choose the one with the shortest path to a paid action.**
That is not project-first. That is a single-sentence offer at a low price point that Louis can send in a single message. Not a €3,000 project. Not a 3-month proof period. A €500 one-time experiment that a single prospect can say yes to without a call.

**3. Treat every "wait for X before doing Y" as a red flag.**
The research has generated a pattern: wait for the domain, wait for the commercial co-founder, wait for the vertical alignment, wait for the binary choice. None of these waits have produced action. The commercial model should not have another prerequisite before execution begins.

**4. Accept that D84 ("Louis can't close") is unvalidated until Louis tries.**
D84 is a prediction. It may be correct. But it has never been tested. The first paid action — even a €500 experiment — produces real data. Until that data exists, the "Louis can't close" claim is an excuse, not a constraint.

**The commercial model is not Alizé's problem. Execution paralysis is Alizé's problem. Fix the execution paralysis first. Every commercial model discussion that happens before that is another pulse of research theater.**
