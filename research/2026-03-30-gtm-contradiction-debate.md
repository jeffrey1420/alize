**Surface contradiction:** D172 names the €1,500 fixed-fee project as the primary revenue motion. D173 says warm referrals come only after delivery. If warm referrals are off the table before delivery, and Louis has zero clients, how does he get the first delivery?

This appears to be a genuine logical deadlock. But the resolution lies in recognizing that D172 and D173 are describing **two different things**: D172 is a delivery and pricing model; D173 is an acquisition model. They address different layers of the same problem.

---

## Challenge 1: If Warm Referrals Come After Delivery, How Does Louis Get the First Delivery?

He does it the same way every bootstrapper with zero case studies does it: **proactive outreach**.

The phrase "warm referrals only after delivery" describes the flywheel that powers growth *after* the first client, not a gate that prevents starting. D173's logic holds for the second, third, and fourth clients — when satisfied clients recommend Louis to their peers. But it says nothing about the origin point.

The origin point requires a different motion: outreach to strangers, made easier by the €1,500 productized format. Without proactive outreach, Louis is waiting for someone to knock on his door. For an unknown brand with zero case studies, zero website, and no published offer, that door knock never comes.

**Verdict:** D173 describes the second-order acquisition mechanism. It does not — and cannot — describe first-customer acquisition.

## Challenge 2: Is "Productized Fixed-Fee Project" a Sales Motion or a Delivery Model?

These are different things, and conflating them is the root of the contradiction.

**"Productized fixed-fee project" as delivery/pricing model:**
- €1,500 flat price
- Specific, named workflow (e.g., "AI meeting report generation for a 30-person service firm")
- 2-week delivery commitment
- Scope locked before work begins
- Structured handoff at completion

This is a **delivery architecture**. It tells Louis what to build, at what price, with what timeline. It answers: "What am I delivering and for how much?"

**"Productized fixed-fee project" as sales motion:**
- The €1,500 price point with a specific named workflow removes the ambiguity that kills cold outreach
- "Would you pay €1,500 for a 2-week AI automation of X?" is a concrete question
- The specificity *is* the conversion mechanism — it converts curiosity into a yes/no decision

The €1,500 offer makes cold outreach viable because it removes the infinite negotiation space. Without a productized format, cold outreach is: "We do AI agents for businesses like yours, want to talk?" With the productized format, it's: "We automate X for €1,500 in 2 weeks, want to try?" The second version has a response option; the first doesn't.

**Verdict:** D172 describes the delivery model. The productized format *enables* the sales motion, but the sales motion itself is still outreach. D173, by saying "warm outreach is a planning artifact," is making a different and narrower point — that warm referrals are not a *reliable* first acquisition mechanism — but it doesn't eliminate the need for cold/outbound.

## Challenge 3: Does the GTM Auditor's "Cold Emails with a Specific Offer" in Week 1 Contradict D173?

No — and this is the key to resolving the contradiction.

The GTM Auditor's cold email recommendation (€1,500 offer, specific workflow, sent to ICP-matched prospects) is **the sales motion that generates the delivery events D173 is describing**. D173 says warm referrals come after delivery. The Auditor says: to get delivery events, run cold outreach with a specific offer. These are not in conflict — they are sequential.

**Cold outreach → First delivery → Warm referrals → More deliveries**

D173's constraint applies to step 3 onward. It says nothing about step 1. The Auditor's recommendation is precisely the bridge that gets Louis from zero to the first delivery event, after which D173's warm-referral flywheel kicks in.

The Auditor also said "warm referrals only AFTER delivery" — but this is descriptive, not prescriptive. It's an observation about sequence, not a rule that eliminates the need for prior acquisition. "Referrals come after delivery" is true. "Therefore do no outreach before delivery" does not follow.

## Challenge 4: Minimum Viable First-Customer Acquisition Sequence

This sequence must be internally consistent: it must not require Louis to already have what he doesn't have yet (case studies, clients, warm referrals, a live website, a credible brand).

### Month 1 GTM Sequence (Zero-Proof Starting Point)

**Week 1: Setup + First Outreach**
- Buy and configure alize.studio (D168 — confirmed Day 1 blocker)
- Deploy landing page with the productized €1,500 offer visible (even if unfinished)
- Draft 10-15 personalized cold emails to ICP-matched prospects (50-200 employee service firms in Île-de-France)
- Email format: one specific workflow offer, €1,500 price, 2-week delivery commitment, no attachment, one clear CTA
- Track open rates; iterate subject lines

**Week 2: Discovery Calls Begin**
- Target: 3-5 discovery calls booked from cold outreach
- Call format: 20-minute structured discovery, qualify on workflow pain + budget fit
- Do not sell a pilot; sell the *concept* of the €1,500 fixed-fee automation
- If no replies: test a different ICP segment, not a different offer

**Week 3: First Pilot Signed**
- First client signs €1,500 fixed-fee scope document
- Payment: 50% upfront / 50% at delivery
- Scope: single workflow, one integration, 2-week delivery
- Explicit case study rights negotiated as commercial term (D111)
- Deliver using Louis solo + documented playbook (D170/D113)

**Week 4: Delivery + Referral Ask**
- Deliver first working agent
- At handoff: ask for one referral — "Do you know another firm that has the same workflow problem?"
- This is the first warm referral; it comes from a delivered client (D173 is now active)
- Begin mapping warm network of this client for Month 2 outreach

### What This Sequence Does Not Require

- It does not require a live website with case studies (D47 gates cold outbound, not warm outreach — D51)
- It does not require warm referrals before delivery (D173's own logic is respected)
- It does not require Louis to be a credible closer (€1,500 is a low-friction ask with a sales coach if needed — D137)
- It does not require the €7,500 pilot price (which was unsellable without references — D84/D107)

## Conclusion

D172 and D173 are not in contradiction — they describe different layers of the same GTM motion:

- **D172** answers: what is the offer format? → €1,500 fixed-fee, specific workflow, 2-week delivery
- **D173** answers: what drives second-order growth? → warm referrals from delivered clients

The contradiction dissolves when you recognize that D173 is a description of the growth *flywheel* (referrals after delivery), not a constraint on *first* acquisition (outreach before delivery). The Auditor's cold email recommendation in Week 1 is the exact mechanism that generates the first delivery event, which then enables D173's warm-referral flywheel.

**The minimum viable first-customer acquisition sequence for Month 1:**
1. Publish the €1,500 productized offer (even on a basic landing page)
2. Send cold emails to 10-15 ICP-matched prospects
3. Book 3-5 discovery calls
4. Close first €1,500 pilot
5. Deliver solo
6. At delivery: ask for referral
7. Use delivery learnings to sharpen Month 2 outreach

Louis doesn't need case studies to start. He needs a specific offer, a short list of strangers, and the willingness to send the email.
