## Assumption 1: Louis Can Personally Close Warm Network Pilots

### Evidence Against

**No sales training or closing experience.**
Louis is a web development intern and M1 master's student. There is no evidence he has commercial training, objection handling experience, or practice navigating procurement conversations. Selling a €7,500 pilot requires leading a buying committee, handling budget questions, and building trust quickly — skills that take years to develop, not weeks of LinkedIn template tweaking.

**Louis's only network is Grinto — which is his current employer.**
Every warm contact Louis reaches out to will know he works at Grinto. Pitching Alizé (a competing or adjacent service) while representing Grinto creates an immediate credibility and conflict problem. Prospects will wonder: whose interest is Louis serving? Is this a Grinto product or a side project? This undermines the trust that warm outreach is supposed to leverage.

**The math on call quality is brutal.**
75–160 discovery calls to close 6–8 pilots implies a 5–10% close rate from discovery calls. That assumes the calls are well-run. An untrained intern leading those calls will likely see 1–3% conversion at best — doubling or tripling the outreach volume required and burning the network with poorly handled conversations.

**A €7,500 engagement is not a casual chat.**
Even in a warm network, signing a pilot involves budget approval, internal alignment, and risk evaluation. Someone needs to answer questions like "what happens if the AI agent makes an error?" or "who do we call at 9am on Monday?" Without a structured demo, ROI framing, and commercial terms, prospects will go quiet after the first call.

### Alternative Approach

1. **Louis generates leads; a founder or senior person closes.** Louis identifies warm contacts, sends initial outreach, and books a call — but the actual sales conversation is led by someone with commercial credibility (e.g., a co-founder or advisor with PME operating experience).
2. **Louis qualifies before pitching.** Use a short screener message or form to confirm ICP fit (company size, current manual processes, budget authority) before offering a formal pilot call. This protects both Louis's time and the prospect's.
3. **Script the first 10 calls with a founder shadowing.** Even 2–3 coached calls with real-time feedback would dramatically improve conversion rates compared to Louis going solo.

### Verdict: **REJECT** — Louis should not be closing pilots alone. Revise to Louis-as-matcher, founder-as-closer.

## Assumption 2: Warm Outreach to Grinto Connections = Qualified Alizé Pipeline

### Evidence Against

**Grinto is a web development agency.** Based on the context, Grinto's network likely consists of clients who hired a web agency — they needed a website or web app. That's not the same signal as "we are drowning in manual processes and actively evaluating AI automation." ICP fit is not guaranteed.

**Company size ≠ readiness.** PME/ETI (20–200 employees) is the stated ICP, but being the right size does not mean being AI-agent-ready. Many small French companies in this range have: no internal IT staff, no structured processes to automate, and founders who are skeptical of AI. Warm outreach to the right-size companies that are not yet buyers wastes the warm relationship.

**€7,500–€20,000/year is a significant commitment for this segment.** French PME decision-makers at this budget level will ask for references, case studies, and proof of ROI before signing. A warm connection to Grinto does not substitute for social proof and a clear value demonstration.

**Louis's warm network is thin.** If Louis has been a web dev intern, his personal Grinto connections are likely other junior employees, developers, or clients he personally worked with — not decision-makers with budget authority for €7,500 AI services. The "warm" in warm outreach is only as valuable as the contact's influence.

**No screening for actual pain points.** The plan assumes warmth converts. But if Grinto connections never had automation problems, they have no reason to buy. Warmth opens the door; pain closes the deal.

### Alternative Approach

1. **Screen before outreach.** Use a 2-question LinkedIn poll or a lightweight value-prop post to identify which Grinto contacts are actively struggling with manual workflows, customer service overload, or data processing bottlenecks — before booking a pilot call.
2. **Map the actual buying committee.** Warm outreach should target founders, COOs, or operations managers at Grinto clients — not just whoever Louis knows from the agency. One well-targeted outreach to an ops manager at a 60-person logistics company beats 10 calls with a web dev contact.
3. **Build a case study from the first pilot before scaling.** If the first pilot comes from a warm Grinto contact, use it as a template and reference for outreach to similar companies. This creates real pipeline, not just borrowed credibility.

### Verdict: **REVISE** — Warmth is not ICP-fit. Build qualification criteria (pain + budget + authority) before calling any Grinto connection a prospect.

## Assumption 3 (Bonus Challenge): LinkedIn DMs Are the Right Format for €7,500–€20,000 B2B Deals in France in 2026

### Evidence Against

**LinkedIn DM response rates for cold/outbound B2B are collapsing.** Inboxes are flooded with AI-generated sales messages. Even warm DMs from connections are competing with hundreds of other messages. Response rates on cold LinkedIn outreach in 2025–2026 are commonly cited at 5–15% for connection-level messages and under 5% for cold messages. At €7,500 pilot value, you need a 20–30% response rate to make the volume math work.

**French B2B buyers are relationship-first.** In the French PME market especially, business is still done through trusted relationships, referrals, and in-person or phone conversations. A LinkedIn DM from an intern pitching a €7,500 service is low-trust by default. A warm introduction via a shared contact (even just an email introduction) has 3–5x higher conversion than a DM.

**LinkedIn DMs cannot close complex sales.** At this price point, a 45-minute discovery call is the minimum viable sales interaction. LinkedIn DMs can start conversations — but they cannot handle: objection responses, pricing negotiation, or multi-stakeholder alignment. The plan underestimates how many steps are required between a LinkedIn DM reply and a signed pilot.

**The "warm" qualifier is being wasted on LinkedIn.** If contacts are genuinely warm (Louis has a real relationship), a direct call or video invitation email performs far better than a LinkedIn message. Warmth should unlock a direct email with a Calendly link — not a LinkedIn DM that looks like every other outreach.

### Alternative Approach

1. **Lead with email, not LinkedIn DM.** A personalized email referencing a specific business challenge, sent to a warm contact's work address, outperforms LinkedIn DMs for B2B at this price point. LinkedIn can be the follow-up or reminder.
2. **Use LinkedIn for social proof, not outreach.** Louis posting about Alizé's work, tagging relevant contacts organically, or commenting on prospects' content builds awareness without the salesy DM friction.
3. **Phone is still underrated in French B2B.** A short voice message or a "can I call you for 10 minutes?" message via WhatsApp or SMS (when appropriate) dramatically outperforms written outreach for qualified warm contacts.

### Verdict: **REVISE** — LinkedIn DMs can be a channel, but not the primary one. Shift to email-first or phone-first for warm contacts, with LinkedIn as follow-up.
