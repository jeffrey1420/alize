# Pulse 75 — Execution Architecture Auditor

## What Assumption You're Challenging

P74's dominant finding: "prerequisites are substitutes for market contact." The implication: buy domain, send message, get on calls. Market contact is the万能 gate that resolves all uncertainty.

## What Supports the Assumption

74 pulses of evidence. Every prerequisite that was supposed to enable execution has instead delayed it: pricing finalized 15 times, vertical changed 12 times, GTM channel revised 8 times. The pattern is real. Domain, Kuroba, pricing, network map — all have been invoked as preconditions for market contact. None produced market contact.

## What Contradicts the Assumption

"Market contact" is being treated as a singular, homogeneous event. But:
1. **A failed market contact is worse than no market contact.** Sending one poorly-framed message to the wrong person could close a door that 10 good messages couldn't reopen.
2. **P74's three agents all assumed market contact was the missing input.** But Louis has been receiving market information continuously — from Kuroba's reception, from Grinto client reactions, from pricing conversations. The information isn't missing. The interpretation framework is missing.
3. **"Send the message" has been the specific action for 20+ pulses.** If market contact was the unlock, one of the 20 prior "send today" findings would have produced a sent message.

The "market contact as magic gate" framing has the same structure as every prior prerequisite: it treats a single event as if it resolves all uncertainty at once.

## Decision

REVISE — market contact is necessary but not sufficient. The quality and framing of market contact matters as much as the fact of it. A badly framed first message to a warm contact could be worse than no message.

## Specific Actionable Finding

The actual blocker is not "no market contact" but "no validated framing to use in market contact." P74's P67-D9 message (the outreach message) was written 8 pulses ago. It hasn't been sent. The question is not "should Louis send a message" — that's been settled. The question is "what makes this specific message worth sending when the last 20 specific messages weren't sent?"

The answer cannot come from research. It requires Louis to either: (a) send the message as-is and accept imperfect framing, or (b) explicitly decide the framing is too imperfect to send. The second option requires him to articulate what's wrong with it — which is a different kind of work than 74 pulses of research.

# Pulse 75 — Conviction Threshold Auditor

## What Assumption You're Challenging

The assumption: Louis doesn't send messages because he lacks information (proof, pricing, positioning, domain). Provide the right information → Louis sends the message.

## The Competing Hypothesis

Louis doesn't send messages because he doesn't believe sending one would be worth doing. This is a conviction problem, not an information problem. The distinction:
- **Information problem**: I don't know what to say
- **Conviction problem**: I know what to say but don't believe it will work, so I don't say it

The evidence for conviction problem over information problem:
- 74 pulses have produced detailed messaging frameworks, outreach templates, pricing scripts, competitive responses
- Louis built Kuroba without 74 pulses of positioning research
- Louis has NOT asked for better messaging in any of the 74 pulses — he's asked for permission to keep researching

## What Evidence Distinguishes the Two

| Information Problem | Conviction Problem |
|--------------------|--------------------|
| Asks "what should I say?" | Asks "but what if it doesn't work?" |
| Engages with frameworks | Engages with caveats |
| Research feels productive | Research feels urgent |
| Finds reasons to act | Finds reasons to wait |

Louis's behavior across 74 pulses matches the conviction problem pattern. The research doesn't feel like stalling — it feels urgent and necessary. That's the signature of conviction failure, not information failure.

## Decision

CONFIRM conviction problem diagnosis. But note: conviction cannot be argued into existence. Evidence of market need (a reply, an expression of interest, even a "we're not interested") would provide the conviction that research cannot.

## Specific Actionable Finding

The fastest path to conviction is a reply. Any reply. The first person who responds — even to say no — gives Louis evidence that the message can work. The first "yes, let's talk" gives him evidence that the problem framing is real.

The question "what makes Louis send one message" may be unanswerable through research. The question "what happens after Louis sends one message" becomes answerable the moment the message is sent.

# Pulse 75 — Product Paradox Auditor

## The Contradiction You're Examining

Alizé's BRIEF.md describes a **service company**: deploy and manage AI agents for clients, professional delivery, ongoing relationship, governance, accountability. Service companies validate by doing. But Louis has spent 74 pulses building Alizé like a **product company**: iterating on positioning, pricing architecture, technical stack, competitive framing in private before any market contact.

## Evidence For

- BRIEF.md: "Alizé sells a managed AI agent service" — service framing throughout
- 74 pulses of research: positioning, pricing tiers, technical architecture, competitive differentiation — all product company behaviors
- Kuroba: built and operating — product company execution pattern
- Alizé: still "being prepared" after 74 pulses — same preparation loop that product companies use before launch

## Evidence Against

- Service companies DO need to define their offer before selling
- The positioning questions (who is the ICP, what is the workflow, what is the price) are legitimate service company questions
- A poorly defined service offer could waste both Louis's and the prospect's time

## The Deeper Contradiction

A service company doesn't need 74 pulses of research to start selling. It needs ONE client willing to have a conversation. The positioning, pricing, and architecture debates are product company pre-launch activities that make sense when:
- You're building something once and selling it many times
- The product needs to be defined before first contact
- The buyer is evaluating a purchase decision

None of these apply to a service company where:
- Each engagement is custom scoped
- The "product" is defined through the sales conversation
- The buyer is buying the person, not the thing

Louis is treating Alizé like a product launch because that's what he knows how to do (iterate in private, launch when ready). But Alizé as described in the BRIEF is a service business that should be selling.

## Decision

CONFIRM product paradox diagnosis. Alizé is being built like a product that will be launched. It should be sold like a service that starts immediately.

## Specific Actionable Finding

Stop the 74-pulse product-launch loop. For a service company, the sequence is:
1. Send message → 2. Have conversation → 3. Define scope → 4. Quote price → 5. Deliver

The current sequence is:
1. Define scope → 2. Finalize pricing → 3. Resolve positioning → 4. THEN send message

The service company sequence doesn't require more research. It requires sending the message.
