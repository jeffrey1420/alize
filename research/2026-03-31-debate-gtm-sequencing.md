### The Delta That Matters

Warm converts at 8.5x the rate of cold. That sounds decisive. **It is not.**

The 8.5x conversion improvement assumes warm leads are qualified and the warm channel is operational. At Alizé's stage, neither is true.

More importantly: **these are aggregate B2B numbers across all deal sizes and all "warm" definitions.** That aggregation buries a critical variable — what counts as "warm"?

## The "Warm" Definition Problem

This is where the existing research is softest.

"Warm outreach" in the literature covers at least four meaningfully different categories:

| Warm Type | What It Actually Means | Real Response Rate |
|-----------|------------------------|-------------------|
| **True personal referral** | A mutual trusted contact vouches for you directly | 30–60% response, highest conversion |
| **Mutual connection introduction** | Someone introduces you via LinkedIn or email, not a direct vouch | 15–25% response |
| **Engagement signal warm** | Prospect liked/commented on your content, visited your site | 8–15% response |
| **Network adjacency warm** | You share a school, former employer, or industry event | 5–10% response |

The Belkins and GrowLeads "10–45% warm response rate" spans all four categories. The high end (45%) comes from true personal referrals. The low end (10%) comes from engagement signals.

**Louis's unmapped warm network is somewhere between category 3 and 4 — and that's being generous.** Without a map, he doesn't even know which category his contacts fall into.

**The research conflates these. Alizé's strategy cannot afford to.**

## Challenged Assumption #1: "Warm Must Come First"

**Assumption:** Cold outreach should wait until warm outreach is underway or exhausted.  
**Reality:** This is not supported by the data for the €1,500–7,500 price range.

At this price point in French B2B services, the buyer persona is typically:
- Head of Ops or DG at a 50–200 person PME
- Sophisticated enough to evaluate a cold email on its merits if the message is relevant
- Not making a buying decision that requires social capital or trusted referral to proceed

For context: a €5,000 engagement is not an enterprise sale. It's a significant decision for an SMB, but it's not a board-level procurement. The decision cycle is weeks, not quarters. These buyers do respond to cold outreach when the message is credible.

**The warm-first premise is borrowed from enterprise sales doctrine where a $50K+ deal requires relationship trust. At €1,500–7,500, the trust bar is lower. The message relevance bar is higher.**

A cold email that demonstrates specific understanding of the prospect's situation will convert at or near the warm range (4–5% reply) at this price point. Waiting to warm up a network that doesn't exist yet costs weeks of learning.

## Challenged Assumption #3: "Warm and Cold Are Sequential"

**Assumption:** Warm outreach happens first, then cold outbound.  
**Reality:** There's no operational reason to sequence them this way for Year 1.

Cold outbound is not a last resort — it's a learning engine. The first 50–100 cold sends will tell you:
1. Whether your ICP targeting is accurate (who replies vs. who ignores)
2. Which message framing works (what language generates responses)
3. What the actual reply rate is in this specific market segment
4. Who the gatekeepers are and what they care about

This learning is not available from warm outreach to Louis's unmapped network. You can't iterate on a list you haven't built.

**Recommendation: Run cold and warm simultaneously, not sequentially.** Cold gives you market learning. Warm (when mapped) gives you higher-conversion touches. There's no dependency between them.

## What Actually Matters for the €1,500–7,500 Price Point

For French B2B services at this range:

| Factor | Impact on Cold vs Warm | Verdict |
|--------|----------------------|---------|
| Decision complexity | Low-to-medium. Not board-level but not casual. | Message quality > relationship |
| Trust requirement | Moderate. Buyer needs credibility signal, not personal connection. | Cold can work if message is specific |
| Sales cycle | 30–90 days typical | Cold slows it somewhat but doesn't kill it |
| ICP specificity | High. Targeting wrong-fit companies wastes everyone time. | Cold targeting precision matters more than warm/cold distinction |
| French market norms | French B2B buyers are notably formal and resistant to unsolicited contact. | Warm is better here, but not by enough to wait for it. |

**The French market nuance is real.** French B2B buyers are more resistant to cold outreach than US counterparts. This is the strongest argument for warm-first. But:

1. The resistance is to generic cold outreach, not targeted cold outreach with a credible specific hook
2. At €1,500–7,500, a well-crafted cold email from an unknown is still viable if it demonstrates specific knowledge of the prospect's situation
3. Louis has no warm network to leverage. "Warm-first" in his case means "do nothing while theorizing about warm contacts."

## The Specific Recommendation

**Cold outbound starts now. No waiting.**

The sequencing is not warm-first. It is:

1. **Cold outbound (immediate):** Start with 50–100 targeted cold emails to ICP-fit prospects. Use this to validate targeting, message, and ICP. Learn fast.

2. **Warm network mapping (parallel, Week 1):** Louis builds the ICP-filtered contact list. This should take 3–5 days of focused work. Not 73 pulses. Not a research project. A specific list: 20–30 named contacts with company, role, and a reason to reach out.

3. **Warm outreach (Week 2+, once list exists):** Once the map is built and validated, warm outreach runs alongside cold. Not before. Not instead.

4. **Kuroba (parallel, not blocking):** Kuroba can run as a delivery learning exercise but should not block or substitute for external outreach. External outreach generates market signal. Kuroba generates internal rehearsal. Only one of those produces external validation.
