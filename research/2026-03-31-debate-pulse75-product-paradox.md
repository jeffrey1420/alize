# Pulse 75 — Product Paradox Auditor

**Date:** 2026-03-31
**Role:** Product Paradox Auditor
**Task:** Find the internal contradiction that keeps Alizé stuck

---

## The Contradiction You're Examining

**The framing says: "Service company."**
The execution pattern says: "Product company."

Alizé is described in BRIEF.md as a managed AI agent service — a professional services firm that deploys agents for clients, manages them, and optimizes them over time. The entire commercial model is built on client relationships: discovery calls, pilots, ongoing managed service. The go-to-market section states the logic plainly: **contact market → receive signal → adjust positioning.**

Kuroba (the sibling venture) executed that pattern. It built something, deployed it, and started selling. It did not wait for positioning to be perfect. It went to market.

Louis is building Alizé like a product company — iterating on positioning, pricing, architecture, competitive framing in private — until some imagined threshold of "readiness" is crossed. Then, and only then, will contact the market. This is backwards for a service company. **A service company validates by sending the email and getting a reply. A product company iterates in private. Louis is doing the product company's homework while the service company's job — talk to people — goes undone.**

---

## Evidence For

**1. The 74+ pulse research pattern is a product company's pattern.**
Product companies do this: they iterate on the product, refine the positioning, benchmark against competitors, finalize the pricing, build the infrastructure — all before shipping. Service companies do not have this luxury because the "product" is the interaction itself. You cannot test a discovery call by writing documents about it. You test it by making the call. Louis has written 260+ decisions about Alizé. Zero discovery calls have been made.

**2. The Kuroba sibling proof.**
Kuroba has executed: built, deployed, selling. Alizé has not. The difference between them is not resources, not capability, not time. It is the fundamental difference in how each venture was approached. Kuroba was approached as a service delivery vehicle — do the work, get paid, iterate. Alizé is approached as a product to be perfected before shipping. Louis knows how to execute a service business. He is choosing not to do that with Alizé.

**3. The technical architecture debates confirm the product-company mindset.**
Pulse 63's Competitive Framing Auditor correctly identified that 10+ research pulses were spent on "how does our message land?" — when no message has been delivered anywhere. The technical stack debates (MCP architecture, pgvector, Mastra, self-improvement layers) are product infrastructure questions. A service company answers these questions by asking a client what they need, not by designing a 4-layer self-improvement architecture in a research file.

**4. "Readiness" as the paralysis mechanism.**
The unspoken belief driving the research loop is: "the positioning must be right before we can approach people." This is a product company's belief. Service companies do not have this option. A consultant does not finalize their methodology before their first client meeting — they learn what the client needs, then deliver. Louis is waiting for a condition (readiness) that only arrives through market contact, not despite it.

**5. The Kuroba Sequencing decision (P56-D1) tried to make Alizé more like Kuroba and got killed.**
P56-D1 demoted Kuroba from Month 1 prerequisite to parallel track. But the deeper pattern — the need to practice internally before going external — survived the Kuroba sequencing kill. Louis still has not sent a single message to a prospect. The internal-rehearsal-before-external-contact instinct is so deeply embedded that removing the specific instance (Kuroba) did not remove the pattern.

---

## Evidence Against

**1. The BRIEF itself frames Alizé as a service company.**
The commercial model, the target client profile, the pricing structure, the competitor positioning — all of it is structured as a professional services offering. Louis knows what Alizé is supposed to be. The contradiction is not in the documents.

**2. The "service vs. product" framing may be too clean.**
It is possible that Alizé genuinely occupies a middle ground — a "productized service" that requires some infrastructure before client delivery. The technical architecture decisions (MCP, pgvector, Mastra) might be necessary components of a scalable service, not product-company thinking. The distinction between "product company" and "service company" may be less clean than this audit suggests.

**3. Some service companies do invest in positioning before outreach.**
A consulting firm launching a new practice area might spend time developing its point of view before going to market. The critique might be overstated — the problem is not the research itself but the ratio of research to execution, and the failure to do any execution after the research.

**4. The Kuroba comparison is imperfect.**
Kuroba is a web agency with Maëli and Gabin involved. Alizé is Louis's solo effort with no team. The difference in execution capacity may be real, not just a framing issue.

---

## Your Decision

**The contradiction is real and it is the primary driver of execution paralysis.**

The evidence for is overwhelming and the evidence against is either definitional quibbling or an attempt to avoid the obvious. Louis is treating Alizé like a product to be shipped when the BRIEF describes it as a service to be delivered. The product-company mindset creates an infinite deferral condition: you can always find another positioning question to answer, another technical decision to make, another competitive angle to refine. The readiness threshold is never reached because market contact is what produces readiness — not the other way around.

Kuroba executed because Kuroba's task was not abstractable into a research loop. Building a website for a client produces a deliverable you can show. Perfecting Alizé's positioning in a document produces another document. The medium of execution for a service company (the meeting, the call, the deployed agent) is inherently less abstractable than the medium of research (the document). Louis has been choosing the less abstractable, less committal path: produce more documents instead of making more calls.

**This is the contradiction: the brief says "service." Louis behaves like "product." The product mindset says readiness comes before contact. The service reality is that contact IS the readiness check.**

---

## Specific Actionable Finding

**Kill the readiness belief explicitly.**

Louis must write down — not in a research file, in his actual workflow — the following as a literal rule:

> *"Positioning is validated by market contact, not by internal iteration. There is no amount of research that makes a first outreach email more likely to succeed than sending it now. Send the email."*

Concrete next action: **Send one email today.** Not "draft the email." Not "finalize the subject line." Not "identify the 20 best prospects." Send one email. Any prospect. Any format. The BRIEF has the discovery call script. The pricing is in the BRIEF. The target ICP is in the BRIEF. The first email takes the same 30 minutes as the next research pulse and produces market signal instead of more speculation.

The specific finding: **Louis's execution paralysis is caused by treating a service company like a product company — waiting for internal perfection before external validation — when the service's quality is only measurable through external contact. The paradox is that the "readiness" Louis is waiting for only exists after he starts, not before.**
