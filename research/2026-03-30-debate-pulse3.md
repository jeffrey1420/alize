## Debate 7: Landing Page & Messaging

**Agent:** Landing Page & Messaging Strategist
**File:** `/data/workspace/alize/research/landing-page-debate.md`

### Assumptions Challenged

**1. "The hero's negative positioning ('Pas des gadgets') is a feature"**

The current hero ("Déployez des agents IA utiles à votre entreprise. Pas des gadgets.") leads with a product statement and a defensive posture. The agent called this inside-out.

> A DG/COO at a 100-person company doesn't arrive at the page thinking "I hope this isn't a gadget." They arrive thinking about operational costs, headcount constraints, and competitive speed. The "not a gadget" angle addresses the AI industry's anxiety about credibility — not the buyer's anxiety about their business. The framing fails the first-second test: if the DG doesn't see their problem named in the headline, they scroll past.

**Alternative direction:** Lead with the problem at the right scale. "80 personnes. 12 outils. Zéro visibilité sur ce que tout le monde fait. On automatise le reste." This names the scale (company of 80-100), creates a cost equation, and opens a question the DG wants answered.

**2. "The problem section creates urgency by listing operational symptoms"**

Listing symptoms (emails piling up, info scattered across 7 tools, responses too slow) gets nods but doesn't create urgency. The DG thinks in costs, headcount, and competitive speed — not operational symptoms. The problem section never converts symptoms into a cost equation.

> "Des coûts cachés, des retards, des erreurs" doesn't quantify: this is worth X euros per month in lost productivity, this is why your lead response time is 3x slower than the market, this is why your best people are data entry instead of selling.

**3. "The differentiation section creates a clear mental model vs. Agentova vs. Copilot"**

The "pourquoi nous" section names the right attributes (orientation métier, sécurité, supervision) but never states the contrast explicitly. Agentova is not mentioned. Microsoft/Copilot is not mentioned. A buyer who's already looked at Agentova reads a list of qualities any vendor could claim.

> What the DG needs: a simple, visceral contrast. "Agentova gives you agents you configure yourself. Alizé gives you agents that work in your business. Copilot gives you a tool you have to manage. Alizé gives you an employee you don't have to manage." That sentence creates a new category. The current page doesn't.

**4. "Réserver un diagnostic (€490) is the right CTA for an unknown brand"**

The page has zero social proof — no client logos, no case studies, no quantified results, no named team. Asking a stranger to pay €490 and give 30 minutes for a completely unknown brand is a conversion killer.

> A better approach for an unknown brand: lower the first step to "Voir les résultats" or "Explorer comment ça marche." The diagnostic offer can appear deeper in the page. The goal of the first CTA is to get the conversation started — not to close the first sale.

### Landing Page Debate Summary Table

| Section | Risk | Core Issue | Alternative Direction |
|---------|------|------------|---------------------|
| Hero | HIGH | Leads with product, not pain. Defensive. No urgency. | Lead with problem at company scale. Make a cost claim. |
| Problem | MEDIUM-HIGH | Lists symptoms, no cost equation, no competitive stakes | Quantify cost of inaction. Connect to competitive speed. |
| Differentiation | MEDIUM | Right attributes, no contrast, no names | Explicitly contrast vs. Agentova and Copilot in one sentence. |
| CTA | HIGH | €490 on zero social proof. Commitment too high too early. | Lower entry barrier. Micro-commitment first. |
| Credibility | HIGH | No logos, numbers, testimonials, proof | Add credibility anchors immediately. |

## Debate 8: Network GTM Scalability

**Agent:** GTM Scalability Strategist
**File:** `/data/workspace/alize/research/network-gtm-debate.md`

### Assumptions Challenged

**1. "Personal network will yield 3-5 pilots in months 1-3"**

Personal network gives access to conversations, not clients. The math:

- Warm intro conversion rate for qualified B2B SaaS: 15-25%
- To sign 3 pilots at 20% conversion: need 15 qualified conversations
- French B2B sales cycle for €5-15k first engagement: 5-13 weeks minimum (2-4 months)

> Phase 1 target of 3-5 pilots in 3 months is optimistic. Discovery + proposal + signature alone is 5-13 weeks. If Louis starts outreach in Month 1, first signed pilots realistically close in Month 2-3 — with zero slack.

**2. "Case studies + LinkedIn will generate qualified inbound in months 4-6"**

No. SEO for a new domain takes 12-18 months to drive meaningful traffic for competitive B2B keywords. LinkedIn organic reach collapsed since 2023 — a post reaches 5-15% of followers without paid amplification.

> Content SEO generates traffic, not qualified leads. 200-500 organic visitors/month at 1-2% conversion = 2-10 leads/month, mostly unqualified. This is not a pipeline engine for a €500-2k/month B2B service.

**3. "Pilot clients will refer one other company"**

French B2B culture is relationship-based but not transactional. A referral carries social capital for the introducer — if the vendor disappoints, the introducer loses face. The ask is premature immediately after a pilot (relationship is tested, not strengthened). French SMEs require 6+ months of demonstrated results before they'll recommend.

> What works in French B2B: partners who vouch for you to their clients, not clients who volunteer you to their network. A partner introduces with credibility. A client refers with risk.

**4. The core structural problem: no scalable pipeline engine after month 3**

The current plan treats three things as independent levers: network → pilots, case studies → inbound, referrals → expansion. In reality these are sequential and fragile. Network exhausts. Content takes 12-18 months. Referrals are earned, not asked.

**Recommended GTM Motion: Partner-First + Systematic Outbound Hybrid**

- **Partners (experts-comptables, cabinets de conseil)** already have the relationships. One active partner generates 2-5 qualified referrals/year. Referral fee: 1 month recurring. The ~20,000 expert-comptable firms in France serve ~130,000 companies — this is a massive channel.
- **LinkedIn outbound** (50 connection requests/week, follow-up sequence, Sales Navigator or free tier) in parallel: 100 outreach → 20 responses → 5 meetings → 1-2 pilots = realistic benchmark.

### GTM Scalability Summary Table

| Assumption | Reality | Impact |
|------------|---------|--------|
| Personal network → 3-5 pilots in 3 months | 2-3 realistic; 2-4 month cycle | Phase 1 timeline is tight; zero slack |
| Case studies + LinkedIn → inbound pipeline | SEO takes 12-18 months; LinkedIn organic is noise | Months 4-6 have no reliable pipeline engine |
| Pilot clients → referrals | French B2B referrals require 6+ months of trust | Ask is premature; partner channel is better |
| Content SEO → sufficient GTM | Necessary but not sufficient | Needs parallel partner + outbound channels |

**Months 1-6 Plan (from agent):**
- M1-2: Close 2-3 pilots from network + build partner pipeline + systematic LinkedIn outbound (20-30 reqs/week)
- M3: Execute pilots flawlessly + activate 1-2 partners + first testimonial/case study
- M4-5: Scale outbound (50 reqs/week) + partner referrals (1-2/month combined) + formal referral incentive after 3 months managed
- M6: Evaluate — partner channel vs. outbound conversion rates, which verticals convert fastest

## Debate 9: Competitive Differentiation Moat

**Agent:** Competitive Differentiation Strategist
**File:** `/data/workspace/alize/research/differentiation-debate.md`

### Assumptions Challenged

**1. "Managed service is a defensible differentiator"**

Any French ESN (Capgemini, Devoteam, Sopra Steria, SQLI, Ekino) can say exactly this. They have the sales force, the credibility, and the technical talent to spin up an AI agents practice in 60-90 days.

> When a buyer with Microsoft 365 asks "why can't Capgemini do this? Why can't my existing IT vendor do this?", Alizé has no good answer in its current positioning. "Managed service" describes the service model, not a unique capability.

**2. "French hosting is a durable differentiator"**

OVHcloud is commodity hosting. Any competitor can host there. Microsoft, Google, and Salesforce all have EU data residency commitments. "French hosting" is baseline expectation in 2026, not a differentiator.

**The Mistral AI brand problem:** Mistral owns "French AI" in the French buyer's mind. Mistral has the brand, the distribution, the capital, and the infrastructure. If Mistral launches a managed agent service (likely within 12-18 months given their capital and ambition), Alizé's two primary claims ("French AI" + "managed service") both evaporate simultaneously.

**3. "Governance + human oversight = durable moat"**

These are configuration choices, not architectural moats. Agentova can add human approval gates in a product sprint. Microsoft Copilot Studio has data loss prevention and compliance policies built in. HubSpot Breeze has workflow approval steps. These features will be commoditized downmarket as AI agents become mainstream.

> Alizé's governance story is credible today because the big platforms don't care about the 50-250 employee segment. But as agents become mainstream, Microsoft and Salesforce will add "governance layer for SMB" as a checkbox feature. The window is 18-24 months.

### What Actually Holds Up as a Moat

| Moat | Status | Why |
|------|--------|-----|
| Managed service | ❌ Commodity | Any ESN can replicate |
| French hosting | ❌ Eroding | Commodity baseline; Mistral also French-hosted |
| "French AI" brand | ❌ Risky | Mistral owns it; Alizé conflicts |
| Governance / human oversight | ⚠️ Temporary | Real today, commoditized in 18-24 months |
| Vertical depth | ✅ Real | Takes years; not on Microsoft's roadmap |
| Data network effects | ⚠️ Potential | Only with significant scale |
| Relationship / trust | ⚠️ Fragile | People-dependent, doesn't scale |
| French tool integrations (Cegid, Sage, Pennylane) | ✅ Real (narrow) | Global players won't bother |

**The agent's key conclusion:** The only potentially durable moat is vertical depth — deep expertise in specific French industries (legal, accounting, healthcare admin) with pre-built workflows, domain-specific prompts, and integrations that no generic platform can replicate overnight.

### The 1-Sentence Buyer Answer (Current vs. Proposed)

**Current (weak):** "We deploy and manage AI agents for you on French servers with governance."

**Proposed (strong):** "Copilot Studio gives you a platform. Alizé gives you a working AI employee that actually knows French accounting workflows, connects to Cegid and Pennylane, and doesn't require your team to figure it out."

**The dangerous scenario:** If Mistral AI launches a managed agent service in the next 12-18 months, Alizé's current positioning is severely compromised. The only thing that saves Alizé is vertical depth that Mistral can't replicate quickly.

**Recommendation:** Stop leading with "managed service + French hosting + governance." Start leading with vertical depth and specific outcome claims. Pick 2-3 verticals and go deep. Without vertical depth, Alizé is a well-positioned boutique that gets squeezed by platforms or ESNs as the market matures.
