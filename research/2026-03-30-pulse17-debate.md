## Agent 1: GTM Challenger — Can Louis Actually Execute Warm Outreach?

### Assumption: Louis (web dev intern, no sales background) can personally lead warm network outreach and close pilots

**VERDICT: REVISE — This is the single most dangerous assumption in Alizé's plan**

#### Evidence Against:

**1. Grinto network conflict of interest**
Louis's warm network via Grinto means he's pitching Alizé to people who know him as an intern at Grinto. These contacts may:
- See Alizé as competing with Grinto (legitimate concern for his current employer)
- Not take a student seriously as a business vendor at €7,500-20,000/year
- Feel uncomfortable being sold to by someone who needs their approval at Grinto

**2. No sales skill = discovery calls will leak**
Discovery calls for AI agent services require:
- Building rapport with DG/COO-level decision makers
- Asking diagnostic questions without pitching
- Handling objections like "this seems expensive" and "we're not ready"
- Reading when a prospect is genuinely interested vs politely saying no

Louis has none of these skills. A bad first call with a warm contact burns that relationship permanently.

**3. LinkedIn cold/warm messaging at €7,500 price point fails**
LinkedIn DM works for recruitment and networking, not for closing B2B service deals at €7,500. French B2B buyers at the DG level expect:
- A proper company introduction (website, one-pager)
- A direct email or call, not a LinkedIn DM
- Proof that this person can deliver, not just pitch

**4. Time math doesn't work**
Louis is an M1 student with an internship at Grinto. He has maybe 10-15 hours/week for Alizé. Warm outreach at quality requires:
- Personalized research per contact (30-45 min each)
- Follow-up sequences (3-5 touches per contact)
- Discovery call prep and execution (1-2 hours per call)
- Proposal writing and revision

75-160 discovery calls × 2 hours each = 150-320 hours. That's 10-20 hours/week for 15+ weeks. Not sustainable during an internship.

#### Alternative Approach:

**Option A: Louis as intro network only**
Louis maps his warm network, sends intro emails, but brings in a sales closer for all calls and negotiations. Louis's role = warm introduction, not sales.

**Option B: Bring in sales co-founder NOW**
The first pilot won't close itself. If Alizé needs a business-focused co-founder or early hire to own sales, that person needs to be in Month 1, not Month 3.

**Option C: Lower price point to fit Louis's credibility**
A €1,500-2,000 pilot with simpler scope could work with Louis as closer. €7,500 requires DG-level conversations Louis can't credibly run.

#### Decision Needed:
- Does Louis have a sales co-founder or partner who can close warm network deals while Louis handles delivery?
- If not, either reduce pilot scope/price to match Louis's credibility, or accept 3-6 months to hire a sales person before starting outreach.

## Agent 2: Delivery Economics — Can the Model Actually Be Profitable?

### Assumption: €7,500 warm pilot + €400-450/day contractor = viable unit economics

**VERDICT: REVISE — Math works only under unrealistic assumptions**

#### Unit Economics Analysis:

**Warm pilot at €7,500:**
- Delivery days: 10-15 (realistic from Pulse 16)
- Contractor cost: 12 days × €450 = €5,400
- Louis time: ~5 days × €200 = €1,000 (opportunity cost)
- Alizé overhead (tools, infra, comms): ~€300
- **Total cost: €6,700-7,400**
- **Margin: €100-800 (1-10%)**

This assumes:
- No scope creep
- Client provides timely feedback
- No rework
- Contractor hits 12 days exactly

**Any adversity blows the model:**
- Client requests scope change: +3 days = loss
- Contractor underestimates: +3 days = loss
- Onboarding takes longer than expected: loss
- Louis can't cover because of Grinto internship: contractor solo = loss

**Managed service at €1,500/month (starter tier):**
- Ongoing monitoring: 2-4 hours/month
- Prompt updates: 1-2 hours/month
- Check-in calls: 1 hour/month
- Total: 4-7 hours/month × €200/hour = €800-1,400/month margin

**At 5 clients at €1,500/month:**
- Revenue: €7,500/month
- Delivery cost: ~5 × 5 hours × €100 = €2,500/month (contractor hourly)
- Margin: €5,000/month

This works at scale, but the pilot phase loses money. First 3 pilots = ~€15,000-20,000 in losses as CAC.

#### Structural Problem:

The €7,500 pilot fee is priced as if it's a productized deliverable. But AI agent deployment is consulting. Consulting engagements routinely run 20-50% over initial estimates because:
- Clients don't know what they want until they see it
- Business processes are messier than scoped
- Stakeholder alignment takes time

At €7,500 with a contractor, Alizé has no buffer for reality.

#### Alternative:

**Option A: Raise to €12,500 pilot**
At €12,500: 12 days × €450 = €5,400 + Louis €1,000 + overhead = €6,700. Margin = ~€5,800. Enough buffer for adversity and compensates Louis for his time.

**Option B: Fixed scope with hard limits**
 €7,500 buys exactly: 10 days delivery, 1 workflow, 2 tool integrations. Any expansion = change order at €750/day. Client must commit to timeline. This protects margin but may lose deals.

**Option C: First 2 pilots as pure CAC**
Accept losing €1,500-2,000 per pilot as marketing spend. Frame as "founding client rate." Build case studies that enable €12,500+ pilots in Month 3+.

## Agent 3: Positioning Challenger — "Delegation with Accountability" Under Fire

### Assumption: This positioning is coherent, differentiated, and persuasive to French PME buyers

**VERDICT: REVISE — Buzzword compliance that means nothing to a DG**

#### Buyer Reality Check:

Ask yourself: if you're a DG at a 60-person accounting firm in Caen, what does "delegation with accountability" tell you about Alizé?

Answer: Nothing. It's HR language applied to software. It sounds like a consulting firm wrote it.

French PME decision makers at 20-200 employees respond to:
- **Concrete numbers**: "We saved 12 hours/week on invoice processing for a 45-person firm"
- **Specific outcomes**: "Your team stops handling FAQ emails. The agent handles them."
- **Risk reduction**: "If it doesn't work, you don't pay" or "We fix it or you get X back"
- **Social proof**: "X companies like yours already use this"

"Delegation with accountability" checks none of these boxes.

#### Specific Weaknesses:

**1. "Delegation" implies giving up control**
French business owners are control-oriented (family businesses, PME culture). "Delegation" language activates loss-aversion before you've explained the benefit.

**2. "Accountability" is corporate noise**
Every vendor claims to be accountable. It has no meaning without specific metrics and consequences.

**3. Positioned against... what exactly?**
The framing doesn't name an enemy. Compare:
- Agentova: "AI agents that work while you sleep" (vs. you doing the work)
- ChatGPT: "Your AI assistant" (vs. starting from scratch)
- Alizé: "Delegation with accountability" (vs. what?)

**4. Doesn't survive first question**
When a DG asks "how is this different from just using ChatGPT?", "delegation with accountability" doesn't answer anything.

#### Stronger Alternative Positioning:

**Option A: Task-specific outcomes**
"Alizé deploys AI agents that handle your [invoice processing / FAQ answering / lead qualification]. Your team stops touching these tasks. You pay for results, not software."

**Option B: Problem-first**
"Every week your team spends 15 hours on repetitive admin tasks. Alizé automates those workflows so your people focus on real work. One workflow at a time."

**Option C: Contrast with DIY**
"You could build this yourself with AI tools. But it would take 3 months, require technical staff, and you'd maintain it yourself. Alizé deploys the same workflow in 2 weeks and manages it forever."

Louis has a technical internship, a Grinto job, and is a master's student. He is not a sales person. The current plan treats him as a potential sales person, which will produce mediocre results at best and damaged relationships at worst.

Resolve U52 (Louis network mapping) AND U15 (delivery model) together. The answer to both is: Louis identifies warm contacts, a sales closer handles outreach, and a delivery contractor handles execution. Louis's job = product, technical delivery, and company building — not sales.
