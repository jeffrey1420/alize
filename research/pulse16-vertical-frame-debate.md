## Agent Analysis

### The "Vertical Creates Moat" Thesis Requires Assets Alizé Doesn't Have

Vertical depth means something when you have:
- Industry-specific workflow templates
- Regulatory expertise in that vertical
- Pre-built agent configurations for common vertical use cases
- Relationships and references in that industry
- Understanding of the vertical's specific buying process

Alizé has none of these at Year 1. Saying "we focus on professional services" without any actual professional services expertise is a marketing claim, not a differentiator. The DG at a consulting firm will ask "what do you know about how consulting firms work?" and Alizé will have no specific answer.

Vertical moat is built through delivery, not declared in a positioning document.

### What Alizé Actually Has Is Horizontal Workflow Expertise

Louis knows how to:
- Design agent workflows around business processes
- Connect agents to business tools via APIs and MCP
- Structure RAG pipelines for business document bases
- Set up guardrails, permissions, and audit logs
- Optimize agent prompts for reliability

These are horizontal capabilities. They apply to:
- Invoice processing (accounting)
- CRM data enrichment (sales)
- Meeting note extraction (all industries)
- Ticket routing (customer service)
- HR policy Q&A (all industries with employees)

The expertise is in the automation, not in the vertical. Positioning as "vertical AI agents" overstates what Alizé is and misleads the buyer about what they're getting.

### "Pick a Vertical" Constrains the Market When Alizé Has No Proof Points

If Alizé commits to professional services as the vertical, then for Months 1-6, every outreach conversation must be with professional services companies. But Louis's warm network may have only 5-10 professional services fits. Alizé has now constrained itself to a tiny market segment before it has any evidence that this segment actually converts.

Meanwhile, the brief says 6-8 pilots is the Year 1 target (D19). If Alizé filters to only one vertical and Louis's network doesn't have enough fits, the entire Year 1 target is at risk.

### The Vertical Debate Is an Avoidance of the Harder Question

The real question the research should be asking is: **what can Alizé do that a generic AI consultant cannot?**

The answer is not "we know professional services." It's probably something like:
- "We have a repeatable system for deploying reliable business agents in 2-4 weeks"
- "We have pre-built integrations with French business tools (Pennylane, Holded, Salesforce FR)"
- "We have a playbook for onboarding agents that makes adoption fast"

These are horizontal capabilities that Alizé can claim today, without having picked a vertical. The vertical emerges from the delivery experience, not from a positioning decision.

### The Right Year 1 Strategy: Evidence-First, Not Theory-First

Louis's warm network is the only real market signal Alizé has. The right move is:
1. Map the warm network against the ICP (50-200 employees, 3+ tools, operational overhead)
2. Identify which contacts fit
3. Pursue the fits regardless of industry
4. Learn from delivery which workflows and which verticals actually convert
5. Let vertical insight emerge from evidence, not precede it

If 70% of warm network fits are in e-commerce, start with e-commerce. If they're in professional services, start there. Strategy follows evidence.

---

## Alternative Frame: Workflow-First vs Vertical-First

| Approach | Definition | Advantage | Risk |
|---------|-----------|-----------|------|
| **Vertical-first** | Pick one industry, go deep | Potential moat, industry expertise | Premature without proof; constrains addressable market |
| **Workflow-first** | Pick workflow type regardless of industry | Flexible, honest about capabilities; responsive to evidence | No industry depth; harder to differentiate |
| **Network-first** | Follow warm network wherever it leads | Highest conversion probability; lowest CAC | Not strategic; opportunistic |

The evidence suggests Alizé should do network-first at Month 1, then use learnings to define the vertical strategy at Month 3-4.

---

## Key Findings / Challenges

1. **"Vertical creates moat" requires vertical assets Alizé doesn't have** — you can't claim vertical expertise before you've delivered in that vertical
2. **Alizé's actual expertise is horizontal** — workflow automation, not industry knowledge
3. **Picking a vertical before having evidence constrains the market unnecessarily**
4. **The vertical debate is an avoidance** — the real question is what makes Alizé different from any other AI consultant
5. **Network-first is the right Month 1 strategy** — follow the warm contacts that fit the ICP, learn from delivery

---

## What This Means for Alizé

**Stop trying to pre-determine the vertical. Start with the workflow.**

The external positioning should say: "Alizé deploys AI agents that handle your repetitive operational tasks — invoice processing, CRM updates, meeting notes, support ticket routing, internal Q&A."

Industry-specific positioning ("for accounting firms" / "for professional services") should only come after Alizé has 2-3 pilots in a specific industry and has learned what works.

Month 1 action: Map Louis's warm network against ICP. Do not make a vertical decision until that's done.
