## Agent 1: Vertical Challenge (Professional Services)

### Challenged Assumption: "Consulting firms are the right first vertical" (D44)

D44 resolved: "Professional services (consulting firms, 10-50 employees) over digital agencies/e-commerce" based on "buy-vs-build problem" and budget fit.

### Key Counter-Arguments

**"Buy vs build" is the wrong framing for small consulting firms.**
Small consulting firms (10-50 people) don't think in software platform decisions. Their instinct when facing a repetitive task is: (a) do it manually, (b) hire a part-time VA/intern, or (c) ask their "guy who knows computers." The buy-vs-build framing presupposes a level of software procurement sophistication that doesn't exist at this size.

**Consulting firm workflows are largely non-standardized.**
A 25-person consulting firm has 3-8 partners, each running client engagements with their own methods and tools. There is no company-wide workflow — only partner A's way, partner B's way, and partner C who uses nothing structured. You cannot systematize what hasn't been systematized.

**The highest-value consulting tasks are NOT automatable.**
Consultants spend time on: client strategy, relationship management, presentations, research, proposal writing. The automatable parts (data entry, scheduling, timesheets) are low-value overhead. A €1,500+/month AI agent needs to justify on high-value work — but the high-value work IS the consulting.

**Budget mismatch.**
10-50 person consulting firms in France have perhaps €500-1,000/month in "operational improvement" budget. The Alizé minimum (€1,200-1,500/month starter) is at or above what this market considers acceptable — before any proof of ROI exists.

**The "Louis's network" logic is proximity, not strategy.**
If Louis's network points to consulting firms because of Grinto's existing relationships, that's a sales trail, not a strategic vertical choice. Following it conflates personal connections with market fit.

### Recommendation
**D44 should be reconsidered.** The "buy-vs-build" argument doesn't hold for small consulting firms that don't think in software platforms. The vertical needs either (a) a different slice of professional services (accounting firms, legal practices — more standardized workflows), or (b) a return to the digital agencies/e-commerce thesis with explicit mitigation of the "can build their own" objection.

## Agent 2: Bridge Channel Challenge (Month 4-6)

### Challenged Assumption: "2-3 bridge channels can be defined for Month 4-6" (D62)

D62 requires "2-3 alternative channels for Month 4-6 when warm network depletes." Nothing specific has been defined. The agent challenged whether any bridge channel is viable without case studies.

### Key Findings

**The uncomfortable truth: Month 4-6 bridge channels are unanswerable without case studies.**
Every realistic bridge channel requires something to show:
- **Malt freelancer marketplace**: Needs 1-2 completed pilots with measurable results, compelling profile, specific use cases. Without case studies, Alizé's Malt profile is indistinguishable from 500 generic "AI consultant" profiles.
- **LinkedIn cold outbound**: Needs case study to transform messaging from "we build AI agents" to "we automated X workflow for a company like yours, saving Y hours." Without it, cold outbound stays at 2-3% reply rates.
- **Partner/referral networks**: Need credibility to be introduced. A new partner won't refer clients to an unknown vendor with no proof points.

**Ranked bridge channels for Month 4-6:**

| Rank | Channel | Realistic? | Timeline | Month 4-6 Target |
|------|---------|------------|----------|-----------------|
| 1 | Malt marketplace | Medium-high | 6-10 weeks to first inquiry | 2-4 discovery conversations |
| 2 | LinkedIn cold outbound (augmented with case study) | Medium | Month 2+ after case study exists | 5-15 reply rate, 1-2 pilots |
| 3 | Paid search (Google Ads) | Low-medium | Immediate but expensive | 10-20 leads/month, low conversion |

**The channel that doesn't exist yet: content/SEO.** Brief says Month 9+. Correct. Content marketing takes 12-18 months to generate meaningful inbound. Do not count on it for Month 4-6.

### Recommendation
**D62 is premature.** The bridge channel question cannot be answered until Alizé has 1-2 completed pilots. The Month 4-6 plan should be: execute Month 1-3 pilots, validate the model, then define bridge channels based on actual learnings. Do not pre-define channels that will be invalidated by reality.

## Agent 3: Delivery Partner & First Case Study Challenge

### Challenged Assumption: "The current delivery model produces a usable first case study"

Current plan: check Gabin first (D48), then Malt for external delivery partner at €350/day + €1,500 success fee (D49). Cap at €3,250 if pilot doesn't convert. Pilot fee: €4,500 warm network.

### Key Findings

**Gabin is a web developer. That is not a delivery lead.**
The delivery model requires: process mapping, change management, stakeholder management, French DG-level conversation, ROI quantification. Gabin has technical execution ability. That's the other half of delivery — not the business half. Unless Gabin has undisclosed PME consulting experience (U41 — still unresolved), Louis + Gabin = two technical people.

**5 delivery days is not enough. Realistic: 10-15 days.**
The €3,250 cap assumes 5 delivery days. The delivery milestones (D64) require: Day 7 process map, Day 14 agent deployed, Day 21 adoption check, Day 28 ROI document. That's 3+ days of direct client-facing time minimum, plus prep, syncs, and documentation. Realistic total: 10-15 delivery days.

At 10 days × €350/day = €3,500 base fee. Already exceeds the cap.

**The success-fee structure misaligns incentives.**
At €350/day, a delivery partner who spends 10 days delivers at a loss (€3,500 > €3,250 cap). The success fee only helps if they spend ≤7 days AND the pilot converts. Quality delivery requires more time than the model allocates.

**What a credible case study actually requires:**
- Real company name or identifiable profile (industry + size + location)
- Specific challenge and what Alizé did
- Measurable outcomes: hours saved, error rate reduction, task completion rate
- Client testimonial or quote
- The pilot must be operationally critical, not "nice to have"

None of this happens if the delivery partner is rushed, under-resourced, or unqualified.

### The Core Problem
The €4,500 pilot fee was designed as an "at-cost + case study rights" investment. But the delivery model adds a delivery partner cost that the pilot fee doesn't fully cover. Scenario math:

| Scenario | Days | Delivery Cost | Revenue | Net |
|----------|------|--------------|---------|-----|
| 5 days, converts | 5 | €3,250 | €4,500 | +€1,250 |
| 10 days, converts | 10 | €5,000 | €4,500 | -€500 |
| 10 days, no convert | 10 | €3,500 | €4,500 | +€1,000 |

The model works only if delivery stays at 5 days AND the pilot converts. Both conditions are optimistic.

### Recommendations
1. **Validate Gabin's consulting background before Month 1** (U41 — Day 1-3 action)
2. **Increase pilot fee to €6,500 for warm network** — covers realistic 10-day delivery with margin
3. **OR reduce delivery scope to match €4,500** — limit to 5 focused days with explicit milestone gates
4. **Do not promise case study rights in the contract** — negotiate it after the pilot if the client is satisfied

## Summary for Louis

Three critical assumptions were challenged:

1. **Vertical:** The "professional services first" decision (D44) may be wrong. Small consulting firms don't think in software platform decisions, their workflows are non-standardized, and €1,500+/month may exceed their budget. Accounting firms or legal practices may be a better fit — more standardized workflows, compliance drivers, and they buy services (not tools).

2. **Bridge channels:** The Month 4-6 bridge channel plan cannot be defined until Alizé has case studies. Every realistic channel (Malt, LinkedIn cold outbound, partners) requires something to show. The right move: execute Month 1-3, then define channels based on what actually worked.

3. **Delivery model:** The delivery partner model may not produce a usable case study. The €3,250 budget assumes 5 days; realistic is 10-15. The success-fee structure misaligns incentives. Gabin's background is still unknown. Either raise the pilot fee to €6,500 or cut delivery scope to match €4,500.
