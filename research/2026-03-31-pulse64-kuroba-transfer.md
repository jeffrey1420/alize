# Pulse 64 — Kuroba Evidence Transfer Challenge

## Assumption Challenged

**"Kuroba internal deployment → Alizé commercial evidence" constitutes a valid learning transfer path.**

The existing debate (Kuroba Sequencing Auditor, Pulse 63) challenged Kuroba-first on scale mismatch and deferral grounds. This challenge takes a different angle: even *if* Kuroba deploys successfully and even *if* the 2-3 person scale were comparable, the evidence generated is categorically wrong for the commercial purpose it claims to serve.

---

## Specific Claims Under Examination

### Claim 1: "Deploying Kuroba for Maëli/Maeli teaches Louis how to sell Alizé"

**False category.** Deploying Kuroba for Maëli produces **operational evidence** — does the workflow function, does it save time, does the automation run reliably. Selling Alizé requires **commercial evidence** — what the pricing conversation sounds like, what objections arise, how to handle a DG's skepticism, what Copilot comparisons prospects actually raise. These are different knowledge types. The first is a product capability question. The second is a market-facing communication skill. Kuroba generates the first. It cannot generate the second.

### Claim 2: "Maëli's feedback is a proxy for client feedback"

**Adversarial dynamics are absent.** Maëli is a co-founder who already trusts Louis. She will not:
- Challenge the value proposition with genuine skepticism
- Ask "why should I pay for this when Copilot does it?"
- Push back on pricing with budget constraints
- Escalate to a DG who doesn't see the ROI
- Raise competitive alternatives unprompted

A prospect who doesn't know Louis will do all of these things. The feedback Louis gets from Maëli is relationship-filtered. It is the easiest, least adversarial feedback possible. It teaches Louis nothing about how his message will land with a stranger who has real alternatives and budget pressure.

### Claim 3: "Internal deployment produces 'real evidence' vs. hypothetical positioning"

This conflates two types of reality:
- **Internal real:** Kuroba actually runs for Maëli. The workflow actually saves time.
- **External real:** A prospect in a 30-minute call decides whether to engage further.

Neither type of reality is produced by the other. Louis can have a fully functional Kuroba deployment for Maëli and still have no idea what D226 sounds like when delivered by a stranger with actual budget authority. The Kuroba deployment is real in a way that is irrelevant to the commercial challenge.

---

## Counter-Evidence

### What Kuroba Actually Teaches Louis

1. **Workflow operation:** Does the automation run reliably for Maëli? → Valid operational evidence, useful for delivery confidence.
2. **Time savings (internal):** Does Maëli save time on her tasks? → Valid internal evidence of product value, relationship-filtered.
3. **Technical friction:** What breaks when Louis deploys to someone else? → Valid ops learning, single-user context.

### What Kuroba Cannot Teach Louis

1. **Pricing conversation dynamics:** Does "€X/month" trigger budget pushback? What price point causes prospects to disengage? What payment terms do French PME DGs actually accept? → Unknown. Maëli has no budget constraint and no external accountability for the purchase decision.

2. **Competitive objection handling:** Does "we're not Copilot, we're project-first" land or confuse? What Copilot comparison does a real prospect actually raise — Copilot Chat, Copilot Studio, Microsoft 365 Copilot overall? → Unknown. Maëli already understands the distinction because she's heard Louis explain it repeatedly.

3. **Decision-maker navigation:** What does a 30-minute discovery call feel like with a DG who didn't take the meeting because of a warm introduction? → Unknown. Maëli took the meeting because she's a co-founder who already believes.

4. **Message resonance:** Does the D226 positioning sentence actually land as compelling to someone who isn't already convinced AI workflow automation is worth paying for? → Unknown. Maëli is already convinced.

5. **Urgency framing:** What makes a prospect move from "interesting, let's talk in Q3" to "let's pilot in March"? → Unknown. Maëli has no reason to create artificial urgency.

### The Core Asymmetry

Louis deploying Kuroba for Maëli is **practice performing a task he already knows how to do** (operational deployment) in a context that doesn't require the skills he's missing (commercial persuasion, objection handling, competitive navigation, budget negotiation).

The Kuroba deployment is practice at the *easy part* of delivery — the part Louis could already do — in a context where the hard part (client-facing commercial dynamics) is removed by design.

---

## What Would Have to Be True For This Assumption to Hold

For Kuroba internal deployment to genuinely transfer to Alizé commercial evidence, all of the following would have to be true simultaneously:

1. **Maëli must behave as a adversarial stranger with budget constraints** — she must challenge pricing, raise competitive alternatives, and apply real budget pressure despite having no financial stake in the decision. (She will not.)

2. **Louis must treat Maëli as a commercial prospect, not a co-founder** — he must run real discovery calls with her, practice pricing conversations, and receive genuine objections. (He cannot. The relationship structure prevents it.)

3. **The workflow outcome must translate to prospect-relevant value proof** — Maëli's time savings must map to "what a DG at a 100-person firm cares about." These may not overlap. A co-founder who saves 2 hours/week on a specific task and a DG who cares about cross-department project visibility have different value frames.

4. **The delivery context must replicate commercial delivery dynamics** — the stakes, accountability, and negotiation must be real. (They are not.)

If even one of these is false — and all four are — the evidence transfer fails.

---

## Decision (kill/revise/hold with conditions)

**Kill: Kuroba internal deployment as a source of commercial evidence for Alizé.**

The assumption that "Kuroba internal use → Alizé commercial learning" is epistemologically broken. Internal deployment generates a different evidence type than external commercial engagement requires. No amount of successful Kuroba operation for Maëli will tell Louis what a prospect says when they hear the pricing for the first time, how to handle a Copilot comparison in a live call, or what a DG actually decides based on D226's positioning sentence.

### Revised Framing (if Kuroba is pursued at all)

Kuroba internal deployment is only legitimate as:
- **Operational rehearsal** (does the technology work?) — useful, limited
- **NOT commercial rehearsal** (does Louis know how to sell it?) — broken transfer

If Kuroba is deployed at all, it should be explicitly scoped to operational learning only, with zero claim attached that it develops commercial capability. Any commercial learning must come from actual external engagement: warm outreach calls, discovery calls, pilot conversations. These are the only evidence sources that produce commercial evidence.

### Immediate Implication

The fastest path to commercial evidence is not Kuroba. It is the warm outreach that has been deferred for 62+ pulses. Louis's warm network (D26, D118) produces commercial evidence on day one of outreach: what prospects say, what objections arise, what pricing framing lands. Kuroba cannot produce this evidence regardless of how many Maëli workflows it automates.

**The commercial evidence gap is not a delivery preparation problem. It is an outreach gap. Close the outreach gap first.**
