### Key Findings

D84 establishes a ceiling at €7,500+. It does NOT establish a floor. The decision says nothing about what Louis *can* close. The inference that "€2,000 is safe because it's well below €7,500" is not supported by D84 — it is an assumption invented to make the €2,000 price point feel viable.

**The credibility ceiling problem has never been quantified below €7,500.** We don't know:
- Can Louis close €5,000? €3,000? €1,500?
- At what price point does Louis's profile (M1 student, intern, unknown brand) become disqualifying?
- Is there any price point at which Louis's profile becomes *credible* to a French PME DG, or does the problem persist at every level?

**D138 attempted to reframe D90 for €1,500 closes** (Louis + sales coach sufficient). But D138 was never validated. The reframed position — "Louis can close €1,500 with a sales coach" — remains theoretical. There is no empirical evidence that a French PME DG will sign a €2,000 contract with a 22-year-old M1 student they've never heard of, regardless of coaching.

**The real question D84 raises:** Is Louis's credibility ceiling actually €0 at solo-close without a senior commercial presence? If so, the €2,000 assumption fails regardless of how far below €7,500 it sits.

#### 2. Louis Is Not a Credible Seller at Any Price Point — The Problem Is Structural

D74 bundles three problems as equivalent:
1. Louis lacks sales skills
2. Louis lacks sales format (scripts, materials)
3. Louis has a Grinto conflict

Pulse 20 (credibility debate) identified that the **Grinto conflict is structural and underaddressed**. Even with perfect sales skills and materials, Louis's credential gap is structural.

**What Louis's profile actually communicates to a French PME DG:**
- "I'm an M1 master student" — not a consultant, not a founder with track record, not a senior practitioner
- "I intern at a web agency" — the buyer will ask what Grinto does; when they learn it's a web development agency, they'll wonder why you're not just building the website yourself
- "Alizé has no brand" — no case studies, no peer references, no third-party validation

**The €2,000 price point does not solve the credibility problem.** It merely reduces the stakes. A DG who wouldn't sign a €7,500 contract with Louis might sign a €2,000 contract — but not because Louis became credible. Because the downside is smaller. This is risk tolerance, not credibility. And risk tolerance has limits: a €2,000 check still requires someone to decide it's worth signing.

**The core problem is not price.** It's that Louis's profile does not signal "someone who should be trusted to deliver a professional services engagement." At any price.

#### 3. The Grinto Conflict Makes "Warm Outreach" a Misnomer — Not Impossible, But Corrupted

**D74's diagnosis:** "Louis should NOT solo-close warm network deals — too many conflicts."

**D87's addition:** The network narrative becomes permanent after 3-6 months of failed closes.

**What this means in practice:**

Every warm contact Louis approaches faces an immediate disqualifying question: *"Why isn't Louis doing this through Grinto?"*

- If Louis approaches a company that could use web development → Grinto competes for that business
- If Louis approaches a company in a different vertical → why should they take a meeting with a student they've known casually when serious vendors exist?
- If Louis approaches a former classmate at a 50-person company → that person will ask "why are you reaching out now? what do you want?" and the Alizé pitch will feel opportunistic

**The "warm" in warm outreach is compromised.** These are not relationships built around Alizé's value proposition. They are relationships Louis built in a different context (MyDigitalSchool, Grinto internship, Kuroba co-founding). The warmth transfers to a social context, not a commercial one.

**The conflict is not that warm outreach is impossible — it's that it corrodes rather than builds.** Every failed warm outreach attempt damages the relationship AND the Alizé narrative simultaneously. D87's credibility window framing applies here: warm contacts who say no become permanent "no's" AND they tell others about the interaction.

**The Grinto conflict problem is actually worse than D74/D87 suggest** because:
- Louis's warm network is concentrated in the web agency / digital product space (per Pulse 35)
- That is precisely the space where Grinto competes
- Therefore Louis's warmest contacts are the ones with the most direct conflict

#### 4. What Sales Motion Fits Louis's Constraints at €2,000

The brief says: "Not LinkedIn DMs, not cold email." Correct — those require either brand credibility (which doesn't exist) or cold prospecting discipline (which Louis hasn't demonstrated in 35 pulses).

**The actual viable motion: Demo-led, prototype-first, zero-pitch.**

Here's the structure that fits Louis's constraints:

**Step 1 — Build the prototype before any outreach.**
Louis builds a working meeting report agent (D81) for himself. He uses it daily. He has real output to show.

**Step 2 — Show, don't sell.**
Louis shows the prototype to ONE warm contact who has the same workflow problem. Not a pitch. A demonstration. "I built this for myself. Would it be useful for your team?" The prospect evaluates a working tool, not a sales pitch.

**Step 3 — If they want one, ask for the pilot.**
If the response is "yes, I want this," the close happens at the product level, not the commercial level. The conversation becomes "would you like me to set one up for you?" not "would you like to sign a €2,000 contract?"

**Step 4 — Frame as research partnership if needed.**
For the first 2-3 pilots, frame as "I'm validating this workflow with real users. No cost for the first month if you give me feedback." This removes the sales close entirely. The prospect opts in based on seeing value, not being pitched.

**Why this works:**
- Louis's credibility gap is sidestepped by showing, not telling
- The prototype does the selling; Louis just demonstrates
- No discovery call required (D90's closing problem avoided)
- The warm contact's social pressure toward a colleague/friend is replaced by their own intrinsic interest in the tool
- Grinto conflict is irrelevant — the prospect is buying a working tool, not contracting a web development project

**Why this is the ONLY motion that fits:**
- Louis can't pitch (no sales skills)
- Louis can't close (no commercial credibility)
- Louis can't cold outreach (brand unknown, no pipeline)
- Louis can't use warm outreach with pitch (burns relationships, D87 risk)
- Louis CAN show a working prototype — that is the one thing his technical profile enables

#### 5. The Commercial Layer Gate (D90) Is Irrelevant at €2,000 — But Not for the Reason D138 Claims

**D138 said:** "Warm outreach starts Month 1 with Louis + sales coach; commercial co-founder not a prerequisite at €1,500."

**What's wrong with this reframing:**

D138 assumes the commercial layer gate was calibrated to €7,500+. Lower the price, remove the gate. This logic is flawed because:

**At €2,000, Louis still cannot:**
- Answer "qui d'autre a déjà fait ça?" (no case studies)
- Provide peer references (none exist)
- Navigate "je dois vendre ça à mon équipe" (no reference clients to point to)
- Handle "c'est trop cher pour un étudiant" (profile-based price resistance)

**The commercial layer gate (D90) should NOT be removed for €2,000.** It should be **reframed from "gate" to "motion change."**

The correct reframe:
- OLD: "Warm outreach blocked until closing-capable person exists"
- NEW: "Warm outreach requires a demo-led motion, not a pitch-led motion. Closing-capable person gates pitch-led close. Demo-led close is not a close — it's a product adoption decision."

At €2,000 with a demo-led motion, Louis doesn't need to close because the prospect decides based on the demo, not the pitch. The commercial layer problem is structurally avoided, not resolved.

### Verdict

**The assumption "Louis can sell and close a €2,000 pilot deal to a warm contact" is WRONG in its current form.**

What it gets right: €2,000 is closer to Louis's capability range than €7,500. The price reduction helps.

What it gets wrong:
1. **No price point within D84's range has been validated.** €2,000 may still be above Louis's credibility ceiling.
2. **Louis's profile is disqualifying at every price point** for pitch-led sales. The solution is not price optimization — it's motion change.
3. **Warm outreach corrodes rather than builds** when conducted as pitch-led sales. D87's credibility window risk applies at €2,000, not just €7,500.
4. **The commercial layer gate (D90) was never the real problem.** The real problem is that pitch-led close requires credibility Louis doesn't have at any price. D90 gates pitch-led close; the solution is to eliminate pitch-led close, not to find a closer.

### What Changed

**Finding 1 — THE WRONG ASSUMPTION (flag for revision):**
D84 says Louis cannot close €7,500+ solo. The implicit inference — that he CAN close €2,000 solo — is not in D84. This is an assumption added after the fact to make €2,000 feel viable. It is not supported by evidence.

**Finding 2 — Credibility ceiling has never been calibrated below €7,500:**
The research has never asked: at what price does Louis's profile become sufficient? There may not be any price at which a 22-year-old M1 student with no brand and a Grinto conflict can pitch-close a French PME DG. The €2,000 assumption assumes the answer is "somewhere above €2,000" without evidence.

**Finding 3 — D90 reframing (D138) was incomplete:**
D138 said Louis + sales coach is sufficient for €1,500 closes. But the coach doesn't solve the structural credibility problem — the coach solves the skills problem. These are different problems. A coach can teach Louis to run a discovery call. No coach can make Louis's profile credible to a DG who has never heard of Alizé.

**Finding 4 — Warm outreach is not just gated, it's structurally compromised:**
The Grinto conflict means Louis's warm network is concentrated in the wrong space (digital/web) AND saturated with conflict-of-interest signals. Warm outreach as pitch should not start in Month 1 regardless of who closes — because the pitch itself damages the relationship before the close is even attempted.

### Recommendations for Louis

**1. Kill the pitch-led warm outreach motion entirely.**
Louis should not approach warm contacts with a sales pitch. The pitch close requires credibility Louis doesn't have and the warm network doesn't have the right composition. D90 (commercial layer gates outreach) should be replaced with: outreach requires demo-ready prototype, not pitch-ready closer.

**2. Build the prototype first. Always.**
The meeting report agent (D81) is the sales motion. Not a deck. Not a discovery call. A working agent that does the job. Louis builds it for himself, uses it, shows it. The demo closes the deal, not Louis's words.

**3. Frame first pilots as research partnerships, not sales.**
"I'm building this for real companies. Would you let me automate one of your workflows for free in exchange for feedback?" This removes the close entirely. The prospect opts in based on curiosity + value, not sales pressure.

**4. Map your warm network for demo readiness, not pitch readiness.**
Before any outreach: who has the meeting report workflow problem? Who would find a working demo compelling? The warm network should be filtered for workflow match, not relationship warmth. A stranger with the exact problem is a better first pilot than a friend with a vague interest.

**5. The commercial co-founder question (U86) is still unresolved — but for a different reason.**
D84/D90 framed U86 around €7,500+. The real U86 question is: at what price point, if any, can Louis pitch-close without burning the relationship? Until that question is answered, any pitch-led close is a risk. The demo-led motion sidesteps this — but it means Alizé's growth is bounded by Louis's ability to build demos, not his ability to sell.

**6. Resolve U170 before any outreach.**
"Can Louis actually build and deploy an AI agent for a real client?" If the answer is no, the demo-led motion fails just as badly as the pitch-led motion. The prototype must work. If it doesn't, there is no motion that saves Month 1.
