# Managed Service Positioning — Asset or Liability?
## Alizé Strategy Debate | Strategy Specialist #1

**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Role:** Devil's Advocate / Strategy Critic
**Mission:** Challenge the assumption that "managed service" is a differentiator for Alizé

---

## Verdict: **LIABILITY** — with caveats

"Managed service" is corporate IT outsourcing language that triggers the wrong mental model in the target buyer. It positions Alizé as a vendor you manage, when the actual value is an AI employee you don't manage. For a French DG/COO of a 50-200 person company, "managed service" reads as SSII/ESN-speak — and that industry has spent 30 years teaching French businesses to distrust it.

---

## The Three Fatal Problems with "Managed Service"

### Problem 1: It Means the Opposite of What Alizé Actually Does

**What "managed service" signals to a French DG/COO:**
- "I will have a vendor" → relationship with overhead
- "Someone is managing something ON MY BEHALF" → I still have to manage them
- "This is an ongoing service contract" → I need to monitor SLAs, chase deliverables, have meetings about the service
- "There will be a steering committee" → French corporate speak for expensive meetings where nothing moves

**What Alizé actually does:**
- Gives you an AI employee that works
- You don't manage it, you don't monitor it, you don't have quarterly reviews with it
- It's simply... working. Like an employee who doesn't need supervision.

The disconnect is fundamental. "Managed service" puts the management burden on the client. The Brief's own promise — "useful AI agents that do real work, not demos, not chatbots" — implies the opposite: the agent just works, you don't have to manage it.

**Challenge to existing assumption #1:**
The Brief frames "managed service" as the differentiator vs. Agentova's self-service model. The logic is: Agentova = you do it yourself. Alizé = we do it for you. This is correct as a product description, but the term "managed service" is the wrong label for it.

"Managed service" is what a French ESN would call their offering. "We manage your IT infrastructure." "We manage your backup systems." It implies infrastructure, maintenance, and a vendor relationship — not an AI employee.

**Better frame:** "AI employees that work for you." Or "Your AI team." Or "AI agents that work, so you don't have to."

---

### Problem 2: "Managed Service" Collapses Against French ESN/SSII Messaging

**What French ESNs say when they pitch "intelligence artificielle":**
- "Nous gérons vos systèmes IA" (We manage your AI systems)
- "Service IA géré" (Managed AI service)
- "Solution IA clé en main" (Turnkey AI solution)
- "Accompagnement IA" (AI support/guidance)

This is not hypothetical. The Brief itself identifies ESNs as a competitive category. When Alizé says "managed service," it is using the exact same vocabulary as the companies it needs to differentiate from.

**A French DG who has sat through 10 ESN pitches in the last 2 years has already heard "service géré IA."** Alizé's positioning lands as: "another managed AI service, like the one Sopra Steria tried to sell me, that cost €200K and delivered a PowerPoint."

**Challenge to existing assumption #2:**
The Brief assumes "managed service" differentiates from ESNs because Alizé is more agile, more focused, and actually delivers working agents. This is a product differentiation, not a messaging differentiation. The term doesn't carry that distinction.

The Brief's own language is more differentiated than "managed service": "useful AI agents that do real work — not demos, not chatbots, not promises." This is direct, credible, and specific. "Managed service" dilutes it back into corporate speak.

---

### Problem 3: It Creates the Wrong First Impression in Cold Outreach

**Scenario:** A French COO sees a LinkedIn DM or visits the landing page. The first thing they process is "service géré."

Their internal reaction:
1. "Service géré... comme chez [ESN name]?" (Managed service... like at [ESN name]?)
2. "Ça va être un prestataire de plus à manager." (This is going to be one more vendor to manage.)
3. "Ils vont me demander des réunions de suivi tous les mois." (They're going to want monthly steering meetings.)
4. "J'ai déjà ça avec mon intégrateur, merci." (I already have that with my integrator, thanks.)

This is the wrong mental model. Alizé's actual value proposition is: you have an AI employee. You don't have a vendor relationship. You don't manage it. It works or it doesn't — and if it doesn't, WE fix it, not you.

**"Managed service" is a B2B SaaS term for vendors. "AI employee" is a business owner's term for what Alizé actually delivers.**

---

## What the Research Gets Right (and Why It's Not Enough)

The competitor analysis correctly identifies that:
- Agentova is self-service (you configure it yourself)
- Alizé is managed (we deploy and maintain for you)
- This is a real and meaningful distinction

The problem is that "managed service" describes the product mechanics, not the buyer outcome. The buyer doesn't want managed AI infrastructure. They want a problem solved. They want repetitive work off their team's plate. They want to stop hiring for roles that are 60% tedious tasks.

**The product truth:** "We build AI employees for your business, connect them to your tools, and make sure they work. You don't lift a finger after onboarding."

**The "managed service" frame:** "We provide a managed service for AI agent deployment with ongoing governance and optimization."

One of these sounds like a business solution. One sounds like an IT contract.

---

## Recommended Alternative Positioning

### Primary Frame: "AI Employees" (or "Your AI Team")

**Language to test:**
- "Des employés IA qui travaillent pour vous" (AI employees that work for you)
- "Votre équipe IA, déployée et gérée" (Your AI team, deployed and managed)
- "L'IA qui bosse. Vraiment." (The AI that works. Really.)
- "Des agents IA qui font le travail — pas des démos" (AI agents that do the work — not demos)

This framing:
1. Creates the correct mental model: an employee, not a vendor
2. Makes the ROI obvious: you're hiring (renting) an employee
3. Sidesteps the ESN vocabulary problem
4. Lands the Brief's promise ("real work, not demos") in a more visceral way

### Secondary Frame: "AI That Works" (Contrast Positioning)

**Language to test:**
- "L'IA qui fonctionne. Résultat inclus." (AI that works. Results included.)
- "Pas un chatbot. Un employee." (Not a chatbot. An employee.)

This directly delivers on the Brief's anti-demos positioning but in a punchier, more memorable way.

### Where to Keep "Managed" (Technical/Backend Context Only)

"Managed" works in:
- Technical documentation
- Pricing page descriptions (Offer 3: "Service géré")
- B2B SaaS audiences who expect this vocabulary

"Managed" should NOT be:
- The primary positioning statement
- The hero headline or subheadline
- The LinkedIn outreach hook
- The domain-level tagline

---

## Concrete Action for Louis

**Replace "managed service" as the primary positioning term on the landing page and in outreach.** Test "AI employees" or "your AI team" as the primary frame. Keep "managed service" as a secondary descriptor (e.g., "we manage the agent so you don't have to") but don't lead with it.

Specifically:
- On the landing page hero: test "Des employés IA qui travaillent pour vous" vs. the current framing
- In LinkedIn outreach: test "On déploie des agents IA dans votre entreprise — vous, vous n'avez rien à gérer" (We deploy AI agents in your company — you don't have to manage anything)
- In the Brief: update the positioning statement from "French managed AI agent service" to something like "French AI employee service" or "AI agents that work for you"

The differentiator vs. Agentova isn't "managed vs. self-service" — it's "an AI employee vs. a SaaS tool you configure yourself." Same distinction, better language.

---

## Conclusion

"Managed service" is a product description masquerading as marketing. It describes HOW Alizé delivers value (we manage it) rather than WHAT the buyer gets (an AI employee that works). For a French DG/COO burned by ESN projects, it triggers the wrong associations and creates the wrong expectations.

Louis should replace "managed service" as the primary positioning term with outcome-oriented, employment-framed language. The product is differentiated. The vocabulary isn't — yet.
