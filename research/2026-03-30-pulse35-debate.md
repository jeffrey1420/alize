## Agent 1: Pricing Stage Auditor — D177

**Challenge target:** The 3-stage staged pilot structure (€500 discovery → €1,000 POC → €1,200-1,500/month)

### Key Findings

**D177's core flaw: the "credited toward" mechanic embedded in Stages 1 and 2.**

The pricing architect intended "credited" as a trust signal — money held toward a future engagement. In practice it is accounting poison:

- **Stage 1 and Stage 2 are not standalone deliverables** — they are installments toward something else
- **The client is not buying a discovery sprint or a POC** — they are prepaying a future contract
- At Month 1, the client correctly observes: *"I've already paid €1,500. Why am I paying again?"*

**The "credited" framing signals that Phases 1 and 2 are not independently valuable.** If they were, you wouldn't need to credit them.

### Five Specific Problems

1. **Three-stage structure multiplies the number of close attempts Louis must make.** D84 established Louis cannot credibly close €7,500+ alone. D177 doesn't eliminate the credibility problem — it multiplies it. Louis must now close Stage 1 (€500), then Stage 2 (€1,000), then Stage 3 (€1,200-1,500/month). Three separate decision points. The client who said yes to €500 has €500 of evidence that Alizé needs the next stage. Power dynamic inverts.

2. **"Credited" creates psychological anchor that poisons Month 1 pricing.** Client math at Stage 3 gate: *"I've already paid €1,500 for discovery and POC. Now they want €1,200-1,500/month on top? That's €2,700-3,000 before the relationship really starts."* The client doesn't experience the €1,500 as prepaid — they experience it as money already spent on unproven work.

3. **A client can exit Stage 2 with a working POC they own and no obligation to continue.** The proof gate at Stage 2 — "working agent in test, connected to live tool" — gives the client exactly what they need to evaluate whether to continue. They can take that evidence to a competitor, build it in-house, or simply wait. The proof gate that was supposed to *secure* the retainer actually gives the client a graceful exit with all the proof they need.

4. **The foot-in-door mechanic trains the client to expect cheap work before committing.** D174's original free pilot was rejected because it "trains the client to expect free work before committing." D177's €500 + €1,000 structure does the same thing, just with a price tag attached.

5. **Louis cannot differentiate D177 from the €490 diagnostic he already tried to charge.** Pulse 7 → D125 raised it to €1,500/2-week → D177 now introduces €500 discovery sprint. The French PME buyer who has seen Alizé's €490 offer, then €1,500, then €500 sprint will conclude: *"Alizé keeps changing their pricing. They don't know what they're selling."*

### Recommended Alternative

**Kill D177. Replace with a single €2,000 flat pilot.**

| Payment | Timing | Deliverable |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| €1,000 | Signed contract | Scoping document, integration map, success metrics — signed before build |
| €1,000 | Working agent | Agent running in production, team trained, documentation delivered |

**Why €2,000 flat beats D177:**
- One close, not three
- No credits, no accounting complexity
- Each payment is a discrete, standalone deliverable
- Second payment releases agent — client pays before receiving
- €2,000 total is within Louis's credibility ceiling (vs €7,500 unsellable alone)
- Month 1 retainer framing is clean: pilot done, retainer follows naturally

**Kill D177.** The staged "credited" structure creates more trust problems than it solves.

## Agent 2: Capacity Reality Auditor — D176/D178

**Challenge target:** The 2-week data sprint and proposed Month 1 GTM sequence

### Key Findings

**The 2-week data sprint cannot produce new information.**

U91 has been listed as "unresolved" for 28+ consecutive pulses. It is not an information deficit — it is a decision deficit. Louis knows his schedule. He does not need 14 days of tracking to know he is overcommitted.

The sprint will produce data that says: "Louis works approximately X hours across all commitments." If X is less than 35-40 total hours (M1 + Grinto + web agency), then Alizé at 25+ is impossible. The data sprint cannot manufacture hours. Either Louis has the hours or he doesn't.

**The proposed Month 1 sequence is 4 weeks of sequential tasks that require simultaneous execution.**

- Days 1-3: Buy domain + set up hour tracking
- Days 4-14: Track actual hours (10 days of parallel activity)
- Week 2: Cold email 10-15 ICP prospects
- Week 3: First €1,500 staged pilot signed
- Week 4: Deliver + ask for referral

"Cold email at quality" in Week 2 requires ICP definition, offer writing, and deliverable infrastructure to exist. Week 4 delivery requires Louis to actually build the agent — technical skill never validated (U170 still unresolved). The delivery timeline assumes skills that don't exist.

**The web agency will consume Louis in Month 1.**

The web agency is 2 weeks old. It has Maëli and Gabin as co-founders. A 2-week-old venture's trajectory is unpredictable — first real client, first scope negotiation, first co-founder conflict. U95 (web agency Alizé conflict) was flagged and remains unresolved. When a web agency client asks "can you also automate our invoice processing?", does Louis walk away from that revenue or does the web agency compete with Alizé internally?

**The 4-week delivery timeline assumes capabilities that don't exist.**

Louis has never: deployed an MCP-connected AI agent to a real client's live data, managed a scoped professional services engagement, or handled a client who pushes back on scope mid-engagement. n8n MCP is documented as "beta-not-stable" (D46). The plan commits to delivering a pilot by Week 4 without knowing if Louis can deliver. This is not a data problem — it is a capability problem.

**"25+ hours/week" was never grounded in reality.**

D91 set 25 hours/week as a threshold. But the number was derived from what the plan *required*, not what Louis could give. If Louis has 12 hours/week for Alizé (realistic for M1 + Grinto + web agency), the plan's revenue model collapses. 12 hrs/week × 4 weeks = 48 hours for Month 1. Cold outreach takes 15-20 hours. Pilot delivery takes 20-40 hours. These are not sequential.

### The Verdict

D176's 2-week data sprint does not produce new information. The binary choice D176 was supposed to replace has not been eliminated — it has been deferred by two weeks, during which Louis spends time he doesn't have documenting a problem he already understands.

The proposed Month 1 sequence is not a plan. It is a sequence of good intentions organized into weeks, with no bearing on Louis's actual capacity, technical skill, or the web agency's demands.

**Five unresolved preconditions that make Month 1 execution impossible regardless of hour tracking data:**
1. Louis's delivery capability (U170)
2. The web agency's Alizé conflict (U95/U107)
3. The ICP target list (U172)
4. Louis's binary choice
5. A realistic delivery timeline accounting for actual hours available

Until all five are resolved, Month 1 is not a GTM plan — it is a press release.

## Agent 3: Vertical Auditor — D126/D145

**Challenge target:** Expert-comptables as first vertical (conditional on Louis's network)

### Key Findings

**The expert-comptable vertical was chosen based on theoretical appeal, not Louis's actual network map.**

Here is the chain of contradictions:

**1. D135 says "go where Grinto relationships exist first." But Grinto is a web development agency.** Grinto's relationships are web agencies, e-commerce companies, digital product businesses — companies that needed a website or web app. These are not expert-comptables. The instruction to follow Grinto relationships leads *away* from accounting firms.

**2. D127 directly undermines D126's core rationale.** D127 states: "governance gap overstated for 70% of ICP." If Copilot's governance gap only disqualifies it for 30% of the market, governance cannot be the *primary* reason to pick expert-comptables. D126 picked expert-comptables *because* of governance (Copilot can't handle regulated data). D127 says that rationale only applies to 30% of prospects. This is a fatal internal contradiction.

**3. U52 is still unresolved after 35+ pulses.** D145 made the expert-comptable vertical *conditional* on Louis having 5+ warm expert-comptable contacts — but nobody has checked whether that condition is actually met. The conditional framing has functioned as a way to make D126 feel validated in the abstract while kicking the actual validation to a task never completed.

**4. Louis's actual network context is Grinto (web agency) + MyDigitalSchool + co-founded web agency.** None of these connections are expert-comptables. Louis has spent his time in digital/tech circles, not in accounting firm networks.

**5. Budget question.** Expert-comptables are typically small (5-20 employees) and cost-conscious. They are SMEs. The French accounting firm market is highly competitive, margins are compressed, and many use Pennylane or Cegid that are actively embedding AI features. The "governance gap" that supposedly makes Copilot insufficient may already be filled by their existing software vendor.

### Recommended Alternative

**Louis's actual warm network points toward web agencies / digital product companies — follow D135 and pursue that vertical first.**

Why web agencies fit better:
- **Louis has actual relationships here:** He co-founded a web agency with Maëli and Gabin. He interned at Grinto. His MyDigitalSchool network is full of people working in digital agencies and e-commerce.
- **Web agencies have real operational pain:** Multiple client websites, content updates, domain renewals, customer support tickets, client reporting. These are repetitive admin workflows — the exact use case Alizé is built for.
- **No governance justification required:** Web agencies are not regulated. This sidesteps the D126/D127 contradiction entirely.
- **Louis can credibly sell to web agencies:** He understands the domain, speaks the language, knows what a web agency's operations look like from the inside.
- **ROI story is concrete:** "This agent does what your CDO does for 20% of their time, at 10% of the cost."

| Step | Action | Rationale |
|------|--------|-----------|
| 1 | Map U52 — filter for web agencies and digital product companies | If Louis's network has 5+ warm web agency contacts, this validates the alternative |
| 2 | Define one specific workflow | e.g., "client ticket triage + escalation routing" — one task, measurable ROI |
| 3 | Pilot 1: One web agency (Maëli/Gabin co-founded, or Grinto client) | Internal warmth, Louis understands domain, no credibility gap |
| 4 | Pilot 2-3: MyDigitalSchool alumni at other agencies | Warm-ish through school network |
| 5 | Month 4+: Expert-comptables via association referrals | Only if warm network in accounting emerges, not as pre-planned vertical |

**What to kill:**
- D126 as pre-determined vertical — expert-comptables not validated by Louis's network
- The governance-gap rationale — D127 already undermined it for 70% of ICP, Louis's network doesn't map to regulated industries
- Treating U52 as "still being resolved" — needs to actually be resolved before any more vertical decisions

**What to keep:**
- D135 ("go where Grinto relationships exist first") — most network-grounded guidance in the entire GTM research
- D145's conditional framing — vertical should follow network mapping, not precede it

## The Pattern Across 35 Pulses

Every pulse generates more decisions. The decisions rarely resolve into action. This pulse produced three structural critiques that identify the root cause of the stall:

1. **The pricing structure (D177) was a patch on a patch** — three stages of "credited" payments to avoid facing the real problem: Louis needs one credible close, not three incremental ones. The €2,000 flat structure solves this cleanly.

2. **The capacity question (D176) was never a data question** — U91 has been unresolved for 28+ pulses not because Louis lacks data, but because making the decision requires him to admit that his time is finite and his ventures are in conflict. Two weeks of tracking produces documentation of a constraint everyone already knows.

3. **The vertical choice (D126) was made without the prerequisite information** — U52 has been listed as critical and unresolved since at minimum Pulse 20. D126 and D145 were decided before U52 was resolved. The conditional framing (D145) has functioned as false validation. The vertical that actually matches Louis's network (web agencies via Grinto) was never seriously considered because the debate was always about theory, not network mapping.

**The common thread:** Every unresolved item traces back to one of two root causes — either Louis has not done the prerequisite work (map his network, track his hours, assess his delivery skill), or the debate has been about abstract theory instead of Louis's specific situation. Both root causes require Louis to actually do something, not to run more research pulses.

**The one action that unblocks everything:** Map your warm network. Not in theory. Actually map it — name by name, company by company, relationship depth by relationship depth. Then pick the vertical that matches the map. Everything else — pricing, sequencing, delivery — follows from that single act.
