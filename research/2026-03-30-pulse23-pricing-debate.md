## Thesis

**The €7,500 warm network pilot is unsellable at current Alizé brand maturity, and the warm/cold price split is built on a false premise.** The D65/D72 framework conflates *relationship proximity* with *buying readiness*, ignores the credibility gap that exists regardless of channel, and traps Alizé in a "too expensive to land, too unknown to charge less" limbo. The correct model for the first 3 pilots is **CAC-as-marketing**: price at €1,500–2,000, extract case study rights and testimonials, and build the reference base that makes future €7,500 pilots sellable.

---

## Challenge 1: Warm Contacts Are Not Committed Buyers

**The D65 premise is wrong.** Warm network contacts are *accessible*, not *converted*. They will take a call, but they still have to:

- Explain Alizé to their boss
- Justify the spend to a finance controller
- Get sign-off from someone who has never heard of the product

A warm contact is someone who answers the phone. A committed buyer is someone who signs a PO. These are not the same person, and the €7,500 price assumes the latter when you often only have the former.

**Evidence from D74/D84:** Louis can't credibly close €7,500+ deals without a senior commercial co-founder. If Louis can't close at that price, neither can his warm contacts — because the warm contact isn't the economic decision-maker.

**The real question:** Is a warm intro worth €0 or €7,500? It's worth €0 toward closing probability. The warm channel reduces *contact friction*, not *purchase friction*.

---

## Challenge 2: €7,500 for an Unknown Brand Is a Category Error

**No reference, no credibility, full price.** This is the pricing paradox. Enterprise software pilots are priced on:

1. **Perceived risk** (will it work?)
2. **Reference availability** (has it worked for anyone like me?)
3. **Switching cost** (how hard is it to change?)

Alizé scores zero on all three for first pilots. A warm relationship reduces none of these. The €7,500 price point was presumably set to signal "we're serious" — but that signal only lands if the market already believes Alizé has substance. It doesn't yet.

**D107 says it plainly:** "Price point unsellable regardless of who closes." This is the correct read. Adding a senior closer doesn't solve the problem — it just changes who can't sell it.

---

## Challenge 3: The Warm/Cold Split Is About Channel, Not Credibility — But That's the Wrong Distinction

D65 differentiated warm vs. cold based on *conversion probability*. This makes sense for sales process efficiency (spend time where it converts), **but it does not make sense for pricing** when the underlying product is unknown.

The price a buyer pays should reflect:
- The value they receive
- The risk they bear
- The reference leverage they provide

On all three dimensions, the **first pilot buyer** (warm or cold) provides *maximum leverage* to Alizé and bears *maximum risk* (they're the guinea pig). They should pay **less**, not the same or more.

---

## Challenge 4: D90 — The Commercial Closer Paradox

D90 states warm outreach is blocked until a closing-capable person exists. But consider:

**If a senior commercial closer joins Louis tomorrow and pitches €7,500 to a warm contact, what changes?**

| Factor | Louis Solo | Louis + Senior Closer |
|---|---|---|
| Product credibility | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| Brand credibility | Unchanged | Unchanged |
| Reference proof | None | None |
| Relationship warmth | Warm contact's relationship to Louis | Warm contact's relationship to Louis (closer is new) |
| Presentation polish | Low | Higher |
| Objection handling | Weak | Stronger |
| **Price objection resolution** | **No** | **Unlikely** |

The senior closer can handle objections better. They cannot manufacture credibility they don't have. The €7,500 price point remains unsupported by market evidence of value — that's not a closing problem, that's a product-market fit problem.

**D90 is a deferral, not a solution.** Even with a senior closer, the price point is the objection. The closer just handles the objection more gracefully before losing the deal.

## The Math: €7,500 vs. €1,500 for First 3 Pilots

### Scenario A: Hold €7,500 (D65/D72 approach)

| Pilot | Price | Margin (est. ~€1,500 each) | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot 1 | €7,500 | €1,500 | Likely lost (price objection, no references) |
| Pilot 2 | €7,500 | €1,500 | Likely lost (price objection, no references) |
| Pilot 3 | €7,500 | €1,500 | Unlikely to reach |
| **Total** | **€22,500** | **€4,500** | **High risk of €0 if all 3 are lost** |

**Reality check:** D107 says the price is unsellable. If all 3 pilots are lost, that's €22,500 in *opportunity cost* plus the case study / testimonial value that was never built.

### Scenario B: €1,500 Pilot with Case Study Rights

| Pilot | Price | Margin | Outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot 1 | €1,500 | ~€500 | Likely won + case study + testimonial |
| Pilot 2 | €1,500 | ~€500 | Likely won + case study + testimonial |
| Pilot 3 | €1,500 | ~€500 | Likely won + case study + testimonial |
| **Total** | **€4,500** | **~€1,500** | **3 case studies, 3 testimonials, proof of concept** |

### The Real Economic Comparison (3 Pilots)

| | €7,500 pilots | €1,500 pilots |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | €22,500 (if all close) | €4,500 |
| Margin | ~€4,500 (if all close) | ~€1,500 |
| **BUT probability of closing** | **Near zero (D107)** | **High** |
| **Expected value** | **~€0–2,000** | **~€1,500** |
| Case studies generated | 0 | 3 |
| Testimonials | 0 | 3 |
| **Reference base for future €7,500 pilots** | **€0** | **3 real clients** |

**The €7,500 approach has a theoretical upside of €22,500 but a near-zero probability of capture. The €1,500 approach has a 90%+ probability of capture and builds the asset that makes future €7,500 pilots sellable.**

The math favors the cheaper pilot. Not because €1,500 is better revenue — because **it generates the references that unlock €7,500 pricing power for pilots 4–10**.

## Concrete Recommendation

**Phase 1: First 3 pilots — CAC model**

- **Price:** €1,500–€2,000
- **In exchange:** Full case study rights + testimonial + right to use logo
- **Scope:** 10-day sprint, well-defined deliverables
- **Rationale:** These clients are paying for the privilege of being the reference. Alizé is paying (in margin) for the case study. This is standard practice for early-stage SaaS landing anchor clients.

**Phase 2: Pilots 4–6 — Transition pricing**

- **Price:** €3,500–€5,000
- **Positioning:** "Early partner rate, normally €7,500"
- **Leverage:** 3 case studies in hand

**Phase 3: Pilots 7+ — Target €7,500**

- **Price:** €7,500 with reference deck from 3–6 previous clients
- **This is when D65/D72 pricing actually makes sense** — when Alizé has enough credibility that warm vs. cold distinction reflects actual conversion probability, not just contact accessibility.

**Stop spending energy trying to close €7,500 pilots without references. Spend that energy building references at a price that generates them.**
