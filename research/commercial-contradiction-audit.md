# Commercial Model Contradiction Audit: Alizé

## The Contradiction: "The Dead Pilot Walking"

**€1,500 pilot is KILLED (D166) — but no replacement has been defined.**

D166 explicitly kills the €1,500 charity-model pilot for losing money. D219 confirms the commercial model is structurally broken with all three paths failing. Yet D179 still proposes a €2,000 flat pilot, and D177 proposes a staged €500→€1,000→€1,200/mo approach. Neither has been formally adopted or killed.

## Why This Blocks Revenue

Louis is trying to sell AI agents to French PME/ETI (50-200 employees) with a 10-person team. Every sales conversation hits a wall: "What does the customer actually pay for the first 90 days?" The sales team cannot answer this consistently because Louis hasn't decided.

- Sales pipeline is frozen — prospects need a clear pilot offer to sign
- D232 killed the downmarket path, narrowing focus
- Every day without a defined pilot offer is a day without signed contracts

The €1,500 pilot was generating learning but losing money. Killing it was correct. But killing it without a replacement leaves Alizé with no market entry product. The contradiction isn't between two pricing models — it's between a dead offer and silence.

## The Louis Decision That Resolves It

**Decide: Is the pilot a loss-leader ( CAC investment) or a standalone product?**

If CAC investment: Accept a subsidized pilot (€1,500-€2,000) for strategic clients, with clear exit criteria and conversion targets. Kill D177 and D179 as standalone offers.

If standalone product: Set the minimum floor at €7,500 (D149, D72) with a defined scope, and stop offering anything below that.

There is no third path. Louis must choose who bears the pilot cost — the customer or Alizé's marketing budget. That single decision unlocks the sales playbook, pricing page, and every pending demo.
