## Debate 1: Kuroba Dependency vs. Alizé Optionality

### Assumption Challenged
D194/D195 — "Kuroba is Alizé's strongest social proof asset." This assumption has not been seriously contested.

### The Challenge

Kuroba-as-proof creates a structural dependency that constrains Alizé's commercial flexibility in three ways:

**1. Kuroba is a 2-3 person web agency.** D242 identified the scale gap as a credibility problem. But the problem is deeper: if Alizé's only proof point is "we built it for ourselves at Kuroba," then Alizé can only credibly sell to companies that look like Kuroba (small, tech-native, digital). The BRIEF.md's ICP is 50-250 employee service firms. A 2-person agency does not demonstrate 50-person firm delivery capability — it demonstrates prototype capability.

**2. Kuroba workflow ≠ Alizé ICP workflow.** D220 redefined Kuroba's scope as "service firm client communications." But Kuroba's actual workflows (web development project management, client asset organization, brief collection) are not the back-office admin workflows Alizé claims to automate (invoice processing, CRM updates, FAQ responses). Using Kuroba as proof requires claiming Kuroba's workflows are representative of Alizé's target workflows. They are not.

**3. Named Kuroba reference limits vertical expansion.** If Kuroba is the named reference, Alizé is publicly anchored to web agency context. Every future prospect learns: "Alizé was founded by web agency people, built a tool for themselves." This is fine if Alizé stays in web agency adjacent markets. It becomes a liability if the evidence later points to a different vertical (e-commerce ops, logistics, professional services).

### The Counter

D195 is correct that "we built it for ourselves first" is the strongest available social proof at Louis's current credibility level. No other proof exists. The question is not whether Kuroba is imperfect — it is. The question is whether waiting for better proof produces better outcomes than using imperfect proof and iterating.

### Resolution

**D194/D195 should be CONDITIONALLY adopted, not unconditionally.** Use Kuroba as proof with explicit scope constraints:
- Kuroba proves Alizé can build and operate an agent for a real company
- Kuroba does NOT prove Alizé can deliver at 50-200 employee scale
- Kuroba does NOT prove Alizé can automate back-office admin workflows
- Kuroba should NOT appear as a named reference in materials targeting non-web-agency prospects

This preserves Kuroba's value as a starting proof point while preventing it from becoming a positioning trap.

## Debate 2: Recurring Revenue vs. Louis's Delivery Irregularity

### Assumption Challenged
All pricing discussions assume a monthly retainer model is the correct destination. This assumption has survived 50 pulses without being directly challenged.

### The Challenge

Louis's documented behavior across 50 pulses is: zero external actions completed in 91 days. This is not a motivation problem — it is a structural constraint. Louis has three concurrent commitments: M1 master (full-time student), Grinto internship, and Kuroba co-founder. Alizé has never been his primary commitment.

The recurring revenue model assumes:
1. Louis can acquire clients
2. Louis can deliver agents to those clients
3. Louis can maintain client relationships month-to-month
4. Louis can respond to issues, optimize prompts, handle escalations

All four assume Louis has regular, reliable time to dedicate to Alizé. The 91-day track record suggests otherwise.

**If Louis cannot reliably deliver month-to-month, the recurring revenue model creates a liability, not a business.** A client paying €1,500/month expects:
- Monthly performance reviews
- Prompt optimization
- New use case expansion (1/quarter)
- Next-business-day SLA on support

This is a structured commitment. Louis's documented inability to execute a 5-minute domain purchase in 50 pulses does not inspire confidence that he can deliver a monthly SLA.

### The Counter

D260 established that solo delivery capacity is 2-3 clients max before Louis hits a ceiling. This ceiling was identified as a delivery constraint, not a motivation constraint. The business model can work at 2-3 clients if Louis can actually acquire and deliver those clients. The issue is not the recurring model — it is whether Louis has the capacity and commitment to operate it.

### Resolution

**The recurring revenue model is valid but premature.** Before committing to recurring revenue as the commercial model:

1. Louis must demonstrate he can deliver ONE agent to ONE client for 30 consecutive days (response time, prompt updates, issue resolution)
2. If that demonstration fails, the commercial model should shift to project-based engagements (fixed-scope, fixed-price, no recurring obligation on Louis's time)
3. The retainer model can be offered as an option to clients who want it, not as the primary commercial structure

This removes the monthly SLA commitment that Louis may not be able to keep, while preserving the revenue potential of recurring engagements.

## Debate 3: The Rebranding Pattern as Diagnostic Signal

**1. Louis does not have a fixed point of reference.** Every vertical decision was made without network data (U52 unresolved for 50+ pulses). The vertical changed whenever a new agent challenge identified a flaw in the previous vertical. This is not iteration — it is circular motion.

A company that was "AI operations company" then "French AI service firm for regulated SMEs" then "back-office admin AI for service firms" then "AI workflow automation for regulated SMEs" then "e-commerce operations" is not iterating — it is signaling that its founders don't know what they're building.

D229 correctly identified that "positioning keeps evolving because the product/market evidence hasn't been generated." This is true. But the solution is not to keep iterating on positioning until evidence appears — evidence appears through execution, not through positioning refinement. The rebranding is a displacement activity. Louis changes the words because changing words requires no external action.

### Resolution

**Positioning should be frozen at the current version (D226 sentence) until one paying client exists.** D226 is specific, testable, and grounded in a named workflow. It does not claim a vertical. It does not claim a category. It claims a task. Freeze there. Do not write another positioning document. Do not run another positioning debate. Use D226 in outreach and measure response. If prospects convert, the positioning is working. If they don't, the problem is not positioning — it is product/market fit, which requires actual clients to diagnose.
