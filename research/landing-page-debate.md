# Landing Page Debate — Alizé

**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Role:** Landing Page & Messaging Strategist
**Context:** Stress-test for conversion effectiveness and messaging coherence. Alizé is unknown brand, French managed AI agent service targeting 50-250 employee companies.

---

## What I Evaluated

- Hero section: framing, CTA choice, entry point
- Problem section: resonance with target buyer pain
- "Pourquoi nous" differentiation: clarity vs. Agentova and Microsoft Copilot
- CTA strategy: commitment level vs. brand trust
- Overall: messaging coherence with ICP (DG/COO of 100-person French company)

---

## Assumption 1 Challenged: "The hero's negative positioning is a feature, not a bug"

**Assumption in current copy:** Opening with "Pas des gadgets" signals credibility by contrast. The target buyer is sophisticated enough to appreciate the implicit dig at hype.

**Why it's wrong:** A DG or COO at a 100-person company does not arrive at this page thinking "I hope this isn't a gadget." They arrive thinking "I have a team of 80 people, my operational costs are too high, my competitor just moved faster than me, and I have 3 open positions I can't fill." The "not a gadget" angle is a solution in search of a problem. It addresses the AI industry's anxiety about credibility — not the buyer's anxiety about their business.

**The framing is inside-out.** It says: "We are different from bad AI" instead of "Your business has this specific problem and we fix it." The target buyer doesn't self-identify as someone who's been burned by AI hype. They self-identify as someone who has operational friction, budget constraints, and a team that can't scale.

**Conversion risk:** High. The hero fails the first-second test. If the DG doesn't see their problem named in the headline, they scroll past.

---

## Assumption 2 Challenged: "Listing operational symptoms creates urgency"

**Assumption in current copy:** Describing the problem (emails piling up, info scattered across 7 tools, responses too slow, procedures not followed) will resonate because these are universal frustrations.

**Why it's wrong:** The DG of a 100-person company does not think in operational symptoms. They think in costs, headcount, competitive speed, and risk. "Mes équipes perdent du temps" is true but it's not a business case. The brief says the target buyer is already using ChatGPT or Copilot — meaning they know AI can theoretically help. What they don't know is whether it will actually deliver ROI for their specific context, whether it will create more problems than it solves, and whether they can afford the disruption.

**The problem section names symptoms but never converts them into a cost equation.** It says "des coûts cachés, des retards, des erreurs" but doesn't say: this is worth X euros per month in lost productivity, or this is why your lead response time is 3x slower than the market, or this is why your best people are doing data entry instead of selling.

**The DG's actual thought process:** "Is this going to move the needle? Can I defend this spend to my board? Will it actually work with my systems? What happens if it fails?" The landing page doesn't answer any of these — it just lists things that are annoying.

**Conversion risk:** Medium-high. The problem section will get nods but won't create urgency. Nods don't fill calendars.

---

## Does the Hero Speak to the Pain of a 50-200 Person Company Director?

**Short answer: No. The hero speaks to the anxiety of the AI industry, not the pain of a business leader.**

"Déployez des agents IA utiles à votre entreprise" is a product statement. Useful AI agents. That's what Alizé does. The target buyer doesn't want agents — they want a solution to their operational overload. These are not the same thing.

The secondary sub-headline lists use cases (support client, traitement d'emails, qualification commerciale) which is better — it starts to show where value lands. But it's buried, defensive in tone ("capables de faire" is weak), and still framed as features rather than outcomes.

**What a DG actually thinks when they land on the page:**
- First 2 seconds: "Who are these people? What do they do?"
- 5 seconds: "Is this relevant to MY business?"
- 10 seconds: "Should I keep reading or leave?"

The current hero answers none of these naturally. It leads with a category ("agents IA") and a negative ("not gadgets") rather than a transformation the buyer can picture themselves in.

---

## Does the Problem Section Resonate or Feel Generic?

**It feels like a well-written checklist, not a diagnosis.**

"Emails à traiter. Demandes internes qui tournent en rond. Informations dispersées entre CRM, ERP, boîte mail, Drive, Slack, Notion, outils RH ou support." — This is accurate. It also sounds like the problem description of every SaaS vendor since 2018. HubSpot, Salesforce, Monday.com, Notion itself — all of them open with "you have too many tools and they're not connected."

The problem section has no sense of **scale** or **stakes**. It doesn't say: in a company of 100 people, this friction costs you the equivalent of 2-3 full-time employees per year. It doesn't say: your competitor with 80 people is moving twice as fast because they've eliminated this layer. It doesn't say: the EU AI Act makes generic AI tools risky for your industry.

**The brief identifies that French companies have labor shortages in administrative functions, which increases ROI of automation.** The landing page never surfaces this. That should be in the problem framing.

---

## Does the Differentiation Section Create a Clear Mental Model vs. Agentova vs. Microsoft Copilot?

**Partially. The differentiation exists but the mental model is unclear because the comparison is missing.**

"Notre approche: orientée métier avant d'être technique, connectée à vos vrais outils, déployée avec un périmètre clair, pensée pour la sécurité et la conformité, suivie dans le temps, pas abandonnée après l'installation." — This is good. It names the right things.

**The problem:** The landing page never mentions Agentova or Copilot by name. The differentiation is delivered as a list of features ("orientée métier," "sécurité," "supervision") without contrast. For a buyer who's already looked at Agentova (and they have, because Agentova is the loudest French AI agent brand), this reads as a list of qualities that any vendor could claim.

**What the DG needs is a simple, visceral contrast:** Agentova gives you agents you configure yourself. Alizé gives you agents that work in your business. Copilot gives you a tool you have to manage. Alizé gives you an employee you don't have to manage.

**The current "pourquoi nous" doesn't create a new category in the buyer's mind — it just lists attributes.** The brief says Alizé's advantage is that self-service tools fail for 100-person companies because they lack time, technical staff, and integration capability. That's the insight. The landing page should state it directly, not bury it in the objections section.

---

## Is "Réserver un diagnostic" the Right CTA for an Unknown Brand?

**No. The CTA asks for too much commitment too early from a buyer with no trust yet.**

The diagnostic is €490 and requires a 30-minute call. For a brand the DG has never heard of, that's a significant commitment of time and money before they've seen a single proof point. The page has:
- No client logos
- No case studies
- No quantified results
- No third-party validation (reviews, press, certifications)
- No named team or founder

Asking a stranger to pay €490 and give you 30 minutes of their time with zero social proof is a conversion killer. The brief even notes the first pilots should be discounted in exchange for case study permission — which means Alizé knows it has a credibility gap. The landing page doesn't bridge that gap.

**A better approach for an unknown brand:** Lower the first step to "Voir les résultats" or "Explorer comment ça marche" or even a simple content exchange. The diagnostic offer can appear deeper in the page or on a secondary screen. The goal of the first CTA is to get the conversation started — not to close the first sale.

## Concrete Alternative Hero Framing Direction

**Current hero framing:** "Déployez des agents IA utiles à votre entreprise. Pas des gadgets."

**Alternative framing direction:**

> "Votre équipe passe 30% de son temps sur des tâches que l'IA pourrait faire. Nous les déployons pour vous."

Or even more direct:

> "80 personnes. 12 outils. Zéro visibilité sur ce que tout le monde fait. On automatise le reste."

**Why this would convert better:**

1. **It names the scale.** "80 personnes" is the DG's world. The generic "votre entreprise" is not.

2. **It makes a cost claim without overselling.** "30% de son temps" is specific, believable, and ties to headcount math the buyer can do in their head (80 people × 30% = 24 FTEs worth of capacity).

3. **It's not defensive.** It doesn't say "we're not like those bad actors." It says "here's your problem, here's what we do with it."

4. **It opens a question the DG wants answered.** "Can that really be true? Show me." That's a better hook than "We deploy useful AI agents. Not gadgets."

5. **It shifts from product to business outcome.** The current hero is a product description. The alternative is a business diagnosis.

**The alternative hero works because it meets the DG where they are:** at the problem level, not the solution level. The DG of a 100-person company is not thinking "I need AI agents." They're thinking "I have a capacity problem and a cost problem and I can't hire fast enough." The hero should reflect that reality.

## Summary: Conversion Risks

| Section | Risk Level | Core Issue |
|---------|-----------|------------|
| Hero | HIGH | Leads with product, not pain. Negative framing. No urgency. |
| Problem | MEDIUM-HIGH | Lists symptoms, doesn't quantify or connect to cost/competition |
| Differentiation | MEDIUM | Right attributes, no contrast, no names, no visceral difference |
| CTA | HIGH | €490 diagnostic on zero social proof. Commitment ladder too high. |
| Credibility | HIGH | No logos, no numbers, no testimonials, no proof. Unknown brand. |

---

## What Needs to Change (Direction, Not Rewrites)

1. **Hero:** Reframe from "what we are" to "your specific situation." Lead with the problem at the right scale (company of 100, 80 people, 50 employees). Make a cost claim.

2. **Problem section:** Add stakes. Quantify the cost of inaction. Connect to competitive speed, not just operational friction.

3. **Differentiation:** State the contrast explicitly. Agentova = you do the work. Alizé = we do the work. Make it a simple sentence.

4. **First CTA:** Lower the entry barrier. Replace "Réserver un diagnostic" with something that gets the first micro-commitment (content, video, conversation start). Move the diagnostic deeper.

5. **Social proof:** Add credibility anchors immediately. Named clients, even anonymized. Results: "X heures gagnées par semaine." Without this, the page is asking the buyer to trust an unknown vendor.
