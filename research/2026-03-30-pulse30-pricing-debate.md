## Challenge 1: D148's €490 Written Deliverable Signals Low Confidence, Not Low Friction

### The Contradiction
D148 sets €490 for a written deliverable. Multiple prior decisions (D84, D87, D107, D108) establish that Louis is a **credibility ceiling** — an M1 master student with no case studies, no references, no track record. The debate log is unambiguous: DG-level buyers need named references and senior commercial presence to trust a €7,500+ engagement.

A €490 price point for a written deliverable from an unknown brand doesn't say "low friction." It says **"the seller doesn't believe this is worth €490 either."**

High-trust professional services price diagnostic deliverables at **€1,500-3,000** precisely because the price *communicates expertise*. The deliverable is the proof of quality signal. A €490 deliverable from an unnamed student agency tells the buyer: "We're not sure this is worth your time, so we'll charge almost nothing."

### Market Reality Ignored
French ESN day rates run €600-900/day (BRIEF.md pricing benchmarks). A written diagnostic with use-case prioritization, tool mapping, ROI estimate, and deployment plan — the D148 deliverable — takes 2-3 days of skilled work. €490 for 3 days of professional services = **€163/day**. This is intern-level pricing, not professional services pricing.

**This contradicts:**
- D150 (single-price framing that signals premium positioning)
- The entire "managed AI agent service" premium positioning in BRIEF.md
- D107 (price point unsellable without credibility — €490 doubles down on the credibility problem)

## Challenge 2: D148 + D149 + D150 Are Philosophically Inconsistent

### The Contradiction
D150 explicitly states: **"Single price framing preferred; price outcome not feature set."**

But D148 creates a **two-step entry funnel**:
1. FREE call (screening)
2. €490 deliverable (credibility builder)
3. €7,500+ pilot (conversion)

D150's rationale is clear: decision paralysis from tiered pricing. But D148/D149 are *more* tiered than the original BRIEF.md structure (Diagnostic → Pilot → Monthly). D148 adds an extra free tier at the top.

### The Free-Call Problem
A free 30-minute call is a **lead capture mechanism**, not a sales tool. It optimizes for maximum leads, not maximum conversion to €7,500 pilots. The debate log itself (D5) raised: "free entry point may outperform €490 for unknown brand." But the reverse is also true: **free captures everyone, including time-wasters and lookie-loos, cluttering Louis's pipeline when his capacity is already oversubscribed** (U91 unresolved across 29+ pulses, U137 still open).

The free call also **devalues the €490 deliverable** — if the call is free, the written deliverable must be doing heavy lifting to justify any price. But €490 can't carry that weight for an unknown brand.

### Contradicts
- D6 (workflow-tier pricing to reduce decision complexity — D148/D149 increases complexity)
- D150 rationale (single-price preferred specifically to avoid tier confusion)
- D107 (conversion problem not just closing-person problem — funnel architecture is part of that)

## Challenge 3: Unit Economics — The Pilot-to-Retainer Recovery Math Doesn't Work

### The Contradiction
The BRIEF.md states managed service at **€800-1,500/month**. D149 sets pilot at **€7,500 minimum warm network**.

Simple arithmetic:
- Pilot acquisition cost: €7,500
- Monthly retainer revenue: €1,000 (midpoint of €800-1,500)
- **Recovery period: 7.5 months of full retainer payments**

The brief claims managed service has "3-month minimum commitment." If the client leaves after 3 months (the minimum), Alizé has collected €3,000 against a €7,500 acquisition cost. **Loss of €4,500 per early-exit client.**

### Market Reality Ignored
For a 20-30 person company (D14/D44 first-pilot target), €1,000/month is a significant line item requiring budget approval, IT sign-off, and demonstrated ROI. The pilot conversion rate is estimated at 35-50% (D28). That means **for every pilot that converts, one likely doesn't.** Each lost pilot = -€7,500 with no retainer offset.

D110/D111 established the first 3 pilots as CAC (customer acquisition cost) model — **explicitly accepting losses on pilots 1-3**. But D149 contradicts this by setting €7,500 as the *minimum* for warm network pilots. If pilots 1-3 are supposed to be CAC investments, the price floor should be lower (D125 set €1,500 for pilots 1-3). D149 creates an internal conflict: either pilots 1-3 are at €1,500 (CAC model) OR they are at €7,500 (D149). **These cannot both be true.**

### Contradicts
- D110/D111 (first pilots as CAC at €1,500-2,000)
- D112 (€7,500 unlock only after 3+ references — D149 ignores this sequencing)
- D28 (35-50% conversion rate assumption built into unit economics)
- D72/D73 (€7,500 pilot giving ~€1,500 margin at 10 days — but D110 says pilots 1-3 are CAC)

## Challenge 4: "Credited Toward Pilot" Creates Awkward Refund Dynamics and Funnel Friction

### The Problem
D148 says the €490 deliverable is "credited toward pilot." But:
1. If the pilot is €7,500, a €490 credit is **6.5%** — barely noticeable. It doesn't drive the decision.
2. If the pilot is €1,500 (pilots 1-3 per D110), a €490 credit is **33%** — meaningful but creates a €1,010 remainder that still requires a decision.
3. If the prospect takes the €490 deliverable but doesn't proceed to pilot (which is likely — conversion rates are 35-50%), does Alizé have an awkward refund conversation? Or does the €490 just sit as a sunk cost?

### The Credit Mechanism Is Poorly Defined
- Is the credit automatic upon signing a pilot contract?
- Does it expire?
- Does it apply to pilot 1 only or any pilot?
- What happens if the prospect gets the deliverable, finds it valuable, but decides not to proceed — do they feel "sold to"?

### Prior Decision Contradicted
D52 addressed payment staging specifically (50% kickoff / 50% day 15 + signed exit criteria). The credit mechanism in D148 sits in an undefined zone between the deliverable and the pilot — it doesn't fit cleanly into the staged payment model. A €490 credit against a €7,500 pilot with 50/50 payment staging (€3,750 kickoff) would reduce the first payment to €3,260. This is awkward pricing theater that adds complexity without adding value.

### Contradicts
- D52 (staged payment model — D148 credit doesn't integrate cleanly)
- D150 (price outcome not feature set — credits are a feature, not an outcome)

## Alternative Recommendations

### Alt 1: Replace €490 with €1,500 Diagnostic (No Credit, No Free Call)

**Rationale:** A priced diagnostic at €1,500 with a clear deliverable (written report + 30-min debrief) is:
- Low enough to be accessible to the first-pilot target (20-30 person companies)
- High enough to signal professional expertise
- Self-qualifying (only serious buyers pay €1,500 for a diagnostic)

Drop the free call entirely. Replace with a discovery form on the website that Louis reviews personally — qualifying happens there, not on a free call.

**If accepted:** Update D148 → "€1,500 diagnostic deliverable, no credit mechanism, includes written report + 30-min debrief call."

### Alt 2: Make the Pilot Price Tiered, Not Fixed

**Rationale:** D149's fixed €7,500 warm / €10,000 cold is too rigid. A tiered structure that mirrors D150's single-price preference (but actually works for conversion) would be:
- **Pilot 1-3 (CAC model):** €2,000 fixed, case study rights required, no credit mechanism
- **Pilot 4+ with 1 reference:** €5,000
- **Pilot 4+ with 3+ references:** €7,500
- **Cold outbound:** +€2,500 complexity premium

This preserves the pilot as a real engagement (not free) while making first-pilots economics sustainable. It also gives a clear growth narrative to the buyer: "Your first pilot is at CAC pricing because you're helping us build references."

**If accepted:** Update D149 → tiered by reference count, not cold/warm.

### Alt 3: Kill the Credit Mechanism, Replace with "Pilot Discount Voucher"

**Rationale:** Instead of a €490 credit (which is awkward at a €7,500 pilot price), offer a **"€490 off your first month of managed service"** if they proceed from diagnostic to pilot. This:
- Converts the credit into a retainer upsell incentive
- Makes the credit feel meaningful (€490 off €1,000/month first bill = 49% first month free)
- Eliminates the awkward refund/refund-doesn't-exist dynamic
- Aligns with D54 (12-month commitment with performance exit clause)

**If accepted:** Update D148 → "€490 diagnostic + €490 off first managed service month upon pilot signing."
