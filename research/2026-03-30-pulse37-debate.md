## What Was Debated

### Topic 1: Is D182's Demo-Led Motion Scalable?

**Assumption challenged:** "D182's demo-led motion is scalable — once Louis builds a prototype, he can use it to generate pilots indefinitely without a commercial layer."

**Verdict: Not scalable beyond first 2-3 pilots.**

**Key findings:**

- One prototype serves one workflow niche, not many. A meeting report agent for consulting firms does not transfer to accounting firms. If vertical-specific prototypes are needed, that's multiple builds — each takes 3-5 days minimum.
- Louis's throughput ceiling: ~3-4 quality demos per month maximum. At 30% opt-in rate, that's ~10 pilots per year. Not "indefinitely scalable" — a very small manual funnel.
- "Zero-pitch" collapses at the first pricing question. The DG will ask "how much?" and D182 provides no answer. The transition from demo to commercial conversation is the hardest part — and Louis has no script, no pricing page, no experience running it.
- D182's core assumption is wrong: the demo solves technical credibility, not operational credibility. The DG's real question is "will this person still be here in 6 months and competent when something breaks at 9pm?" A demo cannot answer that. Louis's profile gap persists regardless of what he shows.

**Specific assumption from existing research found wrong:** D182 claims to solve D84's credibility problem. It doesn't. D84 identified a structural credibility gap (M1 student, unknown brand, Grinto conflict). D182 swaps pitch credibility for technical credibility — but the DG still needs operational credibility, and that gap is not bridged by a demo.

### Topic 2: Does the Free Pilot Model Have a Path to Revenue?

**Assumption challenged:** "D183/D186 — first 1-2 pilots are free research partnerships, no cost for first month in exchange for feedback."

**Verdict: No credible path to revenue as structured.**

**Key findings:**

- **No conversion mechanism defined.** The debate log never specifies when Louis asks for money. "In exchange for feedback" is not a commercial term. After 30 days of free work, the client has a working agent and no reason to pay.
- **"Gratuit = suspect" in French business culture.** French PME buyers at the 50-200 employee level associate free with desperation. A free pilot signals "Alizé isn't confident enough in their own ROI to charge for it."
- **Revenue timeline: zero for 4+ months.** Louis hasn't sent one message in 36 pulses. Starting with free means starting with zero revenue, zero contractual obligation, and zero urgency to convert.
- **Free pilots select wrong prospects.** They attract budget-constrained clients (won't convert to €800-1,500/month), deal collectors (free pilots from every vendor), and internally reluctant buyers using "let's try it free" as political cover. Paying clients self-qualify.
- **"Free ≠ low friction" for the client.** The client still gives up: internal alignment time, data access, management attention, exposure of workflows to an external party. The free pilot shifts cost from money to organizational friction — and that friction is harder to resolve than a price objection.

**Specific assumption from existing research found wrong:** D183/D186 implies the learning from a free pilot has comparable value to monetary payment. It does not. A client who gets something free has no obligation to become a paying client, and no psychological commitment to the engagement.

### Topic 3: Is Louis's Profile a Fundamental Blocker or a Manageable Constraint?

**Assumption challenged:** "Alizé can overcome Louis's profile gap through motion (demo-led) and framing (research partnership) changes."

**Verdict: Manageable constraint — but only if strategy is designed around its edges rather than ignoring it.**

**Key findings:**

- **The research conflates credibility ceiling (D84) with credibility floor.** D84 says Louis can't close €7,500+. D184 says €2,000 is NOT validated as within his ceiling. The research never asked: at what price does Louis's profile go from liability to irrelevant? The answer may be "there is no such price for pitch-led close" — but that was never tested.
- **Kuroba co-founding is completely ignored in credibility debates.** "Co-founded a web agency with two partners" is Louis's most useful credential — it answers "qui c'est" differently than "student + intern." It signals: he can run something, he has partners, he's not a freelancer. This story is absent from all external materials.
- **The product-vs-profile problem is the real question.** The research keeps asking "can Louis's profile sell Alizé?" The more important question: does Alizé have a product that sells itself? A working agent demonstrating real output is worth more than any founder credential at this stage.
- **The Grinto conflict is not disqualifying for all outreach.** Louis's profile as Grinto intern is a problem for pitching web development services to companies that Grinto might serve. It is not a problem for pitching AI workflow automation to non-web companies. The conflict is contextual, not universal.

**Specific assumption from existing research found wrong:** The research treats Louis's credibility as a ceiling problem (price above which he can't close). The more accurate framing: Louis's credibility is a structural constraint that exists at every price point for pitch-led close. The solution is not price optimization but motion change — but even demo-led motion doesn't solve the operational credibility question.
