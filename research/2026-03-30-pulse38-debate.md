### Topic 1: Is Kuroba a Conflict or the Ideal First Deployment Target?

**Assumption challenged:** D187 / U173 framing — "Kuroba is a conflict of interest and therefore cannot be used as the first deployment target."

**Verdict: D187 killed the wrong thing. Kuroba-as-internal-deployment is valid.**

**Key findings:**

- D187 correctly killed "web agencies as a vertical market to sell to." It incorrectly killed "Kuroba as an internal first deployment." These are not the same decision.
- **Kuroba-as-first-client eliminates the D84 credibility problem entirely.** Louis's credibility ceiling applies to external prospects. Kuroba has zero such question — he co-founded it. No sales close, no trust gap, no cold outreach.
- **"We ate our own cooking" is the strongest B2B social proof.** A named Kuroba deployment (Maëli + Gabin as references) is categorically stronger than any anonymized case study or zero-client pitch deck.
- **D187's three grounds don't apply to Kuroba-as-internal-deployment:**
  - Conflict of interest: valid for selling to unrelated web agencies. Not relevant to deploying at your own company.
  - Category mismatch: valid for "should Alizé sell to web agency X." Not relevant to "should Alizé prove itself at Kuroba."
  - Navigation paradox (D135): was about exploiting Grinto employer relationships. Kuroba is Louis's own company with 100% authority. No exploitation issue.
- Kuroba risks are lower than external client risks: internal client = flexible, forgiving, motivated to help you succeed.

**Assumption from existing research found wrong:** D187's conflict-of-interest framing was applied too broadly. The "web agency conflict" (U173) conflates two separate questions — whether to sell AI services to web agencies as a vertical (conflict: yes), and whether to use Kuroba as Alizé's first internal deployment (conflict: no).

### Topic 3: What Does Louis Say After the Demo When Asked "How Much?"?

**Assumption challenged:** "The demo sells itself; pricing comes later."

**Verdict: The commercial close happens inside the demo, not after. Pricing must be embedded in the demo itself.**

**Key findings:**

- **Attention peaks at minute 8-10 of any demo, then decays.** If Louis finishes the demo and says "any questions?" the window is already closing. The pricing callout must be embedded in the demo, not deferred.
- **"How much?" is three questions in disguise:** (1) What does this cost? (2) Is it worth it relative to my problem? (3) Can I justify this to my partner/CFO? Louis must answer all three, in order, without being asked.
- **The demo creates functional credibility only — not governance, relationship, or financial credibility.** A working agent demo proves Alizé can build something that works. It does not prove data security, long-term reliability, or fair pricing. The pricing script must address the unspoken credibility gaps.
- **"Let me think about it" is a pre-negotiated exit ramp, not a real objection.** The correct response is never "pas de souci, merci au revoir." It creates a specific, low-friction next step that keeps Louis in the conversation.
- **Confidence in pricing is itself a credibility signal.** A prospect who sees Louis charge what he's worth without apology perceives competence, not arrogance.

**Assumption from existing research found wrong:** "Demo-led motion" (D182) implies the demo comes first, then the commercial conversation. This is backwards. The commercial conversation (value proposition + price anchor) must precede or be embedded in the demo itself.

## The Pricing Script (French, Post-Demo)

> **"Le coût est de [X] euros par mois — ce qui correspond à peu près à [Y] heures de travail administratif de votre équipe, chaque semaine, automatisées. Sur la base de ce qu'on vient de voir, vous avez probablement [Z] processus de ce type — c'est exactement le genre de charge qu'on élimine en premier. Est-ce qu'on programme une première session de configuration pour la semaine prochaine?"**

**Variables:**
- [X] = €1,200-1,500/mois (retainer after pilot, per D179)
- [Y] = ~2h/semaine = ~8h/mois minimum (anchors to observable workload)
- [Z] = specific number Louis observes from the prospect's context

**"Let me think about it" response:**
> **"Bien sûr, c'est une décision importante. Je vous envoie un accès au agent pour que vous puissiez le montrer à votre équipe — comme ça vous n'avez pas besoin de organiser une deuxième réunion. Je vous rappelle [ Jour + Heure ] pour en parler."**

This keeps Louis in control of the timeline and creates a specific callback appointment rather than a dead lead.
