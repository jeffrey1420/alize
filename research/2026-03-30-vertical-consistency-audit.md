## Executive Summary

After reviewing 45+ pulses of Alizé research and decision-making, one pattern dominates: the "first vertical" decision has reversed itself at least **seven times** in a single day. The trajectory is not a refining strategy — it is a circular firing squad. This document maps that trajectory precisely, identifies the root causes, challenges the core assumptions driving it, and recommends what to do instead.

**The core finding:** Vertical decisions are being used as a proxy for deeper unresolved problems (network mapping, Louis's time commitment, commercial model viability, conflict-of-interest boundaries). Until those foundational problems are resolved, no vertical decision will hold — and treating the symptom (changing the vertical) instead of the disease (the unresolved blockers) is costing Alizé its entire execution window.

## 3. Core Assumption Being Challenged: "Vertical Decisions Can Be Resolved Before Network Data Exists"

**The assumption:** Alizé can and should commit to a first vertical (professional services, expert-comptables, e-commerce) as a strategic decision, and then execute network mapping to find clients in that vertical.

**Why the assumption is wrong:**

D145 (Pulse 29) established an explicit dependency: expert-comptables are valid "only if Louis has 5+ warm expert-comptable contacts." This is the correct logical structure — vertical decisions should follow from network data, not precede it.

But U123 (Louis's expert-comptable network) has been listed as "unresolved" for **45+ consecutive pulses**. It appears in the unresolved queue in Pulse 25, Pulse 27, Pulse 29, Pulse 35, Pulse 39, Pulse 40, and Pulse 45 — repeatedly identified as gating, repeatedly deferred to "next week."

**The evidence suggests instead:** Vertical selection is upstream of the data that should determine it. Alizé is choosing verticals first and hoping the network validates them, when the correct sequence is network mapping → vertical from existing warm relationships → outreach.

D206 attempted this correctly: "Primary target if Grinto referral network yields 3-5 e-commerce warm referrals." But this conditional was suspended at D217 because D187's conflict-of-interest reasoning wasn't resolved — meaning the conditional was never actually tested.

**What this means for the decision tree:**

```
Current (broken) sequence:
Choose vertical → Map network → Find clients in chosen vertical

Correct sequence:
Map network → Identify which verticals have warm contacts → Choose vertical → Outreach
```

The current sequence means every vertical decision is made on the basis of zero data about Louis's actual network. This is why they keep reversing — each decision is made in an evidential vacuum, so the next decision-maker (or the next pulse) has no more data than the last, and reinverts.

## 4. Core Assumption Being Challenged: "Vertical Commitment Is Meaningful Without U123 Resolved"

**The assumption:** Decisions D126 and D145 (expert-comptables as first vertical, conditional on 5+ warm contacts) represent a valid strategic framework, even if the condition hasn't been met yet.

**Why the assumption is incomplete:**

D145 created an explicit conditional: expert-comptables are valid "only if Louis has 5+ warm expert-comptable contacts." This is a gate, not a preference. By the rules established in the same pulse framework, a decision with an unmet gate should not be treated as active strategy.

After 45+ pulses, U123 remains unresolved. The gate has never been tested. Yet the decision was killed at D181 not because the condition was tested and failed, but because D127 undermined the governance-gap rationale that originally justified expert-comptables.

**The evidence suggests instead:** The expert-comptable vertical was killed for the wrong reason. It should have been killed because U123 was never resolved — meaning the condition for D145 was never met, making D145 itself void from the start. Instead, D145 was treated as an active strategy until D127 contradicted its premise, at which point D181 killed it for an independent reason.

**What this means:** Two different decision points used two different kill mechanisms on the same vertical. D145's condition was never tested (U123 unresolved). D127's governance-gap contradiction killed the rationale. The vertical died from accumulated contradictions rather than from the explicit condition failing or succeeding.

**Challenge:** If a decision is conditioned on a gate that has been "unresolved" for 45+ pulses, the decision framework itself is broken. A conditional decision with an unmeetable gate is not a conditional strategy — it is a deferral mechanism dressed as a decision.

## 5. Core Assumption Being Challenged: "D187 and D135 Are Different Mechanisms Applied to the Same Data"

**The assumption:** D187 (kill web agencies as vertical, citing conflict of interest from Louis's co-founded web agency) and D135 (go where Grinto relationships exist) are two separate logical mechanisms that happen to conflict.

**Why the assumption is wrong:**

D187's conflict-of-interest reasoning (Louis co-founded a web agency) and D135's Grinto-relationships reasoning (follow employer relationships) are structurally the same mechanism:

- D135: "Use Grinto relationships for Alizé commercial gain"  
- D187: "Using Grinto relationships for Alizé commercial gain creates a conflict of interest"

D187 is D135's self-negating application. It applies the same logic and reaches the opposite conclusion because the frame shifted from "relationship leverage is a GTM asset" to "relationship leverage is a conflict."

This is not two different mechanisms — it is one mechanism that flipped sign without an explicit decision to flip it. The pulse that introduced D187 (Pulse 36) did not revisit D135 or explicitly kill it. D135 was simply rendered dormant by D187's different framing.

**The evidence suggests instead:** The real question — "Is using Grinto relationships for Alizé's commercial gain acceptable?" — was never explicitly debated. It was answered implicitly by whichever pulse happened to be running at the time. D135 said yes. D187 said no. Neither pulse referenced the other as a contradiction to resolve.

**Challenge:** D135 and D187 are the same question with opposite answers in consecutive pulses. This is not a strategy — it is a contradiction that went unacknowledged. The correct resolution requires Louis to explicitly answer: "Am I allowed to use Grinto relationships for Alizé's commercial gain, with or without explicit permission?"

## 9. What the Evidence Suggests Instead

### Evidence from the trajectory itself:

1. **No vertical has survived more than 9 pulses without being reversed.** The average vertical lifespan across 45+ pulses is approximately 6 pulses. This is not strategic refinement — this is random walk with reversals.

2. **The three verticals being debated (professional services, expert-comptables, e-commerce/digital) are the same three verticals discussed in Pulse 2.** After 45 pulses, the universe of considered verticals has not expanded. The decisions have not been building on each other — they've been cycling.

3. **Every vertical decision has been killed by an internal contradiction, not by market evidence.** None of the verticals have been tested against actual clients. They have been killed because D127 contradicted D126's rationale, or because D187 identified a conflict, or because D217 suspended the decision pending an unresolved conflict. The kill mechanisms are all internal to the research process — none reflect what an actual client said or didn't say.

4. **D135 (the Grinto relationships principle) has produced four opposite conclusions across consecutive pulses.** This is the clearest evidence that the principle is not driving decisions — it is being selected post-hoc.

### What the evidence collectively suggests:

**The vertical is not the blocker.** Louis's ability to execute is the blocker. The vertical decisions are a symptom of the execution paralysis, not its cause. Changing the vertical does not change Louis's hours, the unresolved network map, the domain not being bought, or the commercial model that D219 identified as "structurally broken."

## 10. Recommendations

### Recommendation 1: Stop Treating Vertical as a Research Decision

Vertical should not be decided in a pulse document. It should be derived from U52.

**Specific action:** Louis spends one afternoon (not one pulse, not one week) mapping his actual warm contacts: 20 names, company, size, relationship depth, ICP fit. The vertical emerges from the map — whichever vertical has the most warm, ICP-fit contacts is the vertical.

**If no vertical dominates the map:** Then Alizé has no natural first vertical, and the GTM strategy must be workflow-first (find any ICP-fit client regardless of vertical) rather than vertical-first. This is a valid answer. It just requires acknowledging it.

### Recommendation 2: Resolve D135 and D187 as One Explicit Question

The D135/D187 contradiction must be resolved in a single explicit decision, not debated across multiple pulses producing opposite conclusions.

**Specific action:** Louis answers one question in writing: *"Am I permitted to use Grinto relationships (directly or via Maëli/Gabin referrals) for Alizé's commercial gain?"* If the answer is yes (with or without explicit permission), D135 is active and Grinto-adjacent verticals are on the table. If the answer is no, D187 is active and Grinto relationships are excluded from all commercial GTM. This decision should take 10 minutes. It has been implicitly debated for 9+ pulses.

### Recommendation 3: Apply D228's Reasoning to Vertical Decisions

If "positioning keeps evolving because evidence hasn't been generated" (D228), the same applies to vertical.

**Specific action:** Do not treat any vertical decision as final until U52 is complete. Current status: "undetermined pending network map." This is an honest answer, not a failure. The alternative — treating D206 (e-commerce) or D44 (professional services) as active strategy while U52 remains unresolved after 45+ pulses — is the source of the reversals.

### Recommendation 4: Kill U123 from the Unresolved Queue

U123 (expert-comptable network) should be removed from the decision queue because:

1. Expert-comptables are deprioritized to Month 9+ (D207)
2. D126/D145 are both effectively dead
3. The condition in D145 was never met or tested
4. Continuing to list U123 as unresolved implies expert-comptables remain a live option when they do not

**Specific action:** Mark U123 as "deferred to Month 9 research, not a current gating item." This removes one phantom blocker from the queue.

### Recommendation 5: Accept That the First Vertical May Be "Whichever ICP-Fit Warm Contact Says Yes First"

After 45 pulses of trying to choose a vertical and find clients in it, consider the inverse: reach out to warm contacts who match ICP (regardless of vertical), and let the vertical emerge from who responds.

**This is not a compromise — it is the correct GTM sequence for a service business with no proof points:**

1. Find anyone who will take a meeting (warm network)
2. Learn what they actually need (discovery)
3. Deliver a pilot that works (proof)
4. Derive vertical from which pilot produced the best outcome (strategy)

The current approach has been: decide vertical → find clients → deliver pilot. The correct sequence is: find clients → deliver pilot → derive vertical. D228's reasoning supports this — strategy emerges from evidence, not the other way around.

### Recommendation 6: Acknowledge the Double Standard on Premium Pricing

The regulated framing was killed (D159) but the premium pricing was not adjusted. D167 noted the cost-plus minimum is €7,963/month and €2,000 is 25% of cost-plus. Either:

1. Accept Alizé as a loss-leader CAC business for Years 1-2 (explicit business model choice), or
2. Find a different premium justification that holds without the "regulated" framing

These are not research questions. They are business model decisions that require Louis to choose.
