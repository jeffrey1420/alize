## What Was Debated

### Debate 1: Pricing Credibility — Can an Unknown Brand with Zero Case Studies Charge €2,000-2,500/month?

**Assumption challenged:** D38's "€2,000-2,500/month minimum for consulting economics" applies to Year 1 pilots without modification.

**Challenged by:** Pricing Credibility Auditor

**Key findings:**

1. **The credibility gap is real but not price-determined.** D38 argues from cost-plus logic: Louis needs €2,000+/month per client to cover his own time. But cost-plus pricing for an unknown brand with zero proof points is backwards — you don't set prices based on your costs when the buyer has no reason to trust you. You set prices based on the value you can credibly demonstrate.

2. **Zero sales conversations have happened at any price point.** After 43 pulses, Louis has had zero prospect meetings. No pricing conversation has ever been killed by the quote. The €2,000-2,500 floor is addressing the wrong problem.

3. **The "consulting economics" framing was designed for Louis's sustainability, not the buyer's willingness to pay.** D38 says €2,000-2,500 reflects consulting rates. But a first-time buyer paying €2,500/month to an unknown M1 student with no case studies isn't buying consulting — they're taking a risk on a stranger. The price signals confidence; the proof signals quality. For first pilots, proof must precede premium pricing.

4. **First pilots should be either free (pure learning) or priced to win, not to sustain.** The choice isn't "underpricing vs. overpricing" — it's "do we need case studies more than we need margin right now?" If the answer is yes (it is), first 2 pilots are either free or €500-1,000/month. Case studies unlock full pricing power. You can't skip the proof step.

**Verdict: REVISED — €2,000-2,500/month is the GOAL price, not the ENTRY price. First 2 pilots at €500-1,000/month or free in exchange for case study rights. Full pricing from pilot 3 onward.**

### Debate 2: Technical Architecture Year-1 — Is the Full Stack Overengineered for 3-5 First Pilots?

**Assumption challenged:** The full Nuxt 3 + Hono + Mastra + pgvector + OVHcloud S3 + BullMQ + Redis stack is the right foundation for Year 1 with 3-5 pilots.

**Challenged by:** Technical Architecture Year-1 Auditor

**Key findings:**

1. **€25-40/month VPS cannot run this stack.** The brief specifies this budget. The stack requires: Nuxt 3 server, Hono API, Mastra agent runtime, PostgreSQL with pgvector, Redis, S3-compatible storage, BullMQ workers. This needs minimum 4 vCPU / 16GB. OVHcloud's similar config is €80-120/month. The budget assumption is wrong by 3-4x.

2. **Mastra at 22k stars is not production-hardened for a managed service.** Mastra is a developer framework, not an operations platform. It lacks the monitoring, alerting, and error recovery that a client-facing service requires. Running it on a budget VPS with no ops tooling means every client issue becomes an emergency at 11pm.

3. **For 3-5 pilots, the entire stack should be API calls + a spreadsheet.** The Year 1 goal is validating demand, not building infrastructure. OpenAI API + Zapier/Make.com handles most pilot workflows. Vector search can use Pinecore's free tier or OpenAI's built-in embeddings. No PostgreSQL, no Redis, no BullMQ needed until Month 6+.

4. **Self-hosted MCP servers on OVHcloud are a Month 6+ decision.** MCP is Alizé's differentiator — but MCP servers require maintenance, monitoring, and debugging. Running them on a budget VPS with no DevOps team means they'll break silently and clients will discover it.

**Verdict: REVISED — Kill the full-stack architecture for Year 1. Replace with: OpenAI API + Zapier/Make.com for pilots 1-3. Mastra + pgvector + proper infrastructure only when 3+ paying clients exist and the demand is confirmed.**

### Debate 3: Louis Role Clarity — Can Louis Simultaneously Be Technical Builder, Delivery Lead, and Commercial Lead?

**Assumption challenged:** Louis can split his time across all three roles: building the agent, managing delivery, and closing deals.

**Challenged by:** Louis Role Clarity Auditor

**Key findings:**

1. **Louis is an M1 student + Grinto intern.** His documented, marketable skill is web development. His undocumented gap is client-facing PME consulting experience. Treating him as a delivery lead (D31) requires skills he hasn't demonstrated. Treating him as a commercial lead (D74 says NO) is already resolved. The unresolved question is whether he can BUILD what he needs to sell.

2. **D74 explicitly says Louis should NOT solo-close warm network deals.** The reason: Grinto conflict of interest + no sales skills + LinkedIn DM wrong format. This means someone else must do commercial. But D74 doesn't say WHO. The options are: Gabin (co-founder, web dev background), an external sales person, or Louis becomes technical-only and someone else handles all commercial.

3. **The role conflict is fundamental:** Building AI agents requires deep technical focus (LLM APIs, MCP integration, RAG pipelines, vector DBs). Client delivery requires relationship management, scope negotiation, and expectation setting. Sales requires a different credibility frame and network access. These are three different skill sets, three different time commitments, and three different emotional modes. One person doing all three produces three mediocre outputs instead of one excellent one.

4. **Minimum viable Month 1 team:** Louis (technical build) + one person who can do discovery calls and scope presentations. This doesn't require a hired employee — it requires Louis to acknowledge he cannot be that person and to find a partner or co-founder who can.

**Verdict: KILLED — The "Louis as technical + delivery + commercial" model is structurally impossible. One role must be primary. Recommendation: Louis = technical build only. Find a commercial partner for outreach and discovery. If none exists, Alizé cannot execute Month 1 as designed.**
