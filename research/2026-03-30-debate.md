## Debate 1: Pricing Strategy

**Agent:** Pricing Strategy Specialist  
**Memo:** `/data/workspace/alize-pricing-debate.md`

### Assumptions Challenged

**1. "Agent-based pricing makes sense because agents aren't users"**

The research claims per-agent pricing is logical because agents aren't seat-based users. The agent challenged this as a mental accounting problem — the term "agent" activates the employee cost comparison frame in buyers' minds.

> When a 50-200 person company director hears "agent," they think: *"An agent = someone working for me = should cost like an employee"* (avg. administrative employee in France: €2,500-3,500/month). Alizé's €800-1,500/month per agent looks like a cheap hire — which creates impossible performance expectations.

The agent cited: Zapier (workflow-based, not agent-based), HubSpot Service Hub (per user seat, not per agent), Intercom Fin ("conversation resolution," not per-agent pricing).

**Recommendation:** Replace per-agent pricing with **workflow-tier pricing** — sell capabilities ("Lead Management," "Customer Support," "Admin"), not agents. Each tier includes the agents needed to execute that workflow.

**2. "The €490 diagnostic signals quality and filters serious prospects"**

The research presents the diagnostic fee as a quality filter. The agent challenged this as inverted for a brand with zero proof points.

> A €490 payment before experiencing any Alizé value is a trust ask from a completely unknown vendor. French PME/ETI buyers facing this will say: *"I don't know this company — let me ask my accountant/IT person/chamber of commerce first."* The funnel kills itself at step 1.

Cited: HubSpot (free growth audit), Salesforce (free quickstart), McKinsey/BCG (charge thousands — but have global brands and published case studies Alizé doesn't have).

**Recommendation:** Make the diagnostic free. Use it as a proof tool, not a revenue source. Recover the €490 on the pilot setup fee (€3-6k), which is the real commitment.

### Pricing Agent Summary Table

| # | Current Assumption | Problem | Alternative |
|---|-------------------|---------|------------|
| 1 | Per-agent pricing | Activates employee cost comparison | **Workflow-tier** — sell capabilities, not agents |
| 2 | €490 diagnostic = quality signal | Kills funnel for unknown brand | **Free diagnostic** → proof first, fee on pilot |
| 3 | Range pricing (€800-1,500/agent) | Opaque, invites negotiation | **Fixed tiers** with explicit inclusions |
| 4 | Monthly per-agent billing | Complexity, under-utilization complaints | **Bundle by workflow** — use what's included |

### Final Pricing Recommendation (from agent)

- **Diagnostic:** Free (output-limited) or €0 + €490 for full report
- **Pilot setup:** €3-6k (unchanged)
- **Subscription:** Workflow-tier, fixed prices
  - Starter: 1 workflow — €1,200/month
  - Business: 3 workflows — €3,000/month
  - ETI: Unlimited workflows + SLA + account manager — €5,500/month

## Debate 2: Competitive Strategy

**Agent:** Competitive Strategy Specialist  
**Memo:** `/data/workspace/alize-competitive-debate-memo.md`

### Assumptions Challenged

**1. "Agentova is a different segment — not a threat"**

The research frames Agentova as targeting micro-SME (1-10 employees) while Alizé targets PME/ETI (50-200). The agent challenged this as a **go-to-market distinction, not a product moat**.

> Agentova's product is a horizontal AI agent platform. "Segment" is a sales motion decision, not a product constraint. When Agentova decides to move upmarket, they add SSO/SAML, audit logs, Sage/Cegid/SAP integrations, hire 2-3 enterprise salespeople — and they are directly competing in Alizé's segment. This is the standard SaaS playbook (HubSpot, Salesforce, Pipedrive all moved upmarket this way).

The "segment gap" requires only 12-18 months of product work. If Agentova raised a Series A, this window closes fast.

**Recommendation:** Treat Agentova as a **future threat to monitor**. Build product depth Agentova can't replicate quickly. Don't rely on "different segments" as a moat.

**2. "PME/ETI operational AI is a green field — few competitors"**

The research calls this a green field. The agent called this **dangerously complacent**.

> The actual competitive landscape: Microsoft Copilot Studio + Dynamics 365 (already in most French businesses), HubSpot Breeze Agents (15,000+ French customers, expanding into operations), Salesforce AgentForce, SAP Business AI. These platforms have massive distribution advantages — existing customer relationships, M365/Azure foot-in-the-door. For a 100-person company already paying Microsoft €15k+/year for M365, adding Copilot Studio is a line-item decision, not a new vendor evaluation.

The "green field" assumption breeds complacency: under-investing in differentiation, pricing too low, not building defensible moats, getting surprised when platforms notice the market.

**Recommendation:** Reframe Alizé's competitive position against the **platform giants** (Microsoft, HubSpot), not just Agentova. The real battle is inertia toward self-service tools. Lead with managed service as the differentiator.

**3. (Bonus) "French companies prefer French providers for data sovereignty"**

Real for large enterprises and regulated industries, but questionable for 50-200 employee PME. This segment is pragmatic — they choose what solves the problem at the right price. Microsoft et al. are rapidly closing the EU data residency gap (EU data boundaries). EU AI Act compliance will be table stakes in 18 months, not a differentiator.

**Recommendation:** Keep data sovereignty in messaging, but **lead with outcomes, not compliance features**. Compliance is permission-to-play, not a selling point.

### Competitive Agent Summary

> "Agentova is not a non-threat, the green field is filling up fast, and Alizé's real battle is against the platform giants — the messaging should reflect that."

**Short-term actions:**
1. Build competitive monitoring for Agentova, Copilot Studio, HubSpot Breeze (quarterly review)
2. Reframe external messaging: "managed service for PME operational AI" vs. self-service platform tools
3. Document why 2-3 Alizé customers chose Alizé over Microsoft/HubSpot — sales ammunition

**Medium-term actions:**
1. If Agentova adds enterprise features or raises funding, accelerate Alizé's mid-market differentiation
2. Invest in integrations with French PME software ecosystem (Sage, Cegid, Pennylane) — "French PME-native" vs. American platforms
3. Build deliberate switching costs into Alizé's lock-in

## Debate 3: GTM Strategy (Partial)

**Agent:** GTM Challenge Specialist  
**Status:** Output truncated mid-response — partial findings below

### Assumptions Challenged (partial)

**1. "LinkedIn outreach is the right channel for a €490-6k sale"**

The GTM agent challenged the cold LinkedIn outreach motion for a sub-€500 initial close.

> Rule of thumb in B2B SaaS: <€5k = product-led/self-serve; €5k-50k = hybrid PLG + sales-assisted; >€50k = full consultative sales. At €490 initial close, cold LinkedIn outreach is terrible ROI. Even at 10% reply rate and 20% conversion, 250 prospects needed to close 5 deals = ~62 hours of outreach work before the diagnostic conversation even happens.

Comparable benchmarks: HubSpot (early days) used inbound content + free tools; Notion used completely self-serve + free tier → no cold outreach for growth; Brex used outbound only after building a PLG engine with a free tier.

**Alternative proposed:** Replace cold outreach with a **high-conversion inbound funnel** — free "AI Readiness Audit" self-serve assessment (20 questions, PDF report), captures leads who self-qualify, follow-up call is the diagnostic but now the prospect has experienced value first.

**2. "The €490 diagnostic creates quality filter"**

The GTM agent reached the same conclusion as the pricing agent on this point — the diagnostic at €490 for an unknown brand is friction, not filtering. Both agents independently identified this as a problem.

> At 50-200 person company level, the economic buyer (Founder, COO) is time-poor and decision-fatigued. They need a reason to say yes, not a reason to say no. Paying €490 before experiencing any value asks for trust the brand hasn't earned.

**Note:** Full GTM memo was truncated by model output limit before completion. Assumptions 3 and 4 (personal network scalability, ROI pilot as GTM asset) were not captured.

## Cross-Cutting Themes

Three independent agents reached convergent conclusions on two points:

3. **Platform giants (Microsoft, HubSpot) are the real competitive threat**, not Agentova. Both competitive and GTM agents identified the same gap in how the competitive set is defined. → **Action: reframe competitive messaging**
