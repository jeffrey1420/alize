## Agent 1: Credibility Gap Auditor

### The Assumption Challenged
D84: Louis faces a credibility ceiling because Alizé has zero past clients. This assumption treats "no reference clients" as a structural blocker to closing deals.

**Verdict: Partially wrong.**

### Key Findings

**1. Warm network introductions bypass cold credibility checks.**
Louis's GTM is warm outreach (Phase 1, BRIEF.md), not cold vendor evaluation. In warm-network B2B, trust is transferred from the referrer. Credentials are secondary. French business culture is relationship-driven — a warm intro is often a precondition for a meeting, not a nice-to-have.

**2. "Credibility" conflates credentials with trust.**
D84 conflates the two. Even in cold sales, solo operators win first clients through trust, fit, and urgency — not track record. The first client is not won on credentials.

**3. Capability evidence exists without paid clients.**
- Kuroba deployment (self-built, live)
- Diagnostic deliverables (tangible proof of thinking framework)
- 3-5 discovery calls generates warm testimony without a paid engagement
- Published articles / personal LinkedIn presence

**4. The assumption was never tested.**
D247 confirms zero market data. D84 was reached by argument, not experiment. Treating an untested prediction as a hard constraint is the actual risk.

### Challenged Assumption
D84 (credibility ceiling from zero clients) — untested prediction treated as validated fact. The constraint is real but overstated.

## Agent 3: Unit Economics Stress Test

### The Model Under Test
"€2,000/month per client is sustainable."

**Inconsistency flagged:** BRIEF.md lists €800-1,500/month as managed service. The €2,000/month sustainability claim is asserted, never derived from a cost model.

### Load Test: 1-Person Delivery Capacity

Louis plays three roles simultaneously:
- Delivery lead: 5-8 hrs/client/week (conservative)
- Account manager: 2-3 hrs/client/week
- Sales lead: 10-15 hrs/week

**Active client capacity: 2-3 clients maximum** before something breaks or quality degrades.

At €1,200/month per client (lower bound): €2,400-3,600/month revenue
At €1,500/month per client (midpoint): €3,000-4,500/month revenue

Louis needs at minimum €2,000/month to cover Alizé costs + personal minimum. This means:
- 2 clients at €1,000/month = breakeven (not viable)
- 3 clients at €1,500/month = €4,500/month (above minimum, but tight)
- 5 clients at €1,200/month = €6,000/month (requires doubling capacity)

**The real bottleneck:** At 2-3 clients, Louis is fully loaded. There is no capacity to grow the business (outbound, content, partnerships) while delivering. Growth requires either raising prices, hiring delivery help, or accepting a ceiling.

### Conversion Sensitivity
- If 1 of 2 pilots doesn't convert: cash flow gap within 60 days
- Pilot revenue (€4,500) covers ~1 month of full-load costs
- Without conversion, the model is a expensive networking habit

### Challenged Assumption
The €2,000/month sustainability claim. It's asserted, not derived. Real economics suggest the number should be €2,500-3,000/month minimum, or delivery costs must be reduced via tooling/partnership.
