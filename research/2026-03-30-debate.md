## Agent 1: French/Sovereignty Positioning Debate

**Assumption challenged:** "French companies prefer French providers for data sovereignty reasons" and "EU AI Act compliance creates friction for generic global tools" — treated as meaningful purchasing differentiators.

### The Argument (CON position)

**Challenge 1: The buyer doesn't care about infrastructure**

At a 20-30 person company, the buyer is a DG, founder, or COO — buried in operational fires. They are not thinking about data sovereignty. The "French provider" preference is real, but it's held by **IT and procurement**, not the person signing the check. A 20-person digital agency doesn't have an IT department. If Alizé leads with OVHcloud and EU AI Act compliance, it's talking to an empty room.

**Challenge 2: EU AI Act compliance is not a near-term driver**

The EU AI Act's most impactful provisions for general-purpose AI don't kick in until 2026-2027 at earliest, with full enforcement over 2027-2028. A 20-person French company is not sitting in Q1 2026 thinking about AI vendor audits. That's enterprise procurement conversation. Alizé is positioning for a problem that doesn't yet exist in the buying consciousness of its target market.

**Challenge 3: Sovereignty positioning signals weakness**

Leading with "French hosting on OVHcloud" implicitly says: "We are a small local company, and here is why that is a feature." That framing cedes the narrative to global players. Alizé cannot win on geography. More damaging: "French hosting" is infrastructure. The DG cares about whether tickets get answered and leads get followed up — not where the servers sit.

### Recommendation
Drop French hosting and EU AI Act from any hero, headline, or primary positioning. Lead with: the specific workflow solved, the measurable outcome, the delegation. E.g., "We build and run your AI agents. You stop worrying about the work." The French/s sovereignty angle belongs in "how we're built" — only for the 10% of prospects who ask directly.

**Challenges to existing assumptions:**
- Challenges D11 ("managed service" framing) — OVHcloud/souveraineté framing is equally a liability if overused
- Challenges the market sizing assumption that "French companies prefer French providers" drives purchasing decisions at the DG level

## Agent 2: Price Floor Debate (€2,000-2,500/month)

**Assumption challenged (D38):** "Minimum price: €2,000-2,500/month/client (consulting economics); €1,200 too low"

### The Argument (CON position)

**Challenge 1: The "consulting economics" argument is logically inconsistent**

The D38 argument: Alizé is a productized consulting practice → prices must cover consulting-level margins → €2,000-2,500/month floor. But D14 explicitly says the first pilots should target 20-30 person companies. A 20-30 person company has roughly €500-1,000/month in operational improvement budget. You cannot target those companies AND hold a €2,000-2,500 floor — one of those is wrong.

The competitor research already stated: "For PME (50-250 employees), budgets of €500-2,000/month for operational productivity tools are more realistic." At €2,000-2,500/month, Alizé sits at the absolute ceiling — before a single case study exists, for an unknown brand.

**Challenge 2: The "vs. part-time employee" ROI math collapses at €2,000/month**

At €1,500/month, the ROI story works: Alizé agent vs. part-time employee at €1,500-2,000/month for 20h/week. But at €2,000+/month, Alizé is 25-65% more expensive than the human alternative — before accounting for the fact that humans can do more than one task type. The ROI play stops working somewhere between €1,400-1,600/month for most micro-PME buyers.

**Challenge 3: The tiered offer solves this cleanly**

At €1,500/month: 20-30 person company pays €4,500 pilot + €1,500/month = €22,500/year — significant but justifiable.
At €2,500/month: same company pays €35,500/year — suddenly out of budget for first-pilot targets.

Year 1 revenue at €1,500/month with 8 clients: €144,000/year.
Year 1 revenue at €2,500/month with 5 clients (shrunk TAM): ~€150,000/year — same revenue, 2x the sales effort.

### Recommendation
Add a starter managed service tier at €1,200-1,500/month for single-workflow, single-department clients. Keep €2,000-2,500/month for multi-workflow, cross-department deployments. The floor should be aspirational (Year 2 pricing), not a barrier to first revenue in Months 1-6.

**Challenges to existing assumptions:**
- Directly challenges D38 (€2,000-2,500 floor as consulting minimum)
- Challenges D14 (20-30 person pilot target) if €2,500 floor is held — these are incompatible

## Agent 3: Vertical Choice Debate (Digital agencies/e-commerce)

**Assumption challenged (D13):** "First vertical: digital agencies/e-commerce, NOT professional services"

### The Argument (CON position)

**Challenge 1: Digital agencies have a buy-vs-build problem**

A 15-person digital agency in France has developers who do n8n + LangChain + LLM API integrations for clients. Why would they pay Alizé €1,200-1,500/month to manage something they could build over a long weekend? You're targeting the people who are *sellers of the exact thing you're selling*. This is a fundamental misalignment — the buy-vs-build problem is baked into the vertical itself.

**Challenge 2: E-commerce companies have thin margins and price sensitivity**

E-commerce SMBs in France operate on 8-20% gross margins. A €2,500/month commitment is €30,000/year — before setup fees. For a 15-person e-commerce company doing €2-5M revenue, that's a massive operational expense. Their budget for "automation improvements" is typically a few hundred euros a month, not low thousands.

**The hidden problem with D13: it mistakes proximity for strategy**

Grinto's network skews toward digital agencies because Grinto builds websites. Following that network is following the sales trail to the wrong store. It's not strategy — it's proximity. The question is not "where does Louis know people?" but "where does Alizé actually solve a problem that can't be solved internally?"

**Professional services firms are actually the better first vertical:**
1. Recurring revenue (retainers, subscriptions) → can absorb monthly fees
2. Documented, repeatable workflows (onboarding, reporting, billing) → perfect for AI agents
3. No internal technical capacity to build their own agents
4. They are buyers of managed services, not builders
5. Governance and data sovereignty *actually matter* to them — making Alizé's French positioning a genuine selling point, not an afterthought

### Recommendation
Professional services — consulting firms (10-50 employees) and accounting/reporting practices — should be the first vertical. The governance angle (French provider, EU AI Act-native) that was dismissed as irrelevant for agencies is actually a real selling point for professional services firms with compliance requirements.

**Challenges to existing assumptions:**
- Challenges D13 (digital agencies/e-commerce as first vertical)
- Challenges D14 (20-30 person pilot size) if applied to agencies — those companies can't afford the pricing
- Challenges the assumption that Grinto's network = Alizé's market
