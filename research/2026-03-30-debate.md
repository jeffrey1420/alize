# Alizé — Pulse 46 Debate: Commercial Model Architecture

**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Topic:** Should Alizé keep the managed service + monthly retainer model, or pivot to productized fixed-scope engagements?

## Agent 1: Managed Service Defender

**Position:** Preserve the managed service + monthly retainer model. The real problem is demand, not pricing structure.

### Core Argument

**Alizé does not have a pricing problem. It has a demand problem.**

After 46 pulses and 50+ pricing debates, Louis has exhaustively analyzed every dimension of how to charge — day rates, flat fees, retainers, pilots, tiers, commitment periods. What he has not done is generate a single paying client. This is the critical insight the pricing debate keeps missing:

**Pricing converts demand. It does not create it.**

### Assumption Challenged

The argument for switching to productized/fixed-scope engagements assumes that pricing confusion is blocking deal flow. This is wrong because:

1. **Zero evidence of pricing killing deals** — Louis has had no discovery calls, no prospects in negotiation, no rejections citing pricing complexity. The blocking issue is that no one is in the funnel.

2. **You cannot productize what has never been delivered** — Fixed-scope engagements require spec-able deliverables, completion definitions, and a delivery track record. Louis has none of these.

3. **Fixed-scope trades recurring for transactional** — The stated goal is a sustainable managed service business. Fixed-scope projects are one-off transactions requiring new business development every 60 days.

4. **Wrong market signal** — Fixed-scope signals "consultant for a job." Retainer signals "operational partnership." If Alizé's value is embedded agent workflows, the retainer model is the correct signal.

### Correct Sequencing

- **Phase 1 (Now):** Execute prerequisites — domain, network map, delivery simulation, first paid engagement
- **Phase 2 (After Phase 1):** Fixed-scope as entry points to retainer relationships
- **Phase 3 (After Phase 2):** Monthly retainers offered only to clients who completed a fixed-scope engagement

**The monthly retainer is the destination. Fixed-scope engagements are the on-ramp. But you need vehicles on the road before the toll booth matters.**

### Recommendation

1. Preserve managed service + retainer model as the stated business model
2. Suspend pricing structure debates until at least one paying client exists
3. Execute prerequisites first: domain, network map, delivery simulation
4. Use fixed-scope as on-ramp to retainers, not replacement
5. Validate pricing against real market data — not hypothetical buyer reactions

## Agent 2: Productized Services Challenger

**Position:** Replace managed service retainers with productized fixed-scope engagements. The two-body problem makes retainers unexecutable.

### Assumption Challenged

**"Monthly retainers are the natural evolution from pilot to ongoing relationship."** This assumes clients want ongoing management — when they might want one-time projects that work.

### Core Arguments

**1. The Two-Body Problem**
Monthly retainers require Louis to do two full-time jobs simultaneously:
- Deliver ongoing value every month (monitoring, optimization, support, reviews)
- Prospect for new clients (outreach, demos, pilots, proposals)

Louis is one person. Every month in delivery mode is a month not in prospecting mode. The productized model gives him defined end dates and batchable work.

**2. Unit Economics Fail**
From BRIEF.md's own numbers:
- Cost-plus minimum (Louis's bare cost): **€7,963/month**
- Proposed retainer: **€2,000/month**
- That's **25% of breakeven**

He needs 4 clients at €2,000/month just to break even on delivery — with zero time left for prospecting.

**3. Vendor-Friendly, Not Buyer-Friendly**
The retainer model forces clients to commit 3 months before knowing if the thing works. The productized model: "build this workflow, show me it works, then we'll talk." No lock-in. No monthly invoice. Results or done.

### What Changes

| Dimension | Managed Service (Current) | Productized (Challenger) |
|-----------|--------------------------|-------------------------|
| Client commitment | 3-month minimum | Zero lock-in |
| Louis's delivery | Ongoing, every month | Defined end date |
| Louis's prospecting | Steals from delivery | Protected between projects |
| Client risk | High (prepays uncertain) | Low (pays for scope) |
| Pricing clarity | Complex tiered | Fixed, published |
| Churn risk | Constant | Zero (project complete) |

### Recommendation

1. Kill 3-month managed service commitment — replace with fixed packages
2. Publish: "AI Workflow Setup" (€3,000-5,000, 4 weeks, 1 workflow) and "AI Workflow Bundle" (€6,000-8,000, 8 weeks, 2-3 workflows)
3. Monthly retainers become optional premium add-on — only offered after successful project completion, never required
4. Louis's calendar becomes readable — project, finish, next project
