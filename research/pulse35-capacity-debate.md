# Capacity Reality Check — Pulse 35

## The Assumption Challenged

**D176 and D178 claim:** The 2-week data sprint (track hours + map network + buy domain) replaces the binary forcing function. Capacity data before identity label. D178 resolves that the GTM sequence (cold outreach → delivery → warm referrals) has no internal contradiction.

**What D176/D178 rest on:** D91's 25+ hours/week is treated as achievable *once validated by real data*. The 2-week sprint produces that validation. D178 treats the delivery timeline as executable given a staged pilot structure (€500 → €1,000 → €1,200/mo).

**The specific assumption:** "The 2-week data sprint produces new information that changes the decision calculus." — This is the claim being audited.

---

## The Flaw

### Flaw 1: The 2-Week Data Sprint Cannot Produce New Information

U91 has been listed as "unresolved" for 28+ consecutive pulses. It is not an information deficit — it is a decision deficit. Louis knows his schedule. He is an M1 master student with an internship at Grinto and a 2-week-old web agency co-founded with Gabin and Maëli. He does not need 14 days of tracking to know he is overcommitted.

The sprint will produce data that says: "Louis works approximately X hours across all commitments." If X is less than 35-40 total hours (M1 + Grinto + web agency), then Alizé at 25+ is impossible. If X is greater than 40, the math still doesn't work because the web agency is a newborn that will demand increasing attention, and M1 has exam periods.

**The data sprint cannot change anything.** Either Louis has the hours or he doesn't. The sprint doesn't manufacture hours. D176 replaces a binary forcing function (side-project vs. startup) with a data collection exercise that arrives at the same binary — and the binary doesn't change regardless of what the data shows.

### Flaw 2: The Proposed Month 1 Sequence Is 4 Weeks of Sequential Tasks That Require Simultaneous Execution

The proposed sequence:

- **Days 1-3:** Buy domain + set up hour tracking
- **Days 4-14:** Track actual hours (10 days of parallel activity)
- **Week 2:** Cold email 10-15 ICP prospects with €1,500 offer
- **Week 3:** First €1,500 staged pilot signed
- **Week 4:** Deliver + ask for referral

This is presented as sequential. But cold email at quality (Week 2) requires ICP definition, offer writing, and deliverable infrastructure to exist. "Delivery" in Week 4 requires Louis to actually build the agent — which requires technical skill that has never been validated (U170 is still listed as unresolved: "Does Louis actually have the technical skill to deliver Pilot 1 solo?").

D31 explicitly found that "Louis cannot solo-deliver" and "a business lead with PME consulting experience needed from Day 1." D170 (Louis solo delivery self-assessment) remains unresolved after Pulse 34. The delivery question is not rhetorical — it is an open, documented gap.

### Flaw 3: The Web Agency Will Consume Louis in Month 1

The web agency is 2 weeks old. It has Maëli and Gabin as co-founders. It currently exists in a "figuring it out" phase. But 2-week-old ventures follow a predictable arc: the first real client, the first scope negotiation, the first conflict between co-founders — all of which happen in the first 30-60 days. Louis cannot control when these inflection points arrive.

U95 (web agency Alizé conflict) was flagged and remains unresolved. U107 (web agency conflict gate on delivery rehearsal) gates D100 but is not yet resolved.

**The core question no one has answered:** When a web agency client asks Maëli or Gabin "can you also automate our invoice processing?" — does Louis walk away from that revenue to protect Alizé's ICP? Or does the web agency start competing with Alizé internally?

The web agency is not a neutral actor. It is a competing venture with overlapping client needs, and it is 2 weeks old. In Month 1, Louis will be pulled into founding decisions (who owns what, how split revenue, how handle first disagreement) while simultaneously being asked to run a cold email sprint for Alizé. These are not parallelizable for a 23-year-old managing three commitments.

### Flaw 4: The 4-Week Delivery Timeline Assumes Skills That Don't Exist

D174 revised the pricing to a staged structure (€500 → €1,000 → €1,200/mo). D177 resolves the payment conflict. But the delivery question is untouched by pricing decisions.

Louis has never:
- Deployed an MCP-connected AI agent to a real client's live data
- Managed a scoped professional services engagement at €750/day equivalent
- Handled a client who pushes back on scope mid-engagement

The technical architecture (D176/MCP-first) has n8n MCP documented as "beta-not-stable" (per GTM Readiness findings). Louis is an M1 student who has built course projects — not production AI agent systems.

**U170 (Louis solo delivery capability) is unresolved.** The plan commits to delivering a pilot by Week 4 without knowing if Louis can deliver. This is not a data problem. It is a capability problem. Two weeks of hour tracking does not develop technical delivery skill.

### Flaw 5: "25+ Hours/Week" Was Never Grounded in Reality

D91 set 25 hours/week as a threshold. D121 reclassified it as aspirational pending U91 data. But the number 25 was never derived from Louis's actual capacity. It was derived from what the plan *required* — not what Louis could give.

If Louis has 12 hours/week for Alizé (a realistic estimate for someone with M1 + Grinto + web agency), the plan needs to change. The data sprint will confirm 12 hours. Then what? D176 says capacity data before identity label — but the capacity data leads back to the binary choice D176 was supposed to replace. The loop is closed, and nothing has changed.

## What This Means for Alizé

**If D176's data sprint is theater (it is):** Alizé spends 2 weeks producing a number Louis already knows. No new decisions emerge. The binary choice (side-project vs. startup) arrives at the same conclusion it would have arrived at on Day 1. The sprint consumes time Louis doesn't have.

**If the 4-week delivery timeline is used as a commitment anyway:** Louis signs a €1,500 pilot in Week 3 that he cannot deliver by Week 4. He either fails to deliver (credibility destroyed with first client), delivers substandard work (no case study value, burned reference), or pulls an all-nighter that damages M1 coursework (internship at Grinto at risk). None of these outcomes produces a second pilot.

**If the web agency demands Louis's attention in Month 1:** Alizé gets whatever hours are left over. At 2 weeks old, the web agency is in its most fragile state — it needs founding decisions, first client management, and co-founder alignment. These are not activities that coexist peacefully with a cold email sprint and a pilot delivery.

**If Louis actually has 12-15 hours/week for Alizé:** The plan's revenue model collapses. 12 hours/week × 4 weeks = 48 hours for Month 1. Cold outreach (10-15 quality messages + follow-ups + discovery calls) takes 15-20 hours. Delivery of a pilot AI agent takes 20-40 hours. These are not sequential — a discovery call in Week 2 leads to a signed pilot in Week 3, which must be delivered in Week 4. The hours don't add up.

**The consequence of ignoring this:** Alizé's first pilot is not a success. It is a reference that says "they were overloaded and it showed." The warm referral flywheel (D178's growth model) never starts because there's no referral-worthy delivery. Month 2 begins with zero revenue, one damaged client relationship, and a web agency that's now competing for the same clients Alizé needs.

---

## The Verdict

D176's 2-week data sprint does not produce new information. It produces documentation of a constraint that was never in dispute. The binary choice D176 replaced has not been eliminated — it has been deferred by two weeks, during which Louis spends time he doesn't have documenting a problem he already understands.

The proposed Month 1 sequence is not a plan. It is a sequence of good intentions organized into weeks, with no bearing on Louis's actual capacity, technical skill, or the web agency's demands.

The question D176 was supposed to answer — "can Louis execute Month 1?" — cannot be answered by tracking hours. It can only be answered by making the binary choice D176 was designed to avoid.

**Recommendation:** This audit identifies 5 unresolved preconditions that make Month 1 execution impossible regardless of what the hour tracking data shows. They are: (1) Louis's delivery capability (U170), (2) the web agency's Alizé conflict (U95/U107), (3) the ICP target list (U172), (4) Louis's binary choice, and (5) a realistic delivery timeline that accounts for the actual hours available. Until all five are resolved, Month 1 is not a GTM plan — it is a press release.
