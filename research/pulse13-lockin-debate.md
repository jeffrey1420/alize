# PULSE #13 — Lock-in Debate: Challenging Alizé's Core Positioning Assumptions

**Date:** 2026-03-30  
**Role:** Competitive Strategy  
**Task:** Challenge the "no lock-in" assumption and at least one additional positioning pillar

---

## Assumption 1: "No Lock-in" Is a Competitive Advantage

### Why It May Be Wrong

**"No lock-in" signals instability, not freedom.** French business culture — shaped by conventions collectives, long-term supplier relationships, and a general suspicion of transactional vendor dynamics — rewards vendors who embed themselves. When Alizé leads with "no lock-in," a French PME buyer hears: *"This vendor could leave when things get difficult."* The message that should convey trust instead communicates fragility.

**French buyers value commitment, not portability.** A DGA or DAF evaluating a €1,000/month service wants to know the vendor will still be there in 18 months, that they'll pick up the phone, that the agent won't just disappear if the product pivots. "No lock-in" does the opposite — it promises exit, not investment.

**Counterintuitive but real: lock-in can be a selling point when framed correctly.** The frame isn't "we lock you in" — it's "we're so embedded in your operations that leaving would cost you more than staying." Deep integration, process ownership, custom MCP connectors, training, institutional knowledge — these create natural switching costs that are the mark of a valuable vendor, not a predatory one.

### What to Do Differently

**Replace "no lock-in" messaging with commitment language:**
- *"We build agents that become essential to how you operate."*
- *"You'll never need to rebuild from scratch — we own the integration layer."*
- Contractual commitments (12-month minimums, mutual success clauses) signal investment from both sides.

**Position switching costs as proof of value, not as traps.** If a client leaving Alizé means rebuilding agent workflows, reconnecting integrations, retraining teams — that's a feature of the service, not a bug.

**One specific change:** Remove "no lock-in" from all public-facing positioning. Replace with language around "deep integration" and "operational embedding." Test a new headline like: *"Des agents IA qui font partie de votre équipe — pas un outil que vous remplacez."*

## Assumption 2: "Client Controls the Agent" Is a Positive Frame

### Why It May Be Wrong

**PME buyers don't want control — they want relief.** The primary Alizé client (a 50-200 person company, founder or COO decision-maker) is overwhelmed with operational complexity. They don't have time to manage an AI agent. Telling them "you control the agent" is like telling a tired parent they control their new employee — technically true, operationally burdensome, and not what they hired you for.

**"Client controls" may trigger anxiety, not empowerment.** What if they configure it wrong? What if the agent does something unexpected? Control without expertise is a liability. Most Alizé clients will never want to touch the agent configuration — they'll want to point at a problem and have it solved.

**The real value is accountability, not control.** Alizé should sell: *"We take responsibility for the agent's performance — you just measure the results."* That's a fundamentally different (and more reassuring) promise than giving the client a control panel they didn't ask for.

### What to Do Differently

**Shift the frame from "control" to "accountability":**
- Current: *"You control the agent"*  
- Better: *"We're accountable for the results — you own the outcomes"*

**Sell the absence of operational burden, not the presence of control.** The brief already describes Alizé as a managed service. The messaging should match: Alizé handles everything, client shows up to monthly reviews and sees the numbers.

**Consider removing "client controls" from pitch decks entirely.** If it appears anywhere, it should be as a secondary technical benefit (*"You can always review agent actions in the audit log"*) not as a primary value proposition.

## Assumption 3: Agentova Is the Primary Competitive Reference Point

### Why It May Be Wrong

**The price gap is too large to share a frame.** Agentova at €80-150/month serves micro-enterprises and solo entrepreneurs. Alizé at €500-1,500+/month serves PME/ETI with 50-250 employees. These are different buyers, different problems, different buying processes. Referencing Agentova in positioning implies Alizé is the "premium Agentova" — which is both inaccurate and underselling.

**Category-defining companies don't compete by comparison.** If Alizé becomes known as "the Agentova for bigger companies," it ceded category creation to Agentova. That's a strategic mistake. Alizé should own the category: *managed operational AI agents for French PME/ETI.* Agentova becomes irrelevant — not an enemy to be named, but a context that no longer matters.

**D25 (never name competitors in first outreach) already acknowledges this.** The rule exists because naming competitors in first outreach legitimizes them as the frame. If Alizé is constantly explaining itself relative to Agentova, it has already lost the framing war.

### What to Do Differently

**Stop using Agentova as a primary comparison.** The competitive table in the brief is useful internally but should not appear in external materials. When prospects ask "how is this different from Agentova?", Alizé should answer without validating the comparison: *"Agentova serves very small businesses with self-service tools — we're set up for companies that need someone to own the entire process."*

**Invest in category creation messaging.** Instead of "unlike self-service tools," lead with positive differentiation: *"For companies that need AI agents that actually work in your operations — not just demos — Alizé is the managed service that builds, runs, and optimizes them for you."*

## New Decision / Unresolved Question

**Decision needed:** Should Alizé introduce a formal "commitment tier" in its commercial offer — a 12-month minimum contract that includes enhanced SLA, quarterly business reviews, and priority new use case development — explicitly framed as *"for companies that want a true operational partner"?*

This would:
- Signal Alizé's investment in client success (opposite of "no lock-in")
- Create the switching-cost dynamics that sustain long-term relationships
- Differentiate from both self-service tools (no commitment) and pure consultants (deliver and leave)
- Test whether French PME buyers respond to commitment language vs. flexibility language

**Unresolved:** Does the target buyer (French PME founder/COO/DGA) actually want a vendor who commits, or do they value the escape hatch of a no-lock-in model? No primary research has validated this. The French cultural tendency toward stability suggests commitment resonates — but Alizé should test this in early discovery calls before building it into the offer structure.
