# Pulse 56 — Commercial Model Challenger: Agent 1

## Assumption Being Challenged

**"Louis must deliver Kuroba/internal pilots before any external commercial motion begins."**

This assumption appears across D113, D170, D194, and D241. The logic: Louis learns to deliver via internal deployment (Kuroba), then uses that capability to win external clients. Delivery capability is a prerequisite gate. Commercial motion follows delivery readiness.

---

## Why It's Wrong or Incomplete

**1. The internal rehearsal premise is category-mismatched.**

Kuroba is a 2-3 person web agency. Alizé targets 20-50 person service firms. These are different operational complexities, different decision-maker levels, different workflow stakes, and different trust requirements. Deploying a workflow automation for a 2-person agency teaches Louis nothing meaningful about delivering for a 30-person consulting firm. The operational fidelity of this "rehearsal" is zero. D242 correctly identified that external pilots must match ICP size/complexity — but then the Kuroba internal deployment was still approved as Month 1 activity. This is a direct contradiction.

**2. The sequencing inverts the value creation order.**

The Kuroba-first model says: build delivery capability → then find commercial clients. The evidence says Louis has 91+ days of documented inability to execute on parallel tracks (Grinto + Kuroba + M1). The research itself documents this paralysis repeatedly. The correct order for a capital-constrained, execution-limited founder is: **sell first (find a client who will commit money), then deliver**. Selling creates urgency, focus, and external accountability. Building capability in a vacuum with no client deadline produces infinite deferral — which is exactly what 91+ days of inaction demonstrates.

D183 proposed framing first 1-2 pilots as "research partnerships, no cost for first month in exchange for feedback." D190 killed this. But the underlying logic was right: get a real client in the room, then figure out delivery. The Kuroba-first model keeps Louis in solo-build mode for Month 1 with no external accountability and no commercial validation.

**3. Louis's delivery education has zero commercial value until Alizé exists.**

The Kuroba deployment builds AI agent infrastructure for Louis's own 2-3 person web agency. This work produces:
- Kuroba operational efficiency (useful for Kuroba)
- Louis's delivery learning (useful if Alizé launches)
- Zero external credibility for Alizé's target market

A 2-person web agency case study does not convert a 30-person service firm. The commercial value of the Kuroba-first model is entirely contingent on Alizé existing as a separate business — which requires external commercial validation, not internal rehearsal.

---

## What the Research Has Not Considered

**The asymmetry between delivery capability and commercial credibility.**

These are not the same asset. They have different build trajectories, different scarcity, and different commercial value.

- **Delivery capability** is buildable incrementally. Louis can learn by doing. Contractors can fill gaps. Skills improve over time with practice. This is a ramp function.
- **Commercial credibility** is binary. You either have a paying client who'll take a call, or you don't. You either have a reference with specific metrics, or you're an unknown. This doesn't improve gradually — it requires external validation that cannot be generated internally.

The research treats delivery capability as the scarce resource and commercial credibility as something that follows delivery. The opposite is true. Delivery capability can be contracted, learned, or delegated (D205: "add delivery partner only when turning down work"). Commercial credibility — specifically a named reference client with measurable ROI — cannot be delegated. It requires Louis or someone in Alizé to be in the room with a paying client.

**What the research also missed: the opportunity cost is quantifiable.**

- 91+ days of documented execution failure (U91 unresolved)
- Month 1 dedicated to Kuroba internal deployment = zero external commercial progress
- D82 capacity window: 3-6 months effective
- Kuroba-first burns Month 1 with no commercial output, compressing the already-tight window to 2-5 months of actual commercial motion before exhaustion risk

The research debates delivery sequencing ad infinitum but never prices this cost.

---

## What Should Change

**In the TODO:**

1. **Kill "Kuroba internal deployment as Month 1 prerequisite."** Replace with: Louis identifies one warm contact (any vertical, any size) who will commit to a paid diagnostic this week. Kuroba deployment can happen in parallel with client delivery — it is not a gate.

2. **Flip the delivery model:** The first external pilot IS the delivery education. Not a prelude to it. D113 got this partially right ("Louis solo delivery IS delivery education") but then contradicted it with D194/D241 (Kuroba as Month 1 internal deployment). These cannot both be true.

3. **Add a new unresolved item:** "What is the minimum commercial evidence Louis needs before Kuroba's internal work becomes commercially useful to Alizé?" The answer should be: one named reference client. Until that exists, Kuroba is Kuroba's asset, not Alizé's.

4. **Commercial credibility takes priority over delivery capability.** The TODO should list: Find first paying client → Deliver to them (with contractor support if needed) → Build delivery playbook from that experience. Not: Build delivery capability → Then find clients.

**In the approach:**

- Louis should be in front of potential clients NOW — not after Kuroba is deployed.
- The 3-6 month window (D82) is a credibility window, not a delivery-readiness window. The window closes on credibility, not on capability.
- Delivery contractors (D205) can fill the delivery gap. Nothing in the research says Louis must personally deliver all early pilots. The gate should be commercial credibility (a client in the room), not delivery readiness (Kuroba internal work).

---

## Summary

The Kuroba-first model treats Alizé as a product company that needs to ship before selling. It's a consulting business. In consulting, you sell first, deliver second, and use contractors to bridge delivery gaps. Louis's 91-day execution failure is not a delivery capability problem — it's a commercial activation problem. The Kuroba-first model doesn't fix it; it buries it under another internal project with zero commercial output.

**Recommendation:** Month 1 = one paid diagnostic engagement (any warm contact, any ICP match) + Kuroba deployment in parallel (if Louis has hours). These are not sequential. The commercial client comes first.
