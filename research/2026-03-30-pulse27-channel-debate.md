# Pulse 27 — Malt Channel Feasibility Challenge

## The Assumption Being Challenged

**Assumption:** Malt.fr is a viable primary channel for Alizé's first-client acquisition, supplementing the demoted warm network track.

**Source:** D130 — "Warm network demoted to parallel gated track; supplement with Malt + expert-comptable association outreach." U130 — "Can Louis list on Malt? What specific workflow to offer first? Feasibility check Week 1."

**Context at time of writing:** Zero pilots completed. Zero case studies. Zero testimonials. Louis's expert-comptable network is unverified. Malt listing does not yet exist.

---

## Argument 1: The Cold-Start Problem — Zero Reviews = Zero Visibility

Malt.fr's search ranking is heavily weighted toward **review count and recency**. A new profile with 0 reviews is invisible in any competitive search query. A search for "consultant IA" or "automatisation IA" on Malt returns established profiles with 10–50+ reviews, years of history, and portfolio galleries.

**The math:**
- Malt's algorithm buries unrated profiles
- A new profile needs at minimum 3–5 reviews to appear mid-pack on any keyword
- Those reviews require completed, paid engagements
- With zero engagements, Louis cannot generate reviews without first getting clients
- **This is a chicken-and-egg trap with no bootstrap path**

The warm network demotion compounds this. If Louis's first 2–3 engagements come from his network (the only people who might trust an unknown consultant), he should use those to **build his Malt review base**, not abandon the warm track entirely. Those network engagements ARE the engine that fuels the Malt profile.

**Verdict:** Malt as an *inbound* channel is dead on arrival at Stage 0. It can only work as an *outbound* credibility anchor — i.e., Louis finds clients elsewhere and then routes them through Malt to accumulate reviews.

---

## Argument 2: The Workflow Problem — "AI Automation" Doesn't Convert

U130 correctly identifies this but it remains unanswered. A Malt listing that says "AI workflow automation for regulated industries" is meaningless to a buyer. Malt buyers are typically PMEs/PMIs with finite budgets and specific pain points. They search for things like:

- "Automatiser ma facturation"
- "Automatiser le suivi client"
- "Créer un chatbot pour mon cabinet"

**The problem:** Alizé has no defined, priced, specific workflow offering yet. The product appears to be a platform or framework ("AI agents") rather than a solved, packaged problem. Malt listings that convert are specific:
- *"Automatisation de la saisie factures — 1500€ fixe, 3 semaines"*
- *"Chatbot de prise de RDV pour cabinet médical — 800€/mois"*

Without a specific, priced, understandable offer, the listing is DOA. It will be ignored or filtered out because it doesn't answer the buyer's implicit question: *"Can this person solve MY problem, and what will it cost?"*

**Verdict:** Malt cannot be the primary channel until Alizé has a defined, narrow, priced first workflow. Until then, listing on Malt is performative — it looks like a real channel but generates nothing.

---

## Argument 3: The Competition Problem — Filtering and Buyer Behavior

Malt buyers use the platform because it reduces search and trust costs. They browse, compare, and hire based on:
1. Reviews (social proof)
2. Portfolio relevance (did they solve a similar problem?)
3. Rate (is it within budget?)

A new entrant with none of these is **auto-filtered** by buyer behavior. This is not a marketing problem — it's a structural problem. Malt's buyer segment for "AI consultant" skews toward small businesses who found an established consultant on page 1 and never reached page 3 where Louis would appear.

**The expert-comptable angle makes this worse, not better.** Expert-comptables are a niche vertical. Searching "expert-comptable" + "automatisation" on Malt yields a very small pool. Buyers in this segment are sophisticated about vendor claims — they deal with software vendors, ERP providers, and automation tools regularly. An unproven AI agent startup with no case studies will be dismissed instantly.

**Verdict:** Competing on Malt as a generalist AI consultant is a losing game at Stage 0. Competing as a niche vertical (expert-comptable automation) is theoretically better but requires vertical-specific proof points that don't yet exist.

---

## Viable Malt Strategy (If Any)

The above doesn't mean Malt should be abandoned entirely. It means **Malt should be repositioned from "primary inbound channel" to "review accumulation machine fed by outbound."**

### Concrete approach:

**Step 1 — Define one specific, priced workflow first.**
Example framing for an expert-comptable vertical:
> *"Automatisation de la réconciliation bancaire pour cabinets comptables — audit gratuit puis 2 500€ HT, livrable en 4 semaines."*

This is specific. It has a price. It targets a known pain point (réconciliation bancaire = tedious, manual, universal in expert-comptable firms). It doesn't require explaining "AI agents."

**Step 2 — Use the warm network as the first client source.**
Do not demote the warm network. Use it aggressively for the first 2–3 engagements. The goal of the warm network is NOT to be a long-term channel — it's to generate the first Malt reviews. Every network engagement should be:
- Completed successfully
- Invoiced through Malt (if possible) or followed up with a review request
- Documented as a mini case study (even 1 paragraph)

**Step 3 — List on Malt with specific credentials, not vague positioning.**
Profile should state:
- Vertical: expert-comptables / PME réglementées
- First specific offer: the priced workflow from Step 1
- First "case study": the warm network engagement(s) once completed
- No mention of "AI agents" or "LLMs" — buyer language only

**Step 4 — Accept CAC on the first 2–3 reviews.**
The first reviews are not free marketing — they are paid acquisition. Price the first engagement at cost or below to ensure completion, quality, and a positive review. This is normal for marketplace-based consultants at launch. Treat it as a platform entry fee.

**The platform that might be better than Malt for Stage 0:**
- **LinkedIn outreach** to expert-comptable firms directly — no review dependency, direct conversation
- **Specialized accounting industry events** (UNASA, sessions de formation expert-comptables) where Louis can present
- **Cold email to a curated list of expert-comptables** — with the specific, priced workflow as the offer
- Malt as a secondary credibility layer added AFTER first reviews exist

## Bottom Line

**Malt is premature as a primary channel.** It is a channel that rewards you for already being established. The sequence must be reversed: build proof elsewhere (warm network, direct outreach), then use Malt to amplify and attract inbound. The "Week 1 feasibility check" should not be "can Louis list on Malt" — it should be "what is the one specific workflow Louis will sell for a fixed price to his first client." Everything else follows from that.
