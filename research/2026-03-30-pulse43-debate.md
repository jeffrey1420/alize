## What Was Debated

### Debate 1: Channel Partner Viability — Is D219's Path 3 Actually a Path?

**Challenged assumption:** Channel partners (accounting firms, consulting firms, business software resellers) can serve as Alizé's commercial layer from Month 3-4.

**Verdict: DEAD END for Year 1.**

Five independent challenges, each fatal:

1. **Accounting firms are buyers, not channels.** Expert-comptables are building their own AI practices (Cegid AI, Sage AI integrations). Referring Alizé means referring a competitor. The correct relationship is Alizé selling TO accounting firms as clients, not THROUGH them as partners.

2. **Software resellers are competitors.** Cegid/Sage/Pennylane partner ecosystem is captured by those vendors' own AI roadmaps. A reseller who refers Alizé is either underperforming for their vendor or concealing a conflict of interest.

3. **Channel economics are broken at 15-20%.** Referral fees of €225-1,500 per client don't justify the sales effort (5-10 hours of warm introduction, education, follow-up). Effective hourly rate drops below minimum wage. No recurring revenue-share infrastructure exists in French accounting firms.

4. **Channel timeline is 6-9 months minimum.** First closed referral from cold channel development takes 6-9 months. This arrives after the 3-6 month commercial window closes, not solving it.

5. **Trusted advisor = conservative, not a sales channel.** French advisors recommend within their domain expertise. AI agents are outside it. Trust transfer across service categories is weaker than assumed.

**Core finding:** The "trusted advisor" dynamic is the obstacle, not the vehicle. French advisors are conservative, self-interested, and lack commercial motivation to promote an unproven AI startup to their clients.

### Debate 2: Positioning Clarity — After 42 Demotions, What Actually Survives?

**Challenged assumption:** "Back-office admin AI for service firms" (D77) is a sufficient positive positioning claim.

**Verdict: No coherent positive claim survives the demotions.**

After stripping everything removed across 42 pulses, the honest hero reads:

> "We use AI agents to automate repetitive back-office tasks for service companies. You'll save time. You'll have more control. It's not a chatbot."

This is a product definition, not a positioning claim. Every automation tool — Zapier, Make.com, Power Automate, any RPA vendor for 15 years — says the same thing. There's no differentiated positive claim left.

**The core problem:** Positioning is a symptom of product/market fit, not a cause. The evidence:
- Vertical has flipped 5+ times
- First workflow undefined at Pulse 42 (D81 revised, D126 reversed, U125 unresolved)
- Pricing has swung through an order of magnitude
- Delivery model changed 6+ times

**The ONE sentence that works (D226):**
> "We take over your invoice processing, CRM updates, and client FAQ responses — the 15 hours a week of repetitive data work that your senior staff are doing manually — and run it so you never have to think about it again."

**Verdict:** Positioning refinement is paused until one pilot with a named client and specific metrics exists. No amount of research produces this evidence.
