# Pilot Evidence Assumption — 2026-03-31

## Assumption Challenged
A successful pilot produces usable commercial evidence (case study, testimonial, metric) that accelerates future sales.

## 3 Failure Modes

1. **Client confidentiality locks evidence in place.** French SMBs at 50-200 employees are secretive about operational details — they won't name you, won't let you quote them, won't publish metrics. A pilot that saves 10 hours/week produces zero publishable evidence without explicit written authorization obtained at pilot close. The authorization conversation is a separate sales task most SMB owners deflect or decline. The evidence exists; you can't use it.

2. **Pilot metrics are too narrow to generalize.** A successful pilot for a 100-person accounting firm automating invoice processing generates a case study that only resonates with accounting firms. The hours-saved figure is real but doesn't transfer to a prospect in legal, logistics, or healthcare who has different workflows. The evidence is vertically bounded — it proves Alizé works for one type of company doing one type of task, not that it works for the ICP broadly.

3. **Relationship strength at pilot end is insufficient to extract evidence.** Case studies require asking at the right moment with the right framing, and a client who feels positively disposed toward you. If the pilot relationship ended without a contracted follow-on engagement, most SMB owners go quiet. You have a success story internally; you can't tell it externally. The evidence loop closes only if you negotiated evidence rights and client relationship maintenance as part of the pilot contract — not after the pilot succeeds.

## What French SMB Buyers Actually Require

**Form:** A peer reference — a direct phone call with a known contact in their sector who used Alizé. Not a logo, not a PDF, not a quote with no name.

**Source:** A real person, at a real company, in the same industry or a similar size, who can be identified and contacted. Anonymous case studies carry near-zero weight with French SMB decision-makers — they assume the client was either fictional or under NDA.

**Specificity:**
- Named company (or company type + headcount)
- One concrete outcome: hours saved per week, tasks automated, specific process before/after
- Willingness to take a 10-minute call with a prospect
- Preferably a company the prospect knows or has heard of (warm network effect)

**What they do NOT accept as proof:**
- Generic testimonials ("great service," "saved time")
- Benchmarks from other industries
- Platform capability demos (Kuroba ≠ Alizé delivery)
- Whitepapers or decks
- Self-reported numbers without verification

## Minimum Viable Evidence Package (No Paying Client)

Louis can produce this without a single paying client:

1. **Kuroba deployment as live capability proof** — one documented deployment of Alizé's stack in production, with screenshots, architecture description, and one specific automation demonstrated end-to-end. Not sold as Alizé evidence — framed as Alizé-built capability. Pairs with D194/D195 scope constraints.

2. **One structured diagnostic deliverable** — a written output from a real discovery call (with written permission from the prospect), showing the Alizé analysis framework: use case prioritization, tool map, ROI estimate. The deliverable itself is evidence of rigor and thinking.

3. **3-5 warm network testimonials** — informal written or voice messages from people who know Louis and have seen Alizé work, confirming capability. Not case studies — personal endorsements. Source specificity: name, title, company type, relationship to Louis. These carry weight in French business culture where relationships precede transactions.

4. **Published technical work** — one article or post demonstrating a specific Alizé capability (e.g., "How we built Kuroba's email pipeline with MCP + Mastra"). Source: Louis on LinkedIn, Alizé blog. Establishes domain authority without requiring client permission.

## Verdict: ALTERNATIVE STRATEGY

**Do not plan around pilot→case study as the primary evidence loop.**

The pilot→case study path has a structural gap: it requires a paying client to exist before any commercial evidence can be produced, then requires a second negotiation (evidence rights, testimonial, reference call) that most SMBs decline. The timeline is: pilot → 30 days → evidence extraction negotiation → client says no → zero evidence, 6 weeks lost.

Louis should pursue parallel evidence tracks that don't depend on a paying client:
- Kuroba as the first published proof of delivery capability (already exists)
- One diagnostic call per week, targeting warm network, producing one written deliverable per month
- LinkedIn content establishing domain authority in French AI agent space

The pilot remains valuable for validating delivery capability and converting into recurring revenue, but it should not be the primary evidence vehicle. Evidence generation and client acquisition must run in separate tracks — one that builds commercial proof without requiring a client to give it, and one that acquires clients through the evidence already produced.

**Recommendation:** Replace "pilot→case study" with "diagnostic→warm network→pilot→reference call" as the primary evidence strategy, with Kuroba + published work as the baseline proof available from day one.
