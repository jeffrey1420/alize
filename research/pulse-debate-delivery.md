# DEBATE: Is the Louis-Only Delivery Model Broken?

## The Assumption (D23)
> "Louis delivers Month 1 manually + documents → contractor executes Month 2+ against playbook"

---

## VERDICT: BROKEN. Not fundamentally — but for reasons the team hasn't considered.

---

## Challenge 1: Is Louis the right person for the FIRST pilot?

**No. And the reason matters.**

Louis is a developer. The pilot doesn't need a developer. It needs a business consultant who can:

- Map the client's actual operational workflow (not build an agent — *find the right workflow to automate*)
- Manage the client's expectations, objections, and mid-pilot scope changes
- Translate "this workflow takes 4 hours/week" into measurable ROI language
- Make the client's team *want* to adopt the agent, not resist it

The first pilot's primary job isn't technical. It's *credibility*. If Louis fumbles the business conversation, the client won't care how elegant the Mastra workflow is. They'll pass. The case study won't exist. The playbook doesn't matter.

**Alizé needs a delivery specialist in Month 1 — not Month 2.**

---

## Challenge 2: What happens in 30 days that Louis can't do?

The 30-day pilot requires skills Louis almost certainly doesn't have:

- **Process mapping** — Structured discovery to identify *which* workflow matters most, not just the one the client mentioned
- **Change management** — Getting desk workers to actually use a new tool. This is 80% of why AI projects fail in SMEs
- **Stakeholder management** — Navigating the person who blocking adoption because they feel threatened
- **ROI measurement** — Quantifying "operational ownership transfer" in terms the client CFO cares about

Louis can build. He probably can't consult. These are different professions.

---

## Challenge 3: Does "document everything" actually transfer?

**No. And this is the fatal flaw.**

Tacit knowledge — the judgment calls, the "this client won't accept that approach," the body language reading — *cannot be documented*.

Louis will make dozens of implicit decisions during Month 1. He'll write the playbook from his perspective, which means:

- It'll capture what *he* did, not why *he* did it
- Edge cases won't be documented because he didn't see them coming
- The contractor inherits a map of one terrain, not a compass for new terrain

A playbook is a snapshot, not a transfer protocol.

---

## Specific Risks if Louis Delivers Alone

1. **Wrong workflow selected** — Without process mapping expertise, first pilot hits a "nice to have" not an "operationally critical" workflow → weak case study, poor conversion
2. **Zero adoption** — Agent works, client team doesn't use it → client says "it didn't work" → no conversion, no case study
3. **No measurable ROI** — Cannot demonstrate the "operational ownership transfer" framing from D29 → client has no reason to convert
4. **Credibility damage** — Client sees a student running their pilot → "why am I paying €4,500 for this?"

---

## Minimum Viable Delivery Team for Month 1

| Role | What they do | Who |
|------|-------------|-----|
| **Delivery lead** | Client-facing, process mapping, change management, stakeholder management | Contractor / consultant — needed from Day 1 |
| **Technical executor** | Agent build, integration, monitoring | Louis |

**Louis is the technical half. He needs a delivery half.** This isn't a luxury — it's the minimum to avoid wasting the entire pilot.

---

## 3 Recommendations

**1. Partner Louis with a delivery specialist for Month 1**
   - French-speaking business consultant, even part-time
   - They run the client relationship; Louis runs the tech
   - Cost: €500-800/day, probably 3-5 days total across 30 days
   - This is a €2,000-4,000 investment that protects a €4,500 pilot fee and a case study

**2. Define explicit delivery milestones, not just technical ones**
   - Day 7: Process map signed off by client
   - Day 14: Agent deployed to test group (not full team)
   - Day 21: Adoption check-in — measure actual usage, not just agent accuracy
   - Day 28: ROI document presented, conversion conversation initiated
   - If milestones slip, escalate to Louis + specialist joint session

**3. Write the playbook as decision log, not procedure manual**
   - Instead of "do X": write "client had problem Y, chose X because Z failed"
   - Capture the *reasoning*, not just the output
   - Contractor reviews playbook with Louis before taking over — 2-hour handover session
   - Month 2 contractor should shadow Month 1 delivery, not just read the doc

---

## Bottom Line

D23 is not wrong — it's incomplete. Louis delivering Month 1 is fine. Louis delivering Month 1 *alone* is a recipe for a technically successful pilot that commercially fails. Alizé's first case study is too important to leave to a developer who hasn't done business consulting.

**The fix is cheap. The failure mode is expensive.**
