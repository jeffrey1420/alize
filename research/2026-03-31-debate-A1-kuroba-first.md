# Agent 1: The Challenger — Alizé vs. Kuroba First

**Position:** Louis should NOT build Alizé as a separate venture. He should fold Alizé's positioning into Kuroba and execute that instead.

---

## Assumption Challenged: "Alizé fills a gap in the French PME market at a price point that works"

### Original Assumption
(BRIEF.md) "Alizé fills the gap: a managed, governed, professionally deployed agent service built for French companies that want real results, not another dashboard." / "French companies are behind on AI adoption vs. US peers, creating urgency." / Pricing at €1,500-2,500/month.

### Counter-Argument
The market gap is asserted, not validated. Three structural problems:

1. **Price-point mismatch for stated ICP.** The brief targets 20-250 employee French PME, but 20-30 person companies (established as the right first-pilot target in D14) have administrative budgets that cannot absorb €1,500-2,500/month. At 20 employees, that's €75-125/employee/month for a workflow automation tool. That's not a "measurable ROI" purchase — it's a luxury. The ROI math collapses above €800/month for this segment.

2. **"Managed service" positioning is a liability, not a differentiator.** The debate log shows this was already identified (D11, D69). For 20-50 person French PME, "managed service" signals cost and complexity. These buyers want results, not a vendor relationship. Alizé's managed service framing requires the buyer to trust a vendor's governance promises — but an unknown brand cannot command that trust at €1,500+/month.

3. **No primary research on willingness to pay.** U102 in the debate log explicitly flags this as unresolved: "Buyer willingness to pay €800-1,500/month when Copilot at €30/user already exists? No primary research conducted." Building a venture on an unvalidated willingness-to-pay assumption at a 20x premium over category-entry pricing is not a strategy — it's a hope.

### Evidence
- D43 establishes €1,200-1,500/month as starter tier minimum
- D82 establishes the effective window at 3-6 months (not 6-18) given Louis's capacity
- U102 (unresolved): willingness to pay at Alizé's price point has never been researched
- D107 (Pulse 22): "Price point unsellable regardless of who closes; restructure pilot fee or accept first clients as CAC"
- The brief itself shows conversion mechanism requires hybrid outcome guarantees (D28), which signals the seller knows the price is hard to justify

### Verdict: REJECT

The gap exists in theory but the price point required to sustain Alizé as a business does not match the ICP's actual budget tolerance. Kuroba, positioned as a single AI workflow studio at a lower price or with a tighter scope, might serve the same market more honestly.

## Assumption Challenged: "Expert-comptables as first vertical — Louis has warm access to this vertical"

### Original Assumption
(D126) "Expert-comptables (administrative workflows around client management/regulatory reporting) as first beachhead." / (D81REVISED) Expert-comptable administrative workflow as first use case. / The governance gap is the differentiator that disqualifies Copilot for this vertical.

### Counter-Argument
1. **Louis has no stated warm access to expert-comptables.** The debate log (Pulse 25, U123) explicitly flags: "Does Louis have warm contacts in accounting firms?" This is marked unresolved. The entire vertical strategy rests on a warm-access assumption that has never been confirmed. Meanwhile, Louis's actual network — Grinto, web agency relationships — points toward digital/creative, not accounting.

2. **The "governance gap disqualifies Copilot" argument is circular.** If expert-comptables are already using Copilot or are comfortable with generic AI tools, the governance wedge doesn't exist. The brief claims governance is a gate for this vertical, but there's no evidence that French expert-comptables experience Copilot as inadequate for their administrative workflows. The governance angle was explicitly demoted elsewhere (D69: "Does governance differentiate for non-regulated PME? Likely no — demote from hero/headline positioning").

3. **Expert-comptables are not a homogeneous vertical.** French expert-comptables range from solo practitioners (too small for Alizé's pricing) to 50+ person firms (the target). The 50+ segment has internal IT, procurement processes, and existing software vendors (Pennylane, Sage, Cegid). Breaking through requires relationship + proof + domain credibility Louis does not have.

4. **Pulse 11 already killed this vertical.** D44 (professional services first) was itself reversed in Pulse 15 (D68) because "agencies can build their own agents." Expert-comptables have the same buy-vs-build problem — many already use Pennylane (which has AI features) and have bookkeepers who resist change. The vertical keeps getting picked and unpicked without ever being validated with actual market evidence.

### Evidence
- U123 (unresolved since Pulse 25): "Does Louis have warm contacts in accounting firms?"
- U124 (unresolved): "Does Louis understand expert-comptable admin workflows?"
- U125 (unresolved): "Which specific workflow — client onboarding, regulatory reporting, document collection?"
- D69: governance/accountability does NOT differentiate for non-regulated PME
- D44 → D68 → D126: vertical has been reversed three times without ever being grounded in Louis's actual network
- P69-D6: Alizé is "viable as side project (5-8 hrs/week); not viable as designed startup requiring 25+ hrs/week"

### Verdict: REJECT

Expert-comptables as a first vertical is a theoretical construction that has never been validated against Louis's actual network or actual buyer behavior. The entire vertical-first strategy should be abandoned until Louis maps his network (U52/U88) — and if that map shows no expert-comptable warm contacts, the vertical dies on its own. Forcing this vertical wastes Month 1-3 on a cold outreach target when warm outreach is already acknowledged as the only viable Year 1 channel.

## Assumption Challenged: "Kuroba and Alizé are separate ventures"

### Original Assumption
(P67-D4, P67-D5, P67-D6, P67-D9) "Kuroba workflow deployment IS Alizé's first pilot proof." / "Lead with 'co-founder of Kuroba' in all Alizé outreach." / "Kuroba IS Alizé proof; begin outreach while deploying." / "Kuroba is a separate venture from Alizé."

### Counter-Argument
The debate log already exposes the structural absurdity: Alizé's first proof of concept IS Kuroba. Kuroba's workflow deployment IS being used as Alizé's social proof. Louis is the co-founder of both. The "separate venture" framing is creating artificial complexity with zero strategic benefit. Here's the case for merging:

1. **Alizé has no independent credibility.** The brief acknowledges Louis needs Kuroba's deployment as Alizé's proof. But Kuroba IS Louis's. If Alizé leads with "co-founder of Kuroba," the prospect immediately asks: "What is the relationship between these two companies?" The answer — "they're separate, but Kuroba's work proves Alizé's capabilities" — sounds like a shell structure, not a coherent business story.

2. **Running two ventures is the core capacity problem.** Pulse 69 (P69-D6, P69-D7) identified the structural mismatch: Alizé requires 25+ hrs/week but Louis can't deliver that. The real question isn't "how does Louis split his time?" — it's "why are there two ventures competing for the same limited time?" If Kuroba is Louis's existing AI workflow studio, folding Alizé's positioning into Kuroba eliminates the second venture entirely. One company, one GTM motion, one delivery infrastructure.

3. **The Kuroba positioning already covers Alizé's core claim.** Kuroba.studio is an AI workflow studio. Alizé's positioning is "managed AI agent service for French PME." These are the same market, nearly the same product claim, with Alizé adding a "managed/governed" layer that the debate log shows doesn't differentiate at the price point. If Louis deploys Kuroba's AI workflow capability with a managed-service overlay, that's not a new venture — that's a natural product expansion within Kuroba.

4. **"Separate venture" is a liability for fundraising and recruiting.** If Louis ever wants to raise for Alizé, a VC will immediately ask: "Why is this separate from Kuroba? Are you competing with yourself?" If Louis ever wants to bring on a co-founder or senior hire, the equity split between two ventures creates structural friction. A single venture with a clear product line (Kuroba Core / Kuroba Managed) is cleaner.

5. **The Alizé brand may be worth less than Kuroba.** Alizé has zero deployed clients, no domain, no social proof. Kuroba has at least one internal deployment (the "proof" being used in Pulse 67). From a brand equity standpoint, Kuroba is the more credible entity. Building Alizé as a separate brand from scratch while Kuroba sits unused is destroying value, not creating it.

6. **The delivery infrastructure is identical.** Both ventures use Mastra (or similar agent runtime), OVHcloud, MCP integration, and the same French PME client profile. Two ventures means two websites, two legal structures, two accounting overheads, two CRM systems, two email domains. For a solo technical founder with a 25-hour weekly ceiling, this overhead is fatal.

### Evidence
- P67-D4: "Kuroba workflow deployment IS Alizé's first pilot proof" — Alizé cannot demonstrate independent proof of concept
- P67-D5: "Lead with 'co-founder of Kuroba' in all Alizé outreach" — Kuroba IS the credential, not Alizé
- P69-D6: "Viable as side project (5-8 hrs/week); not viable as designed startup requiring 25+ hrs/week" — structural mismatch
- P69-D7: "Louis executes technical builds; Alizé requires outbound sales" — structural mismatch
- D84: "Louis cannot credibly close €7,500+ deals without senior commercial co-founder" — Alizé's sales ceiling is a person problem, not a positioning problem
- P67-Kill: "Kuroba is a separate venture" is explicitly being questioned (Pulse 67's Kill section)
- D80 was already identified as a trap: "Platform builder in Years 2-3" framing killed in D134

### Verdict: REJECT

Kuroba and Alizé should not be separate ventures. The case for separation has never been made on strategic grounds — only on execution grounds (Louis was planning two things in parallel). The market evidence shows:
- Alizé has no independent proof, credibility, or network access
- Kuroba already exists and already demonstrates the core capability
- Louis does not have the hours to run both
- The "separate venture" framing creates artificial complexity with no compensating upside

**Recommended action:** Rename/reposition Alizé as "Kuroba Managed" or fold Alizé's managed-service positioning into Kuroba's product line. Use Kuroba's existing deployment as the proof point. One company, one domain, one legal entity, one GTM motion.
