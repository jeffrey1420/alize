# Louis-as-GTM Motion Debate: Network Limitations & Founder Sales Capacity

## Challenged Assumption

**"Louis IS the GTM motion."** The current plan assumes Louis can personally drive warm outreach at sufficient volume to generate 6-8 qualified pilots in Year 1 through his personal network. This assumption underpins D26 (network-first warm outreach as primary channel) and the entire Phase 1 pilot strategy.

This assumption has NOT been stress-tested. It was introduced in Pulse 9 as a resolved decision without rigorous challenge to the underlying variables: network size, conversion rates, sales skill, and post-network transition.

---

## Arguments For (The Optimistic Case)

**1. Warm outreach dramatically outperforms cold.**
- D26 correctly identifies that warm LinkedIn outreach has 5-10x higher reply rates than cold outreach for an unknown brand
- A first-degree connection reduces the "who is this?" friction entirely
- Louis's existing relationships mean some level of trust is pre-established

**2. The first 3 pilots don't need volume — they need fit.**
- 6-8 pilots in Year 1 means ~1 every 6-8 weeks
- Louis doesn't need to generate high volume; he needs high conversion on a small number of conversations
- First pilots can come from 10-15 deeply warm contacts who already know Louis

**3. Founder credibility matters at the €4,500-€10,000 range.**
- For a first-time founder selling a new, unproven service, personal relationships compensate for lack of brand
- A DG is more likely to take a meeting with someone they know (even loosely) than respond to cold outreach
- Louis can be honest about being early-stage; that authenticity can work in his favor with PME buyers

**4. The alternative (cold outbound) is even weaker.**
- GTM Readiness (Pulse 12) identified 4 hard blockers before cold outbound can begin
- For an unknown brand with no case studies, cold outbound reply rates are 2-3%
- If Louis's network fails, the backup plan is worse

## Arguments Against (The Critical Challenge)

### 1. The Network-to-ICP Mismatch Problem

**Louis's network almost certainly skews toward his existing world.** The debate-log notes (Pulse 6, Pilot Strategy) already flagged that "Louis's network points to digital/e-commerce, not professional services." But Pulse 11 (D44) decided the first vertical should be professional services (consulting firms, 10-50 employees), NOT digital agencies/e-commerce.

This is a direct contradiction. If Louis's warm network is predominantly digital/e-commerce (agencies, consultants), and the decided first vertical is professional services (different buyer type, different pain points, different buying cycle), then warm network efficiency drops significantly. A warm contact who is an agency founder will not convert to a professional services consulting firm buyer.

**The math:**
- If 60% of Louis's warm network is digital/e-commerce → eliminated by D44
- If 30% is professional services but wrong size (>50 employees) → too large for first pilot
- That leaves maybe 10-20% of the warm network that actually fits the ICP
- For a network of 300-500 LinkedIn connections that map to ICP: ~30-100 qualified prospects
- To generate 6-8 signed pilots from 30-100 warm prospects requires a 6-8% close rate on discovery calls

**Is 6-8% realistic?** For an unknown brand, first-time founder, €4,500-€10,000 commitment, selling a new category to a skeptical French PME buyer — probably not. A realistic close rate for warm outreach to qualified ICP prospects for an unknown brand is 10-15% on discovery call to proposal, then 50-60% proposal to signed. So overall: 5-8% of discovery calls convert to signed pilots.

That means Louis needs to run **75-160 discovery calls** to sign 6-8 pilots. At 3-5 calls per week, that's 15-32 weeks of full-time prospecting. That's the entire Year 1.

### 2. The Sales Skill Gap — Consultative Sales Requires Training

**Louis is a technical founder, not a sales person.** The brief describes him as someone who builds AI agents. The debate log never identifies any sales experience for Louis. The GTM strategy assumes he can run discovery calls, scope engagements, handle objections, and close — but these are distinct skills.

**What €4,500-€10,000 consultative sales actually requires:**

| Skill | What It Entails | Louis's Likely State |
|-------|-----------------|---------------------|
| Discovery | Uncovering real pain, qualifying fit, asking probing questions | Untrained |
| Scoping | Translating pain into a bounded scope document | Needs practice |
| Objection handling | "Too expensive," "we'll think about it," "our IT won't like it," "what happens if it fails" | Untrained |
| Closing | Moving from proposal to signature, handling delays, political navigation | Untrained |
| DG-level conversation | Speaking to a DG/CEO about business outcomes, not technical features | Untested |

**The DG conversation problem (U48 flagged but unresolved):** French PME DGs ask specific questions:
- "Qui d'autre a déjà fait ça?" (Who else has done this?)
- "Qu'est-ce qui se passe si ça ne fonctionne pas?" (What happens if it doesn't work?)
- "Je vais devoir vendre ça à mon équipe" (I'll have to sell this to my team)
- "C'est quoi le ROI exact?" (What's the exact ROI?)

A first-time founder with no reference clients CANNOT answer "qui d'autre." The DG conversation collapses at the first objection.

### 3. The Warm Network Exhaustion Problem — There Is No Transition Plan

**D26 resolves that "network-first warm outreach is primary Year 1; cold outbound is secondary, Month 2+."** But what happens when the warm network is exhausted?

**Timeline of warm network depletion:**
- Month 1-2: Warm outreach to 50-75 best-fit contacts (already identified as U28: "Louis must map his network" — UNRESOLVED)
- Month 3-4: Extended warm contacts, second-degree connections, friends-of-friends
- Month 5-6: Exhausted. Everyone who will respond has responded.

**After Month 6, the plan is "cold outbound."** But cold outbound for an unknown brand with no case studies has a 2-3% reply rate. At that point, without a brand, without proof, without testimonials — cold outbound is nearly dead on arrival.

**The research on founder-led sales ceilings:**
- Y Combinator data suggests founder-led sales works well for the first 10-20 customers
- After that, the founder becomes the bottleneck and the channel hits diminishing returns
- The transition to "outbound team" or "inbound engine" requires capital, brand, or content — none of which Alizé has in Year 1

**The unresolved transition plan:** The debate log has NO plan for what happens after the warm network is exhausted. U29 asks "When to hire cold outbound support — Month 3-4 after case studies?" but this implies case studies come first. Case studies require pilots. Pilots require the network. The chicken-and-egg problem is unresolved.

### 4. The 12-Month Commitment Tier (D58) Is Unsellable by a First-Time Founder

**D58 introduces a formal commitment tier: 12-month minimum + enhanced SLA + quarterly business reviews.** The stated framing: "for companies that want a true operational partner."

**Why this fails for a first-time founder selling to French PME buyers:**

**The "qui a déjà fait ça?" problem:** A DG considering a 12-month commitment will ask:
- "Do you have clients on 12-month contracts?" → No (first-time founder)
- "Can I speak to a reference?" → No (no case studies yet)
- "What happens in month 6 if I'm unhappy?" → Vague exit clauses
- "How do I know you'll still exist in 12 months?" → Honest answer is uncomfortable

**French business culture on vendor commitments:** French PME buyers are notoriously conservative about technology vendor commitments. They prefer:
- Month-to-month or short initial terms
- Easy exit paths
- Proof before commitment
- "nothing happens without my approval" control structures

D58's 12-month tier is positioned as a premium differentiator. But for a first-time founder with zero reference clients, it reads as "I need lock-in because I don't trust my own service." The DG reads it as risk transfer to them.

**The mismatch:** D58 assumes enough trust exists for 12-month commitment. Louis has zero trust equity as a new brand. This tier might work in Year 2 after 5-6 reference clients. It does not work in Year 1.

## Recommendation

**The Louis-as-GTM assumption is conditionally valid, not universally valid.**

**What's valid:**
- Louis driving the FIRST 2-3 pilots through his warm network is realistic and appropriate
- His personal credibility unlocks first meetings that cold outreach cannot
- For the first pilots, his lack of sales experience is offset by authenticity and low ask size (€4,500 pilot)

**What's NOT valid:**
- Louis driving the FULL Year 1 pipeline of 6-8 pilots through network alone
- Assuming the warm network maps to the ICP (D44 vertical decision conflicts with likely network composition)
- Treating cold outbound as a simple "Month 2+ switch flip" without understanding its near-zero efficiency without brand
- Selling 12-month commitment tiers before any trust equity exists

**Recommended adjustments:**

1. **Restate D26:** Network-first is correct for FIRST pilots (Month 1-3), not Year 1 primary channel. Acknowledge the natural ceiling.

2. **Resolve U28 immediately:** Louis must map his network against ICP BEFORE Month 1. How many warm contacts actually fit: 10-50 employees, professional services, decision-maker level, Île-de-France? If the answer is <30, the warm network plan is insufficient.

3. **Accept lower Year 1 targets if network mapping reveals insufficient qualified contacts:** 3-4 pilots in Year 1 is more realistic than 6-8 if network is thin. Revenue target adjusts accordingly.

4. **Kill D58 for Year 1:** The 12-month commitment tier is aspirational for Year 2, not sellable in Year 1. Default to 3-month initial commitment with month-to-month after, or structured 3+3+6 progression.

5. **Add a bridge channel for Month 4-12:** Since cold outbound is inefficient without proof, identify alternative channels: industry events, peer networks (中介), recommended vendor lists, or strategic partnerships with non-competing vendors who serve the same buyer.

6. **Invest in Louis's sales capability NOW, not later:** If Louis is the GTM motion, he needs sales training before Month 1. Even 10 hours with a sales coach on consultative discovery and objection handling would materially improve conversion rates.
