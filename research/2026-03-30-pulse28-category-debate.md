# DEBATE: "French AI Service Firm for Regulated Industries" Is Not a Category

**Pulse 28 — Category Framing Debate**  
*Challenging D133: "French AI service firm for regulated industries"*

---

## The Core Problem: You've Described a Company, Not a Category

Categories are cognitive shortcuts. They tell buyers: *"This is the kind of thing that solves my problem."* When a French DG or COO hears "AI service firm for regulated industries," here is what fires in their brain:

- **"AI service firm"** → "Another consultancy. They'll send someone to audit us and PowerPoint recommendations. Expensive. Probably won't deliver."
- **"For regulated industries"** → "Okay, they understand my sector. But so does my existing vendor/consultant/audit firm."

This is a **vendor description**, not a **buyer category**. It tells buyers what you are. It does not tell them what they just found, why it matters, or why they should care right now.

Compare to how buyers actually think:

> *"I need toautomate these 47 weekly reporting tasks so my team stops working Saturdays. We keep making errors in the compliance checklist. We can't hire more people."*

No DG ever thought: *"I need an AI service firm for regulated industries."*

---

## Why "Service Firm" Is Particularly Damaging

The word **"service"** in French business culture carries heavy baggage:

1. **SSII/ESN stigma** — French buyers immediately think: body shopping, TJM (daily rates), consultants on-site, scope creep, overruns.
2. **"Not a product"** inference → "So it depends on the people they send. Inconsistent. Risky."
3. **Hard to scale in their mind** — If you're a service firm, you're limited by recruitment. Why would a growing company bet their automation on a services provider?

The framing telegraphs: *"We sell labor, not software."* That is a very hard sell to a PME considering whether to buy AI.

## What French PME Buyers Actually Buy

Let's reverse-engineer from the buyer's actual situation:

**The job-to-be-done for a French DG/COO in a regulated SME (legal, healthcare, finance-adjacent, insurance, compliance-heavy sectors):**

> *"Automate the manual, repetitive, error-prone back-office tasks that my team hates, that create compliance risk, and that scale only if I hire more people."*

French PME buyers respond to:

1. **Concrete problem language** — "Reduce reporting time by 60%", "automate compliance checklists", "stop your team from doing data entry on Saturdays"
2. **ROI that passes the CFO test** — Clear cost/benefit, not "AI transformation"
3. **Speed to value** — "Live in 4-6 weeks" resonates. "12-month engagement" triggers procurement fear.
4. **Regulatory confidence** — They need to trust that the AI won't create new compliance problems. This is a *requirement*, not a differentiator.

**What does NOT work:**
- "AI-powered digital transformation" (too vague)
- "AI governance platform" (too abstract — what does it DO?)
- "AI service firm" (see above)

---

## The Actual Category: "AI Process Automation for Compliance-Heavy SMEs"

This is not elegant, but it is **buyable**. Here's why:

- **"AI"** → signals modernity, the conversation they're already having
- **"Process automation"** → concrete, understood, connects to existing RPA/software buying patterns
- **"Compliance-heavy SMEs"** → maps directly to their identity ("we're a regulated business"), their problem ("we spend too much time on compliance tasks"), and their fear ("we'll get an audit finding")

This framing:
- Is searchable ("AI process automation for legal firms")
- Is comparable ("how is this different from UiPath/Automation Anywhere?")
- Has a clear opposite ("continue doing it manually / hire temps")
- Passes the "so what?" test: *"AI process automation for compliance-heavy SMEs... so you help us stop doing manually?"* Yes.

### Preferred Category Name

**"AI-powered workflow automation for regulated SMEs"**

Alternative candidate: **"Intelligent back-office automation for compliance-focused businesses"** — more descriptive but longer. The first is cleaner for headlines and outreach.

## How This Reshapes the GTM Motion

### Current (Broken) GTM Framing:
> "Alizé is a French AI service firm for regulated industries. We help with governance, risk, and compliance through AI."

**Problems:**
- No specific problem stated
- "Governance, risk, and compliance" is a capability, not a benefit
- Fails the "so what?" test immediately

### Reshaped GTM Framing:

**Primary message:**
> "Alizé automates your repetitive back-office tasks with AI — built for regulated businesses where compliance matters."

**Supporting layers:**
- **Lead with:** Task automation (concrete, demoable, ROI-visible)
- **Close with:** Compliance-grade governance (differentiator, trust-builder)
- **Target ICP:** French regulated SMEs (legal, healthcare, finance, insurance) with 20-200 employees, where back-office staff spend 30%+ of time on manual/repetitive tasks

**GTM Motion Changes:**

1. **Outreach changes** — Cold email subject lines shift from "AI services for regulated industries" to *"Automate your compliance reporting: 60% less manual work"* or *"Stop doing your compliance checklist manually — AI does it for you"*
2. **Demo structure changes** — Show a before/after workflow for a specific task (e.g., "this 2-hour monthly reporting task → 10 minutes with AI review"). Not a platform tour.
3. **Website landing page changes** — Above the fold: "AI workflow automation for regulated SMEs — deploy in 4 weeks, not 4 months." Not "French AI service firm."
4. **Sales conversation changes** — Lead with "What manual tasks does your team do repeatedly?" Not "Have you considered AI governance?"
5. **Partnership/channel angle** — Position as additive to Microsoft 365/Copilot ecosystem. Alizé automates the tasks that Copilot doesn't reach (internal compliance workflows, regulated-sector-specific processes).

---

## The D133 Assumption Being Challenged

**The assumption:** "French AI service firm for regulated industries" is a viable identity/category framing.

**The verdict:** It is not. It is a vendor description masquerading as a category. It describes what Alizé is, not what the buyer gets. It fails the "so what?" test, carries negative service-industry connotations in French B2B culture, and has shifted 5 times because none of the framings land — not because the words are wrong, but because the framing direction is wrong.

**The fix:** Flip from inside-out (what we are) to outside-in (what you get). Lead with problem/solution. Category follows from that, not the other way around.
