# Business Model Audit: Does the Service Model Actually Work?

**Auditor:** Alizé Business Model Auditor
**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Status:** Challenge filed

---

## Assumption Challenged

**D201: "Service model is viable: works at 3/6/12 client scale"**

The assumption under examination is that the managed service/retainer model structurally works at small client counts (3, 6, or 12 clients). This claim is being audited because it serves as the foundation for the Year 1-2 strategy — yet the numbers beneath it are ambiguous at best, and at worst, structurally broken.

---

## Arguments Against

### 1. The word "works" is undefined and probably means "survive" not "thrive"

D201 never specifies what "works" means. This is fatal for a business model assumption. Consider three possible definitions:

| Definition of "works" | 3 clients | 6 clients | 12 clients |
|----------------------|-----------|-----------|------------|
| **Survival** (covers cost-plus minimum of €7,963/month) | ❌ €4,500/mo (56%) | ❌ €9,000/mo (113%) | ✅ €18,000/mo (226%) |
| **Profitability** (above cost-plus + 20% margin) | ❌ | ❌ €10,752/mo (135%) | ✅ €21,600/mo (271%) |
| **Growth** (reinvests in BDM + delivery capacity) | ❌ | ❌ | ✅ |

At the documented pricing (€800-1,500/month per agent, BRIEF.md Offer 3), the 3-client scenario produces €2,400-4,500/month — **well below the €7,963/month cost-plus minimum** identified in D167. Even at the high end of the range, 3 clients barely hit 56% of the minimum viable revenue.

**The model does NOT work at 3 clients under any meaningful definition of "works."**

### 2. Louis has zero commercial experience — D201 assumes he can acquire clients the model needs

D260 establishes that "solo delivery capacity = 2-3 clients before business development stops." But this is the wrong bottleneck. The real problem is not delivery capacity — it's **client acquisition**. Louis has:
- No sales training
- No outbound pipeline experience
- No proven offer or positioning in market
- No case studies, no social proof, no testimonials

D201's claim that the model "works at 3/6/12 client scale" assumes Louis can reach those client counts. D219 (commercial model structurally broken) and D246 (commercial model collapse — path A has zero market validation) already flagged this. D201 does not rebut these findings — it simply assumes the acquisition problem is solved.

**You cannot claim a model "works" at a given scale if the pathway to that scale has been called structurally broken with zero validation.**

### 3. The €2,000/month anchor in D167 is misleading

D167 says "€2,000/month = 25% of cost-plus minimum (€7,963/month)." This suggests D167 was using €2,000/month as a target price. But BRIEF.md Offer 3 prices managed service at €800-1,500/month per agent — the low end of that range (€800) is only 10% of the cost-plus minimum. Even the high end (€1,500) is 19%.

This creates a pricing gap: **at the documented prices, the model cannot cover costs until ~6 clients** (€1,500 × 6 = €9,000/mo ≈ cost-plus minimum). The "3 client" scenario in D201 is mathematically fiction.

### 4. The model is consulting economics masquerading as SaaS economics

D37 says "productized consulting practice, not SaaS, for Years 1-2." This is the correct framing. But D201's "works at 3/6/12 client scale" sounds like SaaS thinking — implying the model scales elegantly once you hit the number.

It does not.

A consulting practice at 3 clients requires the same person to do:
- Pre-sales (discovery calls, proposals)
- Delivery (agent deployment, monitoring, optimization)
- Account management (monthly reviews, relationship maintenance)
- Business development (pipeline building, outreach, follow-up)

At 3 clients, Louis is already at capacity. D260 confirms this: "Solo delivery capacity = 2-3 clients before business development stops." But D201 never says what happens at 3 clients — does business development die? Does delivery quality suffer? Does the client relationship atrophy?

**A consulting practice that stops doing business development at 3 clients has already capped its revenue ceiling at 3 clients × €1,500 = €4,500/month.** That is not a viable business.

---

## Arguments For

### 1. The math does work at 6+ clients (but barely)

At €1,500/month (high end of Offer 3):
- 6 clients = €9,000/month → 113% of cost-plus minimum → marginally viable
- 12 clients = €18,000/month → 226% of cost-plus minimum → genuinely profitable

If Louis can acquire 6+ clients AND maintain delivery quality AND continue acquiring clients, the unit economics are real. The model is not fundamentally broken — it just needs more clients than D201 implies.

### 2. Entry fees create early runway

Offer 2 (Pilot Deployment: €3,000-6,000 setup) and Offer 1 (Diagnostic: €490) provide one-time revenue that can fund the ramp period. Early pilots at discounted rates in exchange for case studies are a legitimate go-to-market tactic. These bridge the gap between zero clients and the 6-client threshold.

### 3. D253's "minimum viable evidence" doctrine is the right approach

The instruction to "kill 'proof before action' doctrine; adopt 'minimum viable evidence'" is the correct strategic response to D219/D246. Louis should get 1-3 pilot clients at any price necessary to generate the evidence needed for real sales. The model works at small scale IF you define "works" as "generating learning and social proof" rather than "generating profit."

---

## Finding

**D201's claim that the model "works at 3/6/12 client scale" is dangerously ambiguous and likely false at the 3-client level.**

The managed service model is not a business in Year 1 — it is a **consulting practice that generates learning and proof**. At 3 clients, the model does not generate meaningful revenue (€2,400-4,500/month vs. €7,963/month cost-plus minimum), and Louis's zero commercial experience means reaching 6-12 clients is not guaranteed.

The correct restatement of D201 should be:
> "The service model generates minimum viable evidence at 1-3 clients. It becomes marginally viable at 6 clients. It becomes genuinely profitable at 10-12 clients — but only if Louis can simultaneously acquire clients AND deliver quality AND manage existing accounts, which is structurally impossible without either a commercial co-founder or a systematized outbound process."

**The core risk is not the model — it is Louis's ability to be both the sole salesperson and the sole delivery person simultaneously.** D201 does not address this. D219 and D246 do. The conflict between these findings has not been resolved.
