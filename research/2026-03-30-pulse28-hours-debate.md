## Hour Scenarios and What Each Means for Alizé's Plan

### Scenario A: 10 hours/week for Alizé

**What this means:**
- Month 1 demand (160-245 hrs) requires 16-24 weeks at this rate
- Delivery rehearsal (internal, web agency): 4-6 weeks just for this
- Warm outreach (30-40 hrs): 3-4 weeks just for this
- Playbook documentation: Cannot happen in parallel with delivery
- **Verdict: This is a side-project plan, not a startup execution plan**

Alizé cannot be the primary venture at 10 hours/week. The research's entire execution timeline (D82's 3-6 month window) assumes significantly more than this. At 10 hours/week:
- First external pilot: Month 4-6
- Second paying client: Month 8-10
- Revenue viability: Year 2 minimum
- The "window" from D82 is not exploitable at this level

**The honest conversation:** If Louis can only give Alizé 10 hours/week, the current plan is not a startup plan. It is a part-time consulting engagement that happens to have a name.

---

### Scenario B: 20 hours/week for Alizé

**What this means:**
- Month 1 demand (160-245 hrs) requires 8-12 weeks
- Delivery rehearsal + outreach in parallel: feasible but tight
- Playbook documentation: happening in evenings/weekends — unsustainable beyond 4-6 weeks
- No room for illness, travel, Grinto crisis, M1 exam period, web agency fire
- **Verdict: Viable only if delivery rehearsal and external pilot are sequential, not parallel**

At 20 hours/week, Alizé needs to choose:
1. Month 1: Delivery rehearsal only. Month 2: Outreach + first external pilot. Month 3: Second client.
2. Or: Month 1: Delivery rehearsal + outreach in parallel, playbook deferred to Month 3.

The 20 hours/week scenario requires explicit sequencing decisions that have not been made. It also requires Louis to formally reduce the web agency or Grinto — there is no "efficiency" solution that produces 20 hours/week from a 56-62 hour remaining pool when the remaining pool already assumes full commitment to those three things.

**20 hours/week is the minimum viable commitment for a startup execution plan — but it is not confirmed, not sustainable without structural changes, and not achievable through prioritization alone.**
