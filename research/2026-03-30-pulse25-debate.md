## Debate 1: Pilot Pricing — D123 Challenged (Free Pilot vs Paid Engagement)

### Assumption Challenged
**D123: "Pilot 1 = free performance test (zero strings, internal use only). Pilot 2+ = full price with case study rights."**

### Agent Recommendation: REPLACE D123 with Option C — Time-Boxed Engagement at €1,500 / 2 Weeks

### Case Against D123 (Free Pilot)

**1. Free signals desperation, not confidence.**
French DG/COO/DGA buyers are seasoned executives. When Alizé says "pilot 1 is free," the subtext they hear is: "We don't trust our own results enough to charge you." A managed AI agent service at €1,500–2,500/month cannot afford this signal.

**2. Free attracts bargain hunters, not premium buyers.**
A free engagement filters for commitment-free prospects who will use the pilot minimally, demand discounts afterward, and shop the results to competitors. A paid engagement — even a small one — automatically filters for buyers with real operational problems and real budgets.

**3. "Internal use only" kills the case study pipeline.**
D123 explicitly limits Pilot 1 to zero-strings internal use. This produces no named reference, no public case study, no external proof. Every Pilot 1 client becomes a dead end for business development — Alizé gives away value with zero return.

**4. Downstream pricing becomes awkward.**
The transition from "free pilot" to "now we charge €4,500–7,500" creates a trust-damaging conversation that Option C entirely avoids.

### Why Full Price (€7,500) Is Also Wrong
Jumping straight to full pilot pricing for an unproven first relationship is aggressive. No established trust, no demonstrated understanding of their business. Sticker shock kills conversations before they start.

### Recommended Alternative: Option C — Time-Boxed Engagement (€1,500 / 2 Weeks)

**Structure:**
- Duration: 2 weeks (10 working days)
- Price: €1,500 flat (€750/day — credible French consulting day rate)
- Framing: "Diagnostic + Proof of Concept" — professional services, not a discount
- Deliverables: Business process audit + technical POC on 1-2 workflows + written findings + ROI estimate

**Why Option C Wins:**
| Dimension | D123 Free Pilot | Option C (€1,500 / 2 Weeks) |
|---|---|---|
| Signal | "Unsure of value" | "Time has value" |
| Client commitment | Zero | Skin in the game |
| Buyer filter | None | Committed buyers only |
| Case study rights | "Internal use only" | Publishable (anonymized) |
| Downstream pricing | Awkward "now we charge" | Natural: diagnostic → pilot → retainer |
| Revenue | €0 | €1,500 partial cost recovery |

**Why €750/day is correct:** It's credible for a senior practitioner in France. Not junior (undermines positioning), not partner-level (creates wrong expectations). French DG buyers understand and respect day rates.

**Why Option C Beats All Others:**
- Option A (€500-750 flat): "Scoping workshop" framing undercuts credibility
- Option B (15% of savings): Silicon Valley equity model — culturally wrong for French PME contracts, measurement complexity, administrative burden
- Option D (€7,500 + money-back guarantee): Right confidence signal, wrong entry point, refund creates adversarial dynamic

**Key insight:** Option C's 2-week diagnostic naturally transitions into full pilot → retainer. Clean pipeline: Diagnostic → Full Pilot → Retainer. No pricing whiplash.

### New Decisions from This Debate

| ID | Topic | Decision |
|----|-------|----------|
| D125 | First pilot structure | Replace D123 free pilot with €1,500 / 2-week time-boxed engagement (€750/day), framed as "Diagnostic + Proof of Concept" |

## Debate 2: Regulated Vertical Wedge — Against the Conventional Wisdom

### Assumption Challenged
**Conventional wisdom: Avoid regulated industries (legal, healthcare, finance, expert-comptables) at launch. Start simple. Add compliance burden later.**

### Agent Recommendation: REVERSE THE CONVENTIONAL WISDOM. Target expert-comptables as first beachhead.

### Case For Regulated Verticals at Launch

**1. The governance gap is real — and disqualifying for incumbents.**
Microsoft Copilot has documented compliance gaps in GDPR-sensitive and AI Act-sensitive contexts. For a French expert-comptable managing client financial data, the question "where does our data go when Copilot processes it?" has a legally complex answer that most DSI teams cannot resolve. In regulated contexts, this gap is not an inconvenience — it is disqualifying.

**2. The moat is structural, not just technical.**
Agentova, ChatGPT, and other horizontal AI tools are inference engines — they process queries and return outputs. They do not maintain persistent audit logs, enforce permission boundaries, or provide evidence packages for regulators. This creates natural protection from bottom-up AI adoption that Alizé does not need to manufacture.

**3. The pricing ceiling is higher.**
Regulated firms pay premiums for compliance-assured tools. An expert-comptable firm paying €15,000/year for Cegid and Pennylane is not price-sensitive to an additional €1,500-2,500/month. They are price-sensitive to risk. The case study writes itself: "We helped a cabinet d'expertise comptable automate their review process while maintaining AI Act conformity" — this is a €7,500 story, not a €200/month story.

**4. The case study is unassailable.**
A case study from a regulated vertical cannot be replicated by Copilot. It demonstrates depth, shows Alizé understands the domain, and validates that Alizé can operate where compliance stakes are real. The first case study defines Alizé's market position for the next 18 months.

### Responding to the Challenges

**Challenge: Liability of regulated-industry compliance on day one?**
The liability IS the product, not a burden on the product. If Alizé is not ready to carry regulated-industry compliance, it is not ready to compete where compliance is the differentiator. Alizé needs to be more compliant than the alternatives — not fully certified under the AI Act.

**Challenge: AI Act enforcement timeline (2027-2028 per D12)?**
Uncertainty argues FOR moving faster. Every month that passes, more regulated SMEs read about the AI Act in their industry publications. Alizé wants to be in those conversations before the market crystallizes around established vendors. The window is 18-24 months. Walk through it now.

**Challenge: Expert-comptables already have Pennylane — why buy Alizé?**
Pennylane automates accounting workflows within its platform. It does not connect to a cabinet médical's patient management system, interface with a property manager's lease database, or pull from a legal practice's document management system. Alizé automates the administrative layer around regulated work — not the core accounting. Different problems.

### Recommended First Vertical: Expert-Comptables
Specifically: administrative workflows around client management and regulatory reporting — NOT core accounting work. High administrative task density, clear referral network (through ordres and industry associations), most legible compliance story. Avoid healthcare and legal at launch (too complex for Year 1).
