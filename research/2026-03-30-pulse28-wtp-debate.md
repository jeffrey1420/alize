# Alizé Research — Pulse 28: WTP vs Copilot — The "Additive" Framing Fails the Price Math

**Date:** 2026-03-30
**Pulse:** 28 — 17:06 UTC
**Topic:** Challenge D129 — "Position Alizé as additive to Copilot, not competitive"
**Agent:** WTP vs Copilot Specialist
**Position:** AGAINST the additive framing — it collapses under price scrutiny

---

## The Assumption Under Challenge

**D129 states:** "Position Alizé as additive to Copilot, not competitive."

**The broader assumption this rests on:** French PME buyers at €1,500-2,500/month will accept "additive to Copilot" framing when Copilot costs €30/user and covers many of the same use cases.

**This assumption is unproven AND mathematically hostile to deal closure.**

---

## Argument: The Additive Framing Fails the Price Math for 90% of ICP

### 1. The additive frame forces buyers to stack budgets, not replace them.

Pulse 26 correctly identified that "naming Microsoft" is fine and that Alizé should position alongside Copilot. But "alongside" and "additive" are different words with different buyer implications:

- **"Alongside"** = I understand how these products differ and I can justify both
- **"Additive"** = I must find incremental budget ON TOP of existing Microsoft spend

For a 50-person French PME, the budget conversation looks like this:

| Cost Item | Monthly | Annual |
|-----------|---------|--------|
| Microsoft 365 Business Premium | €1,105 | €13,260 |
| Copilot M365 (50 users × €30) | €1,500 | €18,000 |
| **Alizé managed service** | **€1,500-2,500** | **€18,000-30,000** |
| **Total AI/Productivity Stack** | **€4,105-5,105** | **€49,260-61,260** |

The owner or CFO sees this and asks: "Why am I paying €5,000/month for productivity tools when my revenue is €5M/year and I'm trying to cut costs?"

**The additive framing doesn't just require justification — it requires budget stacking that French PME buyers at this stage cannot approve.**

### 2. Copilot at €30/user is not just a price benchmark — it's a psychological anchor.

Microsoft has done the work of anchoring "AI for productivity" at €30/user/month. Every buyer who has M365 already sees this line item. When Alizé arrives at €1,500-2,500/month flat, the buyer does not think "wow, that's a great deal for an agent." They think:

- "That's 50-80x the per-user cost of Copilot"
- "I need to justify this to my CFO"
- "What's the minimum I can get away with?"

The additive framing doesn't reset the anchor — it builds on top of it. The buyer now has TWO price points to justify: Copilot (already approved, low urgency) and Alizé (new spend, high justification bar).

**Alizé cannot out-anchor Microsoft on price. It must anchor on a different dimension: outcomes, time-to-value, or total cost of ownership that includes internal implementation costs.**

### 3. The "additive" message implicitly concedes Copilot is sufficient.

When Alizé says "we work alongside Copilot," the buyer hears: "Copilot is good enough. We're just extra."

This is a messaging disaster. Here's why:

- If Copilot is sufficient, why pay €1,500-2,500/month more?
- The buyer who believes Copilot is sufficient will delay or reject Alizé
- The sales conversation becomes "do you want more AI?" instead of "Copilot isn't solving your problem"

**The right frame is NOT "alongside Copilot." It's "Copilot handles what Copilot handles. Alizé handles what Copilot can't."** That's a replacement and complement frame — not an additive one.

### 4. French PME buyers at 50-250 employees don't have layered AI strategies.

D129 assumes the buyer thinks like an IT director: "I have Copilot, now I need a layer above it for workflow automation."

But the actual buyer is a owner, operations director, or practice manager. They think in terms of:
- "I need this problem solved"
- "Who do I pay to make it go away?"
- "What's the simplest thing that works?"

For this buyer, "we're additive to Copilot" is not a value prop — it's a complication. They don't want to learn about AI stacks. They want ONE vendor who solves their problem.

**The additive framing is a sales-team message, not a buyer message. It makes internal sense to Alizé's team (we do agents, Copilot does individual productivity) but it doesn't land with the buyer who doesn't think in these categories.**

## Evidence That Would Prove or Disprove the Additive Positioning Works

### Evidence That Would PROVE the positioning works:

1. **Sales cycle data:** First 10 pilots where "additive to Copilot" framing was used explicitly, and the buyer immediately understood the differentiation without confusion. If the buyer asks "so do I need Copilot first?" the framing has failed.

2. **Win/loss ratio:** If Alizé wins deals where the buyer already has Copilot (or is buying Copilot simultaneously), the additive frame is working. If Alizé only wins in greenfield situations where Copilot hasn't been purchased, the additive frame is irrelevant.

3. **Pilot-to-contract conversion:** If buyers who heard "additive to Copilot" in the first call convert to paid pilots at normal rates, the framing doesn't hurt. If it creates confusion or budget objections, conversion drops.

4. **Price objection frequency:** Track how often "we already pay for Copilot" comes up as a reason to reject Alizé. If it appears in >30% of lost deals, the additive framing is creating the objection itself.

5. **Discovery call language:** In the first 3 minutes of a sales call, if the buyer volunteers "we have Copilot" unprompted, that's a buying signal. If the buyer volunteers "Copilot doesn't work for us" that's an even stronger signal.

### Evidence That Would DISPROVE the positioning works:

1. **Budget conversation stalls:** When the buyer says "I need to get Copilot approved first, then I can think about Alizé" — this means the additive frame has created a sequencing dependency that delays or kills deals.

2. **Per-user price anchoring:** If the buyer consistently tries to negotiate Alizé on a per-user basis ("if Copilot is €30/user, why can't you be €50/user for everyone?"), the Copilot price anchor has won.

3. **Pilot inclusion of Copilot:** If buyers in pilots start comparing Alizé outputs directly to Copilot outputs and scoring Alizé on the same dimensions, the additive frame has positioned Alizé as a competitor in the buyer's mind — not a complement.

4. **Contract value compression:** If Alizé's actual contracted price drifts down toward the "what's our Copilot tax?" range (€800-1,200/month) due to buyer pressure, the additive framing has ceded pricing power.

5. **Lost to Copilot directly:** Any deal where the buyer says "we just decided to buy more Copilot licenses instead" is a direct failure of the additive positioning.

## What Changes in Alizé's Strategy if the Additive Framing Fails

### If "additive to Copilot" fails as a positioning:

**1. The sales playbook changes entirely.**

The current playbook structure (per Pulse 26) acknowledges Copilot in the pitch. If the additive frame fails, Alizé needs to move to a **"Copilot doesn't solve your problem" frame** — which is a competitive frame, not an additive one.

This means:
- Sales training: how to handle "we already have Copilot" as a buying signal (the buyer is frustrated, not satisfied)
- Discovery questions: "What does Copilot not do for you?" becomes a primary discovery path
- Objection handling: "We're not an alternative to Copilot, we're what you need when Copilot isn't enough"

**2. Pricing structure must change.**

If buyers resist stacking €1,500-2,500/month on top of Copilot, Alizé has two options:

- **Option A: Lower the entry price** to €500-800/month and position as "replaces one employee, not adds to Copilot." This is the "ROIanchor" play.
- **Option B: Bundle with Copilot** in a joint offering — but this requires a Microsoft partnership that Alizé doesn't have and may not be able to get.

**Option A is the only viable path if the additive frame fails.** The messaging becomes: "Alizé replaces the need for 10 hours/week of manual work. At €750/month, that's cheaper than a part-time employee. Copilot doesn't do that."

**3. Target ICP shifts slightly.**

The additive frame works best for companies that have already adopted Copilot and are underwhelmed. If the additive frame fails, Alizé should prioritize:
- Companies that evaluated Copilot and rejected it (too complex, too expensive per-user, not enough ROI)
- Companies that have Copilot but low adoption (internal friction, no integrations, users not seeing value)
- Greenfield buyers who don't have M365 or are on Google Workspace (no Copilot to be additive to)

**4. The U102 question becomes the #1 priority.**

U102 — "Buyer willingness to pay €800-1,500/month when Copilot at €30/user already exists?" — was flagged as unresolved. If the additive frame fails, this question must be answered with primary research within 30 days. The answer gates all pricing and positioning decisions.

**The specific test:** Call 20 warm contacts (the D125 pilot pipeline). Ask two questions:
1. "Do you have Copilot today? If yes, do you feel it's solving your workflow problems?"
2. "If Alizé cost €1,500/month and Copilot cost €1,500/month for your team, which would you keep?"

The second question reveals true willingness to pay relative to Copilot, not absolute WTP.

## One Clear Recommendation

**D129 should be revised from "additive to Copilot" to "the agent layer for companies where Copilot isn't enough."**

The new framing:
- **What it is:** "Copilot helps individual users draft emails and summarize meetings. Alizé deploys agents that run your business workflows — connected to your CRM, HRIS, and tools — 24/7, managed, governed."
- **What it replaces:** The "additive" message that requires buyers to stack budgets and justify incremental spend
- **What it does:** Positions Alizé as the solution to a Copilot failure, not a complement to Copilot success

**The practical sales script change:**

| Old | New |
|-----|-----|
| "We work alongside Copilot to extend what's possible" | "Copilot helps your team type faster. Alizé eliminates the work your team shouldn't be doing." |
| "We're additive to your Microsoft investment" | "If Copilot was enough, you wouldn't need us. Most companies find it's not." |
| "Think of us as a managed AI layer on top" | "We deploy agents the way you'd hire an employee — they have access to your tools and they do the work." |

That framing justifies the price, fits the buyer's mental model, and creates urgency where the additive frame creates budget delays.*
