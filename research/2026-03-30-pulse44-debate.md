## What Was Debated

### Debate 1: Downmarket Repositioning Auditor — Is Path B (€500-800/month) Viable?

**Challenged assumptions:** D219's Path B claim that downmarket repositioning solves the commercial model problem.

**Verdict: PATH B KILLED.**

Three independent challenges, each fatal:

1. **Unit economics collapse at scale.** At €500-800/month, the managed service model requires 25+ clients for viable revenue. Each client needs 2-4 hours/month of skilled human delivery. That's 50-200 hours/month of labor — 1.5-2.5 FTE — with no margin compression possible. The price point can't sustain a real delivery team without Louis working himself into the ground.

2. **Downmarket does not shorten sales cycles.** Lower price does not proportionally compress the decision to buy an AI agent service. A 50-person company doesn't sign a vendor contract faster because it costs €500 instead of €2,000. The same discovery call, business case, and internal alignment are required. More clients are needed for proof cases anyway — same sales effort, less revenue per deal.

3. **Worse credibility problem.** At €500-800/month, Alize competes directly with Agentova (€49-99/month per agent) and ChatGPT Teams (€22/user). The "managed service premium" that justifies Alize's price over Agentova only holds at €2,000+/month. At €500/month, Alize must justify a 5-10x premium over Agentova WITHOUT the psychological cushion of premium pricing. The competitive position is worse, not better.

**Additional finding:** The ICP (50-200 employee companies) can AFFORD €500-800/month. They cannot justify it more easily than €2,000/month, because the decision maker is the same (founder/CEO) and the competitive comparison (Agentova, ChatGPT) is identical at both price points.

**New decisions:**
- D232: Kill D219's Path B (downmarket repositioning to €500-800/month) — unit economics fail, competitive position worsens, sales cycles don't compress
- D233: The path to proof cases is one excellent reference client at the original price point, not volume at lower price

### Debate 2: Evidence-Positioning Loop Auditor — Is D227's Circular Logic Real?

**Challenged assumption:** D227's claim that all positioning work must stop until pilot evidence exists.

**Verdict: D227 IS TOO BROAD. The loop dissolves with proper framing.**

**The smuggled assumption:** D227 treats positioning as binary — either "proven" (has pilot evidence) or "speculative" (no evidence). This is false. Positioning exists on a spectrum:

| Level | What | Needs Pilot Evidence? |
|-------|------|---------------------|
| Quantified ROI claim | "10 hours/week saved" | YES |
| Reference client quote | Named company with testimonial | YES |
| Category label | "AI workflow automation for regulated SMEs" | NO |
| Differentiator | "Managed vs. self-service" | NO |
| Problem framing | "Senior staff buried in repetitive admin tasks" | NO |

D227 conflates levels 1-2 (need evidence) with levels 3-5 (don't). The chicken-and-egg loop only applies to quantified claims and reference quotes. The rest can and should be used immediately to drive discovery calls.

**The inversion:** D227 assumes pilots are input to positioning. The reverse is true: positioning conversations are input to pilots. You qualify pilot fit through positioning. You don't close positioning first and then find pilots.

**The middle path:** Adopt D226's hero sentence as the current working hypothesis, run 5 discovery calls, update after feedback. Discovery calls ARE the positioning refinement mechanism — not the obstacle to it.

**New decisions:**
- D234: Replace D227's scope with a narrower claim — "Stop making quantified ROI claims until one pilot with named client and specific metrics exists." The broader halt on ALL positioning work is unjustified.
- D235: Treat discovery calls as the positioning refinement mechanism, not as something to defer until positioning is "ready"

### Debate 3: Behavioral Binary Accountability Auditor — Does D221/D223 Have Teeth?

**Challenged assumptions:** D221/D223's behavioral binary (6+ hours + delivery vs. explicit opt-out) as an accountability forcing function.

**Verdict: STRUCTURAL IMPROVEMENT OVER D196, BUT INHERITS THE SAME ENFORCEMENT GAP. Without fixes, it's documentation theater.**

**Four challenges, each targeting a different tooth:**

1. **Measurability without accountability** — 6 hours is objective, but measuring hours doesn't change behavior without an external consequence sink. The "advisor/peer" is unnamed and has no authority. Self-reporting without enforcement is behaviorally equivalent to no tracking.

2. **"One concrete delivery" undefined** — No pre-agreed definition of what counts. After 43 pulses of relabeled failures, Louis can always spin a week as having a delivery. Without third-party validation authority, the delivery is self-certified.

3. **Opt-out friction is near-zero** — Writing "I did not work on Alize this week" takes 5 seconds. The social cost is low if the recipient has no authority. "Dormant status" is mentioned but never defined, never connected to an external party, never made visible.

4. **D221 is D196 with higher administrative overhead** — The binary measures behavior instead of identity and runs weekly instead of one-time. Both improvements are real. Neither addresses the enforcement gap. The fatal flaw of D196 (no external consequence) is fully inherited.

**The missing teeth:**
- No named enforcement authority with genuine follow-up commitment
- No escalating consequences (what happens after 2 consecutive Choice B weeks? 10?)
- No pre-agreement on what counts as delivery — only retroactive self-certification
- The binary only measures Louis; it doesn't address the structural capacity problem (three active commitments)

**New decisions:**
- D236: The behavioral binary needs three structural fixes to function: (1) named advisor/peer with authority to follow up, (2) escalating visible consequences after consecutive failures, (3) prospective delivery definition agreed before the week starts
- D237: Without these fixes, the binary is a documentation mechanism, not an accountability mechanism — it will produce 43 more weeks of failure reports
