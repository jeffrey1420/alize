# Pricing Stage Debate — Pulse 35

## The Assumption Challenged

**D177 claims:** A 3-stage pilot structure (€500 discovery → €1,000 POC → €1,200-1,500/mo month 1) solves the credibility ceiling problem (D84) by breaking a large commitment into smaller, independently purchasable stages — each "proof gate" protects against non-conversion while keeping total investment manageable for a Louis-pitched deal.

**The underlying assumption:** The problem with closing a €7,500 pilot is the *size* of the number. Reduce the number, reduce the risk, increase conversion.

**This assumption is wrong.** The problem is not the size of the number — it's that *every stage* requires Louis to re-earn trust from scratch, and the staging structure actively trains the client to expect heavily subsidized work before the real commitment begins.

---

## The Flaw

**The real problem with D177 is the "credited toward" mechanic embedded in Stages 1 and 2.**

The pricing architect intended "credited" as a trust signal: *"This isn't money you lose — it's money held toward the real engagement."* But in practice, "credit" is accounting poison. It means:

- Stage 1 and Stage 2 are **not standalone deliverables** — they are installments toward something else
- The client is not buying a discovery sprint or a POC — they are prepaying a future contract
- When the retainer conversation starts at Month 1, the client will correctly observe: *"I've already paid €1,500. Why am I paying again?"*

**Worse: the "credited" framing signals that Phases 1 and 2 are not independently valuable.** If they were, you wouldn't need to credit them. You'd charge full price and let them stand alone.

The French PME buyer — a DG or COO who has sat through hundreds of vendor pitches — will decode this instantly. "Credited toward" reads as: *"They're not confident enough to charge me real money for this phase. Something is wrong with what they're selling me."*

---

## Evidence

**Problem 1: Three-stage structure multiplies the number of close attempts Louis must make.**

D84 established: Louis cannot credibly close €7,500+ deals alone. The D177 response: split the deal into three smaller closes. But this doesn't eliminate the credibility problem — it *multiplies* it. Louis must now close Stage 1 (€500), then close Stage 2 (€1,000), then close Stage 3 (€1,200-1,500/month). Three separate decision points, three separate moments where Louis's inexperience shows. The client who said yes to €500 will not automatically say yes to €1,000 — they now have €500 of evidence that Alizé exists and needs the next stage. The power dynamic inverts.

**Problem 2: "Credited" creates a psychological anchor that poisons Month 1 pricing.**

Client math at Stage 3 gate: *"I've already paid €1,500 for the discovery and POC. Now they want €1,200-1,500/month on top? That's €2,700-3,000 before the relationship really starts."* The client doesn't experience the €1,500 as prepaid — they experience it as money already spent on unproven work. Month 1 retainer now carries a hidden resentment load. The "credited" mechanic that was supposed to build trust actually builds resentment.

**Problem 3: A client can exit Stage 2 with a working POC they own and no obligation to continue.**

D177's proof gate at Stage 2 is: *"Working agent in test, connected to live tool."* Once the client has a working agent — even in test — they have what they need to evaluate whether to continue. The POC is built on Alizé's infrastructure, but the client now has concrete evidence the thing works. They can take that evidence to a competitor, build it in-house, or simply wait. The proof gate that was supposed to *secure* the retainer actually gives the client a graceful exit with all the proof they need.

**Problem 4: The foot-in-door mechanic trains the client to expect cheap work before committing.**

D174's original free pilot proposal was rejected because it "trains the client to expect free work before committing." D177's €500 + €1,000 structure does the same thing, just with a price tag attached. The client who enters a €500 discovery sprint has been conditioned to believe that Phase 1 exists to *lead into* something bigger — that the real engagement hasn't started yet. They are a prospect in qualification, not a client buying a completed deliverable. This mindset persists through Stage 2 and into Stage 3, where the retainer conversation happens between parties who have not yet established a real client relationship.

**Problem 5: Louis cannot differentiate D177 from a €490 diagnostic he already tried to charge.**

Pulse 7 established that "diagnostic = free lead generation" was the correct framing for the €490 diagnostic. D125 then raised it to €1,500/2-week diagnostic. D177 now introduces €500 discovery sprint as a *new* lower-priced entry. The French PME buyer who has seen Alizé's €490 offer, then €1,500 offer, and now €500 discovery sprint will correctly conclude: *"Alizé keeps changing their pricing and their offer. They don't know what they're selling."* Price fragmentation is a credibility signal as damaging as price desperation.

---

## Recommended Alternative

**Kill D177. Replace with a single €2,000 flat pilot with defined deliverables and a 50/50 payment structure.**

### The Structure

| Payment | Timing | Deliverable |
|---------|--------|-------------|
| €1,000 | Signed contract | Scoping document, integration map, success metrics — all signed off before build starts |
| €1,000 | Working agent handed over | Agent running in production, team trained, documentation delivered |

### Why €2,000 Flat Beats D177

**One close, not three.** Louis makes one credibility argument, one decision point, one moment where he must perform. At €2,000, he is asking for €1,000 of commitment before he does any work — a real signal of intent from the client. At €2,000, the client's skin in the game is meaningful without being overwhelming.

**No credits, no accounting complexity.** The client pays for Phase 1 (scoping) and Phase 2 (delivery) as two discrete, standalone deliverables. No credit ledger. No "this applies toward..." conversation at Month 1. Each payment is complete in itself.

**The proof gate is built into the structure, not a separate stage.** The working agent delivered at Day 14 IS the proof gate. If Alizé delivers a working agent at the end of Week 2, the retainer conversation happens with the client holding something real — not holding a "credit" for something they prepaid.

**The client's exit option is clean but costly.** If the client walks away after paying €1,000 for scoping and receiving a signed scoping document, Alizé has been compensated for the scoping work. The client who exits after the second €1,000 with a working agent has paid fair value for what they received. Neither party has been cheated. No credit ledger required.

**Louis's credibility ceiling is respected.** €2,000 total pilot is within D84's credibility ceiling for a warm-network pitch by Louis. €7,500 is not. €1,500 across three stages is also not — because the *pattern* of three payments signals a larger total commitment the client will calculate before signing Stage 1.

### The Retainer Conversation

After €2,000 pilot delivery, the retainer pitch is clean:

*"The pilot showed you what the agent can do. The monthly retainer is €1,500/month — that covers monitoring, optimization, and support for the agent we've already built for you. Month 1 is included free if you sign within 30 days of pilot delivery."*

This framing has no credits, no complexity, no accounting fiction. The free Month 1 is a genuine acquisition incentive, not a deferred discount on work already paid for.

### What D177 Gets Right (Preserve This)

D177 correctly identified that a single large close is too big for Louis. The response — one €2,000 flat pilot — preserves this constraint while eliminating the structural damage of three-stage "credited" payments.

D177's proof gate concept is correct: the client should see working output before committing to a retainer. This is achieved by structuring the second €1,000 payment *on delivery* of a working agent, not on entry into a Stage 3 retainer.

| Problem | D177 | €2,000 Flat |
|---------|------|-------------|
| Number of close attempts | 3 (one per stage) | 1 |
| Accounting complexity | High (credits, ledgers, calculations) | Zero |
| Client exit with POC | Stage 2 exit leaves client with working agent | Second payment releases agent — client pays before receiving |
| Foot-in-door conditioning | €500 + €1,000 trains client to expect cheaper work first | One clean €1,000 upfront payment signals serious intent |
| Louis credibility ceiling | Pattern of escalation signals total €2,500-4,000 | €2,000 total — credible for warm pitch |
| Month 1 retainer framing | "Credits" poison the conversation | Clean: pilot is done, retainer follows naturally |

**Kill D177. The staged "credited" structure creates more trust problems than it solves. One flat fee, two payments, one close.**
