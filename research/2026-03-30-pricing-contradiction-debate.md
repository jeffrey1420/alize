The "proof period" framing is an elegant narrative but it doesn't pay the bills.

**Verdict:** D166 is more grounded in actual unit economics. D174 ignores the math entirely.

## Challenge 2: Does D174's "Free Pilot" Solve the Trust Problem or Create a Worse Expectation Management Problem?

**It creates a worse problem.**

The trust problem D174 correctly identifies: Louis hasn't earned the credibility to charge €5,000-7,500 for a first engagement with an unknown brand. A prospect cannot trust that a 2-person operation with no case studies is worth €7,500 upfront. D84 in the debate log is explicit: "Louis's profile IS a credibility ceiling at €7,500+ without a senior commercial co-founder."

But a free pilot creates three expectation problems that are worse than the trust problem:

1. **Zero commitment signal.** A prospect who pays nothing has zero financial skin in the game. They can abandon the engagement at any time, deprioritize it internally, or simply not show up to meetings. D30 (Pulse 9) noted that the pilot workflow must be "operationally critical (not 'nice to have')." A free engagement on a nice-to-have workflow produces exactly zero commitment from the client.

2. **The charity problem.** "Free" signals that Alizé needs the case study more than the client needs the service. This inverts the power dynamic. A client who receives a free pilot feels they are doing Louis a favor. This is not a foundation for a €1,500-2,500/month retainer relationship.

3. **"Proof" is not the same as "value."** D174 frames the proof period as months 1-3 at €1,200-1,500/month. But the client is not receiving proof — they are receiving a working AI agent that is presumably doing useful work. If it's doing useful work, why should they only pay €1,200/month? If it's not doing useful work, why is Alizé continuing to charge? The proof period framing sets up a mismatch between what Alizé is delivering and what Alizé is being paid.

**The real trust problem is not "can Louis charge €7,500?"** The real trust problem is: "can Louis deliver a working AI agent that does a defined job in 30 days?" That is a delivery credibility question, not a pricing credibility question. They are different problems and D174 conflates them.

**Verdict:** D174's free pilot creates worse expectation management problems than it solves. It avoids the pricing credibility question by removing price entirely, which creates a commitment and framing vacuum.

## Challenge 4: What Is the Minimum Viable Pilot Structure That Doesn't Destroy Trust AND Doesn't Lose Money on Every Engagement?

**This requires a third option that borrows from both D166 and D174.**

### The Core Problem

The pilot+retainer model (D174's underlying architecture) is structurally sound for the business but requires trust Alizé hasn't earned. The unit economics (D166's analysis) are structurally correct but require credibility Alizé doesn't have yet.

Neither pure path works. The solution is a **staged commitment model** with explicit proof gates and a clear value progression:

---

### Recommended Structure: Staged Pilot with Proof Gates

**Stage 1 — Discovery Sprint (Week 1)**
- **Price:** €500 (non-refundable, credited toward Stage 2)
- **Commitment:** 5 days of Alizé time + client team time (3-4 hours)
- **Deliverable:** Scoped workflow definition, integration map, success metrics, deployment plan — all documented in a signed scope document
- **Gate:** Client must sign off on the workflow and metrics before Stage 2 begins

*Rationale:* €500 is enough to signal real commitment and filter out casual browsers. It is not enough to be a credibility barrier. The signed scope document creates legal and psychological commitment to the workflow.

**Stage 2 — Proof of Concept (Weeks 2-3)**
- **Price:** €1,000 (credited toward month 1 retainer if converting)
- **Commitment:** 8-10 days Alizé time
- **Deliverable:** Working agent running in test environment, connected to at least one live business tool, with documented outputs
- **Gate:** Live demo to client. If they approve, they commit to Stage 3. If not, engagement ends — client has paid €1,500 total for a working proof of concept, Alizé has covered most of its cost.

*Rationale:* Breaking the pilot into two stages means the client is never writing a large check for an unproven result. The €1,000 Stage 2 payment signals commitment without being a barrier. The live demo gate creates natural decision moment.

**Stage 3 — Month 1 Retainer (Month 1)**
- **Price:** €1,200-1,500/month
- **Commitment:** 12-month minimum with performance exit clause after month 3
- **Structure:** €1,200/month includes one agent, one workflow, monitoring, and support. Stage 1+2 payments (€1,500 total) credited as first month payment.
- **Gate:** Performance metrics reviewed at week 4. If metrics are missed, month 2 is at 50% rate. If metrics miss again, client can exit.

*Rationale:* €1,200-1,500/month is below the €7,963/month cost-plus minimum, but at Stage 3 the client is no longer paying for setup — they are paying for a running service. The credit mechanism (D52, Pulse 13) converts the pilot investment into service investment, aligning incentives.

**Stage 4 — Expansion (Month 4+)**
- **Price:** €1,800-2,500/month
- **Commitment:** 12-month minimum
- **Includes:** Additional workflow, additional integrations, quarterly business review, enhanced SLA

---

### Why This Works

| Problem | Solution |
|---------|----------|
| €1,500 flat pilot = charity (D166) | Staged payments mean no single engagement is a loss; total pilot investment (€1,500) is spread over 3 weeks with proof gates |
| €7,500 pilot unsellable by Louis (D84) | Largest single payment is €1,000 — within Louis's credibility ceiling |
| Free pilot = zero client commitment | Every stage requires payment — client has skin in the game at every gate |
| Proof period framing inverts power | Stages 1-2 ARE the proof; Stage 3 is a service contract, not a proof period |
| Case study rights unclear (U113) | Written into Stage 2 scope sign-off; explicit commercial term |
| Client doesn't know what they're buying | Signed scope document at Stage 1 creates explicit deliverables |

## Recommended Resolution

**Pick neither D166 nor D174. Implement the staged pilot structure above.**

| Pilot Element | D166 Position | D174 Position | Recommended Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| Pilot fee | €5,000-7,500 | €0 | €1,500 total (€500 + €1,000 staged) |
| Monthly during proof | Not applicable | €1,200-1,500 | €1,200-1,500 (credited from pilot) |
| Commitment structure | Upfront payment | Free + ongoing | Staged gates with explicit sign-offs |
| Trust mechanism | Price signals value | Remove price barrier | Payment staged with proof gates |

**The single most important thing this structure does:** It converts D174's "proof period" into a series of explicit, paid proof gates — where payment and proof happen simultaneously, not sequentially.

D166 was right that free pilots are charity. D174 was right that large upfront fees are unsellable. Both were wrong about the alternative being viable on its own.
